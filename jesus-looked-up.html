Jesus Looked Up

Luke 19:1–10 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-22

The crowd shuts Zacchaeus out, but Jesus calls him by name and comes to his home. Reflect on Christ’s welcome, making things right, and sharing the grace that changes our lives.

Forgiveness & graceHumility & obedienceLove & service
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“For the Son of Man came to seek and save those who are lost.”
Luke 19:10 (NLT)

Have you ever felt that people had already made up their minds about you? Zacchaeus knew that feeling well. One day, on a crowded street in Jericho, Jesus stopped, looked up, and called him by name.

Jesus was on His way to Jerusalem, where He would give His life. Luke tells us, “Jesus entered Jericho and made his way through the town.” Luke 19:1, NLT “There was a man there named Zacchaeus. He was the chief tax collector in the region, and he had become very rich.” Luke 19:2, NLT

Tax collectors worked for the Roman authorities, and many collected more than people owed. Their own people treated them as traitors and cheats. Zacchaeus was their chief. But God sees more than a reputation. Ellen G. White writes:

“Yet the wealthy customs officer was not altogether the hardened man of the world that he seemed. Beneath the appearance of worldliness and pride was a heart susceptible to divine influences.”The Desire of Ages, p. 552 · DA 552.4, excerpt

A “susceptible” heart is one that can still be moved. Ellen G. White tells us that Zacchaeus had heard John the Baptist’s call to repentance. When tax collectors asked John what they should do, he told them, “Collect no more taxes than the government requires.” Luke 3:13, NLT, excerpt Zacchaeus had also heard how kindly Jesus treated people others despised. “In this chief of the publicans was awakened a longing for a better life.” The Desire of Ages, p. 552 · DA 552.4, excerpt “Publican” is an older word for a tax collector. Even before he met Jesus, “Zacchaeus began at once to follow the conviction that had taken hold upon him, and to make restitution to those whom he had wronged.” The Desire of Ages, p. 552 · DA 552.4, excerpt To make restitution means to pay back what was wrongly taken.

Long before the crowd could see it, God was already at work in Zacchaeus’ heart.

Then news spread through Jericho that Jesus was coming. Zacchaeus wanted to see Him. “He tried to get a look at Jesus, but he was too short to see over the crowd.” Luke 19:3, NLT Ellen G. White adds a painful detail. “None would give way for him”. The Desire of Ages, p. 553 · DA 553.2, excerpt

She also describes how his neighbours doubted him when he first tried to put things right. “To be misunderstood, to be met with suspicion and distrust in the effort to correct his errors, was hard to bear.” The Desire of Ages, p. 553 · DA 553.1, excerpt Perhaps you know how it feels to take that first step while others still doubt you.

Zacchaeus did not give up. “So he ran ahead and climbed a sycamore-fig tree beside the road, for Jesus was going to pass that way.” Luke 19:4, NLT The tree’s low, spreading branches gave this wealthy official a place to see over the crowd.

When no one would make room for Zacchaeus, he still found a way to look for Jesus.

“When Jesus came by, he looked up at Zacchaeus and called him by name. ‘Zacchaeus!’ he said. ‘Quick, come down! I must be a guest in your home today.’” Luke 19:5, NLT

Above the noise of the crowd, Jesus heard the longing Zacchaeus had never put into words. Ellen G. White writes, “Above the clamor of priests and rabbis and the shouts of welcome from the multitude, that unuttered desire of the chief publican spoke to the heart of Jesus.” The Desire of Ages, p. 553 · DA 553.3, excerpt

Jesus spoke to Zacchaeus without waiting for anyone’s approval. He said, “I must,” and He said, “today.” His kindness came first. Paul writes about this same kindness: “Can’t you see that his kindness is intended to turn you from your sin?” Romans 2:4, NLT, excerpt

Zacchaeus did not hesitate. “Zacchaeus quickly climbed down and took Jesus to his house in great excitement and joy.” Luke 19:6, NLT Ellen G. White says that Jesus still speaks to people in the same way. “As the word of Christ came to Zacchaeus, ‘Today I must abide at thy house’ (Luke 19:5), so the word will come to them; and those who were supposed to be hardened sinners will be found to have hearts as tender as a child’s because Christ has deigned to notice them.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 236 · COL 236.1, excerpt To “deign” is to lower oneself to give attention to someone. The Son of God stopped for a man everyone else had passed by.

While the crowd still doubted him, Jesus called Zacchaeus by name.

“But the people were displeased. ‘He has gone to be the guest of a notorious sinner,’ they grumbled.” Luke 19:7, NLT

Jesus had heard this complaint before. “Tax collectors and other notorious sinners often came to listen to Jesus teach.” Luke 15:1, NLT “This made the Pharisees and teachers of religious law complain that he was associating with such sinful people—even eating with them!” Luke 15:2, NLT Ellen G. White explains why people who had been shunned were drawn to Him. “Christ greeted them as children of God, estranged indeed from the Father’s house, but not forgotten by the Father’s heart.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 186 · COL 186.2, excerpt “Estranged” means separated or cut off.

It is easy to stand with the grumbling crowd and decide that some people are too far gone. Jesus never thought that way about anyone. He saw hope in Zacchaeus. He sees hope in the person we have written off, and in us too.

The crowd saw a notorious sinner. Jesus saw a lost son He had come to bring home.

In his own home, Zacchaeus spoke openly about his money and the people he had wronged. “Meanwhile, Zacchaeus stood before the Lord and said, ‘I will give half my wealth to the poor, Lord, and if I have cheated people on their taxes, I will give them back four times as much!’” Luke 19:8, NLT

Ellen G. White describes what moved him to speak.

“Zacchaeus had been overwhelmed, amazed, and silenced at the love and condescension of Christ in stooping to him, so unworthy. Now love and loyalty to his new-found Master unseal his lips. He will make public his confession and his repentance.”The Desire of Ages, p. 554 · DA 554.2

Here, “condescension” means Christ’s humility in coming down to him. Zacchaeus went far beyond what the law required. The usual rule was to “make full restitution for what they have done, adding an additional 20 percent and returning it to the person who was wronged.” Numbers 5:7, NLT, excerpt The law called for fourfold repayment in the case of a stolen sheep. A thief “must pay back… four sheep for each sheep stolen.” Exodus 22:1, NLT, excerpt Zacchaeus chose that same measure for repaying anyone he had cheated.

Jesus was already a guest in his home when Zacchaeus made that promise. His generosity grew out of the love he had received. John puts it simply: “We love each other because he loved us first.” 1 John 4:19, NLT That change of heart reached all the way to the people Zacchaeus owed. Ellen G. White writes, “No repentance is genuine that does not work reformation. The righteousness of Christ is not a cloak to cover unconfessed and unforsaken sin; it is a principle of life that transforms the character and controls the conduct.” The Desire of Ages, p. 555 · DA 555.6, excerpt

Is there someone we have wronged? Ellen G. White gives this counsel: “we should confess our wrong, and make restitution as far as lies in our power.” The Desire of Ages, p. 556 · DA 556.2, excerpt Grace gives us the courage to name the hurt we have caused and do what we can to put it right.

Zacchaeus did not give to earn Jesus’ welcome. He gave because he had already received it.

Then Jesus spoke about what had happened in that home. “Jesus responded, ‘Salvation has come to this home today, for this man has shown himself to be a true son of Abraham.’” Luke 19:9, NLT His neighbours had treated him as an outsider. Jesus called him a son of Abraham, a true member of God’s family. Paul explains who belongs to that family: “The real children of Abraham, then, are those who put their faith in God.” Galatians 3:7, NLT

“It is when Christ is received as a personal Saviour that salvation comes to the soul. Zacchaeus had received Jesus, not merely as a passing guest in his home, but as One to abide in the soul temple.”The Desire of Ages, p. 556 · DA 556.4, excerpt

Then Jesus explained why He had stopped under that tree. “For the Son of Man came to seek and save those who are lost.” Luke 19:10, NLT Long before, God had promised, “I will search for my lost ones who strayed away, and I will bring them safely home again.” Ezekiel 34:16, NLT, excerpt In Jesus, that promise walked into Jericho. Soon He would go on to Jerusalem and the cross. “But God showed his great love for us by sending Christ to die for us while we were still sinners.” Romans 5:8, NLT

Perhaps you feel like Zacchaeus today, as if there is no room for you. Jesus is not waiting for the crowd to change its mind. He knows your name, and He is still saying, “Quick, come down!” Or perhaps you know someone the crowd has shut out. Make room for them, and let them meet the Jesus who stopped for Zacchaeus.

Jesus still sees those the crowd overlooks and calls them home by name.

Reflect

  1. Is there a part of my past that makes me feel there is no room for me with Jesus? What would it mean to hear Him call my name today?
  2. Who have I quietly written off, as the crowd once wrote off Zacchaeus? How can I make room for that person this week?
  3. Have I hurt someone in a way I can still put right? Knowing that Jesus has welcomed me, what first step can I take?

Key Takeaways

  1. Jesus sees and calls the one others pass by. In the middle of a crowded street, Jesus looked up and called Zacchaeus by name. Luke 19:5 · quoted above
  2. Grace comes first, and real change follows. Zacchaeus gave generously and made things right because Jesus had already welcomed him. Luke 19:8 · quoted above
  3. Jesus came to seek and save the lost. Salvation came to Zacchaeus’ home the day he received Jesus, and He still seeks those who are lost today. Luke 19:9–10 · quoted above

Textual note: The NLT’s “sycamore-fig” (Luke 19:4) is the fig-mulberry tree of the Jordan valley, not the sycamore of Europe or North America. In Luke 19:8 the Greek verbs are in the present tense; the NLT renders them as Zacchaeus’ resolve, “I will give.” “Son of Man” is a title Jesus used for himself (NLT footnote, Luke 19:10). Ellen G. White quotes the King James Version, which calls tax collectors “publicans.”

A prayer for today

Heavenly Father, thank You for seeing us when others pass us by. Lord Jesus, thank You for stopping for Zacchaeus, for calling him by name, and for coming to seek and save the lost. Thank You for going on to the cross for us while we were still sinners. When we feel there is no room for us, help us hear Your call and welcome You with joy. When we have wronged someone, give us the courage to tell them so and to make things right. Keep us from grumbling against those You welcome, and teach us to make room for them. Come into our homes and our hearts today, and stay. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
