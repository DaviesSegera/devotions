Under the Broom Tree: When God Meets Us at Our Lowest

1 Kings 19:1–18 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-02

After Mount Carmel, Elijah fled in fear and exhaustion. Reflect on God's patient care, rest for the weary, the gentle whisper, and the hope and companionship He gives for the road ahead.

Hope & encouragementPrayerFaith & trustWaiting on God

Key Verse
“Then the angel of the LORD came again and touched him and said, ‘Get up and eat some more, or the journey ahead will be too much for you.’”
1 Kings 19:7 (NLT)

Have you ever reached a high point in your faith, only to find yourself exhausted and discouraged soon afterwards? The prophet Elijah’s lowest moment came just after the greatest victory of his life.

On Mount Carmel, God had answered Elijah’s prayer before the whole nation: “Immediately the fire of the LORD flashed down from heaven and burned up the young bull, the wood, the stones, and the dust. It even licked up all the water in the trench!” 1 Kings 18:38, NLT

Then one message changed everything. Queen Jezebel, who had promoted the worship of the Canaanite god Baal in Israel, sent Elijah a threat: “May the gods strike me and even kill me if by this time tomorrow I have not killed you just as you killed them.” 1 Kings 19:2, NLT, excerpt “Elijah was afraid and fled for his life.” 1 Kings 19:3, NLT, excerpt He left his servant at Beersheba and went on alone.

After a day’s walk into the wilderness, “He sat down under a solitary broom tree and prayed that he might die. ‘I have had enough, LORD,’ he said. ‘Take my life, for I am no better than my ancestors who have already died.’” 1 Kings 19:4, NLT, excerpt A broom tree is a desert shrub; older English Bibles call it a juniper.

Ellen G. White explains how a faithful person can reach such a place:

“But he who had been blessed with so many evidences of God’s loving care was not above the frailties of mankind, and in this dark hour his faith and courage forsook him.”Prophets and Kings, p. 159 · PK 159.3, excerpt

She notes that Elijah had hoped the events on Carmel would turn the whole nation back to God. She also describes his exhaustion: “All day on Carmel’s height he had toiled without food.” Prophets and Kings, p. 160 · PK 160.2, excerpt Disappointment, exhaustion and fear had all taken their toll. “Elijah was as human as we are,” James 5:17, NLT, excerpt the New Testament reminds us.

Even a faithful servant of God can come to the end of his strength.

What happens next is one of the gentlest scenes in the Old Testament. “Then he lay down and slept under the broom tree. But as he was sleeping, an angel touched him and told him, ‘Get up and eat!’” 1 Kings 19:5, NLT “He looked around and there beside his head was some bread baked on hot stones and a jar of water! So he ate and drank and lay down again.” 1 Kings 19:6, NLT

Then the angel returned: “Then the angel of the LORD came again and touched him and said, ‘Get up and eat some more, or the journey ahead will be too much for you.’” 1 Kings 19:7, NLT Elijah needed sleep, food, water and a caring touch. God provided them before saying anything about his prayer.

Ellen White answers the question many discouraged people quietly ask:

“Did God forsake Elijah in his hour of trial? Oh, no! He loved His servant no less when Elijah felt himself forsaken of God and man than when, in answer to his prayer, fire flashed from heaven and illuminated the mountaintop.”Prophets and Kings, p. 166 · PK 166.1, excerpt

God’s love did not rise and fall with Elijah’s success. The psalmist explains why: “For he knows how weak we are; he remembers we are only dust.” Psalm 103:14, NLT Our bodies matter to God. Sometimes the most faithful next step is rest, a meal, or an honest conversation. If life feels too heavy to bear, as it did for Elijah, please tell someone you trust today. God often sends His help through other people.

God met Elijah’s weariness with care before He spoke to him about the way ahead.

Strengthened by that food, Elijah travelled “forty days and forty nights to Mount Sinai, the mountain of God.” 1 Kings 19:8, NLT, excerpt In a cave on the mountain where God had once given His law through Moses, he heard God ask, “What are you doing here, Elijah?” 1 Kings 19:9, NLT, excerpt

Elijah poured out his heart: “I have zealously served the LORD God Almighty. But the people of Israel have broken their covenant with you, torn down your altars, and killed every one of your prophets. I am the only one left, and now they are trying to kill me, too.” 1 Kings 19:10, NLT, excerpt

Ellen White is honest about Elijah’s failure as well as God’s kindness: “Elijah should not have fled from his post of duty.” Prophets and Kings, p. 160 · PK 160.1, excerpt Yet God’s question was an invitation, not an accusation. It called Elijah to see where fear had taken him.

Then God showed Elijah something new: “‘Go out and stand before me on the mountain,’ the LORD told him. And as Elijah stood there, the LORD passed by, and a mighty windstorm hit the mountain. It was such a terrible blast that the rocks were torn loose, but the LORD was not in the wind. After the wind there was an earthquake, but the LORD was not in the earthquake.” 1 Kings 19:11, NLT “And after the earthquake there was a fire, but the LORD was not in the fire. And after the fire there was the sound of a gentle whisper.” 1 Kings 19:12, NLT “When Elijah heard it, he wrapped his face in his cloak and went out and stood at the entrance of the cave.” 1 Kings 19:13, NLT, excerpt

Older English Bibles call this “a still small voice.” Ellen White describes its effect on the prophet. She uses the word petulance to mean irritable complaining:

“His petulance was silenced, his spirit softened and subdued. He now knew that a quiet trust, a firm reliance on God, would ever find for him a present help in time of need.”Prophets and Kings, p. 168 · PK 168.4, excerpt

Fire from heaven had not changed Elijah’s heart; quietness did. As Ellen White puts it, “It is the still, small voice of the Spirit of God that has power to change the heart.” Prophets and Kings, p. 169 · PK 169.1, excerpt

God’s quiet voice can do what dramatic events cannot: it can change the heart.

When God asked the question again, Elijah gave the same answer, ending once more with “I am the only one left, and now they are trying to kill me, too.” 1 Kings 19:14, NLT, excerpt God did not argue with him. He told him, “Go back the same way you came,” 1 Kings 19:15, NLT, excerpt and sent him to anoint leaders, including Elisha, who would succeed him as prophet.

Then God corrected the belief that weighed most heavily on Elijah: “Yet I will preserve 7,000 others in Israel who have never bowed down to Baal or kissed him!” 1 Kings 19:18, NLT As Ellen White observes, “Elijah had thought that he alone in Israel was a worshiper of the true God.” Prophets and Kings, p. 170 · PK 170.1, excerpt He was mistaken, and God told him so gently.

Elijah was not meant to carry the work alone. Soon he found Elisha, who left his oxen in the field to follow him: “Then he went with Elijah as his assistant.” 1 Kings 19:21, NLT, excerpt

Discouragement told Elijah he was alone; God showed him that he was not, and that his work was not finished.

Jesus understands this sorrow from His own experience. On the night before His crucifixion, He told His disciples, “My soul is crushed with grief to the point of death. Stay here and keep watch with me.” Matthew 26:38, NLT That is why Hebrews assures us, “This High Priest of ours understands our weaknesses, for he faced all of the same testings we do, yet he did not sin.” Hebrews 4:15, NLT And He still invites the weary: “Then Jesus said, ‘Come to me, all of you who are weary and carry heavy burdens, and I will give you rest.’” Matthew 11:28, NLT

Elijah’s prayer under the broom tree was honest, even desperate, and God did not turn away from it. Ellen White encourages us to bring that same honesty to prayer:

“Keep your wants, your joys, your sorrows, your cares, and your fears before God. You cannot burden Him; you cannot weary Him.”Steps to Christ, p. 100 · SC 100.1, excerpt

Perhaps you are sitting under your own broom tree today. God’s first word to you may be as simple as the angel’s: rest, receive His care, and then take the next step. He knows the journey ahead, and you will not travel it alone.

The God who met Elijah under the broom tree meets weary people today through Jesus Christ.

Reflect

  1. How do I usually feel after a time of great effort or success? What do I need from God in those moments?
  2. Am I willing to receive the care God provides through rest, food and help from others, rather than trying to carry everything alone?
  3. Where might God be speaking to me quietly this week, and who could I share my burden with?

Key Takeaways

  1. God cares for the whole person. Before speaking to Elijah about his fears, God gave him sleep, food, water and a gentle touch. 1 Kings 19:5–7 · quoted above
  2. God often speaks in quietness. God was not in the wind, the earthquake or the fire; He came to Elijah quietly. 1 Kings 19:11–12 · quoted above
  3. We are not as alone as discouragement tells us. God told Elijah about 7,000 faithful people and gave him a companion and new work to do. 1 Kings 19:18 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Heavenly Father, thank You for loving us even when our strength runs out. When we are tired, afraid or discouraged, meet us as You met Elijah under the broom tree. Give us the rest and care we need, and help us welcome the support of the people You send. Quiet our hearts so we can hear Your gentle voice. Remind us that we are not alone, and show us the next step on the road ahead. Thank You for Jesus, who understands our weakness and invites the weary to come to Him. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
