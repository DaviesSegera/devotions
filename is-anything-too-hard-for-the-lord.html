Is Anything Too Hard for the Lord?

Genesis 18:1–15 · 6 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-09-28

Sarah laughed when God's promise seemed impossible. Reflect on Abraham's hospitality, God's faithfulness through waiting, and the hope that reaches us in Christ.

Faith & trustWaiting on GodLove & serviceHope & encouragement
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“Is anything too hard for the LORD? I will return about this time next year, and Sarah will have a son.”
Genesis 18:14 (NLT)
“God has brought me laughter.”
Genesis 21:6 (NLT, excerpt)

Sarah laughed privately when she heard the promise. Later, her joy was shared with others. Between those two moments stands one question: “Is anything too hard for the LORD?” Genesis 18:14 (NLT, excerpt)

God had promised Abraham, “All the families on earth will be blessed through you.” Genesis 12:3 (NLT, excerpt) Years passed, and he and Sarah still had no son. One afternoon, three visitors arrived. “The LORD appeared again to Abraham near the oak grove belonging to Mamre. One day Abraham was sitting at the entrance to his tent during the hottest part of the day. He looked up and noticed three men standing nearby. When he saw them, he ran to meet them and welcomed them, bowing low to the ground.” Genesis 18:1–2 (NLT)

Abraham offered his guests water, shade, and a fresh meal, then served them himself.

Ellen G. White writes:

Ellen G. White“Abraham had seen in his guests only three tired wayfarers, little thinking that among them was One whom he might worship without sin. But the true character of the heavenly messengers was now revealed.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 138 · PP 138.4

The New Testament still calls us to welcome others: “Don’t forget to show hospitality to strangers, for some who have done this have entertained angels without realizing it!” Hebrews 13:2 (NLT)

Abraham did not need to know who the travelers were before deciding they were worth serving.

After the meal, a visitor said, “I will return to you about this time next year, and your wife, Sarah, will have a son!” Genesis 18:10 (NLT, excerpt) Sarah was listening from the tent. “Abraham and Sarah were both very old by this time, and Sarah was long past the age of having children.” Genesis 18:11 (NLT)

“So she laughed silently to herself and said, ‘How could a worn-out woman like me enjoy such pleasure, especially when my master—my husband—is also so old?’” Genesis 18:12 (NLT)

Sarah was not alone in her disbelief. When God first named her as the mother of the promised son, Abraham “laughed to himself in disbelief.” Genesis 17:17 (NLT, excerpt)

Sarah’s laugh showed how wide the gap looked between God’s promise and what seemed possible.

The LORD then addressed her hidden laugh. “Then the LORD said to Abraham, ‘Why did Sarah laugh? Why did she say, “Can an old woman like me have a baby?” Is anything too hard for the LORD? I will return about this time next year, and Sarah will have a son.’” Genesis 18:13–14 (NLT)

God directed Sarah’s attention from her limits to His power. When fear made her deny laughing, “the LORD said, ‘No, you did laugh.’” Genesis 18:15 (NLT, excerpt) He corrected her, but He kept His promise.

God asked this question about a promise He Himself had made. It does not mean every wish will come true. Faith trusts what God has actually said.

God was not troubled by Sarah’s question, but He would not let her unbelief set the limits of His power.

God fulfilled His promise at the time He had said. “The LORD kept his word and did for Sarah exactly what he had promised. She became pregnant, and she gave birth to a son for Abraham in his old age. This happened at just the time God had said it would.” Genesis 21:1–2 (NLT)

Isaac’s name means “he laughs.” Now Sarah laughed again, this time with joy. “God has brought me laughter. All who hear about this will laugh with me.” Genesis 21:6 (NLT, excerpt) Ellen G. White adds, “The birth of Isaac, bringing, after a lifelong waiting, the fulfillment of their dearest hopes, filled the tents of Abraham and Sarah with gladness.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 146 · PP 146.2

The tent that once held a hidden laugh of disbelief later held the laughter of fulfilled promise.

God’s promise to Abraham reached far beyond Isaac. It leads to Jesus: “And now that you belong to Christ, you are the true children of Abraham. You are his heirs, and God’s promise to Abraham belongs to you.” Galatians 3:29 (NLT)

Our hope rests in God, whose promise to Abraham reaches us through Jesus.

Bring your doubts to God, trust what He has truly promised, receive His grace in Christ, and let faith become visible in the way you welcome and serve others.

Reflect

  1. Am I willing to notice, welcome, and serve people even when there is nothing obvious for me to gain?
  2. When God’s word confronts what seems impossible, will I trust His faithfulness without inventing promises He has not made?
  3. How might God’s faithfulness change the way I see my situation?

Key Takeaways

  1. Welcome comes first. Abraham served three strangers before he knew who they were. Simple, sincere hospitality still shows God’s love.
  2. Doubt can be brought to God. Sarah laughed, and so did Abraham. God met their doubt with His promise.
  3. God keeps His word. At just the time He had said, Isaac was born.
  4. Our hope is in Christ. The promise to Abraham leads to Jesus, and in Him we can trust God while we wait.

A prayer for today

Faithful God, nothing is too hard for You. Give me a humble, generous heart to welcome others. When I doubt, help me bring my questions to You, and teach me to trust what You have truly promised. In Jesus’ name, amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
