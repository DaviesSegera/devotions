Philip and the Ethiopian: When God Leads Us to One Soul

Acts 8:26–40 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-20

God leads Philip from a city to one searching traveller on a desert road. Reflect on listening with care, explaining Scripture, and helping others discover the good news of Jesus.

Faith & trustLove & serviceForgiveness & graceHumility & obedience
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“So beginning with this same Scripture, Philip told him the Good News about Jesus.”
Acts 8:35 (NLT)

One Willing Messenger · One Searching Heart · One Saviour

Would you leave a city where people were joyfully receiving the gospel and travel instead to a desert road, without knowing whom you would meet? Philip’s story begins with an unlikely assignment and ends with a traveller rejoicing because he has met Jesus in the Scriptures.

The work in Samaria was bearing fruit: “Philip, for example, went to the city of Samaria and told the people there about the Messiah.” Acts 8:5, NLT “Crowds listened intently to Philip because they were eager to hear his message and see the miraculous signs he did.” Acts 8:6, NLT “Many evil spirits were cast out, screaming as they left their victims. And many who had been paralyzed or lame were healed.” Acts 8:7, NLT “So there was great joy in that city.” Acts 8:8, NLT

Yet an angel directed Philip away from that fruitful work: “Go south down the desert road that runs from Jerusalem to Gaza.” Acts 8:26, NLT, excerpt God was leading him from a crowd to a place where one person needed help.

Philip went. Ellen G. White highlights his response: “He did not question the call, nor did he hesitate to obey” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 107 · AA 107.2, excerpt He had learned to bring his plans into harmony with God’s will. We discern God’s leading through prayer, Scripture, and wisdom—not by treating every interruption as a divine instruction. But our willingness to serve should not depend on whether an assignment looks important to others.

Philip did not yet know whom he would meet, but God knew the man who needed help understanding His Word.

Obedience brought Philip to the traveller: “So he started out, and he met the treasurer of Ethiopia,” Acts 8:27, NLT, excerpt This eunuch held great authority under the Kandake, the queen of Ethiopia. Returning from worship in Jerusalem, he was reading Scripture: “Seated in his carriage, he was reading aloud from the book of the prophet Isaiah.” Acts 8:28, NLT, excerpt Although he held an influential position, he still needed help understanding God’s Word.

Ellen White explains how the Ethiopian could share the gospel with others after receiving it himself:

“God saw that when converted he would give others the light he had received and would exert a strong influence in favor of the gospel.”The Acts of the Apostles, p. 107 · AA 107.3

The Ethiopian’s importance in God’s sight did not depend on his position. Through him, others could also hear the good news. First, however, he needed help understanding what he was reading.

The Holy Spirit told Philip, “Go over and walk along beside the carriage.” Acts 8:29, NLT, excerpt Philip ran up, heard Isaiah being read, and asked, “Do you understand what you are reading?” Acts 8:30, NLT, excerpt The reply was honest: “How can I, unless someone instructs me?” Acts 8:31, NLT, excerpt Then the Ethiopian invited Philip to sit beside him.

People may have sincere questions about faith even when they appear confident and successful. Philip listened before he explained. He showed genuine concern for the Ethiopian and his desire to understand God’s Word. We too can begin with kindness and a willingness to understand another person’s questions.

Reflecting on this encounter, Ellen White draws attention to heaven’s care: “Notice how much effort was put forth for just one man, an Ethiopian.” Testimonies for the Church, vol. 8, p. 57 · 8T 57.4 God had guided Philip to this one man and provided the help he needed to understand Scripture. If heaven cared for this seeker, how can we dismiss the spiritual needs of the person beside us?

Helping one person come to know God is never a small matter.

The Ethiopian was reading the prophecy of the suffering servant: “He was led like a sheep to the slaughter.” Acts 8:32, NLT, excerpt Isaiah describes His silence: “yet he never said a word.” Isaiah 53:7, NLT, excerpt He also explains why the servant suffered: “But he was struck down for the rebellion of my people.” Isaiah 53:8, NLT, excerpt

The Ethiopian wanted to know whom Isaiah was describing. He asked, “Tell me, was the prophet talking about himself or someone else?” Acts 8:34, NLT, excerpt Philip began with the passage before them: “So beginning with this same Scripture, Philip told him the Good News about Jesus.” Acts 8:35, NLT

Philip helped him understand that the passage was pointing to Jesus, the Saviour. Isaiah had described the burden He bore: “Yet it was our weaknesses he carried;” Isaiah 53:4, NLT, excerpt He was “crushed for our sins.” Isaiah 53:5, NLT, excerpt The prophet explains: “Yet the LORD laid on him the sins of us all.” Isaiah 53:6, NLT, excerpt

This is the heart of the gospel Paul also proclaimed: “Christ died for our sins, just as the Scriptures said.” 1 Corinthians 15:3, NLT, excerpt Death was not the end: “He was buried, and he was raised from the dead on the third day” 1 Corinthians 15:4, NLT, excerpt The suffering servant is our living Saviour.

Ellen White describes the Ethiopian’s eagerness to understand what Philip was teaching: “The man's heart thrilled with interest as the Scriptures were explained to him” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 108 · AA 108.2, excerpt His important position did not prevent him from listening humbly or receiving the gospel.

As Seventh-day Adventists, we value the Sabbath, prophecy, the sanctuary message, and Christ’s return. These truths must lead us to Jesus. He said, “You search the Scriptures because you think they give you eternal life. But the Scriptures point to me!” John 5:39, NLT

The sanctuary message reminds us of Christ’s ministry for us in heaven: “we have a great High Priest who has entered heaven, Jesus the Son of God,” Hebrews 4:14, NLT, excerpt He is not distant from our struggles: “This High Priest of ours understands our weaknesses, for he faced all of the same testings we do, yet he did not sin.” Hebrews 4:15, NLT This gives us confidence to respond to the invitation: “So let us come boldly to the throne of our gracious God.” Hebrews 4:16, NLT, excerpt

The same good news is at the heart of our worldwide mission. John describes an angel “carrying the eternal Good News to proclaim to the people who belong to this world—to every nation, tribe, language, and people.” Revelation 14:6, NLT, excerpt The message calls people to worship their Creator: “Worship him who made the heavens, the earth, the sea, and all the springs of water.” Revelation 14:7, NLT, excerpt Philip’s conversation with the Ethiopian shows how we can take part in that mission by helping another person come to know and worship God.

The Scriptures become a message of hope when they lead us to Jesus.

As the carriage continued, the travellers came to water. The Ethiopian asked, “Look! There’s some water! Why can’t I be baptized?” Acts 8:36, NLT, excerpt Luke records the response: “He ordered the carriage to stop, and they went down into the water, and Philip baptized him.” Acts 8:38, NLT The seeker was responding to the good news in faith.

Baptism is a public expression of new life in Christ. Paul asks, “Or have you forgotten that when we were joined with Christ Jesus in baptism, we joined him in his death?” Romans 6:3, NLT He explains, “For we died and were buried with Christ by baptism.” Romans 6:4, NLT, excerpt Because Christ was raised, “now we also may live new lives.” Romans 6:4, NLT, excerpt

Baptism does not earn us salvation. Paul makes clear that we are saved by God’s grace: “God saved you by his grace when you believed. And you can’t take credit for this; it is a gift from God.” Ephesians 2:8, NLT “Salvation is not a reward for the good things we have done, so none of us can boast about it.” Ephesians 2:9, NLT Baptism expresses faith in the Saviour whose grace has reached us.

When they came up from the water, the Spirit of the Lord took Philip away. Yet “The eunuch never saw him again but went on his way rejoicing.” Acts 8:39, NLT, excerpt Philip was gone, but the joy remained. His hope rested in the Saviour, not the messenger.

Philip found himself at Azotus and continued his ministry: “He preached the Good News there and in every town along the way until he came to Caesarea.” Acts 8:40, NLT, excerpt As we reflect on the Ethiopian’s new life of faith, we are reminded that our care for new believers should continue beyond baptism through friendship, Bible teaching, and patient encouragement.

The Ethiopian continued his journey rejoicing in the Saviour he had come to know.

Ellen White connects the Ethiopian’s experience with others who are seeking to understand Scripture: “Many are on the verge of the kingdom, waiting only to be gathered in.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 109 · AA 109.1 We may never know how close someone is to opening their heart to the gospel.

Why send Philip when an angel was already involved? Ellen White explains: “It is His plan that men are to work for their fellow men.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 109 · AA 109.2 God gives ordinary believers the privilege of listening, opening Scripture, and pointing others to Christ.

Perhaps someone near us has questions they have not yet felt able to ask. We cannot force someone to believe, but we can pray, listen with care, and be ready to share the hope we have in Christ. When we have questions of our own, the Ethiopian’s experience reminds us that God cares about our search for understanding too.

One searching heart. One willing messenger. One Saviour who brings light, faith, and joy.

Reflect

  1. When God gives me an opportunity to help someone, am I willing to respond even when no one else will notice?
  2. Do I listen to people’s questions and help them discover Jesus in the Scriptures?
  3. Who is one person I can pray for and lovingly encourage toward Christ this week?

Key Takeaways

  1. Respond when God opens a way to serve. Philip followed the call to the desert road, trusting God even before he knew whom he would meet. Acts 8:26–29 · quoted above
  2. Listen, then explain the Word. The Ethiopian asked for guidance; Philip began with Scripture and led him to Jesus. Acts 8:30–35 · quoted above
  3. Faith in Jesus brings lasting joy. The Ethiopian responded in baptism and continued his journey rejoicing, even after Philip was no longer with him. Acts 8:36–39 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Heavenly Father, thank You for knowing every searching heart and for revealing Your love through Jesus Christ. Give us Philip’s willingness to obey, his readiness to listen, and his care for one person at a time. When we open the Scriptures, help us lead others to the Saviour rather than to ourselves. Encourage those who are seeking truth, guide those considering baptism, and make our church a loving home where faith can grow. Show each of us someone we can serve this week. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
