Moses at Kadesh: When God Said Speak

Numbers 20:1–13 · 5 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-12

God tells Moses to speak to the rock at Kadesh, but Moses strikes it in anger. Reflect on faithful obedience, gentle words under pressure, and the mercy of Christ, our Rock.

Faith & trustHumility & obedienceWisdom & discernmentForgiveness & grace

Key Verse
“But the LORD said to Moses and Aaron, ‘Because you did not trust me enough to demonstrate my holiness to the people of Israel, you will not lead them into the land I am giving them!’”
Numbers 20:12 (NLT)

From the Smitten Rock to Faithful Obedience, Reverence, and Grace

On at least two occasions, Moses stood before a rock while the people were thirsty. On one occasion, God said, in effect, “Strike.” On another, He said, “Speak.” The difference reveals something important about faith.

The story of God providing water in the wilderness begins even earlier, at Marah. Three days after crossing the Red Sea, Israel found water too bitter to drink. Moses cried out to the LORD, and “the LORD showed him a piece of wood. Moses threw it into the water, and this made the water good to drink.” Exodus 15:25 (NLT, excerpt) No rock was struck there. God showed the way, and Moses followed it.

Later, at Rephidim, there was no water at all. God told Moses to take his staff and the elders to the rock at Horeb (the NLT calls it Mount Sinai): “Strike the rock, and water will come gushing out.” Exodus 17:6 (NLT, excerpt) Moses did exactly that, and God provided water.

At Marah, God pointed to a piece of wood. At Horeb, He said to strike the rock. The Provider was the same; the instruction suited the moment.

Many years passed. Near the end of the wilderness journey, Israel camped at Kadesh in the wilderness of Zin. Miriam died and was buried there. Then the water failed, and the people turned on their leaders: “Why have you brought the congregation of the LORD’s people into this wilderness to die, along with all our livestock?” Numbers 20:4 (NLT)

Moses and Aaron went to the entrance of the Tabernacle and fell face down before God. The LORD answered with a new instruction: “You and Aaron must take the staff and assemble the entire community. As the people watch, speak to the rock over there, and it will pour out its water.” Numbers 20:8 (NLT, excerpt)

Take the staff, yes. But speak.

Moses took the staff, just as he was told, and gathered the people. Then years of weariness boiled over. “Listen, you rebels!” he shouted. “Must we bring you water from this rock?” Numbers 20:10 (NLT, excerpt) “Then Moses raised his hand and struck the rock twice with the staff, and water gushed out.” Numbers 20:11 (NLT, excerpt)

What he said about the people was true; they had rebelled. But Ellen G. White draws attention to how he said it:

Ellen G. White’s insight · quotation“This accusation was true, but even truth is not to be spoken in passion or impatience.”Patriarchs and Prophets, ch. 37, “The Smitten Rock,” p. 417 (PP 417.3)

A later psalm recalled that day in similar terms: “They made Moses angry, and he spoke foolishly.” Psalm 106:33 (NLT)

The water still flowed. The people and their animals drank their fill. God had not abandoned the thirsty. But then God gave His verdict, which is the key to the whole story. Moses and Aaron had not trusted Him enough to demonstrate His holiness before Israel, so they would not lead the people into the Promised Land. Years later, God referred again to the same place and the same failure: “you failed to demonstrate my holiness to them at the waters.” Numbers 27:14 (NLT, excerpt)

The water showed God's mercy to the thirsty. It did not signify God's approval of His servant's anger.

So the problem was not simply two blows instead of one. God had not asked Moses to strike the rock at all. The deeper problem concerned trust and honour. In that public moment, Moses' use of “we” made it sound as if the water depended on him and Aaron, and the people saw a frustrated leader rather than a holy God.

There was also a picture at stake. Paul wrote that Israel “drank from the spiritual rock that traveled with them, and that rock was Christ.” 1 Corinthians 10:4 (NLT, excerpt) Christ Himself was with His people, giving what they needed. Ellen White explains why the two commands mattered:

Ellen G. White’s insight · quotation“The rock, being a symbol of Christ, had been once smitten, as Christ was to be once offered. The second time it was needful only to speak to the rock, as we have only to ask for blessings in the name of Jesus.”Patriarchs and Prophets, ch. 37, “The Smitten Rock,” p. 418 (PP 418.1)

Moses' staff could not hurt Jesus, and Jesus never needs to die again. “Christ was offered once for all time as a sacrifice to take away the sins of many people.” Hebrews 9:28 (NLT, excerpt) That is exactly why we come to Him not with blows but with a request.

Moses faced a real consequence, but he was not cast away. He pleaded to enter the land, and God let him look out over it from a mountaintop: “Take a good look, but you may not cross the Jordan River.” Deuteronomy 3:27 (NLT, excerpt) Forgiveness and consequences can coexist.

This story is for everyone who has influence: parents, teachers, elders, youth leaders and friends. Feeling angry is not the same as Moses' sin, and tired people need rest and support. But under pressure, our words can either show God's character or hide it.

Whose voice am I following, and who receives the glory?

Reflect

  1. When God has spoken clearly, am I trusting His instruction or reaching for what worked before?
  2. Under pressure, do my words and actions reveal God's holiness or draw attention to myself?
  3. Do I need to ask anyone to forgive my rash words?

Key Takeaways

  1. Obey what God has actually said. “As the people watch, speak to the rock over there, and it will pour out its water.” Numbers 20:8 (NLT, excerpt)
  2. Truth must still be spoken in the right spirit. Ellen G. White: “even truth is not to be spoken in passion or impatience.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 417 · PP 417.3
  3. Come to Christ, the Rock, and ask. “That rock was Christ.” 1 Corinthians 10:4 (NLT, excerpt)

A prayer for today

Faithful God, You gave water at Marah, at Horeb and at Kadesh, even when Your people complained and Your servant failed. Teach us to listen to what You have actually said. Guard our mouths when we are tired and provoked, and keep us from taking the glory that belongs to You. Thank You, Jesus, for being the Rock who was offered once for us. We come to You now, not with blows, but with a request: forgive us, make our hearts teachable and our words gentle, and let our lives show Your holiness. Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
