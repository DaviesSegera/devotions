The Son Who Changed His Mind

Matthew 21:28–32 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-13

One son says no and later goes to the vineyard. The other says yes and stays behind. Reflect on turning back to God, following through in obedience, and trusting Jesus for the strength to follow Him.

Humility & obedienceForgiveness & graceFaith & trust
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“The son answered, ‘No, I won’t go,’ but later he changed his mind and went anyway.”
Matthew 21:29 (NLT)

Have you ever said “yes” to God and then let the moment pass? Or said “no” and later wished you could take it back? Jesus told a short story about two sons. We may recognise a little of ourselves in both of them.

Just days before the cross, the religious leaders challenged Jesus as He taught in the Temple in Jerusalem. “When Jesus returned to the Temple and began teaching, the leading priests and elders came up to him. They demanded, ‘By what authority are you doing all these things? Who gave you the right?’” Matthew 21:23, NLT In reply, Jesus asked them about John the Baptist. “Did John’s authority to baptize come from heaven, or was it merely human?” Matthew 21:25, NLT, excerpt The leaders were unwilling to commit themselves either way. “So they finally replied, ‘We don’t know.’” Matthew 21:27, NLT, excerpt

Their answer could have ended the exchange. Ellen G. White explains why Jesus kept speaking. “In His contest with the rabbis, it was not Christ’s purpose to humiliate His opponents. He was not glad to see them in a hard place. He had an important lesson to teach.” The Desire of Ages, p. 594 · DA 594.4, excerpt Jesus gave these teachers of God’s law a story to consider.

Jesus did not tell this story to win an argument. He told it to reach their hearts.

The story begins with an ordinary request. “But what do you think about this? A man with two sons told the older boy, ‘Son, go out and work in the vineyard today.’” Matthew 21:28, NLT A vineyard is a field where grapes grow. The father was asking his son to help with the work that very day.

The first son gave a blunt answer. “The son answered, ‘No, I won’t go,’ but later he changed his mind and went anyway.” Matthew 21:29, NLT The second son sounded ready to help. “Then the father told the other son, ‘You go,’ and he said, ‘Yes, sir, I will.’ But he didn’t go.” Matthew 21:30, NLT

Ellen G. White puts the lesson simply. “The test of sincerity is not in words, but in deeds.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 272 · COL 272.2, excerpt She goes on to explain what the father and the vineyard represent. “In this parable the father represents God, the vineyard the church.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 275 · COL 275.2, excerpt A parable is a short story that teaches a spiritual lesson. Here, the father’s request is a picture of God calling us to do His will.

The son who obeyed was not the one with the best answer. He was the one who went.

Then Jesus asked His listeners to judge for themselves. “Which of the two obeyed his father?” They replied, “The first.” Matthew 21:31, NLT, excerpt Ellen G. White points out what the leaders missed in their own answer. “Forgetting themselves, the Pharisees answered, ‘The first.’ This they said without realizing that they were pronouncing sentence against themselves.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 276 · COL 276.2, excerpt

Jesus then explained what the story meant for them. “I tell you the truth, corrupt tax collectors and prostitutes will get into the Kingdom of God before you do.” Matthew 21:31, NLT, excerpt “For John the Baptist came and showed you the right way to live, but you didn’t believe him, while tax collectors and prostitutes did. And even when you saw this happening, you refused to believe him and repent of your sins.” Matthew 21:32, NLT

The same Greek verb is translated “changed his mind” in verse 29 and “repent” in verse 32. To repent is to turn back to God. The change begins in the heart and shows in the direction we take.

Tax collectors worked for Rome, and many took more than people owed. They were widely despised. Yet when John called them to turn back to God, they listened. Luke tells us how they responded when Jesus later spoke about John. “When they heard this, all the people—even the tax collectors—agreed that God’s way was right, for they had been baptized by John.” Luke 7:29, NLT

They began with a “no,” but when God called again, they went.

The second son can be harder to recognise because he looks so much like us. He answered politely and even called his father “sir.” Ellen G. White looks beneath the surface of his answer. “In the parable the son who said, ‘I go, sir,’ represented himself as faithful and obedient; but time proved that his profession was not real. He had no true love for his father.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 278 · COL 278.4, excerpt Here, profession means what he claimed about himself.

Jesus gave the same warning in the Sermon on the Mount. “Not everyone who calls out to me, ‘Lord! Lord!’ will enter the Kingdom of Heaven. Only those who actually do the will of my Father in heaven will enter.” Matthew 7:21, NLT

An empty “yes” can slip quietly into a life of faith. We can sing the songs and agree with every sermon while still going our own way. Ellen G. White describes what happens when obedience comes at a cost.

“The promise of obedience they appear to fulfill when this involves no sacrifice; but when self-denial and self-sacrifice are required, when they see the cross to be lifted, they draw back.”Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 279 · COL 279.2, excerpt

To lift the cross is to do God’s will when it costs us something. That is where a real “yes” is tested.

Ellen G. White reminds us that John’s message reached some of the Pharisees, too. “Some of the Pharisees had repented and received the baptism of John; but the leaders would not acknowledge that he came from God.” The Desire of Ages, p. 595 · DA 595.4, excerpt The story holds up a mirror to our own hearts.

God is not looking for the best promise. He is looking for a heart that goes.

Jesus’ warning also held out hope. Jesus said “before you,” not “instead of you.” Ellen G. White draws out the invitation in His words.

“Christ did not say to them, Ye cannot enter the kingdom of heaven; but He showed that the obstacle which prevented them from entering was of their own creating. The door was still open to these Jewish leaders; the invitation was still held out. Christ longed to see them convicted and converted.”Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 277 · COL 277.2

The leaders’ refusal to turn was what stood in their way. The door was still open to them, and it is open to us. Jesus tells us how heaven responds when someone turns back to God. “In the same way, there is more joy in heaven over one lost sinner who repents and returns to God than over ninety-nine others who are righteous and haven’t strayed away!” Luke 15:7, NLT

The first son’s “no” was nothing to admire. Ellen G. White is careful here. “The son who for a time refused obedience to his father’s command was not condemned by Christ; and neither was he commended.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 280 · COL 280.4, excerpt She urges us to respond when the Holy Spirit speaks to our hearts. “When the appeals of the Holy Spirit come to the heart, our only safety lies in responding to them without delay.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 280 · COL 280.4, excerpt

If you have said “no” to God, you can turn back. If you have said “yes” and put off going, you can still go today.

It is not too late to change your mind and go.

As we think about the two sons, we can look to the Son who told the story. Paul writes, “For Jesus Christ, the Son of God, does not waver between ‘Yes’ and ‘No.’ He is the one whom Silas, Timothy, and I preached to you, and as God’s ultimate ‘Yes,’ he always does what he says.” 2 Corinthians 1:19, NLT Jesus Himself spoke of doing the Father’s will. “Then Jesus explained: ‘My nourishment comes from doing the will of God, who sent me, and from finishing his work.’” John 4:34, NLT His “yes” to the Father took Him all the way to the cross.

Ellen G. White describes the spirit of His obedience. “He served His Father with love, in willingness and freedom.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 282 · COL 282.3, excerpt

The Father gives us His love freely, and that love makes us willing to obey. Ellen G. White puts it this way.

“Good works do not purchase the love of God, but they reveal that we possess that love. If we surrender the will to God, we shall not work in order to earn God’s love. His love as a free gift will be received into the soul, and from love to Him we shall delight to obey His commandments.”Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 283 · COL 283.2, excerpt

God also gives us the strength to do what He asks. “For God is working in you, giving you the desire and the power to do what pleases him.” Philippians 2:13, NLT Ellen G. White brings the Father’s call home with a question. “Will there be deeds as well as words?” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 281 · COL 281.2, excerpt For one of us, going may mean taking the first step towards making peace with someone. For another, it may mean finally keeping a promise we made to God.

Jesus said “yes” to the Father with His whole life, and He gives us the strength to follow.

Reflect

  1. Where have I said “yes” to God without following through?
  2. Where have I told God “no,” and what would it look like to turn back today?
  3. How does knowing that God gives His love freely change the way I answer His call?

Key Takeaways

  1. Obedience shows in what we do. Both sons heard the same call. The first son answered it by going to the vineyard. Matthew 21:28–30 · quoted above
  2. A first “no” does not have to be the last word. The tax collectors who listened to John turned back to God. That same invitation is open to us. Matthew 21:31–32; Luke 7:29 · quoted above
  3. Jesus is the faithful Son. He obeyed the Father all the way to the cross. God gives us the strength to follow Jesus’ example. 2 Corinthians 1:19; John 4:34 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Heavenly Father, thank You for inviting us to share in Your work and for keeping the door open when we have said no. Forgive us for the times we have said yes and stayed where we were. Where we have refused You, give us the courage to turn back and do what You ask. Thank You for Jesus, Your faithful Son, whose yes to You never failed. Give us the desire and the power to do Your will today. Help us obey You out of gratitude for the love You have already given us. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
