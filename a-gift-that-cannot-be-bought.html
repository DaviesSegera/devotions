A Gift That Cannot Be Bought

Acts 8:9–24 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-23

Simon tries to buy the power to give the Holy Spirit, and Peter calls him to repent. Reflect on the motives behind our faith, the grace Christ freely gives, and serving others with a humble heart.

Humility & obedienceForgiveness & graceWisdom & discernmentLove & service

Key Verse
“May your money be destroyed with you for thinking God’s gift can be bought!”
Acts 8:20, NLT, excerpt

Why do we follow Jesus? Most of us would answer without hesitation: because we love Him and need Him. Yet our motives are not always so simple. We may be drawn to the excitement of God’s work or the respect that comes with serving Him, even while our hearts remain unsurrendered. A brief account in Acts invites us to examine those motives.

When persecution scattered the believers from Jerusalem, Philip brought the good news to Samaria. People listened, the sick were healed, and Luke records, “So there was great joy in that city.” Acts 8:8, NLT Ellen G. White describes Philip’s ministry: “Philip left Jerusalem, and preached a risen Redeemer in Samaria. Many believed, and received Christian baptism.” The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 3, p. 301 · 3SP 301.2, excerpt

One man in that city had been famous long before Philip arrived: “A man named Simon had been a sorcerer there for many years, amazing the people of Samaria and claiming to be someone great. Everyone, from the least to the greatest, often spoke of him as ‘the Great One—the Power of God.’” Acts 8:9–10, NLT

Then something surprising happened: “Then Simon himself believed and was baptized. He began following Philip wherever he went, and he was amazed by the signs and great miracles Philip performed.” Acts 8:13, NLT From the outside, Simon appeared to be a changed man.

Being near God’s work is not the same as giving our hearts to God.

Peter and John came from Jerusalem and prayed for the new believers. Luke records, “Then Peter and John laid their hands upon these believers, and they received the Holy Spirit.” Acts 8:17, NLT As Simon watched, an old desire stirred within him:

“When Simon saw that the Spirit was given when the apostles laid their hands on people, he offered them money to buy this power. ‘Let me have this power, too,’ he exclaimed, ‘so that when I lay my hands on people, they will receive the Holy Spirit!’” Acts 8:18–19, NLT

Simon asked for power, not for a changed heart. Ellen G. White explains how his request grew out of his old way of life:

“He hoped thus to add greatly to his fame and wealth, for he made merchandise of his sorceries and Satanic arts, pretending to impart their secrets to others.”The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 3, p. 302 · 3SP 302.1, excerpt

She also observes, using “Holy Ghost,” an older English term for the Holy Spirit: “His darkened mind could not distinguish between the power of the Holy Ghost and that of Satan.” The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 3, p. 302 · 3SP 302.2, excerpt He hoped to impress people again, this time through something holy.

We, too, can seek God’s blessing while trying to stay in control of our lives. We may serve in the hope of being noticed or pray mainly for results that make us look successful. But Jesus gave His followers power for a different purpose: “But you will receive power when the Holy Spirit comes upon you. And you will be my witnesses, telling people about me everywhere—in Jerusalem, throughout Judea, in Samaria, and to the ends of the earth.” Acts 1:8, NLT The Spirit came to Samaria to make Jesus known, not to make anyone great.

The Holy Spirit is God’s gift, given to make us witnesses for Jesus, not a power to use for our own ends.

Peter’s reply was stern: “May your money be destroyed with you for thinking God’s gift can be bought! You can have no part in this, for your heart is not right with God.” Acts 8:20–21, NLT, excerpt Peter went straight to the real problem: Simon’s heart.

Yet even then, Peter held out an invitation: “Repent of your wickedness and pray to the Lord. Perhaps he will forgive your evil thoughts, for I can see that you are full of bitter jealousy and are held captive by sin.” Acts 8:22–23, NLT His warning was strong, yet it called Simon to turn back to God.

Simon’s reply revealed his fear: “‘Pray to the Lord for me,’ Simon exclaimed, ‘that these terrible things you’ve said won’t happen to me!’” Acts 8:24, NLT Ellen White describes the moment:

“The magician trembled with fear as his sin was presented before him in this vivid manner. He began to perceive his own wicked audacity, and entreated Peter to pray that the wrath of God might not come upon him for his presumptuous sin.”The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 3, p. 303 · 3SP 303.1, excerpt

Luke does not tell us how Simon’s story ended. Fear of the consequences may be the first step toward honesty, but God calls us to turn to Him with all our hearts. When we recognize mixed motives in ourselves, we can pray as David did: “Create in me a clean heart, O God. Renew a loyal spirit within me.” Psalm 51:10, NLT

God’s warnings call us to return to Him while there is still time.

Why can’t the Spirit be bought? Ellen G. White’s answer points us to Jesus:

“Peter had, with startling force, shown Simon that he was yet untouched by the grace of God; for if his mind had been thus enlightened, he would have known that the sacred power of the Holy Spirit could not be bought or sold for money. Christ, at the infinite price of himself, had obtained for his people the power of the Holy Spirit, to be given only to his chosen instruments, whose lives must be free from selfishness and sin.”The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 3, p. 303 · 3SP 303.1, excerpt

Grace is free to us because Jesus paid for it with His own life. Her closing words refer to the Spirit’s power for service, given to lift up Christ rather than ourselves.

Paul states the same good news plainly: “For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life through Christ Jesus our Lord.” Romans 6:23, NLT Money, reputation, and good deeds cannot buy God’s gift. Instead, we come to Him and ask, trusting Jesus’ promise: “how much more will your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to those who ask him.” Luke 11:13, NLT, excerpt

What Christ paid for in full, we receive freely by faith.

Peter’s own example shows us what a heart surrendered to God looks like. Earlier, a man who had been unable to walk since birth asked him for money at the Temple gate. Peter answered: “But Peter said, ‘I don’t have any silver or gold for you. But I’ll give you what I have. In the name of Jesus Christ the Nazarene, get up and walk!’” Acts 3:6, NLT Peter had no silver or gold to offer, but he could point the man to Jesus.

Jesus gave His disciples a simple instruction: “Give as freely as you have received!” Matthew 10:8, NLT, excerpt Whenever we teach, sing, pray, or help someone in need, we pass on what we have received from God. Remembering this keeps us humble in our service and frees us from the need to prove our worth.

Today, take an honest look at what motivates your good deeds. If you find mixed motives, bring them to God. Confess them, and ask Him for a clean heart. He gives His Spirit freely to those who ask.

Receive God’s gift freely. Serve Him with a clean heart. Give as freely as you have received.

Reflect

  1. What draws me to God’s work? Am I quietly seeking recognition through any part of my service?
  2. Have I treated a warning in God’s Word only as a threat, instead of hearing His invitation to come back to Him?
  3. This week, how can I freely share what God has freely given me?

Key Takeaways

  1. God’s gift cannot be bought. The Holy Spirit is God’s gift for making Jesus known, not a power we can claim or use to gain recognition. Acts 8:20 · opening key-verse excerpt
  2. God sees our hearts and still calls us back. Even Peter’s stern warning to Simon included an invitation to repent and pray. Acts 8:22–23 · quoted above
  3. Christ paid the price; we receive the gift freely. Receive forgiveness and new life from Jesus, then give as freely as you have received. Romans 6:23 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Heavenly Father, thank You for the gifts You freely give us through Jesus Christ—gifts no money can buy. Search our hearts and show us where we have sought Your blessing without seeking You. Forgive our pride, our desire to be noticed, and every selfish motive in our service. Create in us a clean heart and renew a loyal spirit within us. Fill us with Your Holy Spirit, not to make us great, but to make Jesus known. Teach us to give as freely as we have received and to serve others with humble, grateful hearts. Thank You for the price Jesus paid so that we could receive Your grace. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
