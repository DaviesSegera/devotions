Grace Greater Than the Thorn: Paul's Strength in Weakness

2 Corinthians 12:1–10 · 7 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-06

Paul asks for relief from his thorn and receives Christ’s assurance of grace. Reflect on honest prayer, dependence on Jesus, and sharing one another’s burdens while difficulties remain.

Key Verse
“My grace is all you need. My power works best in weakness.”
2 Corinthians 12:9 (NLT, excerpt)

Prayer, Dependence, and the Sufficiency of Christ

Some prayers return to our lips because the difficulty has not gone away. We may have asked for relief many times and still wake to the same burden. Paul's testimony brings that experience into the light of Christ's grace.

Writing to the Corinthians, Paul describes extraordinary revelations from God, then turns to the weakness he continued to carry. He calls his affliction “a thorn” 2 Corinthians 12:7 (NLT, excerpt). In the same verse, he describes it as a messenger from Satan that tormented him and kept him from becoming proud. Even a life rich in spiritual experience still needed the daily help of Jesus.

Although Paul does not name a particular condition, he makes his longing for relief clear: “Three different times I begged the Lord to take it away.” 2 Corinthians 12:8 (NLT) His repeated request reminds us that dependence on God can include an earnest desire for circumstances to change.

We can bring our requests with the same honesty. A child can tell Jesus what frightens them. A teenager can bring a responsibility that feels overwhelming. An adult can speak about illness, weariness, or a difficulty that has continued for years. We do not need impressive words to be heard.

Paul's three appeals do not set a limit on our prayers. His explanation about pride belongs to his testimony; it does not give us the reason for another person's suffering. We can keep praying and care for one another with humility.

Bring your continuing need honestly to Jesus.

The Lord's answer directed Paul to the help available within his weakness: “My grace is all you need. My power works best in weakness.” 2 Corinthians 12:9 (NLT, excerpt)

Paul had asked for the thorn to leave. Christ answered with an assurance of grace and power while the affliction remained. Paul could depend on the Lord in the situation he had wanted changed.

Ellen G. White’s reflection · paraphraseEllen G. White explains that the Lord answered Paul's request by supplying grace to carry the trial while the thorn remained.In Heavenly Places, “According to God’s Will,” March 16, p. 82

That insight brings our attention to Christ. Grace is His undeserved gift and sustaining help. He meets people who need Him, including those whose difficulties have not yet changed. We can bring Him both our request for relief and our need for strength today.

That assurance changes how Paul speaks about his weakness. Paul explains why he can speak openly about his weakness: “the power of Christ can work through me” 2 Corinthians 12:9 (NLT, excerpt). His willingness to acknowledge weakness makes room for attention to rest on Christ’s strength. In verse 10, he includes the insults, hardships, persecutions, and troubles he suffers for Christ, then concludes: “For when I am weak, then I am strong.” 2 Corinthians 12:10 (NLT, excerpt) Read together, verses 9–10 direct our attention to the source of his strength: his confidence rests in the Lord he serves.

For us, dependence on Jesus can begin with acknowledging a need we have tried to hide. We may need rest, appropriate care, help with a responsibility, or someone to pray alongside us. Such steps can express trust in His care. Our worth is not measured by how much we manage alone.

If someone is harming us, we can seek protection and trustworthy support. Paul's testimony does not require unsafe endurance or tell us God's hidden reason for our circumstances. We can receive care while continuing to ask Him for relief.

Christ's grace welcomes our need and sustains our next faithful step.

As Christ's grace sustains us, it also shapes the care we offer one another. Paul gives believers this practical instruction: “Share each other’s burdens” Galatians 6:2 (NLT, excerpt). We can answer that call by listening, preparing a meal, assisting with a task, or making room for someone to rest. Ask what would help, and offer what you can with kindness.

Receiving support can be harder than giving it. Yet the person who needs help still belongs among God's people. We can let others care for us without having to prove our usefulness or reveal every private detail of our suffering.

The care we receive from others points us toward the welcome Jesus Himself extends. Hebrews presents Jesus as our “great High Priest” Hebrews 4:14 (NLT, excerpt), who has entered heaven and gives us reason to hold firmly to our faith. The next verse assures us that He “understands our weaknesses” Hebrews 4:15 (NLT, excerpt). He faced the testings we face, yet remained without sin. Because of Him, we can approach God confidently and “receive his mercy” Hebrews 4:16 (NLT, excerpt). The same verse assures us of grace to help in our time of need. That invitation reaches us while we are weary.

This sustaining grace also draws us to Jesus for salvation. Paul describes salvation as a “gift from God” Ephesians 2:8 (NLT, excerpt). Paul explains that salvation is “not a reward” Ephesians 2:9 (NLT, excerpt) for our good deeds, so we have no grounds for boasting. Instead, God has “created us anew in Christ Jesus” Ephesians 2:10 (NLT, excerpt), enabling us to do the good He has prepared for us. Good works grow from His saving grace. Our endurance cannot purchase forgiveness. We receive His gift through faith, and His grace leads us to turn from chosen sin and walk with Him.

Bring your need to Him today. Ask for relief, tell Him where you are struggling, and receive the help available to you. If you can offer care, choose one practical act of kindness. Let both receiving and giving grow from His love.

Paul's testimony leaves us looking to the One who answered him. We may still have questions, but we can place our hope in Jesus and take the next step with Him.

Bring your request, weariness, and weakness to Jesus. Receive His grace and walk with Him today.

Reflect

  1. Can I bring both my request for relief and my need for sustaining grace to Jesus?
  2. What help do I need to receive, or what burden could I help someone carry?

Key Takeaways

  1. Prayer can be honest and repeated. Paul prayed, “Three different times I begged the Lord to take it away.” 2 Corinthians 12:8 (NLT) We can bring our continuing need to Jesus.
  2. Christ supplies grace in weakness. Jesus assures Paul, “My grace is all you need. My power works best in weakness.” 2 Corinthians 12:9 (NLT, excerpt) His promise gives us a reason to depend on Him today.
  3. We can receive grace and share burdens. Receive the gift of salvation through faith, and let His grace shape the care you give and receive. The passages quoted above connect trust in Jesus with practical love for one another.

A prayer for today

Lord Jesus, You know the requests we keep bringing to You. We ask for relief and help according to Your will. Sustain us with Your grace while we wait. Forgive our sins, strengthen our trust, and help us receive care without shame. Teach us to share the burdens of others with patience and love. Keep our hope in You, and guide our next faithful step. Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
