Job: Faith When Everything Is Taken Away

Job 1:1–2:13 · 5 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-06

Job worships amid devastating loss and brings his anguish to God. Reflect on faith that can grieve, love for God beyond His gifts, and compassionate care for those who suffer.

Faith & trustHope & encouragementWaiting on GodLove & service

Key Verse
“The LORD gave me what I had, and the LORD has taken it away. Praise the name of the LORD!”
Job 1:21 (NLT, excerpt)

Do We Love God Only for His Blessings?

It is easy to thank God when life feels full. The story of Job raises a harder question: what happens to our faith when the gifts we thanked Him for are suddenly gone?

Job lived in the land of Uz. Scripture describes him as “blameless—a man of complete integrity. He feared God and stayed away from evil.” Job 1:1 (NLT, excerpt) That does not mean he never needed mercy; his life was honest and turned toward God. He had “seven sons and three daughters” Job 1:2 (NLT, excerpt) and great wealth. After his children's feasts he offered a burnt offering for each of them in case they had sinned: “This was Job's regular practice.” Job 1:5 (NLT, excerpt) The book tells us about Job's character before it tells us about his trouble. His suffering was not punishment for a hidden sin.

Then Scripture lets us see something Job never saw. In the heavenly court, the LORD speaks well of Job. Satan, the Accuser, does not deny Job's goodness. He attacks its motive: “Yes, but Job has good reason to fear God.” Job 1:9 (NLT, excerpt) Take away the protection and the blessing, Satan claims, and Job will curse God.

Ellen G. White explains what lies behind that accusation:

Ellen G. White’s insight · quotation“Unselfishness, the principle of God's kingdom, is the principle that Satan hates; its very existence he denies.”Education, ch. 16, “Bible Biographies,” “The Testing of Job,” p. 154 (Ed 154.3)

Behind Satan's words, she shows, lies a denial that anyone loves God freely. Job's life becomes part of the answer. And Satan is not God's equal; he may go only as far as God allows: “don't harm him physically” Job 1:12 (NLT, excerpt), and later, “spare his life” Job 2:6 (NLT, excerpt).

Satan's question reaches beyond Job's possessions to the reason Job loves God.

The losses come quickly: animals, servants, and then the worst report of all: “all your children are dead” Job 1:19 (NLT, excerpt). Job does not pretend. “Job stood up and tore his robe in grief. Then he shaved his head and fell to the ground to worship.” Job 1:20 (NLT) Scripture says, “In all of this, Job did not sin by blaming God.” Job 1:22 (NLT)

Job's worship does not cancel his grief, and his grief does not cancel his worship.

The test then reaches Job's body. Satan “struck Job with terrible boils from head to foot,” and Job “sat among the ashes” Job 2:7–8 (NLT, excerpt). Three friends come to comfort him, and for seven days they sit beside him in silence, “for they saw that his suffering was too great for words” Job 2:13 (NLT, excerpt).

When Job finally speaks, he curses the day he was born: “Let the day of my birth be erased.” Job 3:3 (NLT, excerpt) The man who worshipped in chapter 1 now pours out anguish, and the Bible gives that lament many chapters. Faith can grieve and ask why.

The friends answer with a formula: good people prosper, so great suffering must mean great sin. “Do the innocent die?” asks Eliphaz Job 4:7 (NLT, excerpt). They are sincere but wrong, and their words wound him further. Through it all, Job keeps seeking God, even when God seems hidden: “I go east, but he is not there… But he knows where I am going.” Job 23:8, 10 (NLT, excerpt)

At last “the LORD answered Job from the whirlwind” Job 38:1 (NLT, excerpt). God does not explain the heavenly court. Instead He asks, “Where were you when I laid the foundations of the earth?” Job 38:4 (NLT, excerpt), and shows Job the vastness of His wisdom and care across creation. Job responds, “I had only heard about you before, but now I have seen you with my own eyes.” Job 42:5 (NLT)

Job did not receive an answer to every question; he received a larger vision of the God he was questioning.

Then God corrects the friends: “you have not spoken accurately about me, as my servant Job has” Job 42:7 (NLT, excerpt). Centuries later, Jesus' disciples saw a man born blind and asked, “Was it because of his own sins or his parents' sins?” John 9:2 (NLT, excerpt) Writing about that scene, Ellen G. White notes that Job's story had been given to correct that very idea, yet the error God reproved in Job's friends was repeated in Jesus' day (The Desire of Ages, p. 471, paraphrase).

Job prays for the friends who hurt him, and Scripture says, “When Job prayed for his friends, the LORD restored his fortunes.” Job 42:10 (NLT, excerpt) That is Job's story, not a promise that every faithful person will receive twice as much in this life, and new blessings did not erase the children he had lost.

We can love God for who He is, not only for what He gives. We can bring our grief and questions to Him honestly. We can sit with people who suffer without pretending to know why. And we can keep our eyes on Jesus, “the champion who initiates and perfects our faith” Hebrews 12:2 (NLT, excerpt), who endured the cross for us. Our endurance does not earn His love. His love carries us.

Bring God your gratitude, your grief, and your unanswered questions. Look to Jesus, and take the next faithful step.

Reflect

  1. Do I love God only for His blessings, or do I trust Him because He is God?
  2. What question do I need to bring to God honestly, without pretending I understand it?
  3. Who near me is suffering, and how can I be present without trying to explain their pain?

Key Takeaways

  1. God is worthy of love for who He is. Satan questioned Job's motive, yet after every loss, “In all of this, Job did not sin by blaming God.” Job 1:22 (NLT)
  2. Faith can grieve and question. Job worshipped in sorrow and later lamented honestly, and at the end God still called him “my servant Job” Job 42:7 (NLT, excerpt).
  3. Easy explanations can wound. God told the friends who equated suffering with sin, “you have not spoken accurately about me” Job 42:7 (NLT, excerpt). Jesus said of the blind man, “It was not because of his sins or his parents' sins.” John 9:3 (NLT, excerpt)

A prayer for today

Father, thank You for who You are. Teach us to love You and not only Your gifts. When we lose what we hold dear, let us bring You our tears and our worship. When we do not understand, help us trust Your heart. Make us gentle with others who suffer. Keep our eyes on Jesus, our Saviour, and give us grace for the next faithful step. Amen.
