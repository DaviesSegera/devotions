Bread from Heaven: Trusting God One Day at a Time

Exodus 16; John 6:25–51 · 6 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-21

In the wilderness, God gave Israel bread one day at a time and Sabbath rest as a gift. Jesus reveals Himself as the Bread of Life. Reflect on trusting God today, receiving Christ, and sharing what you have.

Key Verse
“Look, I’m going to rain down food from heaven for you.”
Exodus 16:4 (NLT, excerpt)
“I am the bread of life. Whoever comes to me will never be hungry again. Whoever believes in me will never be thirsty.”
John 6:35 (NLT)

A month after the Israelites left Egypt, the wilderness confronted them with a question they could not answer for themselves: where would tomorrow’s food come from? Their supplies were running low. Remembering Egypt, they recalled the food they had eaten there, while the pain of slavery faded from view. Fear can make the past look safer than it was.

God heard their need and told Moses, “Look, I’m going to rain down food from heaven for you.” Exodus 16:4 (NLT, excerpt) That evening quail covered the camp. In the morning, fine flakes lay on the ground. The people asked, “What is it?” Moses told them it was the food the Lord had given them.

God met a real need and taught His people to depend on Him one day at a time.

Moses gave the people instructions: gather what you need for that day, and do not keep the manna until morning. Some tried to store extra, but it spoiled. Their daily portion invited them to trust God again when morning came.

This was not a lesson that planning is wrong or that people should ignore practical needs. God was forming a people who could receive His provision with gratitude instead of being ruled by fear. The manna reminded them that their lives depended on the Lord, not on Egypt, their own stockpiles, or their ability to control tomorrow.

We, too, can be pulled into tomorrow before today is finished: rehearsing what might go wrong, grasping for certainty, or forgetting the help we have already received. Jesus taught His followers to ask the Father for their daily bread. We can make wise plans and still bring each day to God, asking for what we need and taking the next faithful step.

We do not have to solve tomorrow before we receive God’s care today.

The pattern changed on the sixth day. The people gathered twice as much, and this time the manna did not spoil overnight. No manna fell on the seventh day. The Lord had given Israel a day to stop gathering and rest.

The Sabbath was more than a pause in the work. It was a gift that taught Israel to trust God with what they could not gather or produce that day. They could prepare, then rest, remembering that their worth and security did not depend on nonstop labour. The manna sustained them through their wilderness years, while the weekly Sabbath kept pointing them back to the One who sustained them.

Rest can still feel difficult when our responsibilities are heavy or our future is uncertain. The story invites us to receive the limits God gives, make space for worship and restoration, and trust that we are not abandoned when we stop striving. Rest does not erase every problem; it helps us meet life remembering who holds us.

God provides for our needs and invites us to rest in His care.

Centuries later, after Jesus fed a large crowd, people brought up the manna their ancestors had eaten. Jesus turned their attention from the sign to the greater gift. He said, “I am the bread of life. Whoever comes to me will never be hungry again. Whoever believes in me will never be thirsty.” John 6:35 (NLT)

The manna answered hunger for a day; it was never meant to be Israel’s final hope. Jesus offers life with God. He gave Himself for us, bearing the cost of sin on the cross, and He welcomes all who come to Him in faith. We do not earn this life by working harder or proving ourselves. We receive it through His grace.

This promise does not mean that a Christian will never face physical hunger, grief, or hardship. Scripture does not ask us to pretend those needs are small. Jesus is the source of life and hope through every season, and His people are called to care for one another’s real needs.

Ellen G. White writes, “What food is to the body, Christ must be to the soul.” The Desire of Ages, p. 389 As we return to Christ through Scripture and prayer, we receive the nourishment of His words and the assurance of His presence. This is a daily relationship, not a one-time memory of having sought Him long ago.

Come to Jesus each day. He is the Bread of Life, and His grace is enough for the life He gives.

God’s gifts are also entrusted to us for the good of others. Ellen White writes that God gives to us in trust so that we may feed the hungry. Thoughts From the Mount of Blessing, pp. 111–112 The manna story can lead us to notice who around us needs a meal, a practical kindness, a listening ear, or the hope of the gospel.

We may not be able to meet every need, but we can ask God to show us one person and one helpful response. A shared meal, a generous gift, or a word of encouragement can become a small sign of the care God has shown us.

When you fear tomorrow, remember the One who met Israel in the wilderness. Receive the grace of Jesus today, make room for the rest God offers, and share what you have with someone who needs care.

Reflect

  1. What are you carrying about tomorrow that you can bring to God today?
  2. How might Sabbath rest help you remember that your life is held by God?
  3. Who could receive practical care or encouragement from what God has entrusted to you?

Key Takeaways

  1. God hears real needs. Israel faced uncertainty in the wilderness, and God provided food for the people.
  2. Daily provision teaches daily trust. The manna invited the Israelites to depend on God each morning.
  3. Rest is part of God’s care. The Sabbath gave Israel a weekly reminder to stop working and trust the Provider.
  4. Jesus is the Bread of Life. He gives life with God and calls us to come to Him in faith.
  5. Grace moves us to generosity. What God entrusts to us can become help and encouragement for others.

Sources and Further ReadingScripture: Exodus 16:1–36; John 6:25–51; Matthew 6:11; 2 Corinthians 8:13–15 (NLT). Ellen G. White: Patriarchs and Prophets, chapter 26, “From the Red Sea to Sinai”; The Desire of Ages, p. 389; Thoughts From the Mount of Blessing, pp. 111–112.

A prayer for today

Father, thank You for caring for us day by day. When tomorrow feels uncertain, teach us to trust You with today. Draw us to Jesus, the Bread of Life, and help us receive His grace. Give us wisdom to rest in Your care and generous hearts to share with people in need. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
