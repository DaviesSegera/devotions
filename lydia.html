Lydia: A Businesswoman Whose Heart and Home Were Open to God

Acts 16:11–15, 40 · 5 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-14

The Lord opens Lydia’s heart as she listens to the gospel, and she opens her home. Reflect on God’s grace, attentive faith, hospitality, and encouraging others with what we already have.

Forgiveness & graceFaith & trustLove & serviceHumility & obedience

Key Verse
“One of them was Lydia from Thyatira, a merchant of expensive purple cloth, who worshiped God. As she listened to us, the Lord opened her heart, and she accepted what Paul was saying.”
Acts 16:14 (NLT)

From Attentive Faith to Baptism, Hospitality, and Gospel Partnership

When Lydia first appears in the Bible, she is not a preacher on a platform. Luke introduces her as a woman who did business and worshiped God. Then, by a river outside Philippi, the Lord opened her heart.

Her story begins with God leading His messengers. Paul and his companions were trying to preach in Asia, but the Holy Spirit kept closing the way. Then Paul saw a vision of a man from Macedonia, in northern Greece, pleading, “Come over to Macedonia and help us!” Acts 16:9 (NLT, excerpt) Luke writes, “So we decided to leave for Macedonia at once, having concluded that God was calling us to preach the Good News there.” Acts 16:10 (NLT)

They sailed across the sea and came to Philippi. Luke continues: “On the Sabbath we went a little way outside the city to a riverbank, where we thought people would be meeting for prayer, and we sat down to speak with some women who had gathered there.” Acts 16:13 (NLT)

There was no grand building, only a riverbank and a group of women who had come to pray. Among them was Lydia. She came from Thyatira, sold costly purple cloth, and already worshiped God. Then came the moment that changed her life: “As she listened to us, the Lord opened her heart, and she accepted what Paul was saying.” Acts 16:14 (NLT, excerpt)

Paul spoke. Lydia listened. The Lord opened her heart.

Lydia's trade tells us what she sold. God's grace tells us what changed her life.

Her business did not earn God's favour, and her money did not buy it. “God saved you by his grace when you believed. And you can’t take credit for this; it is a gift from God.” Ephesians 2:8 (NLT)

Lydia's faith did not stay hidden. “She and her household were baptized, and she asked us to be her guests. ‘If you agree that I am a true believer in the Lord,’ she said, ‘come and stay at my home.’ And she urged us until we agreed.” Acts 16:15 (NLT) Ellen G. White describes her joy:

Ellen G. White’s insight · quotation“Lydia received the truth gladly. She and her household were converted and baptized, and she entreated the apostles to make her house their home.”The Acts of the Apostles, ch. 21, “In the Regions Beyond,” p. 212 (AA 212.1)

Before Lydia opened her home, the Lord opened her heart.

Hard days followed. Paul freed a slave girl from an evil spirit, and her angry masters dragged Paul and Silas before the city officials. The two men were beaten and thrown into prison. At midnight, they prayed and sang. A great earthquake shook the prison, and the jailer and his household believed in Jesus. The next morning, the officials released Paul and Silas and begged them to leave the city.

But first, they went somewhere else. “When Paul and Silas left the prison, they returned to the home of Lydia. There they met with the believers and encouraged them once more. Then they left town.” Acts 16:40 (NLT)

Lydia's home had become a place where believers gathered and found courage. Ellen White describes the scene:

Ellen G. White’s insight · quotation“They went rejoicing from the prison to the house of Lydia, where they met the new converts to the faith of Christ, and related all the wonderful dealings of God with them.”Sketches from the Life of Paul, ch. 7, “Imprisonment of Paul and Silas,” p. 79 (LP 79.4)

So what does Lydia's story mean for us? First, it tells us that God's grace comes first. We cannot open our own hearts. But we can go where His Word is read, listen carefully, and ask Him to open our hearts.

Second, it tells us that grace leads to love. You do not need a big house or extra money to welcome people in Jesus' name. “When God’s people are in need, be ready to help them. Always be eager to practice hospitality.” Romans 12:13 (NLT) A shared meal, a phone call, a seat saved for a visitor, a ride to church or a prayer with someone who is lonely can all show the love of Christ. Love is wise, too, so we welcome others in ways that are safe for us and our families.

Behind every open door stands Jesus, who welcomed us first. “Therefore, accept each other just as Christ has accepted you so that God will be given glory.” Romans 15:7 (NLT)

An open heart leads to an open home, and Christ is the One who opens both.

Reflect

  1. When God's Word reaches me, do I listen for Christ's grace and respond with an open heart?
  2. How might God use the time, space, skills and relationships I already have to encourage someone?
  3. Is the gospel changing only what I know or also how I welcome and serve others?

Key Takeaways

  1. The Lord opens hearts. “As she listened to us, the Lord opened her heart.” Acts 16:14 (NLT, excerpt)
  2. Faith responds gladly. Ellen G. White: “Lydia received the truth gladly.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 212 · AA 212.1
  3. An open home can strengthen believers. “There they met with the believers and encouraged them once more.” Acts 16:40 (NLT, excerpt)

A prayer for today

Lord Jesus, thank You for leading Your messengers to a quiet riverbank and for opening Lydia's heart to the Good News. Open our hearts, too. Help us to listen attentively to Your Word and to believe it gladly. Thank You that we are saved by Your grace, not by anything we own or achieve. Show us how to use what You have already given us to welcome, encourage and strengthen others wisely and safely. Make our hearts and our homes places where Your love is seen. Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
