The Strength Samson Could Not Keep

Judges 13–16 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-01

Samson's strength was a gift, yet repeated compromises weakened his faithfulness. Reflect on self-control, repentance, and the mercy and strength Christ gives to those who turn to Him.

Humility & obedienceWisdom & discernmentForgiveness & graceFaith & trustHope & encouragement

Key Verse
“When he woke up, he thought, ‘I will do as before and shake myself free.’ But he didn’t realize the Lord had left him.”
Judges 16:20, NLT, excerpt

Samson had more physical strength than anyone else in the Bible. Yet his life raises a deeper question: what good is great strength if we cannot govern our own hearts?

Samson’s story begins in a dark time for Israel: “Again the Israelites did evil in the Lord’s sight, so the Lord handed them over to the Philistines, who oppressed them for forty years.” Judges 13:1, NLT Yet even then, God was preparing help.

That help began with a promise to a childless couple from the tribe of Dan. The angel of the Lord told Manoah’s wife: “You will become pregnant and give birth to a son, and his hair must never be cut. For he will be dedicated to God as a Nazirite from birth. He will begin to rescue Israel from the Philistines.” Judges 13:5, NLT

A Nazirite was someone set apart for God in a special way. The law of Moses explains the meaning of the uncut hair: “They must never cut their hair throughout the time of their vow, for they are holy and set apart to the Lord.” Numbers 6:5, NLT, excerpt God had called Samson before his birth, and that calling was meant to last a lifetime.

Samson’s strength was a gift from God, given to serve a greater purpose.

That strength became evident near Timnah. When a young lion attacked him, “the Spirit of the Lord came powerfully upon him, and he ripped the lion’s jaws apart with his bare hands.” Judges 14:6, NLT, excerpt Ellen G. White explains where that power came from: “This was not, however, as Samson and his parents well knew, dependent upon his well-knit sinews, but upon his condition as a Nazarite, of which his unshorn hair was a symbol.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 562 · PP 562.2, excerpt “Well-knit sinews” refers to strong muscles. Samson’s power came from belonging to God.

Samson’s troubles began when he let what caught his eye guide his choices. He saw a Philistine woman in Timnah and demanded that his parents arrange for him to marry her. When they objected, “Samson told his father, ‘Get her for me! She looks good to me.’” Judges 14:3, NLT, excerpt Ellen G. White draws attention to the question he failed to ask: “He did not ask whether he could better glorify God when united with the object of his choice, or whether he was placing himself in a position where he could not fulfill the purpose to be accomplished by his life.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 563 · PP 563.1, excerpt

Unchecked desire soon gave way to revenge. After a series of betrayals and killings, Samson told the men of Judah: “I only did to them what they did to me.” Judges 15:11, NLT, excerpt Proverbs gives a vivid picture of a life without self-control: “A person without self-control is like a city with broken-down walls.” Proverbs 25:28, NLT

Even great strength cannot protect a heart that refuses God’s guidance.

Samson led Israel for twenty years, yet he continued to place himself in danger: “One day Samson went to the Philistine town of Gaza and spent the night with a prostitute.” Judges 16:1, NLT He escaped that night. Ellen G. White warns: “But one wrong step prepares the way for another.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 564 · PP 564.3, excerpt

Then came Delilah, whom the Philistine rulers paid to discover the secret of his strength. Three times his enemies waited nearby, yet Samson stayed. At last, he gave in: “My hair has never been cut,” he confessed, “for I was dedicated to God as a Nazirite from birth.” Judges 16:17, NLT, excerpt

While Samson slept, his hair was shaved. Then came the moment described in our key verse: “Then she cried out, ‘Samson! The Philistines have come to capture you!’ When he woke up, he thought, ‘I will do as before and shake myself free.’ But he didn’t realize the Lord had left him.” Judges 16:20, NLT He had pushed aside the One who gave him strength, without realizing what he had lost.

Ellen G. White explains that Samson’s loss was not sudden and that his hair had no power of its own:

“Little by little he had violated the conditions of his sacred calling. God had borne long with him; but when he had so yielded himself to the power of sin as to betray his secret, the Lord departed from him. There was no virtue in his long hair merely, but it was a token of his loyalty to God; and when the symbol was sacrificed in the indulgence of passion, the blessings of which it was a token were also forfeited.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 566 · PP 566.2, excerpt

A “token” is a sign, and “forfeited” means lost. Small compromises, repeated and excused, had gradually weakened Samson’s loyalty. His decision to reveal his secret exposed what had already happened in his heart. Paul’s warning speaks to all of us: “If you think you are standing strong, be careful not to fall.” 1 Corinthians 10:12, NLT

The greatest danger is not losing our strength, but drifting from the Lord without noticing.

Ellen G. White sums up Samson’s life in words that challenge our usual understanding of strength:

“Physically, Samson was the strongest man upon the earth; but in self-control, integrity, and firmness, he was one of the weakest of men. Many mistake strong passions for a strong character, but the truth is that he who is mastered by his passions is a weak man. The real greatness of the man is measured by the power of the feelings that he controls, not by those that control him.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 567 · PP 567.3

Scripture expressed this truth long before: “Better to be patient than powerful; better to have self-control than to conquer a city.” Proverbs 16:32, NLT Self-control involves more than simply trying harder. Paul includes it among the qualities the Holy Spirit develops in a believer’s life: “But the Holy Spirit produces this kind of fruit in our lives: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.” Galatians 5:22–23, NLT, excerpt Samson had experienced the Spirit’s power in moments of crisis, but he did not allow God to govern his desires.

True strength is found in a heart yielded to God.

Samson’s downfall led him to Gaza: “So the Philistines captured him and gouged out his eyes. They took him to Gaza, where he was bound with bronze chains and forced to grind grain in the prison.” Judges 16:21, NLT Yet the next verse carries a quiet note of hope: “But before long, his hair began to grow back.” Judges 16:22, NLT

Ellen G. White describes the change taking place in Samson’s heart during those dark days: “In suffering and humiliation, a sport for the Philistines, Samson learned more of his own weakness than he had ever known before; and his afflictions led him to repentance.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 566 · PP 566.3, excerpt To be “a sport” for his enemies meant being mocked for their entertainment. In his suffering, Samson came to repentance: a turning back to God with a changed heart.

At a festival for Dagon, the Philistines’ god, Samson was brought out to amuse the crowd. There he prayed: “Sovereign Lord, remember me again. O God, please strengthen me just one more time. With one blow let me pay back the Philistines for the loss of my two eyes.” Judges 16:28, NLT, excerpt Scripture honestly records Samson’s desire for revenge, but his response is not a model for how we should treat those who wrong us. The New Testament teaches, “Don’t let evil conquer you, but conquer evil by doing good.” Romans 12:21, NLT Yet his prayer also reveals a humbled man turning to the Lord he had neglected. God heard him, and the temple of Dagon fell.

Remarkably, the book of Hebrews includes Samson among its examples of faith: “It would take too long to recount the stories of the faith of Gideon, Barak, Samson, Jephthah, David, Samuel, and all the prophets.” Hebrews 11:32, NLT, excerpt Christians differ in how they view Samson as an example of faith, but his story offers both a warning and a witness to God’s mercy.

Even after a great fall, God hears the cry of a humbled heart.

Samson’s story leaves us longing for the strength he lacked. Where Samson failed to master his desires, Jesus faced every temptation without giving in: “This High Priest of ours understands our weaknesses, for he faced all of the same testings we do, yet he did not sin.” Hebrews 4:15, NLT Because Jesus understands our weaknesses, we can come to Him for help: “So let us come boldly to the throne of our gracious God. There we will receive his mercy, and we will find grace to help us when we need it most.” Hebrews 4:16, NLT

Ellen G. White reminds us that Samson’s failure need not be ours: “But none need be overcome. Man is not left alone to conquer the power of evil by his own feeble efforts. Help is at hand and will be given to every soul who really desires it.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 568 · PP 568.2, excerpt We receive that help as we surrender our will to Christ. She writes: “By yielding up your will to Christ, you ally yourself with the power that is above all principalities and powers. You will have strength from above to hold you steadfast” Steps to Christ, p. 48 · SC 48.1, excerpt “Principalities and powers” refers to the forces of evil arrayed against us. Christ is stronger than them all.

Perhaps there is an area of your life where desire has taken the lead, or where small compromises have become easy to excuse. Do not wait until the walls have broken down. Bring that weakness to Jesus today: “But if we confess our sins to him, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all wickedness.” 1 John 1:9, NLT The God who let Samson’s hair grow back in a prison is still the God of new beginnings.

Samson could not keep his strength, but Christ can keep all who yield their hearts to Him.

Reflect

  1. Which gifts or strengths am I tempted to claim as my own, forgetting that God has entrusted them to me?
  2. What small compromises have I been excusing, and how can I bring them honestly before God today?
  3. In what area of my life do I most need the Spirit’s gift of self-control, and how can I surrender that area to Christ this week?

Key Takeaways

  1. Our strength is a gift held in trust. God gave Samson strength to fulfill his calling. Judges 13:5 · quoted above
  2. Drifting from God often happens unnoticed. Little by little, Samson allowed his desires to rule his heart. When the Lord left him, he did not even realize it. Judges 16:20 · quoted above
  3. Christ gives strength to those who come to Him. Jesus faced every temptation without sin and offers us grace when we need it most. Hebrews 4:15–16 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Heavenly Father, thank You for the gifts and strengths You have entrusted to us. Forgive us for treating them as our own and for following our desires instead of Your will. Help us recognize the small compromises we have learned to excuse. Let the fruit of Your Spirit grow in our lives, especially self-control. When we fall, remind us that You still hear the prayers of humble hearts. Thank You for Jesus, who faced every temptation without sin and gives us grace when we need it most. We surrender our wills to Him today. In Jesus’ name, Amen.
