A Life That Left Evidence

Acts 9 · 6 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera

Tabitha’s life in Acts 9 shows faith through practical kindness. Reflect on generosity, service, and the people our love reaches.

Love & serviceHope & encouragement
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“There was a believer in Joppa named Tabitha (which in Greek is Dorcas). She was always doing kind things for others and helping the poor.”
Acts 9:36 (NLT)

Before Acts tells us how Dorcas died, it tells us how she lived. “There was a believer in Joppa named Tabitha (which in Greek is Dorcas). She was always doing kind things for others and helping the poor.” Acts 9:36 (NLT) Luke introduces her by her discipleship and by what it produced. While Peter was ministering at nearby Lydda, a believer in the port town of Joppa was quietly doing good, and her faith was not only something she held; it was something the poor of Joppa could feel.

Ellen G. White draws attention to the same Christlike pattern:

“She was a worthy disciple of Jesus, and her life was filled with acts of kindness. She knew who needed comfortable clothing and who needed sympathy, and she freely ministered to the poor and the sorrowful. Her skillful fingers were more active than her tongue”. The Acts of the Apostles, p. 131 · AA 131.2

Notice the two needs she noticed—clothing and sympathy. Dorcas served bodies and hearts.

Dorcas was a disciple before she was a miracle story.

Then she became ill and died. The believers laid her in an upstairs room and sent two men to beg Peter, “Please come as soon as possible!” Acts 9:38 (NLT) What Peter found when he arrived is the heart of this devotion. “The room was filled with widows who were weeping and showing him the coats and other clothes Dorcas had made for them.” Acts 9:39 (NLT) The widows did not describe her faith; they showed him what her love had done. They were holding visible evidence of a life that had served Christ by serving people—love that others could wear, receive and remember.

Ellen White helps us see why the grief in that room ran so deep: “In view of the life of service that Dorcas had lived, it is little wonder that they mourned” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 131 · AA 131.3. The church felt her absence because it had already felt the blessing of her presence.

Dorcas’s faith left evidence in other people’s lives.

Peter did not act as though the power belonged to him. “But Peter asked them all to leave the room; then he knelt and prayed. Turning to the body he said, ‘Get up, Tabitha.’ And she opened her eyes! When she saw Peter, she sat up!” Acts 9:40 (NLT) He knelt before he spoke. Ellen White describes the moment in the same order: “he kneeled down and prayed fervently to God to restore Dorcas to life and health” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 132 · AA 132.1.

Peter served, but God supplied the power. Then Peter “presented her to them alive” Acts 9:41 (NLT)—returned to the very widows and believers she had served—and “The news spread through the whole town, and many believed in the Lord.” Acts 9:42 (NLT) Standing alongside Luke’s account, Ellen White points to the purpose of what God did:

“Dorcas had been of great service to the church, and God saw fit to bring her back from the land of the enemy, that her skill and energy might still be a blessing to others, and also that by this manifestation of His power the cause of Christ might be strengthened”. The Acts of the Apostles, p. 132 · AA 132.1

The miracle did not make Dorcas famous; it made the Lord known. Scripture does not promise that every faithful servant will be restored in this life, and Dorcas’s usefulness did not put God in her debt; her restoration was His gift, and the story ends not with a woman rising but with many believing in the Lord.

Peter served, God supplied the power, and the glory went to the Lord.

Dorcas did not need a platform to have a ministry. She used what God had placed in her hands to meet real needs. Jesus said, “Your love for one another will prove to the world that you are my disciples.” John 13:35 (NLT) We are not saved by such works—Paul is clear that salvation “is not a reward for the good things we have done” Ephesians 2:9 (NLT)—but the grace that saves us produces a faith that serves: “For we are God’s masterpiece. He has created us anew in Christ Jesus, so we can do the good things he planned for us long ago.” Ephesians 2:10 (NLT)

Not everyone can sew. Some can cook, visit, listen, teach, repair, drive someone to hospital, or turn a professional skill toward a person who cannot pay. The question is not whether we can do what Dorcas did but whether Christ’s love is becoming visible through what He has already placed in our hands. This is not a call to exhaustion or a scoreboard: Dorcas served because she followed Jesus, not to prove her worth, and the sick, the grieving and the overburdened are not being measured here—some of the most Christlike service in any church comes from people whose capacity is small.

Ask the question pastorally, then: If I were absent from my church or community tomorrow, what evidence of Christ’s love through my life would people be able to point to? What need near me have I noticed but not yet acted upon?

Use what God has placed in your hands to make Christ’s love visible.

Key Takeaways

  1. A disciple known by her life of service. Dorcas “was always doing kind things for others and helping the poor.” Acts 9:36 (NLT) Ellen White calls her “a worthy disciple of Jesus” whose “life was filled with acts of kindness” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 131 · AA 131.2.
  2. Her faith left visible evidence in the lives of others. The widows were “showing him the coats and other clothes Dorcas had made for them.” Acts 9:39 (NLT) They did not need to explain her faith; they could show what her love had done.
  3. God restored a life that had been a blessing to His church. Peter “knelt and prayed” Acts 9:40 (NLT) and “many believed in the Lord.” Acts 9:42 (NLT) God restored her “that her skill and energy might still be a blessing to others” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 132 · AA 132.1. Peter served; God supplied the power.
  4. Christ calls us to use what He has placed in our hands. “Your love for one another will prove to the world that you are my disciples.” John 13:35 (NLT) Saved by grace and created anew in Christ for good works, let Christ’s love become visible through the way you serve.
