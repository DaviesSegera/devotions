Today's Devotion

When Peter Denied Jesus

Luke 22 · 7 min read
Key Verse
“But I have pleaded in prayer for you, Simon, that your faith should not fail.”
Luke 22:32 (NLT, excerpt)
“At that moment the Lord turned and looked at Peter.”
Luke 22:61 (NLT, excerpt)

Peter was certain he would never deny Jesus. Before the night was over, he had done it three times. On the evening Jesus was betrayed, Peter meant every word he said: “Lord, I am ready to go to prison with you, and even to die with you.” Luke 22:33 (NLT) Peter loved Jesus. His problem was not that he cared too little; it was that he did not know his own weakness. Ellen G. White puts it plainly:

“Peter did not know his own danger. Self-confidence misled him. He thought himself able to withstand temptation; but in a few short hours the test came, and with cursing and swearing he denied his Lord.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 152 · COL 152.2

Sincerity is not the same as strength, and a sincere disciple can trust himself more than he realizes.

Peter trusted his love for Jesus; he needed to learn to trust Jesus more than himself.

Now notice what Jesus said before any of it happened. “Simon, Simon, Satan has asked to sift each of you like wheat. But I have pleaded in prayer for you, Simon, that your faith should not fail. So when you have repented and turned to me again, strengthen your brothers.” Luke 22:31–32 (NLT) Jesus did not flatter Peter, and He did not minimize the danger: “Before the rooster crows tomorrow morning, you will deny three times that you even know me.” Luke 22:34 (NLT) But in the same breath He spoke of a future on the other side of the failure—a repentant Peter, turned back to Christ, strengthening his brothers.

The warning and the prayer belong together. In Gethsemane Jesus warned him once more, by name: “Keep watch and pray, so that you will not give in to temptation. For the spirit is willing, but the body is weak!” Matthew 26:41 (NLT) Peter slept. Ellen G. White helps us see what that cost him: “Had those hours in the garden been spent in watching and prayer, Peter would not have been left to depend upon his own feeble strength. He would not have denied his Lord.” The Desire of Ages, p. 713 · DA 713.4

Christ’s prayer was never permission to be careless; it was the reason Peter had every hope of standing.

Before Peter fell, Jesus was already praying for his faith and looking beyond the failure to restoration.

Then the pressure came. Jesus was arrested and led to the high priest’s home. “And Peter followed at a distance.” Luke 22:54 (NLT) A fire was lit in the courtyard, and Peter joined the company around it. There is nothing wrong with warming yourself at a fire; the trouble was what Peter was trying to hide. Ellen G. White tells us: “In assuming an air of indifference he had placed himself on the enemy’s ground, and he became an easy prey to temptation.” The Desire of Ages, p. 712 · DA 712.1

A servant girl said, “This man was one of Jesus’ followers!” and Peter answered, “I don’t even know him!” Someone else said, “You must be one of them!” “No, man, I’m not!” About an hour later a third insisted that he must be one of them, and Peter said, “Man, I don’t know what you are talking about.” And immediately, while he was still speaking, the rooster crowed. Luke 22:56–60 (NLT) Then comes one of the most tender moments in the Gospels: “At that moment the Lord turned and looked at Peter. Suddenly, the Lord’s words flashed through Peter’s mind … And Peter left the courtyard, weeping bitterly.” Luke 22:61–62 (NLT)

Luke tells us that Jesus turned and looked at Peter. Ellen G. White lingers over what Peter discerned in that moment: “In that gentle countenance he read deep pity and sorrow, but there was no anger there.” The Desire of Ages, p. 712 · DA 712.4 And she adds, “That look of Christ’s broke his heart. Peter had come to the turning point, and bitterly did he repent his sin … The look of Christ assured him of pardon.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 152 · COL 152.3

His tears were not a payment for pardon—grace is never purchased. They were the grief of a man who had been loved, and knew it.

Christ’s look brought conviction together with compassion—not to trap Peter in shame, but to bring him back.

And Peter’s story did not end in the courtyard. Ellen G. White says: “Though Peter had grievously sinned, he was not forsaken.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 155 · COL 155.4 On the resurrection morning the angel at the empty tomb said, “Now go and tell his disciples, including Peter, that Jesus is going ahead of you to Galilee.” Mark 16:7 (NLT) Including Peter. The risen Christ “was seen by Peter” 1 Corinthians 15:5 (NLT), and later, by the Sea of Galilee, He asked him three times, “Simon son of John, do you love me?” and gave him his work back: “Then feed my sheep.” John 21:17 (NLT)

Then He said once more, “Follow me.” John 21:19 (NLT) Ellen G. White tells us: “Although Peter had denied his Lord, the love which Jesus bore him never faltered.” The Desire of Ages, p. 815 · DA 815.1 Peter’s return to apostolic work was the risen Lord’s own act; today, Christ’s grace restores a repentant person to Himself and to meaningful discipleship, while particular roles may rightly involve accountability, time, and the protection of others. That is not the opposite of grace; it is grace being honest.

So where am I trusting my own strength instead of depending on Christ? And if the rooster has already crowed in your life, hear this: the Jesus who prayed for Peter before he fell, who turned and looked at him in the moment of his collapse, and who sought him out after the resurrection is the same crucified and risen Saviour today, and His promise stands: “if we confess our sins to him, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all wickedness.” 1 John 1:9 (NLT)

Peter failed Jesus, but Jesus did not abandon Peter—come back to Him, depend on His strength, and follow Him in humble, faithful service.
