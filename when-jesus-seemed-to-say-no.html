When Jesus Seemed to Say No

Mark 7:24–30 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-09

A desperate mother keeps coming to Jesus when His answer seems like a refusal. Reflect on persistent prayer, trusting His compassion, and welcoming others across the barriers we build.

Faith & trustPrayerWaiting on GodLove & service

Key Verse
“‘Dear woman,’ Jesus said to her, ‘your faith is great. Your request is granted.’ And her daughter was instantly healed.”
Matthew 15:28 (NLT)

Have you ever brought an urgent need to God in prayer and heard only silence? You prayed and waited until the silence began to feel like a closed door. A mother in the Gospels knew that feeling. Her story shows us how to keep trusting Jesus when He seems to say no.

Jesus wanted some time away from the crowds. “Then Jesus left Galilee and went north to the region of Tyre. He didn’t want anyone to know which house he was staying in, but he couldn’t keep it a secret.” Mark 7:24, NLT Tyre was a busy Mediterranean port in what is now Lebanon. Most of its people were Gentiles. Ellen G. White tells us that Jesus hoped to rest there with His disciples. She adds, “Yet this was not His only purpose in taking this journey.” The Desire of Ages, p. 399 · DA 399.1, excerpt

A mother in the region was desperate for help. “Right away a woman who had heard about him came and fell at his feet. Her little girl was possessed by an evil spirit,” Mark 7:25, NLT “and she begged him to cast out the demon from her daughter. Since she was a Gentile, born in Syrian Phoenicia,” Mark 7:26, NLT She was a Gentile from the coast around Tyre and Sidon. She came to Jesus because she believed He could help her daughter. Ellen White describes how that hope took hold.

“As she heard of His power, hope sprang up in her heart. Inspired by a mother’s love, she determined to present her daughter’s case to Him. It was her resolute purpose to bring her affliction to Jesus. He must heal her child.”The Desire of Ages, p. 399 · DA 399.2, excerpt

Ellen White tells us where the mother had already turned for help. “She had sought help from the heathen gods, but had obtained no relief.” The Desire of Ages, p. 399 · DA 399.2, excerpt Jesus was her last hope, and she held on to Him. Yet Jesus had already taken the first step. “Christ knew this woman’s situation. He knew that she was longing to see Him, and He placed Himself in her path.” The Desire of Ages, p. 400 · DA 400.1, excerpt

Before she came to Jesus, He had already drawn near to her.

In Matthew’s account, we hear her cry for mercy. “Have mercy on me, O Lord, Son of David! For my daughter is possessed by a demon that torments her severely.” Matthew 15:22, NLT, excerpt She used the royal title “Son of David” to address Jesus as the Messiah, the King and Saviour God had promised. What happened next is hard to read. “But Jesus gave her no reply, not even a word. Then his disciples urged him to send her away. ‘Tell her to go away,’ they said. ‘She is bothering us with all her begging.’” Matthew 15:23, NLT

The disciples wanted relief from the woman’s cries. Jesus saw the pain behind them. Ellen White explains that His silence had a purpose. “Christ did not immediately reply to the woman’s request. He received this representative of a despised race as the Jews would have done.” The Desire of Ages, p. 400 · DA 400.2, excerpt For a moment, Jesus let the disciples see what their own attitude towards outsiders looked like. Soon He would show them how He wanted them to respond.

Ellen White describes how the woman kept asking for help.

“But although Jesus did not reply, the woman did not lose faith. As He passed on, as if not hearing her, she followed Him, continuing her supplications.”The Desire of Ages, p. 400 · DA 400.3, excerpt

When Jesus finally spoke, He began by describing His mission to Israel. “I was sent only to help God’s lost sheep—the people of Israel.” Matthew 15:24, NLT, excerpt God had chosen to fulfil His promises through Israel first. Paul later described the gospel as “the power of God at work, saving everyone who believes—the Jew first and also the Gentile.” Romans 1:16, NLT, excerpt For Gentiles like this mother, the words that matter are “and also.” Ellen White calls Jesus’ answer “an implied rebuke to the disciples,” The Desire of Ages, p. 400 · DA 400.3, excerpt and reminds us of the compassion beneath it. “But it was a pitying Saviour to whom the woman made her plea,” The Desire of Ages, p. 400 · DA 400.3, excerpt she writes.

God cares for us even when He seems silent.

The mother drew closer. “But she came and worshiped him, pleading again, ‘Lord, help me!’” Matthew 15:25, NLT In that brief prayer, she brought her need to Jesus as plainly as she could.

Jesus answered with a picture of children at the table. “Jesus told her, ‘First I should feed the children—my own family, the Jews. It isn’t right to take food from the children and throw it to the dogs.’” Mark 7:27, NLT Many Bible scholars understand the Greek word for “dogs” here to mean the little dogs of the household, the kind that wait under the family table. The early Christian preacher John Chrysostom understood Jesus’ words as a way of drawing out the woman’s faith. Ellen White sees a similar purpose. Still, His answer sounded like a refusal.

The woman stayed and answered Him. “She replied, ‘That’s true, Lord, but even the dogs under the table are allowed to eat the scraps from the children’s plates.’” Mark 7:28, NLT She took His words seriously and found hope in them. Ellen White describes what the mother saw in Jesus.

“This answer would have utterly discouraged a less earnest seeker. But the woman saw that her opportunity had come. Beneath the apparent refusal of Jesus, she saw a compassion that He could not hide.”The Desire of Ages, p. 401 · DA 401.1, excerpt

The children could eat their fill and still leave something for the dogs beneath the table. Ellen White follows the mother’s reasoning. “So while there were many blessings given to Israel, was there not also a blessing for her?” The Desire of Ages, p. 401 · DA 401.1, excerpt She trusted that a crumb of His mercy would be enough to heal her child.

She heard a hard answer and still trusted Jesus’ heart.

Then Jesus granted her request. “‘Good answer!’ he said. ‘Now go home, for the demon has left your daughter.’” Mark 7:29, NLT Matthew also records how Jesus praised her faith. “Dear woman,” Jesus said to her, “your faith is great. Your request is granted.” Matthew 15:28, NLT, excerpt She took Jesus at His word and went home. “And when she arrived home, she found her little girl lying quietly in bed, and the demon was gone.” Mark 7:30, NLT

For Ellen White, the healing reveals what Jesus had been doing all along.

“The Saviour is satisfied. He has tested her faith in Him. By His dealings with her, He has shown that she who has been regarded as an outcast from Israel is no longer an alien, but a child in God’s household.”The Desire of Ages, p. 401 · DA 401.3, excerpt

The mother who asked for scraps was welcomed as a child of God. Ellen White says she left “acknowledging her Saviour, and happy in the granting of her prayer.” The Desire of Ages, p. 401 · DA 401.3, excerpt Her trust lifted Jesus’ spirit, too. “In the border of Tyre and Sidon His spirit had been refreshed by the confiding trust of the Syrophoenician woman.” The Desire of Ages, p. 405 · DA 405.1, excerpt

Jesus once told a story “to show that they should always pray and never give up.” Luke 18:1, NLT, excerpt This mother shows us what persistence looks like. We may receive a different answer from the one we hoped for, or wait longer than we expected. We can keep bringing our needs to Jesus as we wait. “So let us come boldly to the throne of our gracious God. There we will receive his mercy, and we will find grace to help us when we need it most.” Hebrews 4:16, NLT Ellen White describes the trust Jesus asks of us.

“Against discouragement, regardless of appearances that might have led her to doubt, she trusted the Saviour’s love. It is thus that Christ desires us to trust in Him.”The Desire of Ages, p. 403 · DA 403.2, excerpt

Faith keeps coming to Jesus, trusting who He is before seeing what He will do.

The disciples who had wanted to send the mother away had a lesson to learn from her welcome. Ellen White explains why Jesus brought them to this region. “He wished to lead them from their Jewish exclusiveness to be interested in working for others besides their own people.” The Desire of Ages, p. 402 · DA 402.1, excerpt Jesus and His disciples were Jewish, and the Scriptures they knew had long borne witness to God’s care for all nations. God had promised Abraham, “All the families on earth will be blessed through you.” Genesis 12:3, NLT, excerpt In His hometown synagogue, Jesus had reminded His listeners that Elijah “was sent instead to a foreigner—a widow of Zarephath in the land of Sidon.” Luke 4:26, NLT, excerpt Now, in that same region, another mother received God’s mercy.

Ellen White draws out the lesson Jesus wanted His disciples to learn. “He would show that His love was not to be circumscribed to race or nation.” The Desire of Ages, p. 402 · DA 402.2, excerpt Christ’s love reaches across every boundary of race and nation. At the cross, He broke down the wall that kept people apart. “For Christ himself has brought peace to us. He united Jews and Gentiles into one people when, in his own body on the cross, he broke down the wall of hostility that separated us.” Ephesians 2:14, NLT Paul makes the same point in his letter to the Galatians. “For you are all one in Christ Jesus.” Galatians 3:28, NLT, excerpt

We still build walls between people, as Ellen White observes. “Pride and prejudice have built strong walls of separation between different classes of men.” The Desire of Ages, p. 403 · DA 403.1, excerpt We can echo “send her away,” even without saying it aloud. We do it when we keep people at a distance because of their background, where they come from, or what they have been through. Jesus calls us to welcome them.

Perhaps you feel shut out yourself, as though you are too far away, too unworthy, or too different to belong. Ellen White speaks directly to that fear.

“But let them not feel that they are shut away from Christ. There are no barriers which man or Satan can erect but that faith can penetrate.”The Desire of Ages, p. 403 · DA 403.1, excerpt

Bring your need to Jesus, even if all you can say is “Lord, help me!” He has already come near. He sees what you are carrying and meets you with compassion.

Jesus welcomes everyone who comes to Him in faith.

Questions for Reflection

  1. How do I respond when God seems silent? What helps me keep praying?
  2. What would it look like to trust Jesus’ love when I struggle to understand His answer?
  3. Is there someone I’ve kept at a distance? How can I welcome them this week as Jesus would?

Three Truths to Remember

  1. God cares even in the silence. Jesus had come near to the mother before she asked for help. She kept coming to Him even when He was silent. Matthew 15:23 · quoted above
  2. Faith trusts Jesus’ heart. The mother heard a hard answer and still asked for mercy. Jesus praised her faith. Mark 7:29 · quoted above
  3. Christ’s love crosses barriers. Jesus broke down the wall that divided people and calls us to welcome those we might otherwise keep at a distance. Ephesians 2:14 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Lord Jesus, thank You for coming near when we feel far away. When our prayers seem to meet only silence, give us the humble, persistent faith of this mother. Help us keep coming to You and trusting Your love. Forgive us for keeping people at a distance. Through Your cross, You broke down the walls that divide us. Open our hearts to everyone You welcome, and teach us to bring our needs to You with confidence in Your care. In Your name we pray. Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
