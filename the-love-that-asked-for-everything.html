The Love That Asked for Everything

Mark 10:17–22 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-23

Jesus looks at the rich young ruler with love and invites him to follow. Reflect on what we hold tightly, the grace that frees our hearts, and using what God gives us to bless others.

Faith & trustForgiveness & graceHumility & obedienceLove & service
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“Looking at the man, Jesus felt genuine love for him. ‘There is still one thing you haven’t done,’ he told him. ‘Go and sell all your possessions and give the money to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven. Then come, follow me.’”
Mark 10:21 (NLT)

Have you ever wanted something good, only to draw back when you saw what it would cost? Mark tells us about a man who ran to Jesus and knelt at His feet. A few minutes later, he walked away sad.

Just before this, parents had brought their children to Jesus. “Then he took the children in his arms and placed his hands on their heads and blessed them.” Mark 10:16, NLT Ellen G. White tells us that a wealthy young ruler was watching:

“He saw the love that Christ manifested toward the children brought to Him; he saw how tenderly He received them, and took them up in His arms, and his heart kindled with love for the Saviour. He felt a desire to be His disciple.”The Desire of Ages, p. 518 · DA 518.2, excerpt

His heart “kindled,” or caught fire. He did not wait. “As Jesus was starting out on his way to Jerusalem, a man came running up to him, knelt down, and asked, ‘Good Teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?’” Mark 10:17, NLT

He came running to the right Person with the right question.

Jesus answered with a question of His own. “‘Why do you call me good?’ Jesus asked. ‘Only God is truly good.’” Mark 10:18, NLT Jesus wanted the man to consider what it meant to call Him good. Did he recognize who Jesus really was? Ellen G. White puts His meaning this way: “On what ground do you call Me good? God is the one good. If you recognize Me as such, you must receive Me as His Son and representative.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 390 · COL 390.3, excerpt

Then Jesus pointed him to God’s law. “But to answer your question, you know the commandments: ‘You must not murder. You must not commit adultery. You must not steal. You must not testify falsely. You must not cheat anyone. Honor your father and mother.’” Mark 10:19, NLT The man answered at once. “‘Teacher,’ the man replied, ‘I’ve obeyed all these commandments since I was young.’” Mark 10:20, NLT

He meant it. Yet he still felt uneasy. Ellen G. White writes:

“To a great degree his outward life had been free from guilt; he verily thought that his obedience had been without a flaw. Yet he had a secret fear that all was not right between his soul and God.”Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 391 · COL 391.4, excerpt

Many of us know that feeling. We can keep our promises and treat people fairly and still wonder whether all is well between us and God.

Even after years of obedience, the question still weighed on his heart.

Then comes one of the tenderest verses in the Gospels. “Looking at the man, Jesus felt genuine love for him. ‘There is still one thing you haven’t done,’ he told him. ‘Go and sell all your possessions and give the money to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven. Then come, follow me.’” Mark 10:21, NLT

Notice the order. Jesus loved him before He said the hard thing. Ellen G. White writes, “He loved him, and He hungered to give him that peace and grace and joy which would materially change his character.” The Desire of Ages, p. 519 · DA 519.1, excerpt What was the one thing he lacked? She answers:

“Christ read the ruler’s heart. Only one thing he lacked, but that was a vital principle. He needed the love of God in the soul.”The Desire of Ages, p. 519 · DA 519.4, excerpt

The commandments Jesus listed were about how we treat other people. His test went deeper, to the first commandment: “You must not have any other god but me.” Exodus 20:3, NLT The man’s possessions had taken the place in his heart that belonged to God.

Christians have applied this command in different ways. Some see a call for certain believers to choose a life of poverty. Many understand it as a test of this man’s attachment to his wealth. Jesus did not ask every follower to take the same step. Zacchaeus welcomed Jesus and said, “I will give half my wealth to the poor, Lord”. Luke 19:8, NLT, excerpt Yet the principle reaches us all. Ellen G. White writes, “Self-surrender is the substance of the teachings of Christ.” The Desire of Ages, p. 523 · DA 523.3, excerpt Self-surrender means placing our whole selves in God’s hands.

And notice Jesus’ last words: “Then come, follow me.” He was not only asking the man to let go. He was inviting him to walk with Him. “In the place of that which He called upon him to surrender, Christ offered him the privilege of companionship with Himself.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 393 · COL 393.2, excerpt

Jesus loved him enough to name the one thing that stood between them.

Then Mark gives us the saddest line of the story. “At this the man’s face fell, and he went away sad, for he had many possessions.” Mark 10:22, NLT Ellen G. White names the real problem:

“He loved the gifts of God more than he loved the Giver. Christ had offered the young man fellowship with Himself. ‘Follow Me,’ He said. But the Saviour was not so much to him as his own name among men or his possessions.”The Desire of Ages, p. 520 · DA 520.4, excerpt

Jesus had taught, “Wherever your treasure is, there the desires of your heart will also be.” Matthew 6:21, NLT The question for those who have wealth is where they place their trust. Paul tells Timothy what to teach the rich. “Their trust should be in God, who richly gives us all we need for our enjoyment.” 1 Timothy 6:17, NLT, excerpt

What we hold too tightly is not always money. “Mammon is the idol of many. The love of money, the desire for wealth, is the golden chain that binds them to Satan. Reputation and worldly honor are worshiped by another class. The life of selfish ease and freedom from responsibility is the idol of others.” Steps to Christ, p. 44 · SC 44.1, excerpt “Mammon” is an old word for wealth. What would make my face fall if Jesus asked me for it?

He went away holding everything except the One who loved him.

As the man left, Jesus turned to His disciples. “Jesus looked around and said to his disciples, ‘How hard it is for the rich to enter the Kingdom of God!’” Mark 10:23, NLT “In fact, it is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for a rich person to enter the Kingdom of God!” Mark 10:25, NLT “The disciples were astounded. ‘Then who in the world can be saved?’ they asked.” Mark 10:26, NLT

Jesus’ answer gives us hope. “Jesus looked at them intently and said, ‘Humanly speaking, it is impossible. But not with God. Everything is possible with God.’” Mark 10:27, NLT

“A rich man, as such, cannot enter heaven. His wealth gives him no title to the inheritance of the saints in light. It is only through the unmerited grace of Christ that any man can find entrance into the city of God.”Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 394 · COL 394.3

“Unmerited grace” is kindness we have not earned. Paul says, “God saved you by his grace when you believed. And you can’t take credit for this; it is a gift from God.” Ephesians 2:8, NLT The ruler asked what he must do. Jesus was offering him a gift.

How can a heart that clings to its treasures be set free? Ellen G. White answers, “By beholding the matchless love of Christ, the selfish heart will be melted and subdued.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 394 · COL 394.4, excerpt To behold Christ is to keep our eyes on Him.

What we cannot do by holding on, God can do by showing us Christ.

Jesus was on His way to Jerusalem, and He told the twelve what awaited Him there. “They will mock him, spit on him, flog him with a whip, and kill him, but after three days he will rise again.” Mark 10:34, NLT The One who asked the ruler to give up his riches was about to give His own life.

Paul writes, “You know the generous grace of our Lord Jesus Christ. Though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, so that by his poverty he could make you rich.” 2 Corinthians 8:9, NLT Ellen G. White sets this verse beside the ruler’s story and adds, “And in this Christ did not ask a greater sacrifice than He Himself had made.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 393 · COL 393.1, excerpt When surrender feels too costly, she points us to the cross: “Do you feel that it is too great a sacrifice to yield all to Christ? Ask yourself the question, ‘What has Christ given for me?’ The Son of God gave all—life and love and suffering—for our redemption.” Steps to Christ, p. 45 · SC 45.1, excerpt

Mark does not tell us what became of the man. Now that same choice is ours. “You cannot change your heart, you cannot of yourself give to God its affections; but you can choose to serve Him.” Steps to Christ, p. 47 · SC 47.1, excerpt

Perhaps you have held something back from Jesus. He is still looking at you with love. He asks because He wants nothing to stand between you and Him. Bring Him what you are holding and follow Him.

The love that asks for everything is the same love that gave everything for us.

Reflect

  1. Like the young ruler, do I sense that something is missing in my relationship with God, even when my life looks right from the outside?
  2. If Jesus looked at me with love and named the one thing I hold most tightly, what would it be?
  3. How can I use what God has placed in my hands to bless someone in need this week?

Key Takeaways

  1. Jesus’ hard words come from His love. He looked at the man and loved him before He named the one thing that held him back. Mark 10:21 · quoted above
  2. Nothing may take God’s place in our hearts. The ruler kept his possessions and walked away from the One who offered Himself. Mark 10:22 · quoted above
  3. What is impossible for us is possible with God. No one earns eternal life. By His grace, God can loosen our grip on what we treasure and free us to follow Jesus. Mark 10:27 · quoted above

White, The Desire of Ages, ch. 57, “‘One Thing Thou Lackest,’” pp. 518–523 (DA 518.2, 519.1, 519.4, 520.4, 523.3); Christ’s Object Lessons, ch. 28, “The Reward of Grace,” pp. 390–394 (COL 390.3, 391.4, 393.1, 393.2, 394.3, 394.4); Steps to Christ, ch. 5, “Consecration,” pp. 44–47 (SC 44.1, 45.1, 47.1).

Textual note: “Rich young ruler” is a traditional name that combines the three Gospels: Matthew calls him a young man (Matthew 19:20, 22) and Luke a religious leader, literally a ruler (Luke 18:18). At Mark 10:24 the NLT notes that some manuscripts read “very hard for those who trust in riches.” Ellen G. White quotes the King James Version, which renders Mark 10:21 “One thing thou lackest.”

A prayer for today

Heavenly Father, thank You for the love in Jesus’ eyes when He looked at the young ruler. Thank You that He looks at us with that same love today. Lord Jesus, You were rich, yet for our sakes You became poor and gave Your life for us. Show us what we cling to more tightly than we cling to You. When we cannot let go in our own strength, set our hearts free. Teach us to use what You have given us to bless those in need. When You say, “Follow me,” give us the courage to come gladly. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
