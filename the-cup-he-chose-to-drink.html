The Cup He Chose to Drink

Matthew 26:36–46 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-24

In Gethsemane, Jesus asks His Father to take away the cup of suffering and surrenders to His will. Reflect on praying honestly, trusting God when the answer is hard, and finding strength for what lies ahead.

PrayerFaith & trustHumility & obedienceHope & encouragement

Key Verse
“He went on a little farther and bowed with his face to the ground, praying, ‘My Father! If it is possible, let this cup of suffering be taken away from me. Yet I want your will to be done, not mine.’”
Matthew 26:39 (NLT)

Have you ever prayed for something with all your heart and received a different answer than you hoped for? On the night before the cross, Jesus prayed that kind of prayer. In the quiet of an olive grove, He showed us how to pray when we are afraid and how much He was willing to give for us.

After the Passover meal, Jesus warned His disciples that they would desert Him. “Peter declared, ‘Even if everyone else deserts you, I will never desert you.’” Matthew 26:33, NLT Matthew adds, “And all the other disciples vowed the same.” Matthew 26:35, NLT, excerpt They meant every word.

“Then Jesus went with them to the olive grove called Gethsemane, and he said, ‘Sit here while I go over there to pray.’” Matthew 26:36, NLT Three of His closest friends went farther into the grove with Him. “He took Peter and Zebedee’s two sons, James and John, and he became anguished and distressed.” Matthew 26:37, NLT “He told them, ‘My soul is crushed with grief to the point of death. Stay here and keep watch with me.’” Matthew 26:38, NLT In His sorrow, Jesus wanted the comfort of His friends’ company.

On His hardest night, Jesus did not pretend to be strong. He told His friends the truth and turned to His Father.

Alone with His Father, Jesus prayed. “He went on a little farther and bowed with his face to the ground, praying, ‘My Father! If it is possible, let this cup of suffering be taken away from me. Yet I want your will to be done, not mine.’” Matthew 26:39, NLT

Even in deep anguish, Jesus called God “My Father!” Mark preserves the Aramaic word for “father” in that cry. “‘Abba, Father,’ he cried out, ‘everything is possible for you. Please take this cup of suffering away from me…’” Mark 14:36, NLT, excerpt Jesus told His Father what He wanted. In the same prayer, He entrusted the answer to Him. He did not stop asking, and He did not stop trusting.

What was in that cup? Earlier that evening, at supper, Jesus had held a cup and said, “this is my blood, which confirms the covenant between God and his people. It is poured out as a sacrifice to forgive the sins of many.” Matthew 26:28, NLT, excerpt Long before, Isaiah had written of God’s suffering Servant: “Yet the LORD laid on him the sins of us all.” Isaiah 53:6, NLT, excerpt Ellen G. White describes how heavily our sin weighed on Jesus in the garden.

“He felt that by sin He was being separated from His Father. The gulf was so broad, so black, so deep, that His spirit shuddered before it.”The Desire of Ages, p. 686 · DA 686.3, excerpt

Paul explains why Jesus carried a load that was not His own: “For God made Christ, who never sinned, to be the offering for our sin, so that we could be made right with God through Christ.” 2 Corinthians 5:21, NLT Ellen G. White writes, “Behold Him contemplating the price to be paid for the human soul. In His agony He clings to the cold ground, as if to prevent Himself from being drawn farther from God.” The Desire of Ages, p. 687 · DA 687.2, excerpt

Jesus brought His Father both an honest request and a surrendered will.

“Then he returned to the disciples and found them asleep. He said to Peter, ‘Couldn’t you watch with me even one hour? Keep watch and pray, so that you will not give in to temptation. For the spirit is willing, but the body is weak!’” Matthew 26:40–41, NLT Luke tells us they were “exhausted from grief.” Luke 22:45, NLT, excerpt These were the men who had promised to die with Him.

Ellen G. White saw more than weariness in their sleep.

“But the disciples trusted to themselves. They did not look to the mighty Helper as Christ had counseled them to do. Thus when the Saviour was most in need of their sympathy and prayers, they were found asleep.”The Desire of Ages, p. 688 · DA 688.2, excerpt

Yet Jesus did not give up on them. “The weakness of His disciples awakened the sympathy of Jesus.” The Desire of Ages, p. 689 · DA 689.2, excerpt Like Peter, we can love Jesus, mean every promise we make, and still fail Him. We need to stay close to God and keep asking for His strength.

Good intentions cannot carry us through the night, but staying close to God in prayer can.

When Jesus returned to pray, His words changed. “Then Jesus left them a second time and prayed, ‘My Father! If this cup cannot be taken away unless I drink it, your will be done.’” Matthew 26:42, NLT “So he went to pray a third time, saying the same things again.” Matthew 26:44, NLT

Ellen G. White describes what was at stake:

“The awful moment had come—that moment which was to decide the destiny of the world. The fate of humanity trembled in the balance. Christ might even now refuse to drink the cup apportioned to guilty man.”The Desire of Ages, p. 690 · DA 690.2, excerpt

He did not refuse. She continues, “He beholds its impending fate, and His decision is made. He will save man at any cost to Himself.” The Desire of Ages, p. 690 · DA 690.3, excerpt When Peter drew his sword at the arrest, Jesus put that decision into words. “Shall I not drink from the cup of suffering the Father has given me?” John 18:11, NLT, excerpt He could have asked His Father for “thousands of angels to protect us,” Matthew 26:53, NLT, excerpt but He chose the cup instead.

In the garden, the Father sent help to His Son. “Then an angel from heaven appeared and strengthened him.” Luke 22:43, NLT Ellen G. White explains why the angel came:

“The angel came not to take the cup from Christ’s hand, but to strengthen Him to drink it, with the assurance of the Father’s love.”The Desire of Ages, p. 693 · DA 693.3, excerpt

She adds, “Christ’s agony did not cease, but His depression and discouragement left Him.” The Desire of Ages, p. 694 · DA 694.1, excerpt The words of Hebrews help us understand that night. “While Jesus was here on earth, he offered prayers and pleadings, with a loud cry and tears, to the one who could rescue him from death. And God heard his prayers because of his deep reverence for God.” Hebrews 5:7, NLT The Father heard His Son and gave Him strength to face the cross. Beyond the cross lay the resurrection.

Strengthened by His Father, Jesus walked the way of the cross for us.

Then Jesus stood up. “Up, let’s be going. Look, my betrayer is here!” Matthew 26:46, NLT Paul describes the path He chose: “he humbled himself in obedience to God and died a criminal’s death on a cross.” Philippians 2:8, NLT

Our trials are not that cup. Only Jesus could carry the sins of the world. But because He knelt in Gethsemane, we have a Saviour who knows what it is to pray in anguish. “This High Priest of ours understands our weaknesses, for he faced all of the same testings we do, yet he did not sin.” Hebrews 4:15, NLT That is why we can take this invitation to heart. “So let us come boldly to the throne of our gracious God.” Hebrews 4:16, NLT, excerpt

Ellen G. White points to Jesus’ prayer in the garden as a pattern for our own.

“The consistent course is to commit our desires to our all-wise heavenly Father, and then, in perfect confidence, trust all to Him.”The Ministry of Healing, p. 230 · MH 230.2, excerpt

To pray “your will be done” is to place what we love in hands we can trust. If the answer is different from the one we hoped for, we need never take that as a measure of our faith.

Perhaps you are in a Gethsemane of your own, waiting for news, grieving a loss, or facing a frightening choice. Tell your Father what is on your heart. You can trust Him with it. “If we are called upon to meet bereavement, let us accept the bitter cup, remembering that a Father’s hand holds it to our lips.” The Ministry of Healing, p. 233 · MH 233.2, excerpt The One who drank that cup for us will stay with us through this night, too.

Because Jesus prayed “your will be done” in Gethsemane, we can trust our Father’s will in our own dark nights.

Reflect

  1. What is weighing on my heart that I want to bring to my Father today?
  2. Where have I been relying on my own good intentions instead of watching and praying?
  3. What would it look like for me to pray “your will be done” in the situation I face today?

Key Takeaways

  1. Bring God an honest heart. Jesus told His Father what He wanted, and in the same prayer He surrendered to His Father’s will. Matthew 26:39 · quoted above
  2. Watch and pray. The disciples meant well but trusted their own strength. Jesus calls us to stay close to God in prayer. Matthew 26:40–41 · quoted above
  3. Jesus chose the cup for us. His Father gave Him strength to finish the work of saving us. Matthew 26:42, 44 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Heavenly Father, thank You for Jesus, who knelt in Gethsemane and chose the cup for us. He willingly bore a weight we could never carry. When we are afraid, teach us to come to You as He did, calling You Father and telling You what is on our hearts. Keep us from trusting in our own strength, and help us to watch and pray. When the answer is not what we hoped, give us grace to trust Your will and strength for the road ahead. Thank You that we never face our dark nights alone. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
