The Transfiguration of Jesus

Luke 9:28–43 · 6 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-03

The disciples see Jesus’ glory and hear the Father’s call to listen to Him. Reflect on the cross, trusting His saving grace, and following Him in compassion for others.

Faith & trustHope & encouragementHumility & obedienceLove & service
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“This is my Son, my Chosen One. Listen to him.”
Luke 9:35 (NLT, excerpt)

A powerful moment of worship can encourage us deeply. But what happens when the service ends and the needs of ordinary life are still waiting? The Transfiguration points us not to a moment we would like to keep, but to the Saviour we are called to follow.

A few days before, Peter had said that Jesus was the Messiah. Then Jesus told His disciples about the cross: “He will be killed, but on the third day he will be raised from the dead.” Luke 9:22 (NLT, excerpt) He also told them what it means to follow Him: “If any of you wants to be my follower, you must give up your own way, take up your cross daily, and follow me.” Luke 9:23 (NLT, excerpt)

“About eight days later Jesus took Peter, John, and James up on a mountain to pray.” Luke 9:28 (NLT) “And as he was praying, the appearance of his face was transformed, and his clothes became dazzling white.” Luke 9:29 (NLT)

That is what transfiguration means. Jesus’ appearance was changed, and the disciples were allowed to see His glory.

The glory did not make Jesus the Son of God. It showed who He already was.

Then “two men, Moses and Elijah, appeared and began talking with Jesus.” Luke 9:30 (NLT, excerpt) Moses had led Israel out of Egypt, and Elijah was a great prophet of God. “They were glorious to see. And they were speaking about his exodus from this world, which was about to be fulfilled in Jerusalem.” Luke 9:31 (NLT) Even in the glory, they spoke of the cross.

The disciples had fallen asleep. When they woke up, they saw Jesus’ glory. Peter wanted to make three shelters and stay. Suddenly a cloud covered them, and they were afraid. “Then a voice from the cloud said, ‘This is my Son, my Chosen One. Listen to him.’” Luke 9:35 (NLT)

The glory shows us who Jesus is. The Father’s command tells us how to respond.

To listen to Jesus is more than hearing a voice. It means taking His words to heart, even His hard words about the cross. And He is gentle with those who are afraid. Matthew tells us, “Then Jesus came over and touched them. ‘Get up,’ he said. ‘Don’t be afraid.’” Matthew 17:7 (NLT) Luke adds, “When the voice finished, Jesus was there alone.” Luke 9:36 (NLT, excerpt)

The greatest response to the glory of Jesus is not more excitement, but a heart willing to hear Him.

The mountain was not the end of the story. “The next day, after they had come down the mountain, a large crowd met Jesus.” Luke 9:37 (NLT) A father was waiting there. “A man in the crowd called out to him, ‘Teacher, I beg you to look at my son, my only child.’” Luke 9:38 (NLT) The boy had suffered for years, and the other disciples had not been able to help. But Jesus came down to that hurting family. “But Jesus rebuked the evil spirit and healed the boy. Then he gave him back to his father.” Luke 9:42 (NLT, excerpt)

Ellen G. White helps us see what this teaches us:

“It was an object lesson of redemption,—the Divine One from the Father’s glory stooping to save the lost. It represented also the disciples’ mission. Not alone upon the mountaintop with Jesus, in hours of spiritual illumination, is the life of Christ’s servants to be spent. There is work for them down in the plain.”The Desire of Ages, p. 429 · DA 429.3

We do not leave Jesus behind when we leave the mountain. We follow Him toward the people He loves.

Jesus kept walking toward Jerusalem. There He gave His life for us, and He rose again. “Christ died for our sins, just as the Scriptures said.” 1 Corinthians 15:3 (NLT, excerpt) Our valleys may still be hard, but we do not walk through them alone. The One who shone on the mountain is the Saviour who stays with us below.

Come to Jesus, listen to His Word, trust His saving grace, and follow Him in the next faithful step of love.

Reflect

  1. Am I only admiring Jesus, or am I listening to what He says and allowing it to shape my life?
  2. Where is Jesus calling me to trust Him and show His compassion this week?
  3. Will I listen to Jesus on the mountain and follow Him into the valley?

Key Takeaways

  1. Jesus is the Father’s Son. “This is my Son, my Chosen One.” Luke 9:35 (excerpt)
  2. The glory kept the cross in view. “they were speaking about his exodus from this world” Luke 9:31 (excerpt)
  3. Jesus comes near to the frightened. “Don’t be afraid.” Matthew 17:7 (excerpt)
  4. Weak faith may come to Jesus. “I do believe, but help me overcome my unbelief!” Mark 9:24 (excerpt)

Sources and Further ReadingScripture: Luke 9:18–45 (key verse 9:35); Matthew 17:1–20; Mark 9:2–29; 1 Corinthians 15:3–4 (NLT). Ellen G. White: The Desire of Ages, chapter 46, “He Was Transfigured,” pp. 419–425; chapter 47, “Ministry,” pp. 426–431.

A prayer for today

Father, thank You for Your Son, Your Chosen One, who went to the cross for me and rose again. Help me listen to Him in Your Word. When the bright moments pass, keep me close to Jesus, and help me serve others in His love. Amen.
