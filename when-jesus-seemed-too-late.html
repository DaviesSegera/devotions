When Jesus Seemed Too Late

Mark 5:21–43 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-10

Jairus receives devastating news while Jesus stops to help someone else. Reflect on trusting Christ through delay, His compassion in grief, and the resurrection hope He gives.

Faith & trustHope & encouragementWaiting on GodPrayer
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“But Jesus overheard them and said to Jairus, ‘Don’t be afraid. Just have faith.’”
Mark 5:36 (NLT)

Have you ever prayed for something that couldn’t wait and felt that God was taking too long? Jairus knew that feeling. His story shows us what Jesus says when it seems too late.

Mark’s story begins on the shore of the Sea of Galilee. “Jesus got into the boat again and went back to the other side of the lake, where a large crowd gathered around him on the shore.” Mark 5:21, NLT Among those who came to meet Jesus was a father desperate for help. “Then a leader of the local synagogue, whose name was Jairus, arrived. When he saw Jesus, he fell at his feet,” Mark 5:22, NLT “pleading fervently with him. ‘My little daughter is dying,’ he said. ‘Please come and lay your hands on her; heal her so she can live.’” Mark 5:23, NLT

Jairus helped lead the local synagogue, where people gathered to pray and hear Scripture. Now he was on his knees before Jesus, in full view of the crowd. Luke tells us why. “His only daughter, who was about twelve years old, was dying.” Luke 8:42, NLT, excerpt

Jesus did not hesitate. “Jesus went with him, and all the people followed, crowding around him.” Mark 5:24, NLT Ellen G. White writes that the father’s plea “had touched the tender, sympathetic heart of Christ, and He at once set out with the ruler for his home.” The Ministry of Healing, p. 59 · MH 59.2, excerpt

Jesus heard the father’s cry and went with him at once.

Jairus’s house was close, but getting there took time. Ellen White describes the walk through the crowd.

“The ruler’s house was not far distant, but Jesus and His companions advanced slowly, for the crowd pressed Him on every side. The anxious father was impatient of delay; but Jesus, pitying the people, stopped now and then to relieve some suffering one, or to comfort a troubled heart.”The Desire of Ages, p. 342 · DA 342.4

Along the way, a woman who had been ill for twelve years touched His robe and was healed. Jesus “turned around in the crowd and asked, ‘Who touched my robe?’” Mark 5:30, NLT, excerpt He waited for her to come forward, then spoke gently to her. “And he said to her, ‘Daughter, your faith has made you well. Go in peace. Your suffering is over.’” Mark 5:34, NLT For the woman, twelve years of suffering had come to an end. For Jairus, every minute still mattered.

Ellen White describes what seeing the woman healed meant to Jairus.

“The delay of Jesus had been so intensely interesting in its results that even the anxious father felt no impatience but watched the scene with deep interest. As the healed woman was sent away comforted and rejoicing, it encouraged him to believe still more firmly that Jesus was able to grant his own petition and heal his daughter.”The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 2, p. 322 · 2SP 322.3, excerpt

As Jairus watched the woman go, he had more reason to trust Jesus with his daughter.

Jesus cares for us even while we wait.

Then the worst news arrived. “While he was still speaking to her, messengers arrived from the home of Jairus, the leader of the synagogue. They told him, ‘Your daughter is dead. There’s no use troubling the Teacher now.’” Mark 5:35, NLT Those words sounded final. Some of us know that feeling from a doctor’s report or a phone call in the middle of the night.

Jesus heard the news and spoke to Jairus at once. “But Jesus overheard them and said to Jairus, ‘Don’t be afraid. Just have faith.’” Mark 5:36, NLT Luke also records the promise Jesus made to Jairus. “But when Jesus heard what had happened, he said to Jairus, ‘Don’t be afraid. Just have faith, and she will be healed.’” Luke 8:50, NLT Ellen White describes how deeply the father’s grief moved Jesus.

“The sympathizing ear of Jesus caught the words that smote the father’s heart like the death-knell of his hopes. The pity of the Saviour was drawn out toward the suffering parent.”The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 2, p. 322 · 2SP 322.3, excerpt

“Just have faith” leaves room for an honest prayer. Later in Mark, another worried father told Jesus how hard it was to believe. “The father instantly cried out, ‘I do believe, but help me overcome my unbelief!’” Mark 9:24, NLT Jesus heard that plea. We can hold on to Him even when we struggle to believe.

When the news says it is too late, Jesus says, “Don’t be afraid.”

Jesus took only three disciples with Him to Jairus’s house. “Then Jesus stopped the crowd and wouldn’t let anyone go with him except Peter, James, and John (the brother of James).” Mark 5:37, NLT “When they came to the home of the synagogue leader, Jesus saw much commotion and weeping and wailing.” Mark 5:38, NLT

“He went inside and asked, ‘Why all this commotion and weeping? The child isn’t dead; she’s only asleep.’” Mark 5:39, NLT Luke tells us why the mourners laughed. “But the crowd laughed at him because they all knew she had died.” Luke 8:53, NLT Ellen White describes how the Pharisees later used Jesus’ words to deny that the girl had died. “As she had been sick only a short time, and was raised immediately after death, the Pharisees declared that the child had not been dead; that Christ Himself had said she was only asleep.” The Desire of Ages, p. 534 · DA 534.3, excerpt

Jesus spoke of death as “sleep” because He was about to wake the girl. He used the same image when His friend Lazarus died. “Then he said, ‘Our friend Lazarus has fallen asleep, but now I will go and wake him up.’” John 11:11, NLT When the disciples misunderstood, Jesus told them plainly, “Lazarus is dead.” John 11:14, NLT, excerpt Christians differ in how they understand what happens between death and resurrection. Many believe that those who die in Christ are consciously with Him until the resurrection. Ellen White understood Jesus’ image of sleep as a description of death itself.

“Christ represents death as a sleep to His believing children. Their life is hid with Christ in God, and until the last trump shall sound those who die will sleep in Him.”The Desire of Ages, p. 527 · DA 527.3, excerpt

The “last trump” is the trumpet call at Christ’s return. Whatever our background, we share the hope that Jesus will raise those who belong to Him.

Jesus can wake the dead.

Jesus “made them all leave, and he took the girl’s father and mother and his three disciples into the room where the girl was lying.” Mark 5:40, NLT, excerpt “Holding her hand, he said to her, ‘Talitha koum,’ which means ‘Little girl, get up!’” Mark 5:41, NLT “Talitha koum” is Aramaic, the language the girl would have heard at home. Ellen White lingers on that detail.

“Jesus approached the bedside, and, taking the child’s hand in His own, He pronounced softly, in the familiar language of her home, the words, ‘Damsel, I say unto thee, arise.’”The Desire of Ages, p. 343 · DA 343.2

“And the girl, who was twelve years old, immediately stood up and walked around! They were overwhelmed and totally amazed.” Mark 5:42, NLT Ellen White describes the moment her parents held her again.

“Instantly a tremor passed through the unconscious form. The pulses of life beat again. The lips unclosed with a smile. The eyes opened widely as if from sleep, and the maiden gazed with wonder on the group beside her. She arose, and her parents clasped her in their arms, and wept for joy.”The Desire of Ages, p. 343 · DA 343.3

Then Jesus turned to something as ordinary as a meal. “Jesus gave them strict orders not to tell anyone what had happened, and then he told them to give her something to eat.” Mark 5:43, NLT Ellen White points to that simple instruction as a sign of His care. “When He raised from the dead the daughter of Jairus, He reminded her parents that she must have something to eat.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 357 · COL 357.2, excerpt The One who called her back to life also thought of her hunger.

The One who has power over death also cares about our daily needs.

Some of us have prayed for a loved one to recover and still had to say goodbye. Even Jairus’s daughter would one day die again. Ellen White reminds us that the people Jesus raised were still mortal. “He had raised the son of the widow of Nain, and the ruler’s daughter and Lazarus. But these were not clothed with immortality. After they were raised, they were still subject to death.” The Desire of Ages, p. 786 · DA 786.2, excerpt What happened in Jairus’s house gives us a glimpse of what Jesus will do at the resurrection.

Our hope rests in the resurrection Jesus promised. He told Martha, “I am the resurrection and the life. Anyone who believes in me will live, even after dying.” John 11:25, NLT, excerpt Paul offered that same hope to grieving believers. “For since we believe that Jesus died and was raised to life again, we also believe that when Jesus returns, God will bring back with him the believers who have died.” 1 Thessalonians 4:14, NLT Ellen White looks ahead to the day Jesus calls the dead to life. “That voice will penetrate the graves and unbar the tombs, and the dead in Christ shall arise.” The Desire of Ages, p. 787 · DA 787.2, excerpt

When Jesus seems late, remember how He stayed with Jairus. He heard the news, spoke to the father’s fear, and walked with him all the way home. The risen Jesus has words for our fear, too. “Don’t be afraid! I am the First and the Last. I am the living one. I died, but look—I am alive forever and ever! And I hold the keys of death and the grave.” Revelation 1:17–18, NLT, excerpt

With Jesus, it is never too late for hope.

Questions for Reflection

  1. Where does Jesus seem late in my life right now? What would it look like to keep walking with Him while I wait?
  2. Where am I hearing “there’s no use” right now? What do I hear in Jesus’ words, “Don’t be afraid. Just have faith,” as I face that situation?
  3. Who in my life is grieving or waiting for an answer? How can I stay close to them, as Jesus stayed close to Jairus?

Three Truths to Remember

  1. Jesus goes with us. He heard Jairus’s plea, set out with him at once, and stayed with him on the crowded road. Mark 5:24 · quoted above
  2. Jesus speaks to our fear. When Jairus heard that his daughter had died, Jesus urged him to keep trusting Him. Mark 5:36 · quoted above
  3. Jesus has the last word over death. Jesus raised Jairus’s daughter and promises to raise everyone who believes in Him. John 11:25 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Lord Jesus, thank You for hearing us when we cry out to You. When the answer seems late, help us keep walking with You. When hard news leaves us afraid, help us hear Your words again. “Don’t be afraid. Just have faith.” Be near to everyone who is grieving today. Thank You that You are the resurrection and the life and that death does not have the last word. Teach us to trust You while we wait and to stay beside others who are waiting too. In Your name we pray. Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
