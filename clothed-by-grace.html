Clothed by Grace: Joshua Before the Angel

Zechariah 3:1–10 · 6 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-09-23

Joshua stood accused in filthy garments, but God took away his sins and clothed him anew. Reflect on forgiveness, Christ’s righteousness, and faithful service.

Forgiveness & graceFaith & trustHumility & obedience
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“See, I have taken away your sins, and now I am giving you these fine new clothes.”
Zechariah 3:4 (NLT, excerpt)

Joshua stood where no sinner wants to stand: exposed, accused, and unable to point to spotless garments of his own. But the loudest voice in the vision is not the accuser's.

The exiles had come home from Babylon to a ruined city and an unfinished temple. Joshua — the NLT spells his name Jeshua — was their high priest. Haggai's message came “to Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel, governor of Judah, and to Jeshua son of Jehozadak, the high priest.” Haggai 1:1 (NLT, excerpt) Joshua was the man who stood before God on the people's behalf. Then the prophet Zechariah was shown a vision: “Then the angel showed me Jeshua the high priest standing before the angel of the LORD. The Accuser, Satan, was there at the angel's right hand, making accusations against Jeshua.” Zechariah 3:1 (NLT)

And the charge had substance. “Jeshua's clothing was filthy as he stood there before the angel.” Zechariah 3:3 (NLT) A priest's garments were meant to be clean. His were not, and in the very next verse God Himself connects the filth with sin.

The accuser may name the stain, but he does not decide the verdict.

Before a garment is touched, before Joshua says a word, the LORD answers: “I, the LORD, reject your accusations, Satan. Yes, the LORD, who has chosen Jerusalem, rebukes you. This man is like a burning stick that has been snatched from the fire.” Zechariah 3:2 (NLT, excerpt)

God does not say Joshua's record is spotless. He says something greater: I have chosen these people, and I have pulled this man out of the fire. A stick snatched from the flames is scorched and blackened — and rescued. God does not deny the burn marks. He declares the rescue.

Ellen G. White shows us who stands in that scene for us:

Ellen G. White“In his own strength, man cannot meet the charges of the enemy. In sin-stained garments, confessing his guilt, he stands before God. But Jesus, our Advocate, presents an effectual plea in behalf of all who by repentance and faith have committed the keeping of their souls to Him.” Prophets and Kings, p. 586 · PK 586.2

That is the promise of the gospel: “Who then will condemn us? No one—for Christ Jesus died for us and was raised to life for us, and he is sitting in the place of honor at God's right hand, pleading for us.” Romans 8:34 (NLT) There is a voice that shows us our sin to bring us home, and a voice that shows it to keep us away. The first is God's kindness calling us to repentance. The second is not heaven's verdict.

Then comes the change. “So the angel said to the others standing there, ‘Take off his filthy clothes.’ And turning to Jeshua he said, ‘See, I have taken away your sins, and now I am giving you these fine new clothes.’” Zechariah 3:4 (NLT) Then “they put a clean priestly turban on his head and dressed him in new clothes while the angel of the LORD stood by.” Zechariah 3:5 (NLT, excerpt)

Look at the order: the command, the declaration, the gift. Joshua does not scrub his garments or bargain for new ones. They are taken off him, and clean ones are given to him. Ellen G. White puts it simply: “His own sins and those of his people were pardoned.” Prophets and Kings, p. 584 · PK 584.2

Joshua did not repair his filthy garments; God gave him different ones.

Only then does the charge come: “If you follow my ways and carefully serve me, you will be given authority over my Temple and its courtyards.” Zechariah 3:7 (NLT, excerpt) Cleansing comes first; faithful service follows. Obedience is the response of a restored person, not the price of pardon.

And the vision looks beyond Joshua: “You are symbols of things to come. Soon I am going to bring my servant, the Branch.” Zechariah 3:8 (NLT, excerpt) God promises to “remove the sins of this land in a single day,” Zechariah 3:9 (NLT, excerpt) and the chapter closes with neighbours sitting “peacefully under your own grapevine and fig tree.” Zechariah 3:10 (NLT, excerpt)

That promised Servant has come. Jesus is the High Priest who never needed a change of garments, because He never sinned, and He clothes us in what we could never make: “For God made Christ, who never sinned, to be the offering for our sin, so that we could be made right with God through Christ.” 2 Corinthians 5:21 (NLT) Our hope is not that the accusations are imaginary. Our hope is that Christ's grace is greater than our guilt.

Bring your sin to Christ, receive His mercy, trust His righteousness, and rise to walk faithfully with Him.

Reflect

  1. When my failures confront me, will I hide from God or come to Christ for mercy?
  2. Am I trying to make myself clean, or am I receiving Christ's forgiveness and letting His grace change how I live?
  3. Will I let God's grace have the final word over my guilt?

Key Takeaways

  1. The accusation was real, but it was not final. “Jeshua's clothing was filthy” Zechariah 3:3 (excerpt), yet before anything else happened the LORD said, “I, the LORD, reject your accusations, Satan” Zechariah 3:2 (excerpt). God's voice is the decisive one.
  2. Cleansing is God's work, not ours. “Take off his filthy clothes” comes first, then “See, I have taken away your sins” Zechariah 3:4 (excerpts), and then “they put a clean priestly turban on his head” Zechariah 3:5 (excerpt). In Ellen G. White's words, Christ “places upon the repenting, believing ones His own robe of righteousness” Christ's Object Lessons, pp. 169–170.
  3. Service follows forgiveness. Only after the cleansing does the charge come: “If you follow my ways and carefully serve me…” Zechariah 3:7 (excerpt). We obey because we have been forgiven, not so that we may be.
  4. The vision points to Christ. The priests were “symbols of things to come” Zechariah 3:8, and “we have an advocate who pleads our case before the Father. He is Jesus Christ” 1 John 2:1 (excerpt) — our great High Priest, through whom we “will receive his mercy” Hebrews 4:16.

A prayer for today

Father, thank You that the accuser does not have the last word over Your children. Give me honest repentance and keep me from despair. Thank You, Lord Jesus, for taking away what I could never wash out and for clothing me in Your righteousness. Now give me grace to walk in Your ways and serve You faithfully today. Amen.
