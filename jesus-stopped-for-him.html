Jesus Stopped for Him

Mark 10:46–52 · 9 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-19

The crowd tries to silence Bartimaeus, but Jesus hears his cry and stops for him. Reflect on asking for mercy, answering Christ’s call, and following Him with eyes opened to His grace.

PrayerFaith & trustHope & encouragementForgiveness & grace
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“When Jesus heard him, he stopped and said, ‘Tell him to come here.’ So they called the blind man. ‘Cheer up,’ they said. ‘Come on, he’s calling you!’”
Mark 10:49 (NLT)

Have you ever hesitated to pray because your need seemed too small, you felt it was too late to ask, or you were embarrassed to say it aloud? Bartimaeus’s story is for anyone who has wondered whether Jesus would stop for them.

Jesus was on His final journey to Jerusalem, and He knew what awaited Him there. He told His disciples plainly, “we’re going up to Jerusalem, where the Son of Man will be betrayed to the leading priests and the teachers of religious law.” Mark 10:33, NLT, excerpt

Mark takes us to the road outside Jericho. “Then they reached Jericho, and as Jesus and his disciples left town, a large crowd followed him. A blind beggar named Bartimaeus (son of Timaeus) was sitting beside the road.” Mark 10:46, NLT

Ellen G. White draws our attention to why Bartimaeus was waiting.

“Blind Bartimaeus is waiting by the wayside; he has waited long to meet Christ. Throngs of people who possess their sight are passing to and fro, but they have no desire to see Jesus.”The Review and Herald, March 15, 1887 · RH March 15, 1887, par. 3, excerpt

She goes on to say, “Not so with the poor blind man. His only hope is in Jesus.” The Review and Herald, March 15, 1887 · RH March 15, 1887, par. 3, excerpt

Bartimaeus had waited by that road because he knew how much he needed Jesus.

“When Bartimaeus heard that Jesus of Nazareth was nearby, he began to shout, ‘Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on me!’” Mark 10:47, NLT In asking for mercy, Bartimaeus trusted Jesus to hear him and respond with kindness.

The title “Son of David” points back to a promise God made to King David. “I will raise up one of your descendants, your own offspring, and I will make his kingdom strong.” 2 Samuel 7:12, NLT, excerpt Ellen G. White explains that the name Bartimaeus used “was the title which prophecy had given to the Messiah.” The Desire of Ages, p. 608 · DA 608.3, excerpt Bartimaeus recognised Jesus as the King God had promised.

The crowd heard him too. “‘Be quiet!’ many of the people yelled at him. But he only shouted louder, ‘Son of David, have mercy on me!’” Mark 10:48, NLT

“With the eagerness of intense desire, he cries, ‘Jesus, thou Son of David, have mercy on me!’ They try to silence him, but he cries the more vehemently, ‘Thou Son of David, have mercy on me!’ This appeal is heard. His persevering faith is rewarded.”The Review and Herald, March 15, 1887 · RH March 15, 1887, par. 3, excerpt

Bartimaeus kept calling because he trusted the One who could help him. When other people dismiss our needs, or when we begin to dismiss them ourselves, we can keep bringing them to Jesus.

Matthew’s account of two blind men at Jericho shows what moved Jesus to answer. “Jesus felt sorry for them and touched their eyes. Instantly they could see! Then they followed him.” Matthew 20:34, NLT

Jesus heard the cry the crowd tried to silence.

“When Jesus heard him, he stopped and said, ‘Tell him to come here.’ So they called the blind man. ‘Cheer up,’ they said. ‘Come on, he’s calling you!’” Mark 10:49, NLT At Jesus’ word, people in the crowd were now urging Bartimaeus to come.

“Bartimaeus threw aside his coat, jumped up, and came to Jesus.” Mark 10:50, NLT In a letter urging a man to come to Christ without delay, Ellen G. White describes how Bartimaeus responded. “When Jesus calls him to come, he does not wait a second bidding, he waits not to be led. He rushes in his blindness in the direction of the voice.” Letter 1d, 1890 · Lt 1d, 1890, par. 14, excerpt We can answer Jesus with the same trust when the way ahead is unclear.

On His way to the cross, Jesus stopped for one man by the road.

Then Jesus spoke to Bartimaeus Himself. “‘What do you want me to do for you?’ Jesus asked. ‘My Rabbi,’ the blind man said, ‘I want to see!’” Mark 10:51, NLT Bartimaeus addressed Jesus as his teacher and told Him plainly what he wanted.

Earlier on the journey, two of Jesus’ disciples, James and John, had come to Him with a request of their own. He asked them, “What is your request?” Mark 10:36, NLT, excerpt They answered, “we want to sit in places of honor next to you” Mark 10:37, NLT, excerpt Bartimaeus had come asking for mercy and sight. Jesus used James and John’s request to teach His disciples what kind of King He was. “For even the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve others and to give his life as a ransom for many.” Mark 10:45, NLT His own life would be the price of our freedom.

Ellen G. White hears in Jesus’ question an invitation for each of us.

“Christ is waiting and longing to speak pardon, and to impart the freely offered grace. He is watching and waiting, saying, as he said to the blind man at the gate of Jericho, What wilt thou that I should do unto thee? I will take away your sins, and wash you in my blood.”The Review and Herald, April 12, 1906 · RH April 12, 1906, par. 11, excerpt

We can tell Jesus plainly where we need His forgiveness and help. “So let us come boldly to the throne of our gracious God. There we will receive his mercy, and we will find grace to help us when we need it most.” Hebrews 4:16, NLT

Jesus knows what we need and still invites us to tell Him.

Jesus answered at once. “And Jesus said to him, ‘Go, for your faith has healed you.’ Instantly the man could see, and he followed Jesus down the road.” Mark 10:52, NLT

Ellen G. White describes a healing that reached beyond Bartimaeus’s eyes.

“Not only is physical sight restored, but the eyes of his understanding are opened. In Christ he sees his Redeemer, and the Sun of righteousness shines into his soul.”The Review and Herald, March 15, 1887 · RH March 15, 1887, par. 3, excerpt

Bartimaeus saw Jesus as the One who had come to set him free. Paul describes how God gives us light to recognise His glory in Christ. “For God, who said, ‘Let there be light in the darkness,’ has made this light shine in our hearts so we could know the glory of God that is seen in the face of Jesus Christ.” 2 Corinthians 4:6, NLT Ellen G. White reminds us how much we depend on the Spirit for that light. “Many need the Spirit of Christ and His power to enlighten their understanding, as much as blind Bartimaeus needed his natural sight.” Testimonies for the Church, vol. 4, p. 355 · 4T 355.1, excerpt

Many faithful believers pray for healing and still live with illness or pain. Whatever answer we receive, Jesus can open our eyes to see Him as our Saviour.

At the end of Mark’s account, the man who had been sitting by the road “followed Jesus down the road.” Mark 10:52, NLT, excerpt That road led to Jerusalem, where Jesus would give His life for us.

The first thing Bartimaeus did with his new sight was follow Jesus.

Ellen G. White invites us to look for Christ’s presence in our own streets and homes.

“Today no curious multitudes flock to the desert places to see and hear the Christ. His voice is not heard in the busy streets. No cry sounds from the wayside, ‘Jesus of Nazareth passeth by.’ Luke 18:37. Yet this word is true today. Christ walks unseen through our streets. With messages of mercy He comes to our homes. With all who are seeking to minister in His name, He waits to co-operate. He is in the midst of us, to heal and to bless, if we will receive Him.”The Ministry of Healing, p. 107 · MH 107.2

Perhaps you feel like Bartimaeus, sitting by the road while life passes you by. You can call on Jesus right where you are. If someone comes to mind who needs encouragement, pass on the good news the crowd brought to Bartimaeus. Jesus is calling them, too.

Ellen G. White closes her account of Bartimaeus with an invitation. “All who feel their need of Christ as did blind Bartimaeus, and who will be as earnest and determined as he was, will, like him, receive the blessing which they crave.” The Review and Herald, March 15, 1887 · RH March 15, 1887, par. 3, excerpt

The same Jesus who stopped for Bartimaeus hears you when you call.

Reflect

  1. What voices, including my own, make me hesitate to ask Jesus for help?
  2. If Jesus asked what I wanted Him to do for me today, how would I answer?
  3. Who could I encourage this week with the good news that Jesus is calling them, too?

Key Takeaways

  1. Bring your need to Jesus. Bartimaeus kept asking for mercy even when the people around him told him to be quiet. Mark 10:47–48 · quoted above
  2. Jesus stops for one person. On His way to the cross, He stopped and asked Bartimaeus what he needed. Mark 10:49–51 · quoted above
  3. Follow where Jesus leads. When his eyes were opened, Bartimaeus went with Jesus down the road. Mark 10:52 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Lord Jesus, Son of David, have mercy on us. Thank You for stopping for those the world passes by and for hearing us when we are afraid to speak. Open our eyes to see You as our Saviour. Forgive our sins and give us light when we cannot see the way ahead. Help us trust You when Your answer is different from what we hoped for. When You call, help us come without delay and follow wherever You lead. Give us words of encouragement for those still waiting by the road. In Your name we pray. Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
