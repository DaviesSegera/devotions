A Name That Cannot Be Borrowed

Acts 19:13–20 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-25

The sons of Sceva try to use Jesus’ name without knowing Him. Reflect on personal faith, reverence for Christ, and the Ephesian believers who let go of their former practices in response to His grace.

Faith & trustHumility & obedienceWisdom & discernmentForgiveness & grace

Key Verse
“I know Jesus, and I know Paul, but who are you?”
Acts 19:15, NLT, excerpt

We can speak about Jesus often and yet know Him only from a distance. We may sing familiar songs and speak His name with ease, while quietly relying on the faith of a parent, a pastor, or a friend. The unusual events in Ephesus invite us to consider a searching question: Is our faith truly our own?

Paul taught in Ephesus day after day. Luke tells us that his teaching “went on for the next two years, so that people throughout the province of Asia—both Jews and Greeks—heard the word of the Lord.” Acts 19:10, NLT, excerpt Alongside this teaching, God was working in remarkable ways: “God gave Paul the power to perform unusual miracles. When handkerchiefs or aprons that had merely touched his skin were placed on sick people, they were healed of their diseases, and evil spirits were expelled.” Acts 19:11–12, NLT

Magic and idol worship were deeply rooted in Ephesus. Ellen G. White explains what these miracles revealed to the people: “As these miracles were wrought in the name of Jesus of Nazareth, the people had opportunity to see that the God of heaven was more powerful than the magicians who were worshipers of the goddess Diana.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 286 · AA 286.4, excerpt

The power at work in Ephesus belonged to Jesus, not to a method or a form of words.

Some who watched Paul at work misunderstood what they saw: “A group of Jews was traveling from town to town casting out evil spirits. They tried to use the name of the Lord Jesus in their incantation, saying, ‘I command you in the name of Jesus, whom Paul preaches, to come out!’ Seven sons of Sceva, a leading priest, were doing this.” Acts 19:13–14, NLT Listen to their words: “Jesus, whom Paul preaches.” They spoke of Jesus as Paul’s Lord, without knowing Him as their own.

In her earlier account of Paul’s life, Ellen White explains their mistake:

“Believing that the name of Jesus acted as a charm, they determined to cast out evil spirits by the same means which the apostle had employed.”Sketches from the Life of Paul, p. 136 · LP 136.1, excerpt

A charm is an object or a phrase thought to hold power of its own. The name of Jesus points us to Jesus Himself. When a man who had been lame from birth was healed, Peter explained the source of that healing: “Through faith in the name of Jesus, this man was healed—and you know how crippled he was before. Faith in Jesus’ name has healed him before your very eyes.” Acts 3:16, NLT

Jesus’ name is not a formula to repeat. It is the name of a living Lord to trust.

Then came the question at the heart of this account: “But one time when they tried it, the evil spirit replied, ‘I know Jesus, and I know Paul, but who are you?’ Then the man with the evil spirit leaped on them, overpowered them, and attacked them with such violence that they fled from the house, naked and battered.” Acts 19:15–16, NLT

We do not look to an evil spirit for spiritual guidance. Even so, its reply exposed the men’s mistake. They knew the name of Jesus, but they did not know the Lord Himself.

Jesus Himself warned that people could speak and act in His name without belonging to Him: “On judgment day many will say to me, ‘Lord! Lord! We prophesied in your name and cast out demons in your name and performed many miracles in your name.’ But I will reply, ‘I never knew you. Get away from me, you who break God’s laws.’” Matthew 7:22–23, NLT What matters most is whether He knows us as His own. Listen to the tenderness in His words: “I am the good shepherd; I know my own sheep, and they know me,” John 10:14, NLT

Faith cannot be borrowed. Each of us is invited to know Jesus personally and to be known by Him.

Their failure did not weaken the message about Jesus. It stirred a deep reverence for His name: “The story of what happened spread quickly all through Ephesus, to Jews and Greeks alike. A solemn fear descended on the city, and the name of the Lord Jesus was greatly honored.” Acts 19:17, NLT Ellen White explains why His name was held in such reverence:

“Thus unmistakable proof was given of the sacredness of the name of Christ, and the peril which they incurred who should invoke it without faith in the divinity of the Saviour’s mission.”The Acts of the Apostles, p. 288 · AA 288.1, excerpt

By “the divinity of the Saviour’s mission,” she means that Jesus came from God to save us. The people of Ephesus learned that His name is holy. We cannot borrow His name for its power while withholding our trust from Him. Paul later wrote that God “gave him the name above all other names.” Philippians 2:9, NLT, excerpt

We honour the name of Jesus when we come to Him in faith, not when we use His name for our own purposes.

The account then turns to those who had come to believe: “Many who became believers confessed their sinful practices. A number of them who had been practicing sorcery brought their incantation books and burned them at a public bonfire. The value of the books was several million dollars.” Acts 19:18–19, NLT The original Greek gives the value as fifty thousand pieces of silver, each worth about a day’s wage.

Some believers had continued in their old practices; now they openly confessed them. Ellen White describes their response as evidence of a changed heart:

“By burning their books on magic, the Ephesian converts showed that the things in which they had once delighted they now abhorred. It was by and through magic that they had especially offended God and imperiled their souls; and it was against magic that they showed such indignation. Thus they gave evidence of true conversion.”The Acts of the Apostles, p. 288 · AA 288.3

She also notes the sacrifice they were willing to make: “They had renounced the kingdom of darkness, and to destroy its power they did not hesitate at any sacrifice.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 288 · AA 288.4, excerpt We may not own books of magic, yet we may still cling to habits or hidden practices that draw our hearts away from Christ. To follow Him for ourselves is to bring these things into His light and let them go.

Our courage to let go comes from confidence in what Jesus has already done, rather than from fear of evil: “In this way, he disarmed the spiritual rulers and authorities. He shamed them publicly by his victory over them on the cross.” Colossians 2:15, NLT

Luke brings the account to a close with these words: “So the message about the Lord spread widely and had a powerful effect.” Acts 19:20, NLT

Take a quiet moment today to ask yourself honestly: Do I know Jesus for myself? You do not need to find impressive words before you come to Him. Come as you are, bringing even the things you have kept hidden. When the disciples rejoiced that evil spirits obeyed them, Jesus directed their hearts towards a deeper joy: “But don’t rejoice because evil spirits obey you; rejoice because your names are registered in heaven.” Luke 10:20, NLT

Know Jesus for yourself. Let go of what competes with Him. Rejoice that He knows your name.

Reflect

  1. Am I learning to trust Jesus for myself, or do I depend mainly on the faith of someone close to me?
  2. When I speak Jesus’ name in prayer or worship, am I trusting Him or simply repeating familiar words?
  3. Is there something I am holding on to that draws my heart away from Christ? What step could I take this week to bring it to Him and let it go?

Key Takeaways

  1. Jesus’ name is not a formula. The power of His name rests in who He is. We come to Him in faith, trusting Him for the help we need. Acts 3:16 · quoted above
  2. Faith must be our own. Knowing about Jesus is not the same as knowing Him. What matters most is that we belong to Him and are known by Him. Matthew 7:22–23 · quoted above
  3. True faith lets go of what competes with Christ. The believers in Ephesus confessed their practices and burned their costly books. We too can let go in trust, because Christ has already won the victory. Acts 19:18–19 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Lord Jesus, thank You that Your name is above every name and that You came to save us. Forgive us for the times we have spoken Your name without truly trusting You. Forgive us, too, for relying on someone else’s faith when You invite us to come to You ourselves. We long to know You and to be known by You. Show us whatever we are holding on to that draws our hearts away from You. Give us the courage to bring it into Your light and let it go. Thank You that, through the cross, You have won the victory over every power of darkness. Teach us to honour Your name in all we do, and help us rejoice that our names are written in heaven. In Your name we pray. Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
