The Call of Abram: Following God by Faith

Genesis 12:1–9 · 6 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-09-26

God spoke first, and Abram went before he could see the whole road. Reflect on trusting God’s promise, worship along the way, and the blessing that reaches others through Christ.

Faith & trustHumility & obedienceLove & service

Key Verse
“So Abram departed as the LORD had instructed”
Genesis 12:4 (NLT, excerpt)
“All the families on earth will be blessed through you.”
Genesis 12:3 (NLT, excerpt)

A journey can begin before every question is answered. Abram’s story begins with something stronger than a complete plan: God’s call and God’s promise.

Abram’s family had left Ur and settled in the town of Haran. Then God spoke: “The LORD had said to Abram, ‘Leave your native country, your relatives, and your father’s family, and go to the land that I will show you.’” Genesis 12:1 (NLT) With the call came promises: “I will make you into a great nation. I will bless you and make you famous, and you will be a blessing to others.” Genesis 12:2 (NLT) Abram is the same man God later named Abraham.

The story begins not with Abram knowing the road, but with God making a promise.

Then comes a simple sentence: “So Abram departed as the LORD had instructed, and Lot went with him. Abram was seventy-five years old when he left Haran.” Genesis 12:4 (NLT) He took his wife Sarai, his nephew Lot, his flocks and “all the people he had taken into his household at Haran.” Genesis 12:5 (NLT, excerpt)

Hebrews tells us what moved him: “It was by faith that Abraham obeyed when God called him to leave home and go to another land that God would give him as his inheritance. He went without knowing where he was going.” Hebrews 11:8 (NLT)

Ellen G. White helps us feel the cost:

Ellen G. White“It was no light test that was thus brought upon Abraham, no small sacrifice that was required of him. There were strong ties to bind him to his country, his kindred, and his home. But he did not hesitate to obey the call.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 126 · PP 126.3

Faith is not guessing, and it is not following a strong feeling. Faith is trust in God that answers what He has said.

Faith does not require the whole journey to be visible before the next faithful step is taken.

When Abram reached Canaan, the land was already lived in: “At that time, the area was inhabited by Canaanites.” Genesis 12:6 (NLT, excerpt) The promise was still far from finished. Yet Abram worshipped. “Then the LORD appeared to Abram and said, ‘I will give this land to your descendants.’ And Abram built an altar there and dedicated it to the LORD, who had appeared to him.” Genesis 12:7 (NLT) At the next camp, “he built another altar and dedicated it to the LORD, and he worshiped the LORD.” Genesis 12:8 (NLT, excerpt)

Ellen G. White tells us those altars kept speaking to others: “When his tent was removed, the altar remained.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 128 · PP 128.1 She adds that Canaanites who came that way in later years repaired the altar and worshipped the living God there.

Abram did not wait for the whole promise to unfold before he worshipped the One who gave it.

The promise was never only for Abram. God said, “All the families on earth will be blessed through you.” Genesis 12:3 (NLT, excerpt) Paul shows how: “So all who put their faith in Christ share the same blessing Abraham received because of his faith.” Galatians 3:9 (NLT) That blessing cost Jesus the cross: “But Christ has rescued us from the curse pronounced by the law. When he was hung on the cross, he took upon himself the curse for our wrongdoing.” Galatians 3:13 (NLT, excerpt) And it reaches far: “Through Christ Jesus, God has blessed the Gentiles with the same blessing he promised to Abraham, so that we who are believers might receive the promised Holy Spirit through faith.” Galatians 3:14 (NLT)

So the best blessing is not money or comfort. It is forgiveness, a place in God’s family, and His Spirit in us. We receive it as a gift: “God saved you by his grace when you believed. And you can’t take credit for this; it is a gift from God.” Ephesians 2:8 (NLT)

Then we pass it on. Most of us are not asked to move to a new land. We are asked to obey what God has already made clear, and to share what He has given: time, a skill, a meal, a phone call, a prayer, a kind word about Jesus. A child can share. An older member can pray. Someone who needs help right now is loved just as much.

Trust Jesus, receive His grace, obey what God has made clear, and let His love reach others through your life.

Reflect

  1. What faithful step is God calling me to take, even though I cannot yet see the whole journey?
  2. Who could experience God’s love through the blessings He has already entrusted to me?
  3. Will I trust God with the next step and let His gifts become a blessing to others?

Key Takeaways

  1. God spoke first. The call and the promises came before Abram took a step: “go to the land that I will show you” Genesis 12:1 (excerpt).
  2. Faith answered God’s word. “It was by faith that Abraham obeyed when God called him” Hebrews 11:8 (excerpt). Faith trusts what God has said, even when the road is not yet clear.
  3. Worship came before the promise was complete. “There he built another altar and dedicated it to the LORD, and he worshiped the LORD” Genesis 12:8 (excerpt). Ellen G. White writes of him, “His was a life of prayer” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 128 · PP 128.1.
  4. The blessing reaches others through Christ. God called Abraham “for the purpose of bringing the best gifts of Heaven to all the peoples of earth” Prophets and Kings, p. 15 · PK 15.1, and “all who put their faith in Christ share the same blessing Abraham received” Galatians 3:9 (excerpt).

A prayer for today

Father, thank You for speaking first and for keeping Your promises. When I cannot see the road, help me trust You and obey what I already know. Thank You, Lord Jesus, for the blessing You won for me on the cross. Keep me close to You, and let Your love reach others through my life. Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
