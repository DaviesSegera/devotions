Mercy Reached Him in Chains

2 Chronicles 33:1–20 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-27

In captivity, King Manasseh humbles himself and God hears his prayer. Reflect on the reach of God’s mercy and how repentance changes what we do when we return to Him.

Forgiveness & gracePrayerHumility & obedienceHope & encouragement

Key Verse
“But while in deep distress, Manasseh sought the LORD his God and sincerely humbled himself before the God of his ancestors. And when he prayed, the LORD listened to him and was moved by his request.”
2 Chronicles 33:12–13 (NLT, excerpt)

Is anyone too far gone for God to reach? Many of us have quietly asked that question about someone we love or about ourselves. The story of King Manasseh gives a surprising answer.

Manasseh began with every advantage. His father was Hezekiah, a king who trusted God and saw Him answer prayer. Yet Manasseh chose a different path. “He did what was evil in the LORD’s sight, following the detestable practices of the pagan nations that the LORD had driven from the land ahead of the Israelites.” 2 Chronicles 33:2, NLT He undid his father’s work: “He rebuilt the pagan shrines his father, Hezekiah, had broken down.” 2 Chronicles 33:3, NLT, excerpt

The record of his reign is hard to read. “Manasseh also sacrificed his own sons in the fire in the valley of Ben-Hinnom. He practiced sorcery, divination, and witchcraft, and he consulted with mediums and psychics. He did much that was evil in the LORD’s sight, arousing his anger.” 2 Chronicles 33:6, NLT The writer sums it up: “But Manasseh led the people of Judah and Jerusalem to do even more evil than the pagan nations that the LORD had destroyed when the people of Israel entered the land.” 2 Chronicles 33:9, NLT

Ellen G. White describes what those years did to the nation: “Gross evils sprang up and flourished—tyranny, oppression, hatred of all that is good. Justice was perverted; violence prevailed.” Prophets and Kings, p. 381 · PK 381.1, excerpt It would be easy to conclude that Manasseh was beyond hope. Yet God was already reaching out to him.

“The LORD spoke to Manasseh and his people, but they ignored all his warnings.” 2 Chronicles 33:10, NLT God spoke before He disciplined. Ellen White tells us that the warnings kept coming even when they were rejected: “Faithfully the prophets continued their warnings and their exhortations; fearlessly they spoke to Manasseh and to his people; but the messages were scorned; backsliding Judah would not heed.” Prophets and Kings, p. 382 · PK 382.3, excerpt God did not give up on the king who scorned His messengers. Writing about Jesus’ parable of the lost sheep, Ellen White says, “God has made the first advance. While you were in rebellion against Him, He went forth to seek you.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 188 · COL 188.3, excerpt

Long before Manasseh turned toward God, God was speaking to Manasseh.

When Manasseh refused to listen, God disciplined him. “So the LORD sent the commanders of the Assyrian armies, and they took Manasseh prisoner. They put a ring through his nose, bound him in bronze chains, and led him away to Babylon.” 2 Chronicles 33:11, NLT The king who had led a nation into darkness was now a prisoner in a foreign land. Ellen White describes how the suffering changed him: “This affliction brought the king to his senses” Prophets and Kings, p. 382 · PK 382.3, excerpt

Then comes one of the most hopeful turns in the Old Testament. “But while in deep distress, Manasseh sought the LORD his God and sincerely humbled himself before the God of his ancestors.” 2 Chronicles 33:12, NLT “And when he prayed, the LORD listened to him and was moved by his request. So the LORD brought Manasseh back to Jerusalem and to his kingdom. Then Manasseh finally realized that the LORD alone is God!” 2 Chronicles 33:13, NLT

Manasseh sought the LORD, humbled himself, and prayed. Long before, when Solomon dedicated the Temple, God had made this promise: “Then if my people who are called by my name will humble themselves and pray and seek my face and turn from their wicked ways, I will hear from heaven and will forgive their sins and restore their land.” 2 Chronicles 7:14, NLT Even after years of ignoring God, Manasseh could still turn to Him and trust that promise.

The Bible does not give us the words of his prayer. It tells us something more important: the God whose Temple Manasseh had filled with idols was moved by his request. Reflecting on Jesus’ parable of the lost son, Ellen White describes how God responds to even our most hesitant prayers:

“Never a prayer is offered, however faltering, never a tear is shed, however secret, never a sincere desire after God is cherished, however feeble, but the Spirit of God goes forth to meet it.”Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 206 · COL 206.1, excerpt

Manasseh prayed as a prisoner with nothing to offer, and God was already waiting to hear him.

Chains could not keep Manasseh’s prayer from reaching God.

Was this only the cry of a frightened man? In a crisis, we may turn to God in prayer and then forget Him when the danger passes. Ellen White explains what real repentance involves:

“Repentance includes sorrow for sin and a turning away from it. We shall not renounce sin unless we see its sinfulness; until we turn away from it in heart, there will be no real change in the life.”Steps to Christ, p. 23 · SC 23.2

Back in Jerusalem, Manasseh began to undo the damage he had caused. His actions showed that his repentance was sincere. “Manasseh also removed the foreign gods and the idol from the LORD’s Temple. He tore down all the altars he had built on the hill where the Temple stood and all the altars that were in Jerusalem, and he dumped them outside the city.” 2 Chronicles 33:15, NLT “Then he restored the altar of the LORD and sacrificed peace offerings and thanksgiving offerings on it. He also encouraged the people of Judah to worship the LORD, the God of Israel.” 2 Chronicles 33:16, NLT The king who had led Judah into idolatry now called the nation back to the LORD.

Turning from sin is the heart of repentance. Proverbs puts it simply: “People who conceal their sins will not prosper, but if they confess and turn from them, they will receive mercy.” Proverbs 28:13, NLT Our idols may not be carved images. They may be habits, grudges, or attachments that have taken God’s place in our hearts. As we repent, we bring them into the open and ask God for the strength to leave them behind.

Real repentance shows in what we tear down and in what we rebuild.

Judah still felt the effects of Manasseh’s example. “However, the people still sacrificed at the pagan shrines, though only to the LORD their God.” 2 Chronicles 33:17, NLT Habits formed over many years did not disappear overnight. Ellen White describes the lasting harm:

“But this repentance, remarkable though it was, came too late to save the kingdom from the corrupting influence of years of idolatrous practices. Many had stumbled and fallen, never again to rise.”Prophets and Kings, p. 382 · PK 382.3, excerpt

The saddest example was Manasseh’s own son Amon, who succeeded him as king. “He did what was evil in the LORD’s sight, just as his father, Manasseh, had done.” 2 Chronicles 33:22, NLT, excerpt “But unlike his father, he did not humble himself before the LORD. Instead, Amon sinned even more.” 2 Chronicles 33:23, NLT Amon followed his father’s sins but not his father’s repentance.

God’s forgiveness is full and real, yet it does not always undo the harm our choices have done to others. That is a sobering reason to turn to God today.

Mercy was not too late for Manasseh, but his repentance came too late to undo all the harm.

If God listened to Manasseh, we can trust Him to hear our honest prayers too. Centuries later, Paul, a former persecutor of Jesus’ followers, wrote, “This is a trustworthy saying, and everyone should accept it: ‘Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners’—and I am the worst of them all.” 1 Timothy 1:15, NLT

Jesus told a story about a son who wasted everything and came home with nothing to offer. “So he returned home to his father. And while he was still a long way off, his father saw him coming. Filled with love and compassion, he ran to his son, embraced him, and kissed him.” Luke 15:20, NLT Ellen White writes of that father, “Love is of quick sight. Not even the degradation of the years of sin can conceal the son from the father’s eyes.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 203 · COL 203.1, excerpt

There is a quiet detail in the opening verses of the New Testament. In Jesus’ family line we read, “Hezekiah was the father of Manasseh. Manasseh was the father of Amon. Amon was the father of Josiah.” Matthew 1:10, NLT There, among the Saviour’s ancestors, is the king who led Judah into such evil. Jesus came for people like Manasseh and for people like us. “Therefore he is able, once and forever, to save those who come to God through him. He lives forever to intercede with God on their behalf.” Hebrews 7:25, NLT

Perhaps you feel that you have wandered too far or that your past is too dark. Ellen White speaks to that fear: “As you see the enormity of sin, as you see yourself as you really are, do not give up to despair. It was sinners that Christ came to save.” Steps to Christ, p. 35 · SC 35.3, excerpt You do not need chains to bring you to God. You can come to Him today, just as you are.

The God who heard Manasseh in chains still hears every honest prayer for mercy.

Reflect

  1. Where in my life might I be ignoring what God is trying to tell me?
  2. What would real repentance lead me to tear down, and what would it lead me to rebuild?
  3. Who have I quietly assumed is beyond God’s reach, and how can I pray for that person this week?

Key Takeaways

  1. God speaks before He disciplines. The LORD warned Manasseh long before the chains came, and He still speaks to us patiently. 2 Chronicles 33:10 · quoted above
  2. No one is beyond the reach of God’s mercy. After years of evil, Manasseh humbled himself and prayed. God listened and was moved by his request. 2 Chronicles 33:12–13 · quoted above
  3. Real repentance turns the whole life around. Manasseh removed his idols and restored the worship of the LORD, and Jesus still welcomes everyone who comes home. 2 Chronicles 33:15–16 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Merciful Father, thank You that no one is beyond the reach of Your love. Like Manasseh, we have often ignored Your voice and gone our own way. Today we humble ourselves before You. Forgive our sins and give us the courage to tear down whatever has taken Your place in our hearts and to rebuild what we have neglected. Give us the honesty to face the harm we have caused and the humility to make things right where we can. Please keep speaking to those we love who seem far from You. Draw them home. Thank You for Jesus, who came to save sinners and lives to pray for us. In His name, Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
