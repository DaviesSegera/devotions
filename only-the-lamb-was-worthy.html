Only the Lamb Was Worthy

Revelation 5:1–10 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-20

John weeps when no one can open the scroll, until he sees the Lamb who gave His life and now stands alive. Reflect on Jesus’ victory, His saving love for every nation, and the hope He gives in our tears.

Key Verse
“You are worthy to take the scroll and break its seals and open it. For you were slaughtered, and your blood has ransomed people for God from every tribe and language and people and nation.”
Revelation 5:9 (NLT, excerpt)

Have you ever faced a problem that no one could solve? In Revelation 5, John weeps because no one can open the scroll in God’s hand. Then an elder tells him to look, and John sees Jesus in an unexpected way.

John wrote from exile. “I, John, am your brother and your partner in suffering and in God’s Kingdom and in the patient endurance to which Jesus calls us. I was exiled to the island of Patmos for preaching the word of God and for my testimony about Jesus.” Revelation 1:9, NLT Ellen G. White describes the crisis the church was facing when John was banished. “It was at this critical time in the history of the church that John was sentenced to banishment.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 581 · AA 581.1, excerpt Yet she adds, “But the Lord’s hand was moving unseen in the darkness.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 581 · AA 581.2, excerpt

John was far from home, but God opened heaven to him.

John’s attention turned to a scroll in God’s hand. “Then I saw a scroll in the right hand of the one who was sitting on the throne. There was writing on the inside and the outside of the scroll, and it was sealed with seven seals.” Revelation 5:1, NLT “And I saw a strong angel, who shouted with a loud voice: ‘Who is worthy to break the seals on this scroll and open it?’” Revelation 5:2, NLT

No answer came. “But no one in heaven or on earth or under the earth was able to open the scroll and read it.” Revelation 5:3, NLT

Christians understand the contents of this scroll in different ways. Ellen G. White describes it as God’s record of history:

“There in His open hand lay the book, the roll of the history of God’s providences, the prophetic history of nations and the church.”Letter 65, 1898 · Lt 65, 1898, par. 17, excerpt

By “providences” she means God’s care and guidance at work in history.

“Then I began to weep bitterly because no one was found worthy to open the scroll and read it.” Revelation 5:4, NLT Ellen G. White writes, “John was distressed at the utter inability of any human being or angelic intelligence to read the words, or even to look thereon.” Letter 65, 1898 · Lt 65, 1898, par. 18, excerpt

John’s tears were not a lack of faith. He saw that no one in all creation could do what was needed. We may feel something similar when we carry guilt over what we cannot undo or grief that no one can take away.

John wept because the need was real and no creature could meet it.

John was not left in tears. “But one of the twenty-four elders said to me, ‘Stop weeping! Look, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the heir to David’s throne, has won the victory. He is worthy to open the scroll and its seven seals.’” Revelation 5:5, NLT

The title “Lion of the tribe of Judah” recalls Jacob’s blessing for his son Judah: “Judah, my son, is a young lion” Genesis 49:9, NLT, excerpt Jacob also spoke of a coming ruler: “until the coming of the one to whom it belongs, the one whom all nations will honor.” Genesis 49:10, NLT, excerpt The NLT footnote gives the traditional rendering “until Shiloh comes.” Ellen G. White sees in this blessing a promise of “the Son of David, Shiloh, the true ‘Lion of the tribe of Judah,’ to whom all powers shall finally bow and all nations render homage.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 236 · PP 236.3, excerpt

The elder told John to look for a Lion. When John looked, he saw a Lamb. “Then I saw a Lamb that looked as if it had been slaughtered, but it was now standing between the throne and the four living beings and among the twenty-four elders. He had seven horns and seven eyes, which represent the sevenfold Spirit of God that is sent out into every part of the earth.” Revelation 5:6, NLT

The verse explains the symbolism of the horns and eyes. The Lion who won the victory is the very Lamb who was slaughtered. Isaiah had described God’s Servant this way: “He was led like a lamb to the slaughter.” Isaiah 53:7, NLT, excerpt And when John the Baptist saw Jesus, he said, “Look! The Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!” John 1:29, NLT, excerpt

Ellen G. White explains what the Lion and the Lamb reveal together.

“The Saviour is presented before John under the symbols of ‘the Lion of the tribe of Judah’ and of ‘a Lamb as it had been slain.’ Revelation 5:5, 6. These symbols represent the union of omnipotent power and self-sacrificing love.”The Acts of the Apostles, p. 589 · AA 589.2, excerpt

Jesus did not win by overpowering His enemies. He won by giving His life.

Heaven announced a Lion, and John saw a Lamb.

Notice the words “as if it had been slaughtered, but it was now standing.” The Lamb bears the marks of death, yet He is alive. On Patmos, John had heard this from Jesus Himself. “When I saw him, I fell at his feet as if I were dead. But he laid his right hand on me and said, ‘Don’t be afraid!’” Revelation 1:17, NLT, excerpt “I died, but look—I am alive forever and ever!” Revelation 1:18, NLT, excerpt

Ellen G. White describes the risen Jesus presenting His wounds to His Father after the ascension. “He points to His wounded head, the pierced side, the marred feet; He lifts His hands, bearing the print of nails.” The Desire of Ages, p. 834 · DA 834.2, excerpt

The wounds are not forgotten in heaven. Ellen G. White says of the redeemed, “The cross of Christ will be the science and the song of the redeemed through all eternity. In Christ glorified they will behold Christ crucified.” The Great Controversy, p. 651 · GC 651.2, excerpt By “science” she means a subject the redeemed will study forever. The Lamb on the throne will always be the One who died for us.

Jesus’ victory shines through the cross.

“He stepped forward and took the scroll from the right hand of the one sitting on the throne.” Revelation 5:7, NLT “And when he took the scroll, the four living beings and the twenty-four elders fell down before the Lamb. Each one had a harp, and they held gold bowls filled with incense, which are the prayers of God’s people.” Revelation 5:8, NLT

The verse leaves open how each prayer will be answered. It gives us a picture of the prayers of God’s people held before the throne where the Lamb is worshipped.

Then the worshippers sang a new song: “You are worthy to take the scroll and break its seals and open it. For you were slaughtered, and your blood has ransomed people for God from every tribe and language and people and nation.” Revelation 5:9, NLT, excerpt “And you have caused them to become a Kingdom of priests for our God. And they will reign on the earth.” Revelation 5:10, NLT

The Lamb is worthy because He was slaughtered for us. A ransom is a price paid to set someone free. Peter tells us what our freedom cost. “God paid a ransom to save you from the empty life you inherited from your ancestors. And it was not paid with mere gold or silver, which lose their value.” 1 Peter 1:18, NLT, excerpt “It was the precious blood of Christ, the sinless, spotless Lamb of God.” 1 Peter 1:19, NLT

The song includes people from “every tribe and language and people and nation.” No one is too far away. Those He ransoms become a kingdom of priests, people who belong to God and serve Him. Peter writes to believers, “You are royal priests, a holy nation, God’s very own possession.” 1 Peter 2:9, NLT, excerpt

Ellen G. White describes what these visions taught John about God. “He saw sinners finding a Father in Him of whom their sins had made them afraid.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 589 · AA 589.1, excerpt

The Lamb is worthy, and through Him unworthy people are welcomed home.

The scene began with John’s tears and ended with all of heaven singing. “Then I looked again, and I heard the voices of thousands and millions of angels around the throne and of the living beings and the elders.” Revelation 5:11, NLT “And they sang in a mighty chorus: ‘Worthy is the Lamb who was slaughtered—to receive power and riches and wisdom and strength and honor and glory and blessing.’” Revelation 5:12, NLT Later, John was shown this promise: “For the Lamb on the throne will be their Shepherd. He will lead them to springs of life-giving water. And God will wipe every tear from their eyes.” Revelation 7:17, NLT

You may be carrying a burden no one can lift for you. It may be guilt, a loss that still hurts, or fear of what lies ahead. The elder did not tell John his tears were foolish. He told him where to look.

We are not worthy to save ourselves, and we do not need to be. The Lamb who was slaughtered is worthy, and He is alive. His wounds tell us how much we are loved.

When no one is worthy, look to the Lamb.

Reflect

  1. What burden am I carrying that no one can lift for me? What question am I facing that no one can answer?
  2. What does it mean to me that the Lamb on the throne still bears the marks of the cross?
  3. Whom could I encourage this week with the good news that Jesus’ blood has ransomed people from every nation?

Key Takeaways

  1. Honest tears are not unbelief. John wept when no one was worthy, and heaven answered by pointing him to Jesus. Revelation 5:3–4 · quoted above
  2. The Lion is the Lamb. Jesus won the victory by giving His life, and He stands alive, still bearing the marks of His sacrifice. Revelation 5:5–6 · quoted above
  3. The worthy Lamb welcomes us home. His blood has ransomed people from every nation to belong to God and serve Him. Revelation 5:9–10 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Heavenly Father, thank You that when no one in heaven or on earth was worthy, You gave us the Lamb. Lord Jesus, thank You for giving Your life for us. We praise You because You live forever. When we weep over what we cannot change, turn our eyes to You. Forgive our sins and help us rest in Your worthiness. Thank You that our prayers reach Your throne. Teach us to live as people who belong to You and to share Your love with people of every background. We look forward to the day when You will wipe every tear from our eyes. Until then, help us join heaven’s song. Worthy is the Lamb. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
