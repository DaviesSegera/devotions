Grace Is Not for Sale: Gehazi, Naaman, and the Silver He Hid

2 Kings 5:1–27 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-11

After Naaman’s healing, Gehazi pursues silver and hides his deceit. Reflect on contentment, honest service, and the grace Jesus freely gives when we bring Him what we have been hiding.

Humility & obedienceForgiveness & graceWisdom & discernment
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“Is this the time to receive money and clothing, olive groves and vineyards, sheep and cattle, and male and female servants?”
2 Kings 5:26 (NLT, excerpt)

Can we spend our days serving God and still drift away from Him? Gehazi served the prophet Elisha and saw God do remarkable things. Yet when Elisha turned down Naaman’s gifts, Gehazi was already thinking about what he might get.

When a boy in Shunem died, Gehazi went ahead with Elisha’s staff. When he came back, he told Elisha, “The child is still dead.” 2 Kings 4:31, NLT, excerpt Then Elisha prayed, and God restored the boy to life. Elisha called to Gehazi, “Call the child’s mother!” 2 Kings 4:36, NLT, excerpt

Later, Naaman, the commander of Aram’s army, came to Elisha seeking healing from leprosy. Elisha sent word that he should wash seven times in the Jordan River. Naaman was angry at first, but he went. “So Naaman went down to the Jordan River and dipped himself seven times, as the man of God had instructed him. And his skin became as healthy as the skin of a young child, and he was healed!” 2 Kings 5:14, NLT

Naaman returned to Elisha, grateful for his healing and eager to give him a gift. “Now I know that there is no God in all the world except in Israel. So please accept a gift from your servant.” 2 Kings 5:15, NLT, excerpt Elisha was firm in his refusal. “As surely as the LORD lives, whom I serve, I will not accept any gifts.” 2 Kings 5:16, NLT, excerpt

Ellen G. White explains Elisha’s refusal. “It was not for him to take payment for a blessing that God had in mercy bestowed.” Prophets and Kings, p. 250 · PK 250.1, excerpt Jesus passed that lesson on to His disciples. “Heal the sick, raise the dead, cure those with leprosy, and cast out demons. Give as freely as you have received!” Matthew 10:8, NLT

By refusing the gift, Elisha showed Naaman that his healing came freely from God.

Gehazi had other plans. “But Gehazi, the servant of Elisha, the man of God, said to himself, ‘My master should not have let this Aramean get away without accepting any of his gifts. As surely as the LORD lives, I will chase after him and get something from him.’” 2 Kings 5:20, NLT

Both men took the same oath, “As surely as the LORD lives.” Elisha used those words when he turned down the gift. Gehazi used them to justify going after it. We can use holy words to make a selfish choice sound right, even to ourselves.

Ellen G. White traces that moment back to a desire Gehazi had long indulged.

“The best gifts of Heaven had long been within his reach; yet, turning from these, he had coveted instead the base alloy of worldly wealth.”Prophets and Kings, p. 250 · PK 250.4, excerpt

Coveting begins when wanting something starts to govern our choices. A “base alloy” is a mixture of metals of little value. Gehazi had treasures of far greater worth within reach, yet he set his heart on silver. Jesus warns us how easily greed can distort our sense of what matters. “Beware! Guard against every kind of greed. Life is not measured by how much you own.” Luke 12:15, NLT, excerpt

Our words can sound faithful even when our desires are leading us away from God.

Naaman saw Gehazi running after him and stepped down from his chariot to meet him. He asked, “Is everything all right?” 2 Kings 5:21, NLT, excerpt Gehazi told him all was well, then continued with a lie, “but my master has sent me to tell you that two young prophets from the hill country of Ephraim have just arrived. He would like 75 pounds of silver and two sets of clothing to give to them.” 2 Kings 5:22, NLT, excerpt

Naaman gladly offered more than Gehazi had asked for. “By all means, take twice as much silver,” 2 Kings 5:23, NLT, excerpt he said. Gehazi made sure the silver and clothing stayed with him. “But when they arrived at the citadel, Gehazi took the gifts from the servants and sent the men back. Then he went and hid the gifts inside the house.” 2 Kings 5:24, NLT

Gehazi went in and stood before Elisha as though nothing had happened. Elisha asked, “Where have you been, Gehazi?” 2 Kings 5:25, NLT, excerpt “I haven’t been anywhere,” 2 Kings 5:25, NLT, excerpt Gehazi replied. As Ellen G. White explains, “to shield himself from censure, he uttered a second lie.” Prophets and Kings, p. 251 · PK 251.2, excerpt The first lie got him the silver. The second was meant to spare him the blame.

The words Ellen G. White wrote in one of her letters fit Gehazi’s story. “It is seldom that one sin will stand alone” Letters and Manuscripts, vol. 8 · Lt 22, 1893, par. 9, excerpt.

Each time we choose to hide a wrong, it becomes harder to tell the truth about it.

The gifts were out of sight, but God knew what Gehazi had done. Elisha asked, “Don’t you realize that I was there in spirit when Naaman stepped down from his chariot to meet you?” 2 Kings 5:26, NLT, excerpt The letter to the Hebrews reminds us that God sees us just as clearly. “Nothing in all creation is hidden from God. Everything is naked and exposed before his eyes, and he is the one to whom we are accountable.” Hebrews 4:13, NLT Ellen G. White adds, “Men may think to hide their evil deeds from human eyes, but they cannot deceive God.” Prophets and Kings, p. 252 · PK 252.2, excerpt

Gehazi’s choice affected Naaman, who had only just begun to trust the God of Israel. Ellen G. White describes the harm he did.

“The course of Gehazi was such as to place a stumbling block in the pathway of Naaman, upon whose mind had broken a wonderful light, and who was favorably disposed toward the service of the living God.”Prophets and Kings, p. 252 · PK 252.1, excerpt

Elisha’s refusal had shown Naaman that God gives freely. When Gehazi asked for silver in Elisha’s name, he gave Naaman reason to wonder whether there was a price after all.

Elisha’s next question went deeper than the silver. “Is this the time to receive money and clothing, olive groves and vineyards, sheep and cattle, and male and female servants?” 2 Kings 5:26, NLT, excerpt Then Elisha pronounced the consequence. “Because you have done this, you and your descendants will suffer from Naaman’s leprosy forever.” 2 Kings 5:27, NLT, excerpt “When Gehazi left the room, he was covered with leprosy; his skin was white as snow.” 2 Kings 5:27, NLT, excerpt

Naaman had left Elisha healed. Gehazi walked out with the very disease Naaman had brought with him. Ellen G. White sees in Gehazi a warning for anyone entrusted with spiritual responsibility. “Even those in most responsible positions do fall into sin, while apparently surrounded by the best religious privileges.” Letters and Manuscripts, vol. 11 · Lt 83, 1896, par. 23, excerpt

Paul points us toward a different kind of wealth. “Yet true godliness with contentment is itself great wealth.” 1 Timothy 6:6, NLT He also warns, “For the love of money is the root of all kinds of evil.” 1 Timothy 6:10, NLT, excerpt Hebrews reminds us why we can be content. “Don’t love money; be satisfied with what you have. For God has said, ‘I will never fail you. I will never abandon you.’” Hebrews 13:5, NLT

We can spend our lives around holy things and still need to open our hearts to God.

Centuries later, a man with leprosy wondered whether Jesus would be willing to heal him. “A man with leprosy came and knelt in front of Jesus, begging to be healed. ‘If you are willing, you can heal me and make me clean,’ he said.” Mark 1:40, NLT “Moved with compassion, Jesus reached out and touched him. ‘I am willing,’ he said. ‘Be healed!’” Mark 1:41, NLT “Instantly the leprosy disappeared, and the man was healed.” Mark 1:42, NLT

Touching someone with leprosy would ordinarily make a person ceremonially unclean. Ellen G. White writes, “But in laying His hand upon the leper, Jesus received no defilement. His touch imparted life-giving power.” The Desire of Ages, p. 266 · DA 266.1, excerpt We can trust Jesus to meet us with that same compassion when we bring Him our sin. As she writes, “His presence has healing virtue for the sinner.” The Desire of Ages, p. 266 · DA 266.1, excerpt

Gehazi reached for silver. God offers us something far more precious. “For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life through Christ Jesus our Lord.” Romans 6:23, NLT Long before Paul, Isaiah had spoken of God’s generosity. “Is anyone thirsty? Come and drink— even if you have no money!” Isaiah 55:1, NLT, excerpt

Gehazi tried to keep his sin out of sight. We can tell God the truth about ours, trusting the promise in John’s letter. “But if we confess our sins to him, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all wickedness.” 1 John 1:9, NLT Ellen G. White speaks of what Jesus wants to do for us. “It is His will to cleanse us from sin, to make us His children, and to enable us to live a holy life.” The Desire of Ages, p. 266 · DA 266.2, excerpt

Perhaps you’ve served God for years and seen Him answer your prayers. If there’s a desire you’ve been reluctant to name, you can speak honestly about it with Jesus today.

Jesus welcomes us when we come to Him honestly and freely gives us the grace we could never buy.

Reflect

  1. Has serving God become so familiar that I’ve stopped asking Him to change my heart?
  2. Where might my desire for money or possessions be shaping what I say or do?
  3. What have I been reluctant to bring to Jesus? How can I speak honestly with Him about it today?

Key Takeaways

  1. God gives His grace freely. Elisha’s refusal helped Naaman see that his healing was a gift from God. 2 Kings 5:15–16; Matthew 10:8 · quoted above
  2. One lie makes room for another. Gehazi lied to get the silver and again to hide what he had done. 2 Kings 5:20–25 · quoted above
  3. Jesus receives us with compassion. He reached out to the man with leprosy, and He welcomes us when we come to Him for forgiveness and cleansing. Mark 1:40–42; 1 John 1:9 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Heavenly Father, thank You for giving us Your grace so freely. Forgive us for letting the desire for more shape our choices and for hiding the things we’ve done. Teach us to be content in You and to speak truthfully even when it costs us. Help us encourage those who are just beginning to trust You. Thank You for Jesus, who reaches out in compassion and makes us clean. We bring Him what we’ve been hiding and receive His grace with grateful hearts. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
