The Cross Prepared for Barabbas

Matthew 27:15–26 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-12

Barabbas walks free while the innocent Jesus is condemned. Reflect on choosing Christ over approval, the Saviour who took our place, and the mercy that welcomes us home to God.

Forgiveness & graceFaith & trustHumility & obedience
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“So Pilate released Barabbas to them. He ordered Jesus flogged with a lead-tipped whip, then turned him over to the Roman soldiers to be crucified.”
Matthew 27:26 (NLT)

Have you ever been spared a punishment you knew you deserved? On the morning Jesus was condemned, a man named Barabbas walked free.

Matthew sets the scene with a Passover custom. “Now it was the governor’s custom each year during the Passover celebration to release one prisoner to the crowd—anyone they wanted.” Matthew 27:15, NLT “This year there was a notorious prisoner, a man named Barabbas.” Matthew 27:16, NLT Mark tells us why Barabbas was in prison. “One of the prisoners at that time was Barabbas, a revolutionary who had committed murder in an uprising.” Mark 15:7, NLT

Ellen G. White tells us what was at stake for Barabbas. “The Roman authorities at this time held a prisoner named Barabbas, who was under sentence of death. This man had claimed to be the Messiah.” The Desire of Ages, p. 733 · DA 733.1, excerpt The Messiah was the King God had promised to send. Now the crowd would have to choose between the man who claimed to be that King and the One who truly was.

Barabbas was waiting to die for his crimes. Jesus had done nothing wrong.

Pilate, the Roman governor, hoped the crowd would choose to release Jesus. “As the crowds gathered before Pilate’s house that morning, he asked them, ‘Which one do you want me to release to you—Barabbas, or Jesus who is called the Messiah?’” Matthew 27:17, NLT Pilate understood why the leaders had brought Jesus before him. “(He knew very well that the religious leaders had arrested Jesus out of envy.)” Matthew 27:18, NLT

The leaders had other plans. “Meanwhile, the leading priests and the elders persuaded the crowd to ask for Barabbas to be released and for Jesus to be put to death.” Matthew 27:20, NLT “So the governor asked again, ‘Which of these two do you want me to release to you?’ The crowd shouted back, ‘Barabbas!’” Matthew 27:21, NLT

The crowd had chosen Barabbas. Now Pilate had to decide what to do with Jesus. “Pilate responded, ‘Then what should I do with Jesus who is called the Messiah?’ They shouted back, ‘Crucify him!’” Matthew 27:22, NLT “‘Why?’ Pilate demanded. ‘What crime has he committed?’ But the mob roared even louder, ‘Crucify him!’” Matthew 27:23, NLT

Ellen G. White explains why Pilate’s appeal failed. “It was not evidence of Christ’s innocence that they wanted, but His condemnation.” The Desire of Ages, p. 733 · DA 733.3, excerpt When we are determined to have our own way, even the clearest truth about Jesus can be hard to hear.

Pilate’s question is ours to answer. What will we do with Jesus?

Pilate made one more appeal to the crowd. Ellen G. White describes the scene. “He then presented the two prisoners side by side” The Desire of Ages, p. 735 · DA 735.3, excerpt John records Pilate’s words to the crowd. “Look, here is the man!” John 19:5, NLT, excerpt

Jesus stood before them, beaten and mocked. His face showed what was in His heart.

“Every feature expressed gentleness and resignation and the tenderest pity for His cruel foes.”The Desire of Ages, p. 735 · DA 735.4, excerpt

Here, resignation means that Jesus calmly accepted what He was facing. Still, the crowd kept crying out against Him.

Jesus looked with compassion on the very people who were calling for His death.

Pilate knew Jesus was innocent. When a riot seemed likely, he looked for a way to shift the blame. “Pilate saw that he wasn’t getting anywhere and that a riot was developing. So he sent for a bowl of water and washed his hands before the crowd, saying, ‘I am innocent of this man’s blood. The responsibility is yours!’” Matthew 27:24, NLT The water washed his hands clean. The decision was still his. Mark tells us why Pilate gave in. “So to pacify the crowd, Pilate released Barabbas to them.” Mark 15:15, NLT, excerpt

Ellen G. White sees in Pilate’s choice a struggle many of us recognise.

“Rather than lose his worldly power, he chose to sacrifice an innocent life. How many, to escape loss or suffering, in like manner sacrifice principle. Conscience and duty point one way, and self-interest points another.”The Desire of Ages, p. 738 · DA 738.2, excerpt

We face a choice like Pilate’s whenever doing what is right might cost us someone’s approval or make life harder. How often do we know what we ought to do and still take the easier path?

The crowd’s answer was reckless. “And all the people yelled back, ‘We will take responsibility for his death—we and our children!’” Matthew 27:25, NLT The leaders had stirred up the crowd that shouted those words. Yet the sins Jesus carried to the cross belong to us all. Isaiah had written centuries before, “All of us, like sheep, have strayed away. We have left God’s paths to follow our own. Yet the LORD laid on him the sins of us all.” Isaiah 53:6, NLT

Standing with Jesus means doing what we know is right, even when it costs us something.

Pilate gave the crowd what it wanted. “So Pilate released Barabbas to them. He ordered Jesus flogged with a lead-tipped whip, then turned him over to the Roman soldiers to be crucified.” Matthew 27:26, NLT Luke tells us what Pilate’s decision meant for each man. “As they had requested, he released Barabbas, the man in prison for insurrection and murder. But he turned Jesus over to them to do as they wished.” Luke 23:25, NLT Insurrection means armed rebellion against the government. The rebel went free, and the innocent One was condemned.

Ellen G. White tells us whose cross Jesus carried. “As Jesus passed the gate of Pilate’s court, the cross which had been prepared for Barabbas was laid upon His bruised and bleeding shoulders.” The Desire of Ages, p. 741 · DA 741.4, excerpt The sight of Jesus carrying a rebel’s cross brings to mind what Isaiah wrote about the Lord’s servant. “He was counted among the rebels. He bore the sins of many and interceded for rebels.” Isaiah 53:12, NLT, excerpt

In Barabbas’s release we catch a glimpse of the gospel. Peter tells us what Jesus’ death means for us. “Christ suffered for our sins once for all time. He never sinned, but he died for sinners to bring you safely home to God.” 1 Peter 3:18, NLT, excerpt

Ellen G. White describes what Christ took upon Himself and what He gives us in return.

“Christ was treated as we deserve, that we might be treated as He deserves. He was condemned for our sins, in which He had no share, that we might be justified by His righteousness, in which we had no share. He suffered the death which was ours, that we might receive the life which was His.”The Desire of Ages, p. 25 · DA 25.2, excerpt

To be justified is to be declared right with God. We receive that as a gift through Christ.

The innocent Son of God carried the cross that had been prepared for a guilty man.

What became of the people who had shouted for Barabbas? About seven weeks later, Peter preached about Jesus in Jerusalem at the festival of Pentecost. Ellen G. White tells us who was among those listening. “Some of those who listened to the apostles had taken an active part in the condemnation and death of Christ. Their voices had mingled with the rabble in calling for His crucifixion.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 42 · AA 42.3, excerpt A rabble is a disorderly crowd.

Ellen G. White describes how the people responded when they realised whom they had rejected. “Conviction and anguish seized the people.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 43 · AA 43.1, excerpt Peter called them to turn back to God and receive His forgiveness.

Soon afterward, Peter spoke in the Temple and reminded the people of the choice they had made. “You rejected this holy, righteous one and instead demanded the release of a murderer.” Acts 3:14, NLT Yet he addressed them as friends. “Friends, I realize that what you and your leaders did to Jesus was done in ignorance.” Acts 3:17, NLT “Now repent of your sins and turn to God, so that your sins may be wiped away.” Acts 3:19, NLT

God offered mercy to the very people who had chosen Barabbas. He offers us that same mercy today. “But God showed his great love for us by sending Christ to die for us while we were still sinners.” Romans 5:8, NLT

Perhaps you feel far from God. Perhaps you carry something you think He could never forgive. Jesus carried that cross for you, too. You can answer Pilate’s question today by trusting Him and giving your life back to Him.

Jesus took our place so that we could go free and come home to God.

Questions for Reflection

  1. When have I known what Jesus was asking of me and still chosen what was easier?
  2. How does knowing that Jesus took my place change the way I see myself?
  3. What will I do with Jesus today? What part of my life do I need to entrust to Him?

What to Remember

  1. Pilate’s question still speaks to us. The crowd gave its answer that morning. Each of us must decide whether to trust and follow Jesus. Matthew 27:17–18, 20–23 · quoted above
  2. Jesus took the place of the guilty. Barabbas walked free while Jesus carried the cross meant for him. At the cross, Jesus bore the penalty for our sins. Matthew 27:26; Luke 23:25; 1 Peter 3:18 · quoted above
  3. God’s mercy reaches even those who rejected Him. Peter invited people who had called for Jesus’ death to turn to God and receive forgiveness. That invitation is open to us too. Acts 3:14, 17, 19 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Heavenly Father, thank You for sending Jesus to take our place. Forgive us for straying from Your ways and taking the easier path when we knew what was right. Give us the courage to stand with Jesus, even when it costs us something. Thank You that Your mercy reached even those who called for His death and that it reaches us today. Keep us from washing our hands of Your Son. Help us place our whole lives in His hands with grateful hearts. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
