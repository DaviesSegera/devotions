Today's Devotion

An Understanding Heart

1 Kings 3 · 8 min read
Key Verse
“Give me an understanding heart so that I can govern your people well and know the difference between right and wrong.”
1 Kings 3:9 (NLT, excerpt)
“They saw the wisdom God had given him for rendering justice.”
1 Kings 3:28 (NLT, excerpt)

Solomon’s most famous judgment did not begin when two women walked into his court. It began earlier, at Gibeon, on a night when God came to a young king with an open invitation. “That night the Lord appeared to Solomon in a dream, and God said, ‘What do you want? Ask, and I will give it to you!’” 1 Kings 3:5 (NLT) Solomon began with God’s faithfulness to his father David, and then with his own need: “Now, O Lord my God, you have made me king instead of my father, David, but I am like a little child who doesn’t know his way around.” 1 Kings 3:7 (NLT)

So he asked: “Give me an understanding heart so that I can govern your people well and know the difference between right and wrong. For who by himself is able to govern this great people of yours?” 1 Kings 3:9 (NLT) He was not pretending to be incompetent; he was a king facing a responsibility greater than his own wisdom, and his first instinct was dependence on God. Ellen G. White helps us see the heart behind the words:

“He realized that without divine aid he was as helpless as a little child to fulfill the responsibilities resting on him. He knew that he lacked discernment, and it was a sense of his great need that led him to seek God for wisdom.” Prophets and Kings, p. 30 · PK 30.2

Solomon’s wisdom began with a confession of need, not a claim of ability.

Heaven’s response tells us what God thinks of such a prayer. “The Lord was pleased that Solomon had asked for wisdom.” 1 Kings 3:10 (NLT) God named what Solomon had not asked for—“a long life or wealth or the death of your enemies” 1 Kings 3:11 (NLT, excerpt)—and then He answered: “I will give you what you asked for! I will give you a wise and understanding heart such as no one else has had or ever will have!” 1 Kings 3:12 (NLT)

The gift had a purpose: “wisdom in governing my people with justice.” 1 Kings 3:11 (NLT, excerpt) Solomon had not asked to look wise; he had asked for what he needed to serve God’s people well. Biblical wisdom is not merely knowing more. It is receiving from God the discernment needed to do what is right—and it was never Solomon’s own invention. Ellen G. White carries the same truth to us:

“He who gave to Solomon the spirit of wise discernment is willing to impart the same blessing to His children today. … When a burden bearer desires wisdom more than he desires wealth, power, or fame, he will not be disappointed.” Prophets and Kings, p. 31 · PK 31.1

God gave Solomon wisdom as a gift, for the sake of the people he was called to serve.

Then came a case that words alone could not settle. “Some time later two prostitutes came to the king to have an argument settled.” 1 Kings 3:16 (NLT) One woman told the king her account: they shared a house, each had given birth, they had been alone, the other woman’s baby had died in the night, and—she said—the other woman had exchanged the dead child for her living son while she slept. The other woman denied it: “It certainly was your son, and the living child is mine.” 1 Kings 3:22 (NLT, excerpt)

There was no witness to call. Solomon restated the two claims, called for a sword, and gave a startling command: “Cut the living child in two, and give half to one woman and half to the other!” 1 Kings 3:25 (NLT) Scripture does not narrate what the king was thinking; it records what the command revealed. “Then the woman who was the real mother of the living child, and who loved him very much, cried out, ‘Oh no, my lord! Give her the child—please do not kill him!’ But the other woman said, ‘All right, he will be neither yours nor mine; divide him between us!’” 1 Kings 3:26 (NLT)

The king ruled at once: “Do not kill the child, but give him to the woman who wants him to live, for she is his mother!” 1 Kings 3:27 (NLT) The real mother’s love was revealed not by insisting on possession at any cost, but by valuing her child’s life above her own claim. Ellen G. White remembers this early judgment as the time Solomon “displayed so much wisdom and sympathy in restoring a helpless babe to its unfortunate mother” Prophets and Kings, p. 57 · PK 57.2.

God-given wisdom reached a just decision that protected the child’s life and recognised a mother’s love.

Notice where the story ends. “When all Israel heard the king’s decision, the people were in awe of the king, for they saw the wisdom God had given him for rendering justice.” 1 Kings 3:28 (NLT) The people did not admire a clever man; they recognised a gift from God, used for justice. Every one of us faces something bigger than our own understanding—a family, a class, a church, a workplace, a decision with no easy facts. James writes to believers like us: “If you need wisdom, ask our generous God, and he will give it to you. He will not rebuke you for asking.” James 1:5 (NLT)

Wisdom from God is not a feeling of certainty or a clever answer; it works through prayer, the Scriptures, honest attention to the facts, godly counsel, and care for everyone a decision will touch. Ellen G. White writes of those who carry responsibility:

“The path of men who are placed as leaders is not an easy one. But they are to see in every difficulty a call to prayer. Never are they to fail of consulting the great Source of all wisdom.” Prophets and Kings, p. 31 · PK 31.3

When I face what I cannot solve by my own understanding, do I first seek an understanding heart from God? Yet the story must not end in admiration of Solomon. Jesus said, “Now someone greater than Solomon is here.” Matthew 12:42 (NLT, excerpt) Solomon received wisdom; of Christ, Paul writes, “For our benefit God made him to be wisdom itself.” 1 Corinthians 1:30 (NLT, excerpt) We do not merely need better decisions; we need the Saviour who becomes for us the wisdom we could never produce ourselves.

The wisdom Solomon displayed before people began with dependence upon God—ask Him for an understanding heart, use His gifts with truth and compassion, and keep your eyes on Christ, who is greater than Solomon.

Key Takeaways

  1. Solomon’s wisdom began with a confession of need, not a claim of ability. “I am like a little child who doesn’t know his way around.” 1 Kings 3:7 (NLT, excerpt) Ellen White tells us that “it was a sense of his great need that led him to seek God for wisdom” Prophets and Kings, p. 30 · PK 30.2—dependence on God, not self-confidence, was his first instinct.
  2. God gave Solomon wisdom as a gift, for the sake of the people he was called to serve. “I will give you a wise and understanding heart such as no one else has had or ever will have!” 1 Kings 3:12 (NLT, excerpt) Ellen White writes that God “is willing to impart the same blessing to His children today” Prophets and Kings, p. 31 · PK 31.1—wisdom is received from God, and it is meant for faithful service.
  3. God-given wisdom reached a just decision that protected the child’s life and recognised a mother’s love. “Give her the child—please do not kill him!” 1 Kings 3:26 (NLT, excerpt) Ellen White recalls Solomon’s “wisdom and sympathy in restoring a helpless babe to its unfortunate mother” Prophets and Kings, p. 57 · PK 57.2. Real love seeks the beloved’s good, not merely possession.
  4. The wisdom Solomon displayed before people began with dependence upon God—ask Him for an understanding heart, use His gifts with truth and compassion, and keep your eyes on Christ, who is greater than Solomon. “They saw the wisdom God had given him for rendering justice.” 1 Kings 3:28 (NLT, excerpt) Ellen White says leaders “are to see in every difficulty a call to prayer” Prophets and Kings, p. 31 · PK 31.3; and Jesus says, “Now someone greater than Solomon is here.” Matthew 12:42 (NLT, excerpt)
