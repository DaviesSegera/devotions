Stopped by Grace: Saul on the Road to Damascus

Acts 9:1–31 · 6 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-09-30

Jesus meets Saul on the road to Damascus and gives his life a new direction. Reflect on surrender, the mercy of Christ, and the courage to welcome someone His grace has reached.

Forgiveness & graceHumility & obedienceLove & serviceHope & encouragement

Key Verse
“But the Lord said, ‘Go, for Saul is my chosen instrument to take my message to the Gentiles and to kings, as well as to the people of Israel.’”
Acts 9:15 (NLT)

Saul knew exactly where he was going, and that was the problem. He had conviction, authority, and religious zeal, but his mission set him against Jesus Christ.

He had already approved of Stephen’s death. After that, “Saul was going everywhere to destroy the church.” Acts 8:3 (NLT, excerpt) Now he set his sights on Damascus: “Meanwhile, Saul was uttering threats with every breath and was eager to kill the Lord’s followers. So he went to the high priest.” Acts 9:1 (NLT)

Saul was sure he was serving God. Ellen G. White tells us he set out “fired with mistaken zeal.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 113 · AA 113.3

Sincere zeal can still lead us in the wrong direction.

“As he was approaching Damascus on this mission, a light from heaven suddenly shone down around him. He fell to the ground and heard a voice saying to him, ‘Saul! Saul! Why are you persecuting me?’” Acts 9:3–4 (NLT)

Saul asked who was speaking. The answer shattered his certainty: “I am Jesus, the one you are persecuting!” Acts 9:5 (NLT, excerpt) Ellen G. White explains what that means: “Christ here identifies Himself with His people. In persecuting the followers of Jesus, Saul had struck directly against the Lord of heaven.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 117 · AA 117.1

Jesus did more than rebuke Saul. He gave him a next step: “Now get up and go into the city, and you will be told what you must do.” Acts 9:6 (NLT)

Jesus stopped Saul’s journey and gave his life a new direction.

Saul rose from the ground unable to see. “So his companions led him by the hand to Damascus. He remained there blind for three days and did not eat or drink.” Acts 9:8–9 (NLT, excerpt) The man who had planned to lead others away in chains now had to be led by the hand. He spent the next three days in prayer. The Lord said of him, “He is praying to me right now.” Acts 9:11 (NLT, excerpt)

The Lord then called a believer named Ananias to visit Saul. Ananias openly expressed his fear: “I’ve heard many people talk about the terrible things this man has done to the believers in Jerusalem!” Acts 9:13 (NLT, excerpt) But the Lord answered that Saul was “my chosen instrument.” Acts 9:15 (NLT, excerpt)

Ananias went to Saul. “He laid his hands on him and said, ‘Brother Saul, the Lord Jesus, who appeared to you on the road, has sent me so that you might regain your sight and be filled with the Holy Spirit.’” Acts 9:17 (NLT, excerpt) “Instantly something like scales fell from Saul’s eyes, and he regained his sight. Then he got up and was baptized.” Acts 9:18 (NLT)

Jesus saved Saul, and He chose to involve Ananias in that work. Ellen G. White writes: “All is done in the name and by the authority of Christ. Christ is the fountain; the church is the channel of communication.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 122 · AA 122.4

Jesus met Saul on the road to Damascus. Then He sent Ananias to meet him there.

Saul stayed with the believers, and “immediately he began preaching about Jesus in the synagogues, saying, ‘He is indeed the Son of God!’” Acts 9:20 (NLT, excerpt) The man who came to destroy the church now belonged to it. Saul needed time to grow in faith, and the believers needed time to trust him. In Jerusalem, Barnabas stood beside him. Later Luke calls him “Saul, also known as Paul.” Acts 13:9 (NLT, excerpt) On the Damascus road, Saul encountered the Lord who would transform his life.

Most of us will never see a light from heaven. Yet Jesus still speaks through His Word and by His Spirit. He calls us to turn and follow Him.

Bring your certainty, sin, plans, fears, and past to Jesus. Receive His grace, follow His direction, and take the next faithful step He gives you.

Reflect

  1. Could I be sincere, active, and confident while still resisting what Jesus is showing me?
  2. Is there someone I am afraid to approach whom Christ may be asking me to treat as a person grace can reach?
  3. If Jesus changes my direction, am I willing to follow where He leads?

Key Takeaways

  1. Sincerity is not enough. Saul was zealous for God and still wrong about Jesus. Paul later wrote, “I was so zealous that I harshly persecuted the church.” Philippians 3:6 (excerpt)
  2. Jesus takes His people’s pain personally. “Why are you persecuting me?” Acts 9:4 (excerpt) The way we treat God’s people matters deeply to Christ.
  3. Christ works through ordinary believers. Ananias went, and his first words were “Brother Saul.” Acts 9:17 (excerpt) Christ is the source of grace, and He works through His people.
  4. Grace gives a new direction. “But God had mercy on me so that Christ Jesus could use me as a prime example of his great patience with even the worst sinners.” 1 Timothy 1:16 (excerpt) No one is beyond His reach.

A prayer for today

Lord Jesus, stop me when I am sure of myself but wrong. Open my eyes to who You are. Thank You for mercy that reaches even the worst sinner. Give me courage like Ananias, humility like Saul, and the faith to take the next step You give me. Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
