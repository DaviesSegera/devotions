The Gift That Hid a Lie: Ananias, Sapphira, and the God of Truth

Acts 5:1–11 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-05

Ananias and Sapphira's gift concealed a lie. Reflect on sincere generosity, the danger of seeking admiration, and the mercy Jesus offers when we bring Him the truth about ourselves.

Humility & obedienceWisdom & discernmentLove & serviceForgiveness & grace

Key Verse
“The property was yours to sell or not sell, as you wished. And after selling it, the money was also yours to give away. How could you do a thing like this? You weren’t lying to us but to God!”
Acts 5:4 (NLT)

Have you ever done something good, then wished others would see it as more generous than it really was? We want to be generous, but we also want to be seen as generous. The story of Ananias and Sapphira shows how dangerous that quiet desire for approval can become.

The story opens in a church marked by love. Luke writes, “All the believers were united in heart and mind. And they felt that what they owned was not their own, so they shared everything they had.” Acts 4:32, NLT

Luke points to Barnabas as an example: “He sold a field he owned and brought the money to the apostles.” Acts 4:37, NLT Ellen G. White describes the love behind their generosity: “Their love for their brethren and the cause they had espoused, was greater than their love of money and possessions.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 70 · AA 70.3, excerpt

The early church’s generosity flowed from hearts united by God’s Spirit.

Against this backdrop of generosity, Luke introduces Ananias and Sapphira: “But there was a certain man named Ananias who, with his wife, Sapphira, sold some property.” Acts 5:1, NLT “He brought part of the money to the apostles, claiming it was the full amount. With his wife’s consent, he kept the rest.” Acts 5:2, NLT

Ellen White explains that the couple had promised the proceeds to God but later regretted their promise. Even so, they still wanted the respect shown to generous givers. She describes their plan: “Thus they would secure their living from the common store and at the same time gain the high esteem of their brethren.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 72 · AA 72.1, excerpt They wanted the honour of a great sacrifice without bearing its cost. This is hypocrisy: presenting ourselves as more devoted than we really are.

Peter saw through the deception. He asked, “Ananias, why have you let Satan fill your heart? You lied to the Holy Spirit, and you kept some of the money for yourself.” Acts 5:3, NLT, excerpt Then he made two things clear. Giving had been voluntary: “The property was yours to sell or not sell, as you wished.” Acts 5:4, NLT, excerpt And the lie had been told to God Himself: “You weren’t lying to us but to God!” Acts 5:4, NLT, excerpt

The gift was never the problem. The pretence was.

What happened next is hard to read: “As soon as Ananias heard these words, he fell to the floor and died. Everyone who heard about it was terrified.” Acts 5:5, NLT

Several hours later, Sapphira came in, unaware of her husband’s death. Sapphira gave her own answer: “Peter asked her, ‘Was this the price you and your husband received for your land?’ ‘Yes,’ she replied, ‘that was the price.’” Acts 5:8, NLT Peter answered, “How could the two of you even think of conspiring to test the Spirit of the Lord like this?” Acts 5:9, NLT, excerpt To conspire is to plan wrongdoing together. “Instantly, she fell to the floor and died.” Acts 5:10, NLT, excerpt

Why was this judgment so severe? Ellen White explains that it served as a warning at the very beginning of the church’s life: “Infinite Wisdom saw that this signal manifestation of the wrath of God was necessary to guard the young church from becoming demoralized.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 73 · AA 73.4, excerpt In the same paragraph, she adds, “This judgment testified that men cannot deceive God, that He detects the hidden sin of the heart, and that He will not be mocked.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 73 · AA 73.4, excerpt

Luke describes the response: “Great fear gripped the entire church and everyone else who heard what had happened.” Acts 5:11, NLT The letter to the Hebrews expresses the same truth plainly: “Nothing in all creation is hidden from God.” Hebrews 4:13, NLT, excerpt

Today, God does not usually deal with dishonesty so suddenly, and this story is not meant to make us fear every mistake. Yet Ellen White warns that “though it may not be attended by the visible manifestation of God’s displeasure, it is no less heinous in His sight now than in the apostles’ time.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 76 · AA 76.1, excerpt She also writes that their loss extended beyond this life: “In pretending that they had given all, Ananias and Sapphira lied to the Holy Spirit, and, as a result, they lost this life and the life that is to come.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 75 · AA 75.3, excerpt

The fear that followed did not stop the church from growing. Luke goes on: “Yet more and more people believed and were brought to the Lord—crowds of both men and women.” Acts 5:14, NLT

God guarded His young church by showing that He sees the heart.

How had their deception begun? Ellen White traces it to a desire they had quietly cherished:

“It was covetousness that Ananias and Sapphira had first cherished. The desire to retain for themselves a part of that which they had promised to the Lord, led them into fraud and hypocrisy.”The Acts of the Apostles, p. 74 · AA 74.1, excerpt

Covetousness is wanting to have or keep something so much that the desire begins to rule us. For Ananias and Sapphira, that desire combined with a longing for admiration and led them into a lie.

Jesus warned His disciples about this very danger: “Beware of the yeast of the Pharisees—their hypocrisy.” Luke 12:1, NLT, excerpt Like yeast working through dough, pretence can quietly spread through a life.

Jesus also taught a better way to give: “Give your gifts in private, and your Father, who sees everything, will reward you.” Matthew 6:4, NLT Ellen White writes, “We are to give in sincerity, not to make a show of our good deeds, but from pity and love to the suffering ones. Sincerity of purpose, real kindness of heart, is the motive that Heaven values.” Thoughts From the Mount of Blessing, p. 81 · MB 81.1, excerpt

This story does not call us to give under pressure. Paul writes, “You must each decide in your heart how much to give. And don’t give reluctantly or in response to pressure. ‘For God loves a person who gives cheerfully.’” 2 Corinthians 9:7, NLT God looks beyond the size of our gift to the sincerity of our hearts.

The same sincerity should shape our words. Ellen White puts it simply: “Everything that Christians do should be as transparent as the sunlight.” Thoughts From the Mount of Blessing, p. 68 · MB 68.3, excerpt Such honesty grows as we stay close to Jesus, who said, “I am the way, the truth, and the life.” John 14:6, NLT, excerpt

God does not ask us to look generous. He asks us to be honest.

Peter himself knew what it was to lie. On the night Jesus was arrested, he denied knowing Him: “Man, I don’t know what you are talking about.” Luke 22:60, NLT, excerpt Luke continues, “At that moment the Lord turned and looked at Peter.” Luke 22:61, NLT, excerpt “And Peter left the courtyard, weeping bitterly.” Luke 22:62, NLT

After His resurrection, Jesus restored Peter. Peter answered Him, “Lord, you know everything. You know that I love you.” John 21:17, NLT, excerpt Jesus entrusted him with work to do: “Then feed my sheep.” John 21:17, NLT, excerpt Peter, too, had failed. Yet he returned to Jesus with nothing hidden.

The way back is open to us, too. John writes, “But if we confess our sins to him, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all wickedness.” 1 John 1:9, NLT Ellen White writes, “The confession that is the outpouring of the inmost soul finds its way to the God of infinite pity.” Steps to Christ, p. 37 · SC 37.4, excerpt

We can come to God because of Jesus. Peter later wrote of Him, “He never sinned, nor ever deceived anyone.” 1 Peter 2:22, NLT He also wrote, “He personally carried our sins in his body on the cross so that we can be dead to sin and live for what is right.” 1 Peter 2:24, NLT, excerpt The One who never deceived anyone carried our sins so that we no longer need to pretend. We can bring Him the truth about ourselves and find His grace waiting for us.

The God who sees through every pretence is the God who welcomes every honest heart.

Reflect

  1. Do I ever give or express my faith mainly to win the approval of others?
  2. Have I allowed a small desire or a wish for admiration to lead me into words or actions that are not entirely truthful?
  3. What do I need to bring honestly before Jesus today, trusting Him to forgive and restore me?

Key Takeaways

  1. God values truth more than appearances. Ananias and Sapphira were free to decide what to give. Their sin was claiming to have given the full amount. Acts 5:1–4 · quoted above
  2. Nothing is hidden from God. The judgment warned the young church that God sees the heart. The church continued to grow in honesty and reverence. Acts 5:11; Hebrews 4:13 · quoted above
  3. Honest hearts find mercy in Jesus. Peter had lied about knowing Jesus, yet he wept, returned to Him, and was restored. God forgives those who confess their sins rather than hide them. John 21:17; 1 John 1:9 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Heavenly Father, You see every heart, and nothing in our lives is hidden from You. Forgive us for wanting to appear better than we are in our giving, our words, and our service. Free us from the love of money and the hunger for praise. Teach us to be honest in both small matters and great ones. When we fail, help us return to You quickly, as Peter did, with nothing hidden. Thank You for Jesus, who never deceived anyone and yet carried our sins. Give us sincere hearts and help us live honestly before You. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
