Grace at Five O’Clock

Matthew 20:1–16 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-17

In Jesus’ parable, the workers hired at the last hour receive a full day’s pay. Reflect on God’s generosity, answering His call today, and rejoicing when He shows kindness to others.

Forgiveness & graceHumility & obedienceLove & service
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“Is it against the law for me to do what I want with my money? Should you be jealous because I am kind to others?”
Matthew 20:15 (NLT)

Have you ever worked hard for something, only to watch someone else receive the same reward for far less work? Jesus told His own disciples a story about that very feeling.

It began with a question from Peter. A rich young man had just walked away from Jesus because he could not let go of his wealth. Peter thought about everything the disciples had left behind. “Then Peter said to him, ‘We’ve given up everything to follow you. What will we get?’” Matthew 19:27, NLT Ellen G. White gives us a glimpse of what Peter was thinking. “He thought with satisfaction of what he and his brethren had given up for Christ.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 395 · COL 395.1, excerpt Jesus assured them that nothing they had given up for Him would be forgotten. Then He showed them what God’s generosity looks like.

Peter asked what he would get. Jesus answered by showing him what God is like.

The story opens at sunrise. “For the Kingdom of Heaven is like the landowner who went out early one morning to hire workers for his vineyard.” Matthew 20:1, NLT “He agreed to pay the normal daily wage and sent them out to work.” Matthew 20:2, NLT One silver coin was the usual pay for a full day’s work. The first workers knew exactly what they would earn.

The landowner did not stop there. “At nine o’clock in the morning he was passing through the marketplace and saw some people standing around doing nothing. So he hired them, telling them he would pay them whatever was right at the end of the day.” Matthew 20:3–4, NLT “So they went to work in the vineyard. At noon and again at three o’clock he did the same thing.” Matthew 20:5, NLT The later workers had no set wage. They took the landowner at his word.

Late in the afternoon, he went out once more. “At five o’clock that afternoon he was in town again and saw some more people standing around. He asked them, ‘Why haven’t you been working today?’” Matthew 20:6, NLT “They replied, ‘Because no one hired us.’ The landowner told them, ‘Then go out and join the others in my vineyard.’” Matthew 20:7, NLT They had waited all day for someone to give them a chance. With an hour of daylight left, someone finally said, “Come.”

Ellen G. White sees trust in the way the later workers answered the call. “They showed their confidence in him by asking no question in regard to wages. They trusted to his justice and equity.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 397 · COL 397.1, excerpt

The landowner kept going out until he found the people no one else had hired.

When evening came, it was time to pay the workers. “That evening he told the foreman to call the workers in and pay them, beginning with the last workers first. When those hired at five o’clock were paid, each received a full day’s wage.” Matthew 20:8–9, NLT

Older Bible translations call five o’clock “the eleventh hour,” with the hours counted from sunrise. Ellen G. White uses that phrase as she describes the last workers’ surprise.

“Those who came into the vineyard at the eleventh hour were thankful for an opportunity to work. Their hearts were full of gratitude to the one who had accepted them; and when at the close of the day the householder paid them for a full day’s work, they were greatly surprised. They knew they had not earned such wages.”Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 397 · COL 397.3, excerpt

She sees that same joy in those who come to God late and feel they have little to offer. “His time of service seems so short, he feels that he is undeserving of reward; but he is filled with joy that God has accepted him at all.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 397 · COL 397.3, excerpt

The last workers knew they hadn’t earned a full day’s pay. Imagine their joy when they received it.

Then the first workers came forward. “When those hired first came to get their pay, they assumed they would receive more. But they, too, were paid a day’s wage.” Matthew 20:10, NLT “When they received their pay, they protested to the owner, ‘Those people worked only one hour, and yet you’ve paid them just as much as you paid us who worked all day in the scorching heat.’” Matthew 20:11–12, NLT

It’s easy to understand why they complained. The older brother in Jesus’ story of the lost son felt much the same way. “All these years I’ve slaved for you and never once refused to do a single thing you told me to.” Luke 15:29, NLT, excerpt Ellen G. White recognized the same habit of keeping score in the disciples. “They still worked with the thought of meriting a reward in proportion to their labor. They cherished a spirit of self-exaltation and self-complacency, and made comparisons among themselves.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 396 · COL 396.1, excerpt To merit a reward means to earn it.

The landowner answered gently. “Friend, I haven’t been unfair! Didn’t you agree to work all day for the usual wage?” Matthew 20:13, NLT “Take your money and go. I wanted to pay this last worker the same as you.” Matthew 20:14, NLT “Is it against the law for me to do what I want with my money? Should you be jealous because I am kind to others?” Matthew 20:15, NLT

He called the man “Friend.” The landowner had kept his promise. What troubled the first workers was how much he had given the others.

The landowner was fair to the first workers and generous to the last.

Jesus told this story to show us what the Kingdom of Heaven is like. Eternal life is God’s gift. Paul puts it plainly. “God saved you by his grace when you believed. And you can’t take credit for this; it is a gift from God. Salvation is not a reward for the good things we have done, so none of us can boast about it.” Ephesians 2:8–9, NLT

Ellen G. White draws the same lesson from the parable. “The reward is not of works, lest any man should boast; but it is all of grace.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 401 · COL 401.2, excerpt She goes on to show how grace should change the way we see one another.

“The first and the last are to be sharers in the great, eternal reward, and the first should gladly welcome the last. He who grudges the reward to another forgets that he himself is saved by grace alone. The parable of the laborers rebukes all jealousy and suspicion. Love rejoices in the truth and institutes no envious comparisons. He who possesses love compares only the loveliness of Christ and his own imperfect character.”Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 402 · COL 402.1

When we start comparing ourselves with others, gratitude can quickly give way to grumbling. We may count our years in church or think of all we’ve sacrificed. God sees all of it, and our place with Him is still a gift. His grace frees us to share in someone else’s joy. “Be happy with those who are happy, and weep with those who weep.” Romans 12:15, NLT

We owe our place in God’s kingdom to grace, so we can rejoice when He welcomes someone else.

Does our service still matter? It does, and God remembers what we do for Him. Ellen G. White reminds us what should move us to serve. “The Lord desires us to rest in Him without a question as to our measure of reward. When Christ abides in the soul, the thought of reward is not uppermost.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 398 · COL 398.1, excerpt Later in the same passage, she writes, “Love to God and to our fellow men should be our motive.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 398 · COL 398.1, excerpt

God cares about the spirit in which we serve. “It is not the length of time we labor but our willingness and fidelity in the work that makes it acceptable to God.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 402 · COL 402.3, excerpt Fidelity means faithfulness.

The workers hired at five o’clock were answering their first invitation. Ellen G. White cautions us to take God’s first call seriously. “Those who refuse and afterward repent, do well to repent; but it is not safe to trifle with the first call of mercy.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 399 · COL 399.1, excerpt Paul speaks with the same urgency. “Indeed, the ‘right time’ is now. Today is the day of salvation.” 2 Corinthians 6:2, NLT, excerpt

Even at the last hour, God’s call is an invitation to come now.

Jesus ended the story with a surprising turn. “So those who are last now will be first then, and those who are first will be last.” Matthew 20:16, NLT Soon afterward, He told His disciples why He had come. “For even the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve others and to give his life as a ransom for many.” Matthew 20:28, NLT A ransom is the price paid to set someone free. We receive grace because Jesus gave His life for us.

Ellen G. White closes her chapter with a word of hope for the least of us. “That which even the greatest and wisest cannot earn, the weakest and most humble may receive.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 404 · COL 404.2, excerpt

Perhaps you have served God for many years. Give thanks for those years and rejoice when someone else comes at the last hour. Perhaps you feel you’ve come late and have little to offer. The Master is still calling, and His grace is free.

Whether we come early or late, we stand before God by His grace alone.

Reflect

  1. When have I compared my service or my blessings with someone else’s? How did that affect my gratitude?
  2. Is there someone God has welcomed whose joy I find hard to share?
  3. Have I been putting off something God is calling me to do today?

Key Takeaways

  1. God keeps calling. The landowner went out again and again. Even at five o’clock, he was still looking for people no one had hired. Matthew 20:6–7 · quoted above
  2. Eternal life is God’s gift. We receive it by grace, so none of us has anything to boast about. Ephesians 2:8–9 · quoted above
  3. Be glad for others. The landowner kept his promise to the first workers and gave generously to the last. Grace leaves no room for jealousy. Matthew 20:13–15 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Gracious Father, thank You for coming to find us and calling us to serve You. Thank You that we stand before You by grace alone. Forgive us when we compare ourselves with others or grumble at Your kindness to them. Give us glad hearts when You welcome someone new. Whether You called us early or late, help us serve You faithfully and joyfully out of love for You. If anyone is hearing Your call today, give them the courage to answer now. In the name of Jesus, who gave His life for us, we pray. Amen.
