When Did We See You, Lord?

Matthew 25:31–46 · 6 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-09-25

Jesus calls us to see Him in the people we might overlook. Reflect on Matthew 25, practical compassion, and the love His grace produces.

Love & serviceHumility & obedienceForgiveness & grace

Key Verse
“And the King will say, ‘I tell you the truth, when you did it to one of the least of these my brothers and sisters, you were doing it to me!’”
Matthew 25:40 (NLT)

Imagine discovering at the judgment that you had met Jesus many times without recognizing Him. Matthew 25 tells us that something very much like this will happen.

Jesus was on the Mount of Olives, teaching His disciples about His return and about staying faithful while they waited: “So you, too, must keep watch! For you don't know what day your Lord is coming.” Matthew 24:42 (NLT) Then He described the end itself: “But when the Son of Man comes in his glory, and all the angels with him, then he will sit upon his glorious throne. All the nations will be gathered in his presence, and he will separate the people as a shepherd separates the sheep from the goats.” Matthew 25:31–32 (NLT)

The One on the throne is the King — and He has been paying attention to how we treat people.

To those on His right the King says, “Come, you who are blessed by my Father, inherit the Kingdom prepared for you from the creation of the world.” Matthew 25:34 (NLT, excerpt) Then He tells them why: “For I was hungry, and you fed me. I was thirsty, and you gave me a drink. I was a stranger, and you invited me into your home. I was naked, and you gave me clothing. I was sick, and you cared for me. I was in prison, and you visited me.” Matthew 25:35–36 (NLT)

Nothing on that list is spectacular: a plate of food, a cup of water, an open door, something warm to wear, a visit. And the people who did those things are astonished: “Lord, when did we ever see you hungry and feed you? Or thirsty and give you something to drink?” Matthew 25:37 (NLT, excerpt)

The King answers with the words at the heart of the passage: “I tell you the truth, when you did it to one of the least of these my brothers and sisters, you were doing it to me!” Matthew 25:40 (NLT, excerpt)

We often imagine that serving Jesus would be unmistakable. He says some of our most important opportunities to serve Him arrive looking like ordinary human need.

Ellen G. White shows us where His steps are found today:

Ellen G. White“We need not go to Nazareth, to Capernaum, or to Bethany, in order to walk in the steps of Jesus. We shall find His footprints beside the sickbed, in the hovels of poverty, in the crowded alleys of the great city, and in every place where there are human hearts in need of consolation.” The Desire of Ages, p. 640 · DA 640.2

Then the King turns to those on His left, and the same six needs return with two words added: “For I was hungry, and you didn't feed me. I was thirsty, and you didn't give me a drink.” Matthew 25:42 (NLT) They are just as surprised: “Lord, when did we ever see you hungry or thirsty or a stranger or naked or sick or in prison, and not help you?” Matthew 25:44 (NLT) And the answer comes: “I tell you the truth, when you refused to help the least of these my brothers and sisters, you were refusing to help me.” Matthew 25:45 (NLT, excerpt) Jesus ends with both destinations: “And they will go away into eternal punishment, but the righteous will go into eternal life.” Matthew 25:46 (NLT)

No one on the left is accused of cruelty. Everything on the list was simply left undone. Indifference rarely feels like sin; it feels like being busy.

One of the most searching words in Matthew 25 is not “did.” It is “did not.”

None of this is a way to buy heaven. “God saved you by his grace when you believed. And you can't take credit for this; it is a gift from God.” Ephesians 2:8 (NLT) But the grace that saves also remakes: “For we are God's masterpiece. He has created us anew in Christ Jesus, so we can do the good things he planned for us long ago.” Ephesians 2:10 (NLT) Good works are not the price of salvation. They are evidence of what grace is doing in a life.

So we come to Christ first, and let His love change what we notice. Then we act, small and nearby: learning a newcomer's name, taking a meal to a worn-out family, calling someone who is housebound, supporting a trustworthy ministry that visits people we could not safely visit ourselves. We are not asked to meet every need, only to stop walking past the ones He places within our reach.

Receive Christ's love, ask Him to open your eyes, and let His grace become visible in one person you can serve today.

Reflect

  1. Who might be easy for me to overlook but precious to Christ?
  2. What needs have I become so accustomed to seeing that I no longer really see them?
  3. Lord, when did I see You — and did I stop long enough to care?

Key Takeaways

  1. Christ is the King and the Judge. “He will sit upon his glorious throne” Matthew 25:31 (excerpt), and every nation stands before Him.
  2. Jesus takes our treatment of people personally. “When you did it to one of the least of these my brothers and sisters, you were doing it to me!” Matthew 25:40 (excerpt)
  3. Neglect reaches Him too. “When you refused to help the least of these my brothers and sisters, you were refusing to help me.” Matthew 25:45 (excerpt) In Ellen G. White's words, those on the left “had been self-absorbed, and cared not for others' needs” The Desire of Ages, p. 639 · DA 639.3.
  4. Grace comes first, and grace produces love. “Dear children, let's not merely say that we love each other; let us show the truth by our actions.” 1 John 3:18

A prayer for today

Lord Jesus, thank You for loving me before I had anything to give. Forgive me for the needs I have learned not to see. Open my eyes to the people You have placed within my reach, give me compassion and wisdom, and let Your love become visible in the way I treat them today. Amen.
