Jacob’s Ladder at Bethel: God Meets Us in Our Lonely Places

Genesis 28:10–22 · 6 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-02

God meets Jacob on a lonely night at Bethel. Reflect on God’s presence, the grace of Jesus who brings us to God, and our response of grateful faith.

Faith & trustHope & encouragementForgiveness & graceHumility & obedience

Key Verse
“What’s more, I am with you, and I will protect you wherever you go. One day I will bring you back to this land. I will not leave you until I have finished giving you everything I have promised you.”
Genesis 28:15 (NLT)

On a lonely night, regrets can weigh heavily and the future can feel uncertain. Jacob’s night at Bethel reminds us of God’s promise and the Saviour who brings us close to Him.

Jacob had deceived his father and taken the blessing his brother wanted. With Esau angry, Jacob set out for his uncle Laban’s home. His father sent him on his way with a blessing: “May God pass on to you and your descendants the blessings he promised to Abraham.” Genesis 28:4 (NLT, excerpt) Jacob faced a long journey alone. “At sundown he arrived at a good place to set up camp and stopped there for the night. Jacob found a stone to rest his head against and lay down to sleep.” Genesis 28:11 (NLT)

Jacob could not undo the past or control the future, but God met him that very night.

“As he slept, he dreamed of a stairway that reached from the earth up to heaven. And he saw the angels of God going up and down the stairway.” Genesis 28:12 (NLT) God came to Jacob while he slept. The LORD spoke from the top of the stairway and renewed His promise to Abraham’s family: “And all the families of the earth will be blessed through you and your descendants.” Genesis 28:14 (NLT, excerpt) Then God assured Jacob that He would be with him, as our key verse tells us.

Feeling alone does not mean God has abandoned us. Sometimes loneliness follows our wrong choices; often it comes through no fault of our own. Whatever has brought us here, God knows where we are. When we have done wrong, we can be honest with Him and receive His mercy.

The night made Jacob’s need clear; God’s promise showed that His grace still reached him.

Many years later, Jesus told Nathanael, “I tell you the truth, you will all see heaven open and the angels of God going up and down on the Son of Man, the one who is the stairway between heaven and earth.” John 1:51 (NLT, excerpt)

Ellen G. White explains how the dream answered Jacob’s deepest need:

“Thus was made known to Jacob that which met the need and longing of his soul—a Saviour. With joy and gratitude he saw revealed a way by which he, a sinner, could be restored to communion with God. The mystic ladder of his dream represented Jesus, the only medium of communication between God and man.”Steps to Christ, p. 19 · SC 19.2

We do not have to earn our way to God. His grace is a gift: “God saved you by his grace when you believed. And you can’t take credit for this; it is a gift from God.” Ephesians 2:8 (NLT)

Bethel points us to the Saviour who brings us to God. We do not have to earn our way to Him.

“Then Jacob awoke from his sleep and said, ‘Surely the LORD is in this place, and I wasn’t even aware of it!’” Genesis 28:16 (NLT) In the morning he set up the stone as a memorial pillar and poured oil over it. “He named that place Bethel (which means ‘house of God’), although it was previously called Luz.” Genesis 28:19 (NLT)

Jacob then promised that the LORD would be his God and that he would return a tenth of all God gave him. God had spoken first; Jacob responded with worship and gratitude.

We worship and give because God has shown us grace, not to earn His love.

Wherever you are today, you can come to God through Jesus. He has opened the way.

Bring your need to Jesus, receive His mercy, trust that God is with you, and respond with grateful obedience.

Reflect

  1. Have I mistaken my loneliness, guilt, or uncertainty for proof that God has abandoned me?
  2. Am I trying to earn my way to God, or am I trusting Christ and responding with gratitude?
  3. Will I trust the God who meets me where I am and draws me to Himself through Jesus?

Key Takeaways

  1. God comes to us first. Jacob did not climb to heaven; God met him where he lay. “I am with you” Genesis 28:15 (excerpt)
  2. Feeling alone does not mean God has abandoned you. “I will never fail you. I will never abandon you.” Hebrews 13:5 (excerpt)
  3. Jesus is the way to God. “No one can come to the Father except through me.” John 14:6 (excerpt)
  4. Gratitude is our response to God’s grace, not a way to buy it. Jacob’s worship and vow followed God’s promise.

A prayer for today

Father, thank You for being with Jacob in his loneliness and for being with me in mine. Forgive my sins through Jesus and help me trust that You are near. Thank You for opening the way to You through Christ. Help me respond to Your grace with a grateful heart. Amen.
