Hidden Beneath the Tent: Achan and the Valley of Trouble

Joshua 7:1–26 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-07

After Jericho, hidden sin brings trouble to Israel. Reflect on seeking God after victory, confessing freely, and the hope Jesus offers when we bring what is hidden into His light.

Humility & obedienceForgiveness & graceWisdom & discernmentHope & encouragement

Key Verse
“Then Joshua said to Achan, ‘My son, give glory to the LORD, the God of Israel, by telling the truth. Make your confession and tell me what you have done. Don’t hide it from me.’”
Joshua 7:19 (NLT)

Have you noticed how some of our hardest falls follow close behind our greatest joys? A prayer is answered, a battle is won, and before long we begin to rely less on the God who brought us through. Israel learned this painful lesson in the days after Jericho.

Jericho’s walls had fallen, and Israel knew who had given the victory: “So the LORD was with Joshua, and his reputation spread throughout the land.” Joshua 6:27, NLT Before the city fell, God had made His instructions about its contents clear: “Do not take any of the things set apart for destruction, or you yourselves will be completely destroyed, and you will bring trouble on the camp of Israel.” Joshua 6:18, NLT “Everything made from silver, gold, bronze, or iron is sacred to the LORD and must be brought into his treasury.” Joshua 6:19, NLT

“Set apart for destruction” meant that everything in Jericho belonged wholly to God. It was to be destroyed or placed in His treasury; none of it was theirs to keep. The victory belonged to the LORD, and so did the city. Yet the next chapter opens with one small, troubling word: “But Israel violated the instructions about the things set apart for the LORD. A man named Achan had stolen some of these dedicated things, so the LORD was very angry with the Israelites.” Joshua 7:1, NLT, excerpt The reader learns at once what Israel has yet to discover.

What belongs to God is never ours to keep in secret.

The next town, Ai, seemed an easy conquest. The spies reported, “There’s no need for all of us to go up there; it won’t take more than two or three thousand men to attack Ai.” Joshua 7:3, NLT, excerpt Israel followed their advice: “So approximately 3,000 warriors were sent, but they were soundly defeated.” Joshua 7:4, NLT, excerpt The men of Ai gave chase, and “they killed about thirty-six who were retreating down the slope. The Israelites were paralyzed with fear at this turn of events, and their courage melted away.” Joshua 7:5, NLT, excerpt

The people who had watched a great city fall were now fleeing from a small town. Ellen White explains the change that had taken place in their hearts:

“The great victory that God had gained for them had made the Israelites self-confident. Because He had promised them the land of Canaan they felt secure, and failed to realize that divine help alone could give them success. Even Joshua laid his plans for the conquest of Ai without seeking counsel from God.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 493 · PP 493.4

The defeat left Joshua overwhelmed with grief: “Joshua and the elders of Israel tore their clothing in dismay, threw dust on their heads, and bowed face down to the ground before the Ark of the LORD until evening.” Joshua 7:6, NLT

God’s answer was direct: “Get up! Why are you lying on your face like this?” Joshua 7:10, NLT, excerpt “Israel has sinned and broken my covenant! They have stolen some of the things that I commanded must be set apart for me. And they have not only stolen them but have lied about it and hidden the things among their own belongings.” Joshua 7:11, NLT In secret, Israel had broken its covenant with God, the binding agreement between Him and His people. Ellen White writes: “It was a time for prompt and decided action, and not for despair and lamentation. There was secret sin in the camp, and it must be searched out and put away before the presence and blessing of the Lord could be with His people.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 494 · PP 494.2, excerpt

Hidden sin never stays as private as we think.

God made the cause of Israel’s defeat plain: “Hidden among you, O Israel, are things set apart for the LORD.” Joshua 7:13, NLT, excerpt The next morning, the nation came before Him, tribe by tribe, clan by clan, and family by family. Finally, “Every member of Zimri’s family was brought forward person by person, and Achan was singled out.” Joshua 7:18, NLT

Why did God not simply name Achan from the beginning? Ellen White explains:

“The sinner was not directly pointed out, the matter being left in doubt for a time, that the people might feel their responsibility for the sins existing among them, and thus be led to searching of heart and humiliation before God.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 494 · PP 494.3, excerpt

Each stage of that long morning gave Achan another opportunity to speak. Ellen White reminds us that he had already seen the defeated army return and Joshua lying face down on the ground: “Had he then made confession, he would have given some proof of true penitence; but he still kept silence.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 497 · PP 497.4, excerpt

When Achan was finally singled out, Joshua addressed him with the tenderness of a father: “Then Joshua said to Achan, ‘My son, give glory to the LORD, the God of Israel, by telling the truth. Make your confession and tell me what you have done. Don’t hide it from me.’” Joshua 7:19, NLT Telling the truth gives glory to God: it means agreeing with Him about what is right.

God calls us to come forward before we are found out.

At last, Achan spoke: “It is true! I have sinned against the LORD, the God of Israel.” Joshua 7:20, NLT, excerpt “Among the plunder I saw a beautiful robe from Babylon, 200 silver coins, and a bar of gold weighing more than a pound. I wanted them so much that I took them. They are hidden in the ground beneath my tent, with the silver buried deeper than the rest.” Joshua 7:21, NLT

Listen to the steps: I saw. I wanted. I took. I hid. His sin began in the heart, long before his hand reached for the plunder. James describes the same progression: “Temptation comes from our own desires, which entice us and drag us away.” James 1:14, NLT “These desires give birth to sinful actions. And when sin is allowed to grow, it gives birth to death.” James 1:15, NLT

Ellen White identifies the root of Achan’s sin: “The deadly sin that led to Achan’s ruin had its root in covetousness, of all sins one of the most common and the most lightly regarded.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 496 · PP 496.2, excerpt Covetousness is the desire for what is not ours, cherished until it begins to govern us. She describes its gradual growth:

“Covetousness is an evil of gradual development. Achan had cherished greed of gain until it became a habit, binding him in fetters well-nigh impossible to break. While fostering this evil, he would have been filled with horror at the thought of bringing disaster upon Israel; but his perceptions were deadened by sin, and when temptation came, he fell an easy prey.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 496 · PP 496.3

Achan had not set out to bring disaster on his people. Yet a desire he had long indulged had taken hold of his heart.

Achan admitted the truth, but only after he had been exposed. Ellen White makes a searching distinction: “There is a vast difference between admitting facts after they have been proved and confessing sins known only to ourselves and to God.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 497 · PP 497.4, excerpt

Achan’s story ends painfully, and Scripture records it plainly: “Then Joshua said to Achan, ‘Why have you brought trouble on us? The LORD will now bring trouble on you.’ And all the Israelites stoned Achan and his family and burned their bodies.” Joshua 7:25, NLT A heap of stones was raised over him. The account closes with these words: “That is why the place has been called the Valley of Trouble ever since. So the LORD was no longer angry.” Joshua 7:26, NLT, excerpt

Sin grows stronger when we nourish it in secret.

Yet the Valley of Trouble appears again in Scripture, this time in a promise of hope. Centuries later, God spoke through the prophet Hosea about His wandering people: “I will return her vineyards to her and transform the Valley of Trouble into a gateway of hope.” Hosea 2:15, NLT, excerpt The place that bore the memory of Israel’s shame would become a doorway to mercy.

That doorway is open to us through Jesus. Achan could not undo his sin. Jesus, who had no sin to hide, bore the burden we could never bear: “Yet the LORD laid on him the sins of us all.” Isaiah 53:6, NLT, excerpt Paul explains what this means for us: “For God made Christ, who never sinned, to be the offering for our sin, so that we could be made right with God through Christ.” 2 Corinthians 5:21, NLT

We do not have to wait for our sin to be exposed before we come to God. David wrote, “Finally, I confessed all my sins to you and stopped trying to hide my guilt.… And you forgave me! All my guilt is gone.” Psalm 32:5, NLT, excerpt The promise still stands: “But if we confess our sins to him, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all wickedness.” 1 John 1:9, NLT

Ellen White describes the kind of confession God welcomes:

“The humble and broken heart, subdued by genuine repentance, will appreciate something of the love of God and the cost of Calvary; and as a son confesses to a loving father, so will the truly penitent bring all his sins before God.”Steps to Christ, p. 41 · SC 41.2, excerpt

God waits for us to come freely, not because we are trapped, but because we trust His love. As she writes, “The confession that is the outpouring of the inmost soul finds its way to the God of infinite pity.” Steps to Christ, p. 37 · SC 37.4, excerpt

After the hidden sin had been dealt with, God spoke to Joshua once more: “Do not be afraid or discouraged.” Joshua 8:1, NLT, excerpt Ellen White records what followed: “The power of God was with His people, and they were soon in possession of the city.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 499 · PP 499.1, excerpt

This story calls us to examine our own hearts, rather than search for an “Achan” among others. In Ellen White’s words, “With humiliation and searching of heart, let each seek to discover the hidden sins that shut out God’s presence.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 497 · PP 497.3, excerpt Is there something buried beneath your tent today? Bring it into the light while the door of mercy stands open. Scripture gives us this invitation: “So let us come boldly to the throne of our gracious God. There we will receive his mercy, and we will find grace to help us when we need it most.” Hebrews 4:16, NLT

The God who sees what is hidden is the God who makes the Valley of Trouble a gateway of hope.

Reflect

  1. After a blessing or a victory, have I begun making plans without first seeking God’s counsel?
  2. Am I nurturing a desire in secret that could lead me where I would never wish to go?
  3. Is there something I need to bring before God today, freely and honestly, rather than waiting for it to be exposed?

Key Takeaways

  1. Sin grows in secret. Achan’s fall unfolded step by step: he saw, he wanted, he took, and he hid. Covetousness had taken root in his heart long before he reached for the plunder. Joshua 7:20–21 · quoted above
  2. God holds the door open. Joshua called Achan “my son” and urged him to honor God by telling the truth. There is a deep difference between acknowledging what has already been proved and freely bringing our sins before God. Joshua 7:19 · quoted above
  3. Trouble can become a gateway of hope. God promised to turn the Valley of Trouble into a doorway of mercy. Through Jesus, who bore our sins, that door stands open to everyone who confesses. Hosea 2:15 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Heavenly Father, thank You for every victory You have given us. Guard our hearts against the carelessness that can follow blessing. Teach us to seek Your counsel before making our plans. Search us, Lord, and reveal any desire we have been nurturing in secret. Give us courage to bring our sins freely into Your light, before they are exposed, and help us trust Your faithfulness to forgive. Thank You for Jesus, who bore the sins of us all and turns our valleys of trouble into gateways of hope. Help us to walk before You with open, honest hearts and nothing hidden. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
