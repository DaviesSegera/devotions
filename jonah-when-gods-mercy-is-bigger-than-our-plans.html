Jonah: When God's Mercy Is Bigger Than Our Plans

Jonah 1–4 · 6 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-15

Jonah ran from God's call, received a second chance, and struggled when Nineveh received mercy too. Reflect on repentance, obedience, and sharing God's compassion.

Forgiveness & graceHumility & obedienceLove & servicePrayer

Key Verse
“But Nineveh has more than 120,000 people living in spiritual darkness, not to mention all the animals. Shouldn’t I feel sorry for such a great city?”
Jonah 4:11 (NLT)

We can be grateful for God's mercy and still struggle when He extends it to someone else. Jonah's story brings that struggle into view, beginning with a runaway prophet and ending with a question about compassion.

Jonah was a prophet in Israel. The Bible records a promise God made “through Jonah son of Amittai, the prophet from Gath-hepher.” 2 Kings 14:25, NLT, excerpt Then God gave him a difficult assignment: “Get up and go to the great city of Nineveh. Announce my judgment against it because I have seen how wicked its people are.” Jonah 1:2, NLT

Nineveh was a great Assyrian city marked by wickedness and violence. Yet God saw people who could turn to Him. Ellen G. White writes that God “perceived in that city many who were reaching out after something better and higher, and who, if granted opportunity to learn of the living God, would put away their evil deeds and worship Him.” Prophets and Kings, p. 265 · PK 265.3 God's warning was an act of mercy.

Jonah resisted the assignment. “But Jonah got up and went in the opposite direction to get away from the LORD.” Jonah 1:3, NLT, excerpt He went down to Joppa and bought passage on a ship bound for Tarshish.

God sent a violent storm. As the terrified sailors prayed and threw cargo overboard, Jonah slept. When the lot fell on him, he told them to throw him into the sea. They tried to save his life. “Instead, the sailors rowed even harder to get the ship to the land.” Jonah 1:13, NLT, excerpt When they could no longer make headway, they threw him overboard. The storm stopped at once, and the sailors worshiped the LORD.

“Now the LORD had arranged for a great fish to swallow Jonah. And Jonah was inside the fish for three days and three nights.” Jonah 1:17, NLT In that darkness, Jonah turned to God in prayer: “I cried out to the LORD in my great trouble, and he answered me.” Jonah 2:2, NLT, excerpt He ended his prayer with these words: “For my salvation comes from the LORD alone.” Jonah 2:9, NLT, excerpt Then God commanded the fish, and it spat Jonah out onto the beach.

Jonah ran from his assignment, but he could not run beyond the reach of God's mercy.

“Then the LORD spoke to Jonah a second time.” Jonah 3:1, NLT, excerpt This time Jonah obeyed. In Nineveh's streets, he announced, “Forty days from now Nineveh will be destroyed!” Jonah 3:4, NLT, excerpt

The people believed God, fasted, and put on burlap. Even the king left his throne and sat in ashes. He ordered, “everyone must pray earnestly to God. They must turn from their evil ways and stop all their violence.” Jonah 3:8, NLT, excerpt Their repentance went beyond outward signs of sorrow: they changed the way they lived.

“When God saw what they had done and how they had put a stop to their evil ways, he changed his mind and did not carry out the destruction he had threatened.” Jonah 3:10, NLT

God gave Jonah a second opportunity, and He gave Nineveh one too.

Then the story takes a surprising turn. “This change of plans greatly upset Jonah, and he became very angry.” Jonah 4:1, NLT He told God, “I knew that you are a merciful and compassionate God, slow to get angry and filled with unfailing love.” Jonah 4:2, NLT, excerpt Jonah knew that God was merciful, but did not want Nineveh to receive that mercy.

Ellen G. White helps us understand his reaction:

Ellen G. White’s insight · quotation“When Jonah learned of God’s purpose to spare the city that, notwithstanding its wickedness, had been led to repent in sackcloth and ashes, he should have been the first to rejoice because of God’s amazing grace; but instead he allowed his mind to dwell upon the possibility of his being regarded as a false prophet. Jealous of his reputation, he lost sight of the infinitely greater value of the souls in that wretched city.”Prophets and Kings, ch. 22, “Nineveh, That Great City,” p. 271 (PK 271.1)

God patiently asked, “Is it right for you to be angry about this?” Jonah 4:4, NLT As Jonah waited outside the city, God provided a leafy plant to shade him, “and Jonah was very grateful for the plant.” Jonah 4:6, NLT, excerpt The next morning, a worm destroyed the plant and a scorching east wind blew. Jonah's anger returned.

God's final question exposed Jonah's priorities. If Jonah grieved over a short-lived plant, how much more should God care for a city full of people and animals? “Shouldn’t I feel sorry for such a great city?” Jonah 4:11, NLT, excerpt

Jonah welcomed God's gift of shade, yet resented His mercy toward Nineveh.

The book ends with that question, leaving us to consider our own response.

Centuries later, Jesus spoke about Jonah and Nineveh: “Now someone greater than Jonah is here—but you refuse to repent.” Matthew 12:41, NLT, excerpt

Jesus is greater than Jonah. Jonah fled his mission; Jesus came willingly to seek the lost. Jonah sank into the sea through disobedience; Jesus obeyed His Father even to death, then rose again. Jonah resented mercy for his enemies; Jesus gave His life for His enemies.

Jonah's story invites us to examine our response to God. Will we obey when His call is difficult? Will we rejoice when He shows mercy to people we find hard to love? Mercy does not excuse evil; Nineveh had to stop its violence. It does not require us to stay in danger, but it can change how we pray for others.

God showed mercy to a runaway prophet and a repentant city. His question still speaks to us.

Reflect

  1. What is God calling me to do that I have been putting off?
  2. Do I rejoice when God shows mercy to people outside my circle?
  3. Do I welcome God's mercy for myself but resent it when He shows mercy to others?

Key Takeaways

  1. God graciously calls us back. “Then the LORD spoke to Jonah a second time.” Jonah 3:1, NLT, excerpt
  2. People matter more than our comfort or reputation. Ellen G. White: “Jealous of his reputation, he lost sight of the infinitely greater value of the souls in that wretched city.” Prophets and Kings, p. 271 · PK 271.1
  3. God's compassion reaches beyond our circle. “Shouldn’t I feel sorry for such a great city?” Jonah 4:11, NLT, excerpt

A prayer for today

Merciful God, thank You for Your patience with Jonah and with me. When I resist Your call, draw me back to You. When I value my comfort or reputation above people, help me see them as You do. Thank You, Jesus, for coming to seek the lost and giving Your life for sinners like me. Give me courage to obey Your Word and compassion to love the people You love. Amen.
