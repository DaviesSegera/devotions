Taken Home Alive

Acts 20:7–12 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-21

A long gathering at Troas turns to grief when Eutychus falls from a window. Reflect on God’s compassion for the weary, caring for one another, and the resurrection hope Jesus gives.

Hope & encouragementLove & serviceFaith & trustPrayer
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“Paul went down, bent over him, and took him into his arms. ‘Don’t worry,’ he said, ‘he’s alive!’”
Acts 20:10 (NLT)

Have you ever sat among God’s people, wanting to listen but barely able to keep your eyes open? Acts 20 tells of a night like that. During a long gathering, a young man drifted off to sleep and fell from an upstairs window. What happened next shows how God meets us in our weakness and grief.

On his way to Jerusalem, Paul spent a week in Troas, a port town on the Aegean coast. On his last evening there, the believers gathered to say goodbye. “On the first day of the week, we gathered with the local believers to share in the Lord’s Supper. Paul was preaching to them, and since he was leaving the next day, he kept talking until midnight.” Acts 20:7, NLT

Ellen G. White describes the scene.

“The fact that their beloved teacher was about to depart, had called together a larger company than usual. They assembled in an ‘upper chamber’ on the third story. There, in the fervency of his love and solicitude for them, the apostle preached until midnight.”The Acts of the Apostles, p. 391 · AA 391.2, excerpt

“Solicitude” means deep concern. In her earlier account, Ellen G. White writes that “Paul’s mind was impressed with a sense of the perils that awaited him, and the uncertainty of again meeting with his brethren”. Sketches from the Life of Paul, p. 196 · LP 196.4, excerpt

Paul kept speaking out of love for friends he might never see again.

The room was crowded and warm. “The upstairs room where we met was lighted with many flickering lamps.” Acts 20:8, NLT

Then the evening took a sudden turn. “As Paul spoke on and on, a young man named Eutychus, sitting on the windowsill, became very drowsy. Finally, he fell sound asleep and dropped three stories to his death below.” Acts 20:9, NLT

The name Eutychus means “fortunate.” Ellen G. White describes the fear and grief that followed his fall.

“In one of the open windows sat a youth named Eutychus. In this perilous position he went to sleep and fell to the court below. At once all was alarm and confusion. The youth was taken up dead, and many gathered about him with cries and mourning.”The Acts of the Apostles, p. 391 · AA 391.3, excerpt

Luke tells this story without a word of blame. Eutychus had come to hear God’s Word and stayed late into the night. Eventually, his strength gave out. Isaiah puts words to that kind of weariness. “Even youths will become weak and tired, and young men will fall in exhaustion.” Isaiah 40:30, NLT Weariness is part of being human. When someone reaches their limit, they need compassion.

A night of worship suddenly became a night of grief.

Luke tells us how Paul responded. “Paul went down, bent over him, and took him into his arms. ‘Don’t worry,’ he said, ‘he’s alive!’” Acts 20:10, NLT

Paul’s response brings to mind two Old Testament prophets. When a widow’s son died, Elijah “stretched himself out over the child three times and cried out to the LORD, ‘O LORD my God, please let this child’s life return to him.’” 1 Kings 17:21, NLT, excerpt “The LORD heard Elijah’s prayer, and the life of the child returned, and he revived!” 1 Kings 17:22, NLT Later, when another child died, Elisha did something similar. “And as he stretched out on him, the child’s body began to grow warm again!” 2 Kings 4:34, NLT, excerpt In both stories, God restored the child to life. His servants brought the need before Him.

Ellen G. White describes Paul’s prayer for Eutychus.

“But Paul, passing through the frightened company, embraced him and offered up an earnest prayer that God would restore the dead to life. His petition was granted. Above the sound of mourning and lamentation the apostle’s voice was heard, saying, ‘Trouble not yourselves; for his life is in him.’”The Acts of the Apostles, p. 391 · AA 391.3, excerpt

Paul prayed before he spoke those reassuring words. He stopped preaching and went to the young man who had fallen. When someone is lying on the ground, love goes down to meet them.

Jesus came down to us in our need. Paul describes how far Christ humbled Himself. “Instead, he gave up his divine privileges; he took the humble position of a slave and was born as a human being.” Philippians 2:7, NLT, excerpt “he humbled himself in obedience to God and died a criminal’s death on a cross.” Philippians 2:8, NLT Jesus said of His own mission, “For the Son of Man came to seek and save those who are lost.” Luke 19:10, NLT

Paul went down to the young man who had fallen, and God restored him to life.

After Paul prayed, the gathering continued. “Then they all went back upstairs, shared in the Lord’s Supper, and ate together. Paul continued talking to them until dawn, and then he left.” Acts 20:11, NLT Ellen G. White describes the mood in the room. “With rejoicing, yet in deep humility at this signal manifestation of God’s power and mercy, the believers again assembled in the upper chamber.” Sketches from the Life of Paul, p. 197 · LP 197.1, excerpt Here, “signal” means especially clear or remarkable.

The bread they shared that night pointed to Jesus’ death and His return. Paul wrote, “For every time you eat this bread and drink this cup, you are announcing the Lord’s death until he comes again.” 1 Corinthians 11:26, NLT Ellen G. White writes of the Lord’s Supper, “The Communion service points to Christ’s second coming. It was designed to keep this hope vivid in the minds of the disciples.” The Desire of Ages, p. 659 · DA 659.3, excerpt

The believers at Troas had just seen God give Eutychus back his life. At the Lord’s table, they remembered the One who gave His life for them. Ellen G. White reminds us where our hope rests. “His sacrifice is the center of our hope.” The Desire of Ages, p. 660 · DA 660.1, excerpt She also writes, “The union and love between brother and brother must be cemented and rendered eternal by the love of Jesus.” The Desire of Ages, p. 660 · DA 660.1, excerpt That night at Troas, Christ was the One who held them together.

Around the Lord’s table, the believers remembered the sacrifice at the heart of their hope.

Then morning came. “Meanwhile, the young man was taken home alive and well, and everyone was greatly relieved.” Acts 20:12, NLT A night of flickering lamps, teaching, and tears ended with Eutychus going home alive.

Eutychus would still face death one day. His story points to the greater hope of resurrection when Jesus returns. Jesus said to Martha, “I am the resurrection and the life. Anyone who believes in me will live, even after dying.” John 11:25, NLT, excerpt Ellen G. White writes of these words, “In Christ is life, original, unborrowed, underived.” The Desire of Ages, p. 530 · DA 530.3, excerpt Jesus has life in Himself, and He gives it to others. Paul writes of what this means for believers who have died. “For since we believe that Jesus died and was raised to life again, we also believe that when Jesus returns, God will bring back with him the believers who have died.” 1 Thessalonians 4:14, NLT

After a night without sleep, Paul needed strength from God, too. Luke writes, “Paul went by land to Assos, where he had arranged for us to join him, while we traveled by ship.” Acts 20:13, NLT Ellen G. White describes what that walk gave Paul. “This gave him a short season for meditation and prayer.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 391 · AA 391.4, excerpt The young man who fell and the apostle who prayed both depended on the same God.

Perhaps you’re the one who feels worn out today. Jesus invites you to come to Him. “Come to me, all of you who are weary and carry heavy burdens, and I will give you rest.” Matthew 11:28, NLT, excerpt Isaiah also speaks to the weary. “But those who trust in the LORD will find new strength.” Isaiah 40:31, NLT, excerpt Or perhaps you know someone worn down by exhaustion, grief, or hardship. Think of Paul leaving the upper room. Go to them and help carry what weighs on them. “Share each other’s burdens, and in this way obey the law of Christ.” Galatians 6:2, NLT

God’s love goes down to the one who fell, and His life brings us home in the morning.

Reflect

  1. When have I felt too tired to keep going, even among God’s people? What would it look like to bring that weariness to Jesus?
  2. Who in my church or family is worn down by exhaustion, grief, or hardship? What can I do this week to help carry their burden?
  3. When I share in the Lord’s Supper or pray with other believers, how can I keep Jesus’ sacrifice and His return at the heart of my hope?

Key Takeaways

  1. Meet weakness with compassion. Eutychus came to hear God’s Word, and Luke tells of his fall without a word of blame. Acts 20:9 · quoted above
  2. Love goes down to the one who fell. Paul went down to Eutychus, and God restored the young man to life. Acts 20:10 · quoted above
  3. The night ended in comfort and hope. The believers shared the Lord’s Supper, and by dawn Eutychus was on his way home alive. Acts 20:11–12 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Heavenly Father, thank You for seeing us when our strength gives out. You know when we are tired, grieving, or afraid, and You stay near. Lord Jesus, thank You for coming down to us, giving Your life on the cross, and rising again. You are the resurrection and the life. When we are weary, help us come to You for rest. When others fall, move us to go to them and help carry their burdens. As we share in Your Supper, keep Your sacrifice at the center of our hope until You come again. Turn our nights of alarm into mornings of comfort. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
