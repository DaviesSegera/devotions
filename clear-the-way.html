Clear the Way

Matthew 3:1–17 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-29

John the Baptist calls people to turn to God and prepares the way for Jesus. Reflect on repentance, humility, and the Father’s welcome in His Son.

Key Verse
“He is a voice shouting in the wilderness, ‘Prepare the way for the Lord’s coming! Clear the road for him!’”
Matthew 3:3, NLT, excerpt

When we are expecting an important guest, we get ready and make room. Before Jesus began His public ministry, God prepared the way by sending a messenger.

Matthew introduces him: “In those days John the Baptist came to the Judean wilderness and began preaching. His message was, ‘Repent of your sins and turn to God, for the Kingdom of Heaven is near.’ The prophet Isaiah was speaking about John when he said, ‘He is a voice shouting in the wilderness, “Prepare the way for the Lord’s coming! Clear the road for him!”’” Matthew 3:1–3, NLT

Isaiah’s prophecy goes on to describe how that road would be made ready: “Fill in the valleys, and level the mountains and hills. Straighten the curves, and smooth out the rough places.” Isaiah 40:4, NLT Ellen G. White explains the custom behind this picture:

“Anciently, when a king journeyed through the less frequented parts of his dominion, a company of men was sent ahead of the royal chariot to level the steep places and to fill up the hollows, that the king might travel in safety and without hindrance. This custom is employed by the prophet to illustrate the work of the gospel.… When the Spirit of God, with its marvelous awakening power, touches the soul, it abases human pride.”The Desire of Ages, p. 135 · DA 135.1, excerpt

To “abase” something is to bring it low. The road John came to prepare ran through human hearts, where pride needed to be brought down and the downcast lifted up.

Before Jesus came, God sent someone to help people make room for Him.

Crowds responded to John’s call: “People from Jerusalem and from all of Judea and all over the Jordan Valley went out to see and hear John. And when they confessed their sins, he baptized them in the Jordan River.” Matthew 3:5–6, NLT Repentance means more than feeling sorry. It calls us to turn away from sin and “turn to God.”

John’s message was often stern, but its purpose was not to crush people. Ellen G. White describes the mercy behind God’s warnings:

“God does not send messengers to flatter the sinner.… Then the hand that has humbled in the dust, lifts up the penitent.”The Desire of Ages, p. 104 · DA 104.1, excerpt

A “penitent” is someone who is truly sorry for sin. The God who exposes our sin is the same God who raises us up.

Not everyone came with an open heart. Matthew records: “But when he saw many Pharisees and Sadducees coming to watch him baptize, he denounced them. ‘You brood of snakes!’ he exclaimed. ‘Who warned you to flee the coming wrath? Prove by the way you live that you have repented of your sins and turned to God. Don’t just say to each other, “We’re safe, for we are descendants of Abraham.” That means nothing, for I tell you, God can create children of Abraham from these very stones.’” Matthew 3:7–9, NLT

The Pharisees and Sadducees were respected religious leaders. Some regarded their descent from Abraham, the father of the Jewish nation, as proof that they were safe with God. John warned that belonging to the right family or group cannot replace a changed heart. Ellen G. White applies the same warning to us: “How often we misinterpret God’s blessings, and flatter ourselves that we are favored on account of some goodness in us!” The Desire of Ages, p. 106 · DA 106.3, excerpt

What does a changed life look like? When people asked John what they should do, his answer was practical: “The crowds asked, ‘What should we do?’ John replied, ‘If you have two shirts, give one to the poor. If you have food, share it with those who are hungry.’” Luke 3:10–11, NLT Ellen G. White draws out the lesson in John’s image of a tree:

“Not by its name, but by its fruit, is the value of a tree determined. If the fruit is worthless, the name cannot save the tree from destruction.”The Desire of Ages, p. 107 · DA 107.1, excerpt

True repentance turns us back to God and changes the way we treat others.

Although John drew large crowds, he kept directing attention away from himself: “I baptize with water those who repent of their sins and turn to God. But someone is coming soon who is greater than I am—so much greater that I’m not worthy even to be his slave and carry his sandals. He will baptize you with the Holy Spirit and with fire.” Matthew 3:11, NLT

John’s words carry both promise and warning. Ellen G. White saw God’s power to cleanse in this image of fire: “In all who submit to His power the Spirit of God will consume sin. But if men cling to sin, they become identified with it.” The Desire of Ages, p. 107 · DA 107.4, excerpt To be “identified with” sin is to become one with it.

Later, John’s disciples were troubled as people began leaving him to follow Jesus. John replied: “You yourselves know how plainly I told you, ‘I am not the Messiah. I am only here to prepare the way for him.’… He must become greater and greater, and I must become less and less.” John 3:28, 30, NLT, excerpt Ellen G. White comments on John’s joy in stepping aside:

“He sought not to attract men to himself, but to lift their thoughts higher and still higher, until they should rest upon the Lamb of God. He himself had been only a voice, a cry in the wilderness.”The Desire of Ages, p. 179 · DA 179.4, excerpt

We do not need to be the centre of attention. At home, at church or at work, our calling is to help others see Jesus more clearly.

The more clearly we see Jesus, the less we need to be noticed ourselves.

Jesus then came to John with an unexpected request: “Then Jesus went from Galilee to the Jordan River to be baptized by John. But John tried to talk him out of it. ‘I am the one who needs to be baptized by you,’ he said, ‘so why are you coming to me?’ But Jesus said, ‘It should be done, for we must carry out all that God requires.’ So John agreed to baptize him.” Matthew 3:13–15, NLT

John’s hesitation is understandable. His baptism was for people who confessed their sins, and Jesus had no sins to confess. Ellen G. White explains why Jesus insisted:

“Jesus did not receive baptism as a confession of guilt on His own account. He identified Himself with sinners, taking the steps that we are to take, and doing the work that we must do. His life of suffering and patient endurance after His baptism was also an example to us.”The Desire of Ages, p. 111 · DA 111.2

Christians from many traditions have understood this moment in a similar way. The Catechism of the Catholic Church, for example, says that Jesus “allows himself to be numbered among sinners.” The apostle Paul shows where this path would lead: “For God made Christ, who never sinned, to be the offering for our sin, so that we could be made right with God through Christ.” 2 Corinthians 5:21, NLT At the Jordan, Jesus stood among sinners. At the cross, He would carry their sin.

Jesus stood beside sinners so that sinners could be made right with God.

Heaven answered at once: “After his baptism, as Jesus came up out of the water, the heavens were opened and he saw the Spirit of God descending like a dove and settling on him. And a voice from heaven said, ‘This is my dearly loved Son, who brings me great joy.’” Matthew 3:16–17, NLT In Matthew’s account, the Father declares His love for His Son and His delight in Him before Jesus has preached a single sermon.

Ellen G. White saw good news for us in these words:

“And the word that was spoken to Jesus at the Jordan, ‘This is My beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased,’ embraces humanity. God spoke to Jesus as our representative. With all our sins and weaknesses, we are not cast aside as worthless.… The voice which spoke to Jesus says to every believing soul, This is My beloved child, in whom I am well pleased.”The Desire of Ages, p. 113 · DA 113.1, excerpt

A “representative” is someone who stands in the place of others. We are welcomed not because we have earned it, but because we belong to Jesus.

This is where John’s preparation led. The Gospel of John records how he later pointed to Jesus: “The next day John saw Jesus coming toward him and said, ‘Look! The Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!’” John 1:29, NLT In the Old Testament, lambs were offered as sacrifices for sin. John’s stern preaching about sin pointed people to the One who would take sin away.

What needs to be cleared from your heart today? It may be pride, discouragement or a quiet confidence in your own goodness. Bring it to Jesus. Turn to God, let that change show in your life, and point others to Him. The One who stood beside sinners at the Jordan welcomes all who come to Him.

The road John prepared leads to Jesus, the Lamb of God, and to the Father’s welcome in Him.

Reflect

  1. Is there pride, discouragement or a habit in my heart that God wants to clear away so that I can welcome Jesus more fully?
  2. Where might I be relying on my background, my church or my reputation rather than allowing God to change my life?
  3. How can I point someone towards Jesus this week rather than drawing attention to myself?

Key Takeaways

  1. Repentance changes how we live. Our actions show that we have turned to God; our name or background cannot do so. Matthew 3:8 · quoted above
  2. Our calling is to point to Jesus. Like John, we prepare the way for Christ when we let Him become greater in our lives. John 3:30 · quoted above
  3. Jesus stood with sinners, and in Him we are welcomed. The sinless Son was baptised among sinners, and the Father declared His love for Him. Matthew 3:16–17 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Heavenly Father, thank You for preparing the way for Jesus and for calling us to turn back to You. Humble our hearts and lift us up when we feel discouraged. Forgive our sins and let our lives show the change You have made. Keep us from seeking attention for ourselves, and help us point others to Your Son. Thank You that Jesus stood beside sinners and became the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world. Help us rest in Your love because we belong to Him. In Jesus’ name we pray. Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
