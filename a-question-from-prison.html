A Question From Prison: Faith That Brings Its Doubts to Jesus

Matthew 11:2–6 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-07

From prison, John the Baptist brings his question to Jesus. Reflect on honest doubt, Christ’s gentle answer, and trusting His presence when circumstances remain unchanged.

Faith & trustHope & encouragementWaiting on GodPrayer

Key Verse
“Jesus told them, ‘Go back to John and tell him what you have heard and seen—the blind see, the lame walk, those with leprosy are cured, the deaf hear, the dead are raised to life, and the Good News is being preached to the poor.’”
Matthew 11:4–5 (NLT)

Have you ever served God with your whole heart, only to find yourself somewhere you never expected to be? Perhaps you prayed, worked, and trusted, yet the door remained closed. John the Baptist knew the weight of such disappointment. From a prison cell, this faithful messenger of God sent Jesus an honest question.

John lived to point people to Jesus. When he saw Jesus approaching, he declared, “Look! The Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!” John 1:29, NLT, excerpt

Then John’s public ministry came to an abrupt halt. “John also publicly criticized Herod Antipas, the ruler of Galilee, for marrying Herodias, his brother’s wife, and for many other wrongs he had done.” Luke 3:19, NLT “So Herod put John in prison, adding this sin to his many others.” Luke 3:20, NLT The man who had preached under the open sky now sat confined behind stone walls. Ellen G. White describes those days:

“The life of John had been one of active labor, and the gloom and inaction of his prison life weighed heavily upon him. As week after week passed, bringing no change, despondency and doubt crept over him.”The Desire of Ages, p. 214 · DA 214.2, excerpt

Despondency is a sense of deep discouragement that can settle over us when nothing seems to change. John’s disciples brought him news of Jesus, but Ellen White tells us that their visits also brought painful questions: “But they questioned why, if this new teacher was the Messiah, He did nothing to effect John’s release.” The Desire of Ages, p. 214 · DA 214.2, excerpt The Messiah was the Saviour and King whom God had promised. If Jesus was that King, why did His messenger remain in prison?

Even faithful servants of God can feel their hearts grow heavy in the dark.

Part of John’s struggle lay in his expectations. He had preached about the One who would come after him: “He is ready to separate the chaff from the wheat with his winnowing fork. Then he will clean up the threshing area, gathering the wheat into his barn but burning the chaff with never-ending fire.” Matthew 3:12, NLT A winnowing fork was a farm tool used to toss grain into the air, allowing the wind to carry away the husks. John expected the Messiah to sweep evil away quickly.

Ellen White explains why Jesus’ quiet ministry left John puzzled: “Like the Saviour’s disciples, John the Baptist did not understand the nature of Christ’s kingdom. He expected Jesus to take the throne of David; and as time passed, and the Saviour made no claim to kingly authority, John became perplexed and troubled.” The Desire of Ages, p. 215 · DA 215.2, excerpt Jesus continued to heal and teach, while Herod still ruled and John remained in prison.

So John took his question to Jesus. “John the Baptist, who was in prison, heard about all the things the Messiah was doing. So he sent his disciples to ask Jesus,” Matthew 11:2, NLT “Are you the Messiah we’ve been expecting, or should we keep looking for someone else?” Matthew 11:3, NLT

Yet John still held on to his faith. Ellen White writes, “But the Baptist did not surrender his faith in Christ.” The Desire of Ages, p. 216 · DA 216.3, excerpt She describes how he responded to his troubled thoughts:

“John would not discuss his doubts and anxieties with his companions. He determined to send a message of inquiry to Jesus. This he entrusted to two of his disciples, hoping that an interview with the Saviour would confirm their faith, and bring assurance to their brethren. And he longed for some word from Christ spoken directly for himself.”The Desire of Ages, p. 216 · DA 216.4

Christians have understood John’s question in different ways. Many early church teachers, including John Chrysostom, believed that he asked only for his disciples’ sake. Ellen White describes both his own distress and his desire to strengthen his disciples’ faith. In either case, the question went to the right Person.

John did not hide his question or let it pull him away from Jesus. He brought it straight to Him.

Jesus did not answer immediately. Luke records what John’s messengers witnessed as they waited: “At that very time, Jesus cured many people of their diseases, illnesses, and evil spirits, and he restored sight to many who were blind.” Luke 7:21, NLT

Then Jesus replied: “Go back to John and tell him what you have heard and seen—the blind see, the lame walk, those with leprosy are cured, the deaf hear, the dead are raised to life, and the Good News is being preached to the poor.” Matthew 11:4–5, NLT, excerpt His answer pointed John to the evidence of His mercy.

His words echoed promises John knew well. Isaiah had written of God’s coming: “And when he comes, he will open the eyes of the blind and unplug the ears of the deaf. The lame will leap like a deer, and those who cannot speak will sing for joy!” Isaiah 35:5–6, NLT, excerpt Ellen White explains the significance of what the messengers witnessed: “The evidence of His divinity was seen in its adaptation to the needs of suffering humanity.” The Desire of Ages, p. 217 · DA 217.1, excerpt Jesus revealed who He was by caring for those who were suffering.

Ellen White continues, “The disciples bore the message, and it was enough.” The Desire of Ages, p. 217 · DA 217.2, excerpt She tells us that John recalled Isaiah’s prophecy of the Messiah: “The Spirit of the Sovereign LORD is upon me, for the LORD has anointed me to bring good news to the poor. He has sent me to comfort the brokenhearted and to proclaim that captives will be released and prisoners will be freed.” Isaiah 61:1, NLT Yet Jesus’ message offered no promise that John’s own prison door would open. It revealed the kind of King Jesus is. Ellen White explains:

“So Jesus was to do His work, not with the clash of arms and the overturning of thrones and kingdoms, but through speaking to the hearts of men by a life of mercy and self-sacrifice.”The Desire of Ages, p. 217 · DA 217.2, excerpt

Jesus often answers our questions in much the same way. He may leave some mysteries unresolved, yet He gives us reason to trust Him. Ellen White writes, “God never asks us to believe, without giving sufficient evidence upon which to base our faith.” Steps to Christ, p. 105 · SC 105.2, excerpt She adds, “Yet God has never removed the possibility of doubt. Our faith must rest upon evidence, not demonstration.” Steps to Christ, p. 105 · SC 105.2, excerpt Here, “demonstration” refers to proof so complete that no trust would be needed.

Jesus ended His message with a blessing: “God blesses those who do not fall away because of me.” Matthew 11:6, NLT, excerpt To fall away is to stumble over Jesus and turn from Him because He has not acted as we expected.

Quoting these words from an older Bible translation, Ellen White describes how John received them:

“The Saviour’s words, ‘Blessed is he, whosoever shall find none occasion of stumbling in Me,’ were a gentle reproof to John. It was not lost upon him. Understanding more clearly now the nature of Christ’s mission, he yielded himself to God for life or for death, as should best serve the interests of the cause he loved.”The Desire of Ages, p. 218 · DA 218.2

Jesus answered John with His works, His Word, and a gentle call to keep trusting.

As soon as the messengers had left, Jesus spoke to the crowd about John. “What kind of man did you go into the wilderness to see? Was he a weak reed, swayed by every breath of wind?” Matthew 11:7, NLT, excerpt The answer was no. Jesus went on to say, “I tell you the truth, of all who have ever lived, none is greater than John the Baptist.” Matthew 11:11, NLT, excerpt

Jesus did not let one hard question define His faithful servant. Ellen White writes, “The Saviour’s heart went out in sympathy to the faithful witness now buried in Herod’s dungeon. He would not leave the people to conclude that God had forsaken John, or that his faith had failed in the day of trial.” The Desire of Ages, p. 218 · DA 218.3, excerpt

Matthew later uses Isaiah’s words to describe Jesus: “He will not crush the weakest reed or put out a flickering candle.” Matthew 12:20, NLT, excerpt Jesus treats struggling faith with gentleness. His example should shape the way we care for others: “And you must show mercy to those whose faith is wavering.” Jude 22, NLT

One question asked in the dark did not erase a lifetime of faithfulness.

John was never released. At Herod’s birthday feast, a rash promise led to his death: “So John was beheaded in the prison,” Matthew 14:10, NLT In their grief, his followers turned to the One their teacher had pointed them towards: “Later, John’s disciples came for his body and buried it. Then they went and told Jesus what had happened.” Matthew 14:12, NLT

Why did Jesus not set him free? Ellen White acknowledges that we cannot fully explain his suffering: “The mystery of this dark providence our human vision cannot penetrate; but it can never shake our confidence in God when we remember that John was but a sharer in the sufferings of Christ.” The Desire of Ages, p. 223 · DA 223.4, excerpt Providence means God’s care and guidance through the events of our lives.

Some of those events remain beyond our understanding. Yet Ellen White offers this assurance: “Though no miraculous deliverance was granted John, he was not forsaken.” The Desire of Ages, p. 224 · DA 224.4, excerpt Jesus later gave His followers this promise: “And be sure of this: I am with you always, even to the end of the age.” Matthew 28:20, NLT, excerpt

Jesus Himself walked the road of suffering before us. Ellen White says of John, “He, the forerunner, was but drinking of the cup which Christ Himself must drain to its dregs.” The Desire of Ages, p. 218 · DA 218.1, excerpt Because Jesus has suffered, we can bring Him our questions with confidence. “This High Priest of ours understands our weaknesses, for he faced all of the same testings we do, yet he did not sin.” Hebrews 4:15, NLT “So let us come boldly to the throne of our gracious God. There we will receive his mercy,” Hebrews 4:16, NLT, excerpt

We cannot yet see the whole story, but Ellen White reminds us, “God never leads His children otherwise than they would choose to be led, if they could see the end from the beginning, and discern the glory of the purpose which they are fulfilling as co-workers with Him.” The Desire of Ages, p. 224 · DA 224.5, excerpt

John’s witness lived on after his death. At the place where he had first baptized, people later said: “John didn’t perform miraculous signs,” they remarked to one another, “but everything he said about this man has come true.” John 10:41, NLT, excerpt Perhaps you, too, feel imprisoned by illness, a long wait, loss, or a prayer that seems unanswered. Bring your questions to Jesus. Look again at what He has done, and keep trusting Him.

When Jesus does not change our circumstances, He still gives us enough light to keep trusting Him.

Reflect

  1. What question about God’s ways have I been carrying quietly? Am I ready to bring it honestly to Jesus?
  2. When God does not act as I expected, where can I see evidence of His mercy?
  3. Who around me is struggling to believe, and how can I show them patience and mercy this week?

Key Takeaways

  1. Bring your questions to Jesus. John was troubled in prison, yet he took his question directly to Jesus. Matthew 11:2–3 · quoted above
  2. Jesus answers with evidence and a call to trust. He pointed John to His works of mercy and the promises of Scripture, then blessed those who do not fall away because of Him. Matthew 11:4–6 · quoted above
  3. Not rescued, yet not forsaken. John was never released, yet Jesus honoured his faithfulness. His promise to be with His people still stands. Matthew 28:20 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Heavenly Father, thank You for welcoming our honest questions. When our hearts grow heavy and our prayers seem unanswered, help us draw near to Jesus. Open our eyes to the evidence of Your mercy. Teach us to be patient and kind towards those whose faith is wavering. When our circumstances remain unchanged, give us enough light to keep trusting You. Thank You for Jesus, who understands our weakness and has promised to be with us always. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
