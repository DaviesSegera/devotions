Show Me Your Glory

Exodus 33:12–34:9 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-26

Moses asks to see God’s glory, and God reveals His goodness, compassion, and faithful love. Reflect on seeking God’s presence, knowing His heart through Jesus, and being changed by His Spirit.

PrayerFaith & trustForgiveness & graceHope & encouragement

Key Verse
“Then show me your glorious presence.” The Lord replied, “I will make all my goodness pass before you.”
Exodus 33:18–19, NLT, excerpt

Most of our prayers ask God to meet a need: to heal, to guide, or to provide. He welcomes these prayers. Yet in Exodus, Moses prayed a different kind of prayer: he asked to see God Himself.

His request came at one of the darkest moments in Israel’s history. While Moses was on Mount Sinai receiving God’s law, the people made a golden calf and worshipped it. Afterwards, God gave them a stern message: “But I will not travel among you, for you are a stubborn and rebellious people. If I did, I would surely destroy you along the way.” Exodus 33:3, NLT, excerpt Moses still had to lead them through the wilderness, but now he faced the journey without the assurance of God’s presence.

Ellen G. White describes the lesson Moses had learnt. To “prevail with the people” means to lead and influence them effectively: “But he had learned that in order to prevail with the people, he must have help from God.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 327 · PP 327.3, excerpt Moses therefore prayed: “If you don’t personally go with us, don’t make us leave this place. How will anyone know that you look favorably on me—on me and on your people—if you don’t go with us? For your presence among us sets your people and me apart from all other people on the earth.” Exodus 33:15–16, NLT

Moses would rather remain in the desert with God than enter the Promised Land without Him. He was praying for his people as well as for himself.

Moses knew that God’s presence mattered more than any place God could lead him.

God promised to go with them, yet Moses asked for more: “Moses responded, ‘Then show me your glorious presence.’” Exodus 33:18, NLT In the Bible, God’s “glory” speaks of His greatness and splendour—the fullness of who He is. Ellen G. White highlights the boldness of this request. She quotes the King James Version, in which “I beseech Thee” means “I earnestly ask You”:

“Every prayer had been answered, but he thirsted for greater tokens of God’s favor. He now made a request that no human being had ever made before: ‘I beseech Thee, show me Thy glory.’”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 328 · PP 328.2, excerpt

God answered with grace: “The Lord replied, ‘I will make all my goodness pass before you, and I will call out my name, Yahweh, before you. For I will show mercy to anyone I choose, and I will show compassion to anyone I choose. But you may not look directly at my face, for no one may see me and live.’” Exodus 33:19–20, NLT Yahweh is God’s personal name, usually rendered “the Lord” in the NLT. Moses asked to see God’s glory, and God promised to reveal His goodness. He did not rebuke Moses for asking so much.

God welcomes our longing to know Him more deeply. He answers that longing with His goodness.

God then explained how He would reveal Himself: “The Lord continued, ‘Look, stand near me on this rock. As my glorious presence passes by, I will hide you in the crevice of the rock and cover you with my hand until I have passed by. Then I will remove my hand and let you see me from behind. But my face will not be seen.’” Exodus 33:21–23, NLT

Earlier in the chapter, Exodus describes the closeness Moses already shared with God: “Inside the Tent of Meeting, the Lord would speak to Moses face to face, as one speaks to a friend.” Exodus 33:11, NLT, excerpt Moses knew God as a close friend, yet no human being could survive the full sight of God’s face. Seeing God “from behind” describes, in human terms, an experience beyond ordinary human sight. Moses truly saw God’s glory, but only as much as he could bear. Ellen G. White describes the scene with wonder, quoting Job:

“…the hand that made the world, that hand that ‘removeth the mountains, and they know not’ (Job 9:5), took this creature of the dust, this mighty man of faith, and placed him in a cleft of the rock, while the glory of God and all His goodness passed before him.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 328 · PP 328.3, excerpt

The hand that moves mountains was gentle enough to shelter one man in a narrow opening in the rock.

God shows us as much of Himself as we can bear, and He shelters us while He does.

Early the next morning, Moses climbed Mount Sinai alone. The Lord came down in a cloud and fulfilled His promise: “The Lord passed in front of Moses, calling out, ‘Yahweh! The Lord! The God of compassion and mercy! I am slow to anger and filled with unfailing love and faithfulness. I lavish unfailing love to a thousand generations. I forgive iniquity, rebellion, and sin. But I do not excuse the guilty.’” Exodus 34:6–7, NLT, excerpt

God revealed His character to Moses. His mercy does not treat sin as harmless; He does not “excuse the guilty.” The passage goes on to warn that sin’s harmful effects reach through families for generations. Yet His love reaches “a thousand generations.”

Ellen G. White draws the same conclusion in Steps to Christ:

“The word of God reveals His character. He Himself has declared His infinite love and pity. When Moses prayed, ‘Show me Thy glory,’ the Lord answered, ‘I will make all My goodness pass before thee.’ Exodus 33:18, 19. This is His glory.”Steps to Christ, p. 10 · SC 10.2, excerpt

The heart of God’s glory is His goodness: His mercy, patience, and faithful love.

Moses saw God only “from behind.” Yet John’s Gospel announces something greater: “So the Word became human and made his home among us. He was full of unfailing love and faithfulness. And we have seen his glory, the glory of the Father’s one and only Son.” John 1:14, NLT The NLT describes Jesus with the very words God used of Himself on Mount Sinai: “unfailing love and faithfulness.”

John goes on: “No one has ever seen God. But the unique One, who is himself God, is near to the Father’s heart. He has revealed God to us.” John 1:18, NLT

Reflecting on Jesus’ kindness to the sick, the poor, and little children, Ellen G. White concludes: “Such is the character of Christ as revealed in His life. This is the character of God.” Steps to Christ, p. 12 · SC 12.2, excerpt As we watch Jesus welcome sinners and heal those who are hurting, we see God’s goodness pass before us. She also sees in the rock that sheltered Moses a picture of Christ, who shelters us. “Riven” means split open; here she has the cross in mind:

“It was when Moses was hidden in the cleft of the rock that he beheld the glory of God. It is when we hide in the riven Rock that Christ will cover us with His own pierced hand, and we shall hear what the Lord saith unto His servants.”Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 162 · COL 162.3, excerpt

Through Jesus Christ, we come to know the God whose face Moses could not see.

When Moses came down from the mountain, his encounter with God had left a visible mark. Exodus tells us that “he wasn’t aware that his face had become radiant because he had spoken to the Lord.” Exodus 34:29, NLT, excerpt He had simply been in God’s presence.

Paul shows how this applies to everyone who trusts in Christ: “So all of us who have had that veil removed can see and reflect the glory of the Lord. And the Lord—who is the Spirit—makes us more and more like him as we are changed into his glorious image.” 2 Corinthians 3:18, NLT Our faces may not shine, but as we spend time with Jesus, His mercy and love become more visible in our lives. The longing behind Moses’ prayer can be ours too:

“It is our privilege to reach higher and still higher for clearer revealings of the character of God. When Moses prayed, ‘I beseech Thee, show me Thy glory,’ the Lord did not rebuke him, but He granted his prayer.”The Ministry of Healing, p. 464 · MH 464.5, excerpt

Today, make Moses’ prayer your own: “Lord, show me Your glory.” Look for His answer in the life of Jesus. Read a Gospel story slowly, asking what it reveals about God’s heart. One day, the limit Moses faced will be lifted. Of God’s people in the age to come, Scripture promises: “And they will see his face, and his name will be written on their foreheads.” Revelation 22:4, NLT

Ask to see God’s glory, look for it in Jesus, and let His goodness change you.

Reflect

  1. Do my prayers focus mainly on what I need, or do they also express a desire to know God more deeply?
  2. How might recognising God’s glory in His mercy and faithful love change the way I see Him?
  3. Which Gospel story could I read slowly this week, and what might it teach me about God’s character?

Key Takeaways

  1. God’s glory is seen in His goodness. In answer to Moses’ request to see His glory, God revealed His compassion and unfailing love. Exodus 34:6–7, excerpt · quoted above
  2. In Jesus, we see what God is like. The Son reveals the God whom Moses could not fully see. John 1:18 · quoted above
  3. Time in God’s presence changes us. As we look to Jesus, His Spirit shapes us more and more into His likeness. 2 Corinthians 3:18 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Lord God, like Moses, we ask You to show us Your glory. Thank You for revealing Yourself as compassionate and merciful, slow to anger and filled with unfailing love. Thank You for showing us Your heart in Jesus, who gave Himself for us. Shelter us in Him and draw us close to You. Transform us by Your Spirit, so that others may see Your goodness in our lives. Go with us and with those we love. In Jesus’ name we pray. Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
