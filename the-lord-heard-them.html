The Lord Heard Them

Numbers 12:1–16 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-28

Miriam and Aaron speak against Moses, and the Lord hears them. Reflect on jealousy hidden in criticism, responding with humility, and praying for those who have hurt us as God works to restore His people.

Key Verse
“So Moses cried out to the Lord, ‘O God, I beg you, please heal her!’”
Numbers 12:13, NLT

Criticism hurts most when it comes from people we love. Moses had already borne the complaints of a whole nation in the wilderness. Now he faced criticism from his own family.

The book of Numbers records it plainly: “While they were at Hazeroth, Miriam and Aaron criticized Moses because he had married a Cushite woman. They said, ‘Has the Lord spoken only through Moses? Hasn’t he spoken through us, too?’ But the Lord heard them.” Numbers 12:1–2, NLT

Miriam and Aaron were Moses’ sister and brother. They found fault with his marriage, but beneath their complaint lay a question of status: why should Moses be treated as more important than they were? Then comes a quiet but sobering reminder: the Lord heard them.

Who was this “Cushite woman”? Ellen G. White comments:

“Though called a ‘Cushite woman’ (Numbers 12:1, R.V.), the wife of Moses was a Midianite, and thus a descendant of Abraham.… Though not an Israelite, Zipporah was a worshiper of the true God.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 383 · PP 383.3, excerpt

Moses had married Zipporah years earlier in Midian. Yet the complaint about her was only part of the story.

No word spoken about another person is ever truly private. The Lord hears.

What lay beneath the complaint? The previous chapter tells how God shared the burden of leadership by placing His Spirit on seventy elders of Israel. When two of them prophesied in the camp, Joshua wanted Moses to stop them. Moses answered, “Are you jealous for my sake? I wish that all the Lord’s people were prophets and that the Lord would put his Spirit upon them all!” Numbers 11:29, NLT, excerpt Moses was willing to share the privilege of serving God. Ellen G. White describes a different response in his brother and sister:

“In the appointment of the seventy elders Miriam and Aaron had not been consulted, and their jealousy was excited against Moses.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 382 · PP 382.4, excerpt

Miriam had served God faithfully. After Israel crossed the Red Sea, “Miriam the prophet, Aaron’s sister, took a tambourine and led all the women as they played their tambourines and danced.” Exodus 15:20, NLT, excerpt She was gifted and honoured. Yet even a faithful heart can grow resentful when it feels overlooked. Ellen G. White describes how that disappointment shaped Miriam’s treatment of Moses’ wife:

“Yielding to the spirit of dissatisfaction, Miriam found cause of complaint in events that God had especially overruled. The marriage of Moses had been displeasing to her. That he should choose a woman of another nation, instead of taking a wife from among the Hebrews, was an offense to her family and national pride. Zipporah was treated with ill-disguised contempt.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 383 · PP 383.2

Here, “overruled” means that God had guided these events for good. In Ellen G. White’s account, wounded pride led to contempt for a woman from another nation. James describes where such feelings lead: “For wherever there is jealousy and selfish ambition, there you will find disorder and evil of every kind.” James 3:16, NLT Jealousy rarely names itself. It often comes out as criticism of someone’s choices, background or family.

When we feel overlooked, we need to ask God to show us what is really in our hearts.

How did Moses respond? Scripture records no reply from him, but gives us this glimpse of his character: “(Now Moses was very humble—more humble than any other person on earth.)” Numbers 12:3, NLT Ellen G. White puts it simply:

“Their accusations were borne by Moses in uncomplaining silence.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 384 · PP 384.2, excerpt

Moses did not have to defend himself; God spoke on his behalf. The Lord called all three to the Tabernacle, the sacred tent where He met with His people, and addressed Aaron and Miriam: “Now listen to what I say: If there were prophets among you, I, the Lord, would reveal myself in visions. I would speak to them in dreams. But not with my servant Moses. Of all my house, he is the one I trust. I speak to him face to face, clearly, and not in riddles! He sees the Lord as he is. So why were you not afraid to criticize my servant Moses?” Numbers 12:6–8, NLT, excerpt

God did not dismiss Miriam and Aaron’s prophetic gift. Ellen G. White explains the distinction:

“Their claim to the prophetic gift was not denied; God might have spoken to them in visions and dreams. But to Moses, whom the Lord Himself declared ‘faithful in all Mine house,’ a nearer communion had been granted.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 384 · PP 384.3, excerpt

“Communion” means close fellowship with God. Moses’ quiet trust points us towards Jesus. Peter writes of Him: “He did not retaliate when he was insulted, nor threaten revenge when he suffered. He left his case in the hands of God, who always judges fairly.” 1 Peter 2:23, NLT

Those who are humble can leave their defence in God’s hands.

The consequences were sobering: “The Lord was very angry with them, and he departed. As the cloud moved from above the Tabernacle, there stood Miriam, her skin as white as snow from leprosy.” Numbers 12:9–10, NLT The Hebrew word translated “leprosy” can describe various skin diseases. Miriam’s illness made her unclean and excluded her from community life. Scripture does not explain why only Miriam was struck, though she is named first. Ellen G. White observes that “Miriam had been the instigator in this evil work.” The Review and Herald, August 14, 1900, par. 5, excerpt

Aaron immediately turned to Moses: “Oh, my master! Please don’t punish us for this sin we have so foolishly committed.” Numbers 12:11, NLT, excerpt Moses, the brother they had criticised, now prayed for his sister:

“So Moses cried out to the Lord, ‘O God, I beg you, please heal her!’” Numbers 12:13, NLT Even in his hurt, Moses prayed for his sister. Ellen G. White points to the hand of Jesus Himself in Miriam’s healing:

“And the Lord heard him. The same Saviour who hundreds of years later said to the leper, ‘I will, be thou clean,’ removed the stroke.”The Review and Herald, August 14, 1900, par. 5, excerpt

Here, “the stroke” refers to Miriam’s illness. Her words recall the day when a man with leprosy knelt before Jesus: “Jesus reached out and touched him. ‘I am willing,’ he said. ‘Be healed!’ And instantly the leprosy disappeared.” Matthew 8:3, NLT The Lord who heard Miriam’s criticism also heard Moses’ prayer. That prayer points us towards Jesus, who still prays for us when we fail: “Therefore he is able, once and forever, to save those who come to God through him. He lives forever to intercede with God on their behalf.” Hebrews 7:25, NLT To “intercede” is to plead with God on someone else’s behalf.

Moses prayed for the sister who had wronged him, and Jesus still prays for us.

Miriam was healed, but she did not return to the camp immediately. God said, “So keep her outside the camp for seven days, and after that she may be accepted back.” Numbers 12:14, NLT, excerpt The discipline was real, yet it had a clear end. What follows is one of the gentlest lines in the chapter:

“So Miriam was kept outside the camp for seven days, and the people waited until she was brought back before they traveled again.” Numbers 12:15, NLT The whole nation waited for Miriam. Ellen G. White explains why:

“In respect for her high position, and in grief at the blow that had fallen upon her, the whole company abode in Hazeroth, awaiting her return.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 384 · PP 384.3, excerpt

Miriam’s failure did not erase her calling. Centuries later, God still spoke of her with honour: “For I brought you out of Egypt and redeemed you from slavery. I sent Moses, Aaron, and Miriam to help you.” Micah 6:4, NLT God’s purpose in disciplining her was to restore her.

God’s discipline is meant to bring us back to Him.

This story speaks both to those who have been criticised and to those tempted to criticise. Ellen G. White gives a sober warning:

“It should not be regarded as a light thing to speak evil of others or to make ourselves judges of their motives or actions.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 385 · PP 385.2, excerpt

In Christ, we are welcome in God’s house, whatever our nation or family background: “There is no longer Jew or Gentile, slave or free, male and female. For you are all one in Christ Jesus.” Galatians 3:28, NLT

Perhaps you have felt overlooked, and your disappointment has begun to show in harsh words. Bring it honestly to Jesus before resentment takes root. Or perhaps you are the one who has been criticised. You can entrust your reputation to God and pray for those who have hurt you.

The Lord hears every word we speak, and He also hears every prayer for healing.

Reflect

  1. When I criticise someone, could jealousy or the pain of feeling overlooked be shaping my words?
  2. Where am I struggling to entrust my reputation to God?
  3. Whose words have hurt me, and how can I begin to pray for that person as Moses prayed for Miriam?

Key Takeaways

  1. Jealousy often hides behind criticism. Miriam and Aaron objected to Moses’ marriage, but jealousy over their own standing lay beneath their complaint. The Lord heard them. Numbers 12:1–2 · quoted above
  2. The humble can pray for those who hurt them. Moses left his defence to God and pleaded for his sister’s healing. Numbers 12:13 · quoted above
  3. God disciplines in order to restore. The people waited for Miriam’s return, and God still honoured her calling. Numbers 12:15 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Lord, You hear every word we speak. Forgive us when disappointment turns into criticism, and help us to see what is really in our hearts. Keep us from looking down on others because of their nationality or family background. When others speak against us, teach us to respond with humility and to entrust our reputation to You. Give us grace to pray for those who have hurt us. Thank You for Jesus, who healed the sick with a touch and still intercedes for us. When we wander, draw us back to You, and unite us as one family in Him. In Jesus’ name we pray. Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
