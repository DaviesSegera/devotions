Seen from a Distance

Deuteronomy 34:1–12 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-27

Moses sees the Promised Land from a distance and entrusts his life to God. Reflect on forgiveness when consequences remain, serving faithfully through disappointment, and the hope Jesus brings beyond the grave.

Faith & trustHope & encouragementForgiveness & graceWaiting on God

Key Verse
“The eternal God is your refuge, and his everlasting arms are under you.”
Deuteronomy 33:27, NLT, excerpt

Most of us hope to finish life well and see our deepest longings fulfilled. Yet many faithful people reach the end with prayers still unanswered. Deuteronomy closes with the story of one such man.

For forty years, Moses had led Israel through the wilderness towards the land God had promised. Now, at the age of 120, he stood at its border: “Then Moses went up to Mount Nebo from the plains of Moab and climbed Pisgah Peak, which is across from Jericho. And the Lord showed him the whole land…” Deuteronomy 34:1, NLT, excerpt Then God spoke: “This is the land I promised on oath to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob when I said, ‘I will give it to your descendants.’ I have now allowed you to see it with your own eyes, but you will not enter the land.” Deuteronomy 34:4, NLT, excerpt

Moses had pleaded to be allowed to enter: “Please let me cross the Jordan to see the wonderful land on the other side, the beautiful hill country and the Lebanon mountains.” Deuteronomy 3:25, NLT, excerpt God’s answer was firm: “But the Lord was angry with me because of you, and he would not listen to me. ‘That’s enough!’ he declared. ‘Speak of it no more.’” Deuteronomy 3:26, NLT

Even a man who knew God as a friend heard God say no.

Why was Moses not allowed to enter? Years earlier, at Kadesh, the people had no water and turned angrily against their leaders. God told Moses to speak to a rock, and water would flow from it. Instead, Moses lost his temper: “Then he and Aaron summoned the people to come and gather at the rock. ‘Listen, you rebels!’ he shouted. ‘Must we bring you water from this rock?’ Then Moses raised his hand and struck the rock twice with the staff, and water gushed out. So the entire community and their livestock drank their fill. But the Lord said to Moses and Aaron, ‘Because you did not trust me enough to demonstrate my holiness to the people of Israel, you will not lead them into the land I am giving them!’” Numbers 20:10–12, NLT

Here, to “demonstrate my holiness” meant showing the people that the power and glory belonged to God alone. But Moses spoke as though he and Aaron were the ones providing the water, and he failed to follow God’s instructions. Although Moses later told the people that the Lord was angry with him “because of you,” God held Moses and Aaron responsible for their own actions at Kadesh.

Yet the story does not end in rejection. Ellen G. White describes how Moses and Aaron responded:

“They had been overcome by a sudden temptation, and their contrition was immediate and heartfelt. The Lord accepted their repentance, though because of the harm their sin might do among the people, He could not remit its punishment.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 419 · PP 419.1, excerpt

“Contrition” is sincere sorrow for sin; to “remit” a punishment is to cancel it. God forgave Moses, yet the consequence of his actions remained. We, too, can be fully forgiven for a hasty word or a wrong choice and still live with its lasting effects.

God’s forgiveness is real, even when the consequences of our choices remain.

Moses could have allowed bitterness to overshadow his final days. Instead, he encouraged Joshua, who would lead the people across the Jordan: “Do not be afraid or discouraged, for the Lord will personally go ahead of you. He will be with you; he will neither fail you nor abandon you.” Deuteronomy 31:8, NLT Ellen G. White observes:

“He knew that he must die. Yet he had not for a moment faltered in his care for Israel.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 469 · PP 469.3, excerpt

In his final blessing on the people, Moses spoke words that have comforted believers for centuries: “The eternal God is your refuge, and his everlasting arms are under you.” Deuteronomy 33:27, NLT, excerpt A refuge is a place of safety. With death approaching, Moses pointed the people to God’s strength. The everlasting arms that would carry Israel into the land would also hold him.

Moses spent his final days strengthening others and pointing them to God.

Then Moses climbed the mountain alone. Ellen G. White describes the moment with tenderness:

“Moses knew that he was to die alone; no earthly friend would be permitted to minister to him in his last hours. There was a mystery and awfulness about the scene before him, from which his heart shrank.… But he had learned to trust in God, and with unquestioning faith he committed himself and his people to His love and mercy.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 470 · PP 470.3, excerpt

Here, “awfulness” conveys a sense of solemn awe. Scripture records his death in simple words: “So Moses, the servant of the Lord, died there in the land of Moab, just as the Lord had said. The Lord buried him in a valley near Beth-peor in Moab, but to this day no one knows the exact place. Moses was 120 years old when he died, yet his eyesight was clear, and he was as strong as ever.” Deuteronomy 34:5–7, NLT

Notice who buried Moses: the Lord Himself. The psalmist speaks of this same loving care: “The Lord cares deeply when his loved ones die.” Psalm 116:15, NLT Ellen G. White describes Moses’ final moments on the mountain:

“Again the vision faded, and his eyes rested upon the land of Canaan as it spread out in the distance. Then, like a tired warrior, he lay down to rest.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 477 · PP 477.2, excerpt

God was with Moses as he faced death, and He remains with His children to the end.

Yet Moses’ story did not end there. Centuries later, Jesus took three of His disciples up a mountain, where His appearance was transformed with heavenly glory. During this event, known as the Transfiguration, something remarkable happened: “Suddenly, two men, Moses and Elijah, appeared and began talking with Jesus. They were glorious to see. And they were speaking about his exodus from this world, which was about to be fulfilled in Jerusalem.” Luke 9:30–31, NLT

Moses, who had once been forbidden to enter the land, now stood there beside Jesus. They spoke of His “exodus”: His coming death in Jerusalem, which would bring a deliverance greater than Israel’s escape from Egypt. Ellen G. White sees in this meeting a merciful answer to the prayer Moses had offered so long before:

“A wilderness grave was the goal of those years of toil and heart-burdening care. But He who is ‘able to do exceeding abundantly above all that we ask or think’ (Ephesians 3:20), had in this measure answered His servant’s prayer.”The Desire of Ages, p. 421 · DA 421.3, excerpt

How had Moses come to be there? The Bible does not explain. The letter of Jude briefly refers to an event that “took place when Michael was arguing with the devil about Moses’ body.” Jude 9, NLT, excerpt Ellen G. White understood this verse, together with the account of the Transfiguration, to mean that Christ had raised Moses from the grave: “Moses came forth from the tomb glorified, and ascended with his Deliverer to the City of God.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 479 · PP 479.1, excerpt Scripture clearly teaches that Jesus came to defeat death itself: “For only as a human being could he die, and only by dying could he break the power of the devil, who had the power of death. Only in this way could he set free all who have lived their lives as slaves to the fear of dying.” Hebrews 2:14–15, NLT, excerpt

Through Jesus, God’s promises reach beyond the grave.

Moses was not the only one to die before seeing every promise fulfilled. Speaking of Abraham, Sarah and others who lived by faith, the book of Hebrews says: “All these people died still believing what God had promised them. They did not receive what was promised, but they saw it all from a distance and welcomed it.” Hebrews 11:13, NLT, excerpt Ellen G. White points to both justice and love in God’s dealings with Moses:

“He could not grant the prayer of Moses that he might share the inheritance of Israel, but He did not forget or forsake His servant.… On the top of Pisgah, God called Moses to an inheritance infinitely more glorious than the earthly Canaan.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 479 · PP 479.2, excerpt

Perhaps God has not answered a prayer as you had hoped, or you are still living with the consequences of a mistake He has forgiven. Moses’ story does not promise that every disappointment will be resolved in this life. It does assure us that God remains faithful, even when His answer is no. Matthew’s account of the Transfiguration ends with these words: “And when they looked up, Moses and Elijah were gone, and they saw only Jesus.” Matthew 17:8, NLT Keep your eyes on Him. Serve faithfully, encourage those who will follow you, and trust Him with the hopes that still lie beyond your reach.

When we see God’s promises only from a distance, we can still trust the One who keeps them.

Reflect

  1. What prayer do I need to place in God’s hands again, even if His answer has been “no” or “not yet”?
  2. Which consequences do I still find difficult to accept, even though I have received God’s forgiveness?
  3. Who might God be asking me to encourage and strengthen, just as Moses did for Joshua?

Key Takeaways

  1. God forgives, even when consequences remain. God accepted Moses’ repentance, yet he still could not lead Israel into the promised land. Numbers 20:10–12 · quoted above
  2. God holds His servants to the end. Moses faced death trusting that God’s everlasting arms would hold him. Deuteronomy 33:27, excerpt · quoted above
  3. God’s promises find their fulfilment in Jesus. Moses finally stood in the land beside Jesus, speaking with Him about the death that would set us free. Luke 9:30–31 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Eternal God, thank You for being our refuge and for holding us in Your everlasting arms. Thank You for forgiving our sins, even when their consequences remain. We bring You the prayers You have not answered as we hoped. Teach us to trust Your goodness, to serve faithfully and to encourage those who will follow us. Help us to finish well. Thank You for Jesus, who has broken the power of death and holds our future. Keep our eyes on Him until we see Your promises fulfilled. In Jesus’ name we pray. Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
