Remember the Creator

Genesis 1:1–2:3 · 6 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-09-21

Creation reveals whose world this is and whose we are. Reflect on human dignity, stewardship, and the seventh-day Sabbath as God’s gift.

Faith & trustHumility & obedienceLove & service
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“And God blessed the seventh day and declared it holy, because it was the day when he rested from all his work of creation.”
Genesis 2:3 (NLT)
“Then God looked over all he had made, and he saw that it was very good!”
Genesis 1:31 (NLT)

Creation tells us more than how the world began; it tells us whose world this is, and whose we are. It opens not with an argument but with God: “In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth” Genesis 1:1 (NLT). And not with a struggle but with a word: “Then God said, “Let there be light,” and there was light” 1:3 (NLT).

Light and darkness are separated and named. A sky is opened. Dry ground appears and the land sprouts “every sort of seed-bearing plant” 1:11 (NLT, excerpt). Waters swarm, skies fill, animals come from the ground — and again, before anyone has said a word of thanks, God looks at what He has made and calls it good. Ellen G. White describes it: “As the earth came forth from the hand of its Maker, it was exceedingly beautiful.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 44 · PP 44.2

Before there was a human voice to praise Him, the Creator’s work already testified to His goodness.

Then the telling changes. “So God created human beings in his own image. In the image of God he created them; male and female he created them” 1:27 (NLT). Everything else is made to match its own kind. Human beings are made to be like God. Ellen G. White puts it in a line: “Man was to bear God’s image, both in outward resemblance and in character.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 45 · PP 45.2 Male and female together bear it, and no one carries it more fully than anyone else.

With the image come a blessing and a trust: “Be fruitful and multiply. Fill the earth and govern it” 1:28 (NLT, excerpt). Genesis 2 shows what that governing looked like at the start: “The LORD God placed the man in the Garden of Eden to tend and watch over it” 2:15 (NLT). To tend and watch over is a gardener’s work, not a conqueror’s.

Our first identity is not what we produce, possess or prove. We are made in the image of God.

Then the verdict: “Then God looked over all he had made, and he saw that it was very good!” 1:31 (NLT). That is the world as God finished it, before sin entered in Genesis 3. It does not deny the grief we live with now. It is the memory of what He intended, and the promise of what He will restore.

And the week is not over. “So the creation of the heavens and the earth and everything in them was completed” 2:1 (NLT). “On the seventh day God had finished his work of creation, so he rested from all his work” 2:2 (NLT). Then He did to one day what He had done to nothing else: “And God blessed the seventh day and declared it holy” 2:3 (NLT, excerpt). Four things, every one of them God’s: completed, finished, rested, blessed, set apart. He did not rest because He was tired. Ellen G. White: “God looked with satisfaction upon the work of His hands. All was perfect, worthy of its divine Author, and He rested, not as one weary, but as well pleased with the fruits of His wisdom and goodness and the manifestations of His glory.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 47 · PP 47.2

The day did not become holy because people began treating it that way. Ellen G. White traces the order: “After resting upon the seventh day, God sanctified it, or set it apart, as a day of rest for man.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 47 · PP 47.3

The first Sabbath turns our attention from what we accomplish to what God has completed.

Genesis records what God did. Exodus puts the command into words much later, at Sinai, reaching back to the Creation week for its reason. “Remember to observe the Sabbath day by keeping it holy” Exodus 20:8 (NLT); “For in six days the LORD made the heavens, the earth, the sea, and everything in them; but on the seventh day he rested. That is why the LORD blessed the Sabbath day and set it apart as holy” 20:11 (NLT). The command does not invent a day. It points back to one God had already blessed. Ellen G. White names it: “In Eden, God set up the memorial of His work of creation, in placing His blessing upon the seventh day.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 48 · PP 48.1

Remembering is practical. “On that day no one in your household may do any work. This includes you, your sons and daughters, your male and female servants, your livestock, and any foreigners living among you” 20:10 (NLT, excerpt). Sabbath rest was never meant to be bought with somebody else’s tiredness. And it is a gift, not a burden: “The Sabbath was made to meet the needs of people, and not people to meet the requirements of the Sabbath. So the Son of Man is Lord, even over the Sabbath!” Mark 2:27–28 (NLT). The day has an Owner, and “God created everything through him, and nothing was created except through him” John 1:3 (NLT). Ellen G. White follows it through: “Since He made all things, He made the Sabbath. By Him it was set apart as a memorial of the work of creation. It points to Him as both the Creator and the Sanctifier.” The Desire of Ages, p. 288 · DA 288.2

Nothing here is earned. Keeping a day never bought anyone a place with God. Christ did that. Obedience answers the Creator and Redeemer; it never pays Him. Come to the seventh day tired, grieving or behind. It is still what God made it: time He set apart, pointing to the Son through whom He made us. Even a child can pray it: Jesus, You made me and You made this day — help me spend it with You.

Creation tells us whose we are; the Sabbath helps us remember.

Reflect

  1. What changes when I remember that my life, my worth and my world are gifts from the Creator?
  2. Whose burden could my Sabbath lighten this week instead of increase?
  3. How can I receive and honour the seventh-day Sabbath as God’s gift rather than merely discuss it as a doctrine?

Key Takeaways

  1. God made it, and He called it good. “Then God said, “Let there be light,” and there was light” Genesis 1:3 (NLT), and at the end “he saw that it was very good!” 1:31 (NLT, excerpt) Ellen G. White: “As the earth came forth from the hand of its Maker, it was exceedingly beautiful.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 44 · PP 44.2
  2. Human worth is received, not achieved. “In the image of God he created them; male and female he created them” 1:27 (NLT, excerpt). The task that follows is care, not conquest: “to tend and watch over it” 2:15 (NLT, excerpt).
  3. God completed, rested, blessed and made the seventh day holy. “And God blessed the seventh day and declared it holy” 2:3 (NLT, excerpt). He rested “not as one weary, but as well pleased with the fruits of His wisdom and goodness” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 47 · PP 47.2, and the holiness is His doing, not ours.
  4. Exodus tells us to remember; Christ shows us why it is a gift. “Remember to observe the Sabbath day by keeping it holy” Exodus 20:8 (NLT), because “in six days the LORD made the heavens, the earth, the sea, and everything in them” 20:11 (NLT, excerpt). And “the Son of Man is Lord, even over the Sabbath!” Mark 2:28 (NLT, excerpt) Ellen G. White: “By Him it was set apart as a memorial of the work of creation.” The Desire of Ages, p. 288 · DA 288.2

Sources and Further ReadingScripture: Genesis 1:1–2:3, with the key text at 2:3 and the goodness anchor at 1:31, read with Exodus 20:8–11. Quoted on this page: Genesis 1:1, 3, 11, 27, 28, 31; 2:1, 2, 3, 15; Exodus 20:8, 10, 11; Mark 2:27–28; John 1:3 — all NLT, with every excerpt marked. The NLT footnote at Genesis 2:2 gives “ceased” as an alternative rendering of “rested,” also applying to 2:3. Genesis 2:4–25 is the complementary, closer account of humanity, the garden and vocation; 2:15 comes from it. The word “remember” belongs to Exodus 20:8, not to Genesis 2: Genesis records God’s rest, blessing and sanctification of the seventh day, and Exodus gives the command and grounds it in Creation. Deuteronomy 5:12–15 gives the deliverance reason for the same command and is developed in Devotion 2. Ellen G. White: Patriarchs and Prophets, chapter 2, “The Creation,” pp. 44–51, quoted at pp. 44, 45, 47 and 48 (PP 44.2, 45.2, 47.2, 47.3, 48.1); chapter 9, “The Literal Week,” pp. 111–116, read and used in Devotion 2; The Desire of Ages, chapter 29, “The Sabbath,” pp. 281–289, quoted at p. 288 (DA 288.2). She tells us what the finished world was like, what bearing God’s image means, why God’s rest was satisfaction rather than weariness, in what order the day was sanctified, that the memorial was set up in Eden, and that the Sabbath belongs to Christ. Genesis does not explain the mechanism of any created event, and Genesis 2:1–3 records no instruction given to Adam and Eve about keeping the day; neither does this page.
