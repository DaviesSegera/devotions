He Saw Jesus Standing

Acts 6–7 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-15

Stephen serves people who have been overlooked, sees Jesus standing at God’s right hand, and prays for those who harm him. Reflect on faithful service and trusting Jesus when others treat us unfairly.

Faith & trustForgiveness & graceLove & servicePrayer
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“And he told them, ‘Look, I see the heavens opened and the Son of Man standing in the place of honor at God’s right hand!’”
Acts 7:56 (NLT)

How do we stay faithful when doing what is right brings trouble instead of thanks? Stephen is remembered as the first follower of Jesus to die for his faith. But Luke first introduces Stephen in a quieter moment, when he is chosen to help share out food.

As the church in Jerusalem grew, a problem arose. “But as the believers rapidly multiplied, there were rumblings of discontent. The Greek-speaking believers complained about the Hebrew-speaking believers, saying that their widows were being discriminated against in the daily distribution of food.” Acts 6:1, NLT The apostles asked the believers to choose seven trusted men to oversee the distribution.

The first name on the list was Stephen. “Everyone liked this idea, and they chose the following: Stephen (a man full of faith and the Holy Spirit), Philip,” Acts 6:5, NLT, excerpt Ellen G. White calls the seven “deacons,” a title that comes from the Greek word for a servant. “Stephen, the foremost of the seven deacons, was a man of deep piety and broad faith.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 97 · AA 97.1, excerpt Piety is a deep love and reverence for God. Stephen first showed his faith by caring for others. That same faith soon made him a bold witness for Jesus.

Before Stephen faced the council, he faithfully served people who had been overlooked.

God’s grace was at work in him. “Stephen, a man full of God’s grace and power, performed amazing miracles and signs among the people.” Acts 6:8, NLT Luke tells us what happened when some men argued with Stephen. “None of them could stand against the wisdom and the Spirit with which Stephen spoke.” Acts 6:10, NLT

When their arguments failed, his opponents turned to lies. “So they persuaded some men to lie about Stephen, saying, ‘We heard him blaspheme Moses, and even God.’” Acts 6:11, NLT To blaspheme is to speak against God. Stephen was arrested and brought before the high council, Jerusalem’s Jewish governing body. Yet the lies could not hide what God was doing in him. “At this point everyone in the high council stared at Stephen, because his face became as bright as an angel’s.” Acts 6:15, NLT

Being misrepresented hurts, and we may want to hit back. Stephen answered the charges without hatred. Peter describes how Jesus faced false accusations. “He did not retaliate when he was insulted, nor threaten revenge when he suffered. He left his case in the hands of God, who always judges fairly.” 1 Peter 2:23, NLT

Lies could not hide the grace of God that people saw in Stephen’s face.

When the high priest asked whether the charges were true, Stephen answered as a son of Israel speaking to his own people. “Brothers and fathers, listen to me. Our glorious God appeared to our ancestor Abraham” Acts 7:2, NLT, excerpt Ellen G. White describes the spirit of his defence. “He made plain his own loyalty to God and to the Jewish faith” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 99 · AA 99.2, excerpt

As Stephen retold the story of God’s people, a pattern emerged. God sent help through people who had been rejected. Moses was pushed aside by his own people, but God had not finished with him. “So God sent back the same man his people had previously rejected” Acts 7:35, NLT, excerpt Moses himself had spoken of a prophet yet to come. “Moses himself told the people of Israel, ‘God will raise up for you a Prophet like me from among your own people.’” Acts 7:37, NLT Ellen G. White sums up the connection Stephen made. “He connected Jesus Christ with all the Jewish history.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 99 · AA 99.2, excerpt

Then, like Israel’s own prophets before him, Stephen ended with a sharp warning. “Must you forever resist the Holy Spirit? That’s what your ancestors did, and so do you!” Acts 7:51, NLT, excerpt These were hard words, spoken by a man who loved his people. They are a warning for us too. We can know the story well and still resist the Spirit who points us to the Saviour at its centre.

Stephen told his people’s story so they could see Jesus in it.

The council was furious. “The Jewish leaders were infuriated by Stephen’s accusation, and they shook their fists at him in rage.” Acts 7:54, NLT But Stephen looked beyond the angry faces. “But Stephen, full of the Holy Spirit, gazed steadily into heaven and saw the glory of God, and he saw Jesus standing in the place of honor at God’s right hand.” Acts 7:55, NLT “And he told them, ‘Look, I see the heavens opened and the Son of Man standing in the place of honor at God’s right hand!’” Acts 7:56, NLT

Ellen G. White describes Stephen’s calm. “In the cruel faces about him the prisoner read his fate; but he did not waver. For him the fear of death was gone.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 100 · AA 100.3, excerpt

“Son of Man” was the title Jesus most often used for Himself. At His own trial before the same council, Jesus had said, “But from now on the Son of Man will be seated in the place of power at God’s right hand.” Luke 22:69, NLT Now Stephen saw Him standing. Christians have understood this in different ways. Some see Jesus rising to welcome His servant. Others see Him standing as Stephen’s advocate. Ellen G. White describes what Stephen saw.

“To him the gates of heaven were ajar, and, looking in, he saw the glory of the courts of God, and Christ, as if just risen from His throne, standing ready to sustain His servant.”The Acts of the Apostles, p. 100 · AA 100.3, excerpt

Ajar means slightly open. Stephen was not alone. Hebrews describes Jesus’ continuing care for His people. “Therefore he is able, once and forever, to save those who come to God through him. He lives forever to intercede with God on their behalf.” Hebrews 7:25, NLT To intercede is to plead on someone’s behalf.

When the council refused to listen, heaven was open, and Jesus was standing.

The leaders would hear no more. “Then they put their hands over their ears and began shouting. They rushed at him” Acts 7:57, NLT “and dragged him out of the city and began to stone him. His accusers took off their coats and laid them at the feet of a young man named Saul.” Acts 7:58, NLT

Stephen’s last words echo his Lord’s. Luke records what Jesus said as He died on the cross. “Then Jesus shouted, ‘Father, I entrust my spirit into your hands!’” Luke 23:46, NLT, excerpt Stephen prayed in the same spirit, addressing Jesus Himself. “As they stoned him, Stephen prayed, ‘Lord Jesus, receive my spirit.’” Acts 7:59, NLT Luke also records Jesus’ prayer for those who crucified Him. “Jesus said, ‘Father, forgive them, for they don’t know what they are doing.’” Luke 23:34, NLT, excerpt Stephen’s final prayer was for the people who were killing him. “He fell to his knees, shouting, ‘Lord, don’t charge them with this sin!’ And with that, he died.” Acts 7:60, NLT

Stephen took their wrongdoing seriously, calling it “this sin.” He asked God not to hold it against them. That is what Jesus taught. “But I say, love your enemies! Pray for those who persecute you!” Matthew 5:44, NLT Forgiveness does not pretend that wrong is right. It leaves the wrong in God’s hands and asks God to show mercy to the one who did it.

Stephen died as he had lived, trusting Jesus and praying for others.

Luke tells us more about the young man who watched over the coats. “Saul was one of the witnesses, and he agreed completely with the killing of Stephen.” Acts 8:1, NLT, excerpt

Ellen G. White describes how deeply Stephen’s death affected Saul.

“His death was a sore trial to the church, but it resulted in the conviction of Saul, who could not efface from his memory the faith and constancy of the martyr, and the glory that had rested on his countenance.”The Acts of the Apostles, p. 101 · AA 101.3, excerpt

To efface is to erase, and constancy is steady faithfulness. Later, on the road to Damascus, Jesus met Saul. “He fell to the ground and heard a voice saying to him, ‘Saul! Saul! Why are you persecuting me?’” Acts 9:4, NLT Ellen G. White connects that encounter with what Saul witnessed at Stephen’s death. “He had witnessed Stephen’s forbearance toward his enemies and his forgiveness of them.” The Acts of the Apostles, p. 116 · AA 116.2, excerpt Forbearance is patient self-control when we are wronged.

Years later, Saul, by then known as Paul, still remembered that day. He told the Lord, “And I was in complete agreement when your witness Stephen was killed. I stood by and kept the coats they took off when they stoned him.” Acts 22:20, NLT The word martyr comes from the Greek word for a witness. Paul also wrote about the grace that changed him. “But whatever I am now, it is all because God poured out his special favor on me” 1 Corinthians 15:10, NLT, excerpt

Stephen did not live to see any of this, and we may not see what God does with our faithfulness either. You may be serving where few people notice or struggling to forgive someone who has hurt you. Jesus sees you and stands with His people. We can trust Him with our cause and pray for those who have hurt us.

Stephen never saw what God would do with his forgiveness, but Jesus saw it all.

Reflect

  1. Where is God asking me to serve faithfully, even if few people notice?
  2. When others misunderstand me or treat me unfairly, how can I leave my case in God’s hands?
  3. Who do I need to pray for and entrust to God’s mercy, as Stephen did?

Key Takeaways

  1. Faithfulness begins in ordinary service. The believers chose Stephen, a man full of faith and the Holy Spirit, to help care for widows who were being overlooked. Acts 6:5 · quoted above
  2. Jesus stands with His people. When the council refused to listen, Stephen saw the heavens opened and Jesus standing at God’s right hand. Acts 7:55–56 · quoted above
  3. Forgiveness leaves the wrong with God. Stephen prayed for those who were killing him. God later met Saul, who had witnessed Stephen’s death. Acts 7:58–60 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Lord Jesus, thank You that You see Your people and stand with them when they are treated unfairly. Fill us with Your Spirit, as You filled Stephen, so that we may serve faithfully in small things and speak about You with grace. When others misunderstand us, help us leave our case in Your hands. When we are hurt, give us the courage to pray for those who have wronged us and ask You to show them mercy. Keep our eyes on You when the way is hard. Use our quiet faithfulness in ways we may never see. In Your name we pray. Amen.
