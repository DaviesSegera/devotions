When the Iron Floated: The God Who Notices Small Losses

2 Kings 6:1–7 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-06

A borrowed ax head fell into the Jordan, and God noticed the loss. Reflect on honest prayer, His care in ordinary work, and bringing every worry to Jesus, who understands our daily lives.

PrayerFaith & trustHope & encouragementHumility & obedience
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“‘Where did it fall?’ the man of God asked. When he showed him the place, Elisha cut a stick and threw it into the water at that spot. Then the ax head floated to the surface.”
2 Kings 6:6 (NLT)

Have you ever felt that a problem was too small to pray about? Perhaps it is a lost key, a broken tool, or a small debt that keeps you awake. We readily pray about sickness and grief, yet we may hesitate to bring God the worries of everyday life. A short story from the life of the prophet Elisha reminds us that God cares about those worries too.

The story begins with a building project. “One day the group of prophets came to Elisha and told him, ‘As you can see, this place where we meet with you is too small.’” 2 Kings 6:1, NLT They had a plan: “Let’s go down to the Jordan River, where there are plenty of logs. There we can build a new place for us to meet.” 2 Kings 6:2, NLT, excerpt

These men belonged to a community of students preparing to serve God. Ellen G. White describes how such students lived: “The pupils of these schools sustained themselves by their own labor in tilling the soil or in some mechanical employment.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 593 · PP 593.3, excerpt They studied God’s Word and worked with their hands.

Then someone made a simple request: “‘Please come with us,’ someone suggested. ‘I will,’ he said.” 2 Kings 6:3, NLT “So he went with them.” 2 Kings 6:4, NLT, excerpt

Ellen G. White writes that “Elisha went with them to Jordan, encouraging them by his presence, giving them instruction, and even performing a miracle to aid them in their work.” Prophets and Kings, p. 260 · PK 260.3, excerpt She also shows the source of his strength: “Wherever he was, God was with him, giving him words to speak and power to work miracles.” Prophets and Kings, p. 260 · PK 260.3, excerpt They asked for Elisha’s company, and with him came the presence of God.

They invited God’s servant into their ordinary work, and he went with them.

Work began at the river: “When they arrived at the Jordan, they began cutting down trees.” 2 Kings 6:4, NLT, excerpt Then something went wrong: “But as one of them was cutting a tree, his ax head fell into the river. ‘Oh, sir!’ he cried. ‘It was a borrowed ax!’” 2 Kings 6:5, NLT

Notice what troubled him most: the ax belonged to someone else. In Israel, borrowing came with responsibility. The law of Moses addressed this responsibility in the case of a borrowed animal: “If someone borrows an animal from a neighbor and it is injured or dies when the owner is absent, the person who borrowed it must pay full compensation.” Exodus 22:14, NLT, excerpt Compensation means paying for what was lost.

He did not hide what had happened. He immediately cried out to the man of God. We often hide our small failures out of embarrassment, but God invites us to bring them to Him just as they are. Paul writes, “Don’t worry about anything; instead, pray about everything. Tell God what you need, and thank him for all he has done.” Philippians 4:6, NLT

He did not hide his loss; he brought it straight to the one who could help.

Elisha responded with a question: “‘Where did it fall?’ the man of God asked.” 2 Kings 6:6, NLT, excerpt Elisha wanted to know exactly where the ax head had fallen. Then “When he showed him the place, Elisha cut a stick and threw it into the water at that spot. Then the ax head floated to the surface.” 2 Kings 6:6, NLT, excerpt

Iron does not float. The Bible does not explain how the miracle happened or suggest that the stick had any power of its own. It simply shows God at work at the very spot where the loss took place.

Jesus taught the same truth about His Father’s care: “What is the price of two sparrows—one copper coin? But not a single sparrow can fall to the ground without your Father knowing it.” Matthew 10:29, NLT He went on: “And the very hairs on your head are all numbered.” Matthew 10:30, NLT “So don’t be afraid; you are more valuable to God than a whole flock of sparrows.” Matthew 10:31, NLT

Ellen G. White encourages us to take Jesus at His word:

“Keep your wants, your joys, your sorrows, your cares, and your fears before God. You cannot burden Him; you cannot weary Him. He who numbers the hairs of your head is not indifferent to the wants of His children.”Steps to Christ, p. 100 · SC 100.1, excerpt

A little later in the same paragraph, she adds, “Nothing that in any way concerns our peace is too small for Him to notice.” Steps to Christ, p. 100 · SC 100.1, excerpt Peter gives the same invitation: “Give all your worries and cares to God, for he cares about you.” 1 Peter 5:7, NLT

This does not mean that everything we lose will be restored as we hope. Some losses remain in this life. Yet the God who noticed a borrowed ax head also sees us in our losses and offers us His peace: “Then you will experience God’s peace, which exceeds anything we can understand. His peace will guard your hearts and minds as you live in Christ Jesus.” Philippians 4:7, NLT

No loss that troubles God’s children is too small for Him to notice.

The story ends with a short command: “‘Grab it,’ Elisha said. And the man reached out and grabbed it.” 2 Kings 6:7, NLT God brought the iron to the surface, but the man still had to reach out and take it. He did not earn the miracle; he received what God had provided.

Faith often looks like this: God does what we cannot do, then invites us to take the next simple step of trust. Reflecting on Elisha’s life, Ellen G. White writes, “Always, when the way before him seemed utterly closed, he had still advanced by faith, and God had honored his confidence and opened the way before him.” Prophets and Kings, p. 263 · PK 263.2, excerpt

God lifted what was lost, and the man reached out to receive it.

This story of men cutting timber also brings to mind Jesus, who worked with wood Himself. When He taught in His hometown, the people said, “He’s just a carpenter, the son of Mary” Mark 6:3, NLT, excerpt Ellen G. White describes those years in Nazareth: “He learned a trade, and with His own hands worked in the carpenter’s shop with Joseph.” The Desire of Ages, p. 72 · DA 72.3, excerpt She reminds us that this ordinary work mattered to God:

“He was doing God’s service just as much when laboring at the carpenter’s bench as when working miracles for the multitude.”The Desire of Ages, p. 74 · DA 74.4, excerpt

Jesus knows what it is like to work with tools and meet the needs of daily life. The book of Hebrews helps us see what this means for us: “This High Priest of ours understands our weaknesses, for he faced all of the same testings we do, yet he did not sin.” Hebrews 4:15, NLT That is why we can pray with confidence: “So let us come boldly to the throne of our gracious God. There we will receive his mercy, and we will find grace to help us when we need it most.” Hebrews 4:16, NLT

Perhaps you are carrying a worry that seems too small to mention: something you broke, something you owe, or something you lost. Do not hide it. Tell Jesus where it fell. He cares about you, and He knows how to help.

Jesus, who worked with wood and tools, understands our daily lives and welcomes every care we bring to Him.

Reflect

  1. Have I invited God into my ordinary work, or only into my times of worship?
  2. Have I kept a small loss or worry from God because it seemed too unimportant to mention?
  3. What simple step of trust is God asking me to take today?

Key Takeaways

  1. Invite God into ordinary work. The group of prophets asked Elisha to come with them to cut logs. He went with them, bringing God’s presence into their daily work. 2 Kings 6:3–4 · quoted above
  2. No care is too small for God. Elisha asked where the ax head had fallen, and God acted at that very spot. Jesus taught that not even a sparrow falls without the Father knowing. 2 Kings 6:6 · quoted above
  3. Bring every care to Jesus. Our High Priest worked with His hands and understands our weaknesses. We can come boldly to Him for help. Mark 6:3; Hebrews 4:15–16 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Heavenly Father, thank You that even our smallest concerns matter to You. Thank You for being with us in our ordinary work as well as in our worship. Forgive us for hiding our worries and trying to carry them alone. Today we bring You our losses, our debts, and the cares that weigh on our hearts. Give us honest hearts and the faith to reach out when You show us the way. Thank You for Jesus, who worked with His own hands and understands our weaknesses. In His name we pray. Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
