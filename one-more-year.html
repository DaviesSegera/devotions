One More Year: God's Mercy and the Call to Bear Fruit

Luke 13:1–9 · 5 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-10

Jesus’ parable of the barren fig tree joins a call to repentance with a plea for mercy. Reflect on God’s patient care and the fruit His grace grows as we remain close to Him.

Forgiveness & graceHumility & obedienceLove & serviceFaith & trust

Key Verse
“Sir, give it one more chance. Leave it another year, and I’ll give it special attention and plenty of fertilizer.”
Luke 13:8 (NLT, excerpt)

Is God Finding Fruit in My Life?

Imagine a tree that is cared for year after year but never gives the fruit it was planted to bear. What happens when its future is questioned? Jesus told a story like that. It holds a serious warning, and a remarkable plea for mercy.

It began with bad news. “About this time Jesus was informed that Pilate had murdered some people from Galilee as they were offering sacrifices at the Temple.” Luke 13:1 (NLT) The people who told Jesus expected Him to agree that those Galileans had been punished for their sins. Instead He asked, “Do you think those Galileans were worse sinners than all the other people from Galilee?” Luke 13:2 (NLT, excerpt) And He answered, “Not at all! And you will perish, too, unless you repent of your sins and turn to God.” Luke 13:3 (NLT)

Then He spoke of a second tragedy: “And what about the eighteen people who died when the tower in Siloam fell on them? Were they the worst sinners in Jerusalem?” Luke 13:4 (NLT) His answer was the same: “No, and I tell you again that unless you repent, you will perish, too.” Luke 13:5 (NLT)

Jesus would not let anyone judge a person by their suffering. He turned every eye back to its own heart. Ellen G. White describes what He was doing:

Ellen G. White’s insight · quotation“Christ in His teaching linked with the warning of judgment the invitation of mercy.”Christ’s Object Lessons, ch. 17, “Spare it this Year Also,” p. 212 (COL 212.1)

When we hear of someone else's pain, Jesus calls us to look at our own hearts, not to judge theirs.

Then Jesus told His story. “A man planted a fig tree in his garden and came again and again to see if there was any fruit on it, but he was always disappointed.” Luke 13:6 (NLT, excerpt) At last the owner said to his gardener, “I’ve waited three years, and there hasn’t been a single fig! Cut it down. It’s just taking up space in the garden.” Luke 13:7 (NLT, excerpt)

The owner was right to look for fruit. That is why a fig tree is planted. Three years of nothing is a long time, and the warning is real.

But the gardener answered, “Sir, give it one more chance. Leave it another year, and I’ll give it special attention and plenty of fertilizer.” Luke 13:8 (NLT, excerpt)

The gardener did not say that fruit no longer mattered. He asked for time so that fruit could come, and he promised to give the tree more care, not less. That is mercy still at work.

Ellen White helps us see the heart behind this plea:

Ellen G. White’s insight · quotation“The owner and the dresser of the vineyard are one in their interest in the fig tree. So the Father and the Son were one in their love for the chosen people.”Christ’s Object Lessons, ch. 17, “Spare it this Year Also,” p. 216 (COL 216.1)

There is no angry Father here and no kind Son holding Him back. Both long for the same thing: a tree full of life. Paul asks, “Don’t you see how wonderfully kind, tolerant, and patient God is with you? Does this mean nothing to you? Can’t you see that his kindness is intended to turn you from your sin?” Romans 2:4 (NLT)

The gardener asks for one more chance, not so the tree can stay the same, but so it can finally bear fruit.

The gardener's plea ends, “If we get figs next year, fine. If not, then you can cut it down.” Luke 13:9 (NLT) And there the story stops. Ellen White notes, “Jesus did not in the parable tell the result of the gardener’s work. At that point His story was cut short.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 216 · COL 216.2 The ending is left open, because the next part belongs to those who hear it, and that includes us.

So what fruit is God looking for? Not money, titles or busy schedules. “But the Holy Spirit produces this kind of fruit in our lives: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.” Galatians 5:22–23 (NLT, excerpt) It looks like kindness when we are tired, honesty when a lie would be easier, and a sincere apology after we have hurt someone. A child can bear this fruit. So can someone who is old or unwell.

That fruit comes from Jesus. He said, “Those who remain in me, and I in them, will produce much fruit. For apart from me you can do nothing.” John 15:5 (NLT, excerpt) We do not grow fruit to earn His love. “God saved you by his grace when you believed. And you can’t take credit for this; it is a gift from God.” Ephesians 2:8 (NLT)

Fruit is not the price we pay for God's care. It is what His care grows in us.

God's patience is a gift. It is not a reason to wait. Ellen White writes of Jesus, “So His care and labor for you are not lessened, but increased.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 218 · COL 218.1 Come to Him today, just as you are. Turn to Him, stay close to Him, and let His Spirit make your life fruitful for the blessing of others.

Come to Jesus today. Receive His mercy, and let Him grow His fruit in you.

Reflect

  1. When I hear of someone else's trouble, do I judge them, or do I look at my own heart?
  2. Where is Jesus patiently inviting me to turn back to Him today?
  3. Can others see Jesus' love in the way I treat them?

Key Takeaways

  1. Jesus calls us to repent, not to judge. “No, and I tell you again that unless you repent, you will perish, too.” Luke 13:5 (NLT)
  2. Mercy pleads for more time and more care. “Sir, give it one more chance.” Luke 13:8 (NLT, excerpt)
  3. Fruit grows from staying close to Jesus. “Those who remain in me, and I in them, will produce much fruit.” John 15:5 (NLT, excerpt)

A prayer for today

Father, thank You for Your patience with us. Thank You, Jesus, for pleading for us and caring for us when we have given You so little fruit. Keep us from judging others in their pain. Turn our hearts to You today, and let Your Spirit grow love, honesty and kindness in us, so that our lives bless the people around us. Amen.
