Water from the Rock at Horeb: Christ Our Living Rock

Exodus 17:1–7 · 6 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-09-27

At Horeb, God gave water to a thirsty people despite their doubt. Remember His faithfulness and bring your thirst to Christ, the living Rock.

Faith & trustForgiveness & graceHope & encouragement
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“Strike the rock, and water will come gushing out. Then the people will be able to drink.”
Exodus 17:6 (NLT, excerpt)
“For they drank from the spiritual rock that traveled with them, and that rock was Christ.”
1 Corinthians 10:4 (NLT, excerpt)

At Rephidim, Israel faced an urgent need: the camp had no water. “At the LORD’s command, the whole community of Israel left the wilderness of Sin and moved from place to place. Eventually they camped at Rephidim, but there was no water there for the people to drink.” Exodus 17:1 (NLT)

God Himself had led them there. Now the families in the camp were thirsty. In the weeks before, He had opened the Red Sea, made the bitter water at Marah good to drink, and begun to send manna every morning.

Their thirst was real. God does not ask us to pretend we are not thirsty.

Soon, their fear began to shape their response. “So once more the people complained against Moses. ‘Give us water to drink!’ they demanded. ‘Quiet!’ Moses replied. ‘Why are you complaining against me? And why are you testing the LORD?’” Exodus 17:2 (NLT) Their fear turned into blame: “Why did you bring us out of Egypt? Are you trying to kill us, our children, and our livestock with thirst?” Exodus 17:3 (NLT, excerpt)

Ellen G. White describes what happened in their hearts:

Ellen G. White“When they had been so abundantly supplied with food, they remembered with shame their unbelief and murmurings, and promised to trust the Lord in the future; but they soon forgot their promise, and failed at the first trial of their faith.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 297 · PP 297.3

Needing water was not their sin. Need says, “Lord, we need Your help.” Unbelief says, “Your past kindness means nothing, and today’s trouble proves You have left us.”

At Rephidim, the people needed water. They also needed to remember God’s faithfulness.

Moses could not give them water, so he cried out to God. “Then Moses cried out to the LORD, ‘What should I do with these people? They are ready to stone me!’” Exodus 17:4 (NLT) God told him to walk ahead of the people with some of the elders and his staff. Then He spoke of the rock at Horeb, which is another name for Mount Sinai: “I will stand before you on the rock at Mount Sinai. Strike the rock, and water will come gushing out. Then the people will be able to drink.” Exodus 17:6 (NLT, excerpt) “So Moses struck the rock as he was told, and water gushed out as the elders looked on.” Exodus 17:6 (NLT, excerpt)

God provided for the people even after they had doubted Him. The place names would help them remember. “Moses named the place Massah (which means ‘test’) and Meribah (which means ‘arguing’) because the people of Israel argued with Moses and tested the LORD by saying, ‘Is the LORD here with us or not?’” Exodus 17:7 (NLT) Later, Moses warned, “You must not test the LORD your God as you did when you complained at Massah.” Deuteronomy 6:16 (NLT)

God welcomes honest prayer. We may tell Him we are thirsty, tired or afraid, and we may ask others for help. The danger comes when one hard day makes us forget what He has already shown us.

Their unbelief was real, but so was God’s mercy.

Paul identifies the One who was with Israel in the desert: “For they drank from the spiritual rock that traveled with them, and that rock was Christ.” 1 Corinthians 10:4 (NLT, excerpt) Ellen G. White adds, “Christ, the true Rock, was with them in all their wanderings.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 411 · PP 411.2

The Lord was with Israel, even in their doubt. Jesus still invites thirsty people to come to Him: “Anyone who is thirsty may come to me! Anyone who believes in me may come and drink!” John 7:37–38 (NLT, excerpt)

When life feels dry, remember how God has helped you. Tell Him what you need, ask a friend to pray with you, and come to Jesus for the life He gives.

Bring your thirst to Christ. Remember His faithfulness, trust His presence, and receive the life He gives.

Reflect

  1. When I reach a dry place, how can I remember God’s faithfulness?
  2. Where am I trying to satisfy a thirst that only Christ can meet?
  3. When life feels dry, will I still trust that the Lord is among us?

Key Takeaways

  1. The need was real. “There was no water there for the people to drink” Exodus 17:1 (excerpt). Faith does not pretend the desert has water.
  2. Forgetting led to testing. The people asked, “Is the LORD here with us or not?” Exodus 17:7 (excerpt). Remembering what God has done helps us trust Him today.
  3. God answered with mercy. Ellen G. White writes that “the Lord in His great mercy made the rod His instrument to work their deliverance” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 298 · PP 298.1.
  4. The Rock points to Christ. “That rock was Christ” 1 Corinthians 10:4 (excerpt). He is still with His people, and He gives living water to all who come.

A prayer for today

Faithful God, thank You for carrying me. In dry places, help me remember Your care and bring my needs to You honestly. Lord Jesus, thank You for being the true Rock. Satisfy my thirst with Your grace, and teach me to trust that You are with us. Amen.
