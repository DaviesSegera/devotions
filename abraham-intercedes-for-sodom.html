Abraham Intercedes for Sodom

Genesis 18:16–33 · 6 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-09-29

Abraham drew near to God and pleaded for Sodom. Reflect on justice, mercy, persistent prayer, and Jesus, who intercedes for us.

PrayerLove & serviceForgiveness & graceFaith & trust
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“Should not the Judge of all the earth do what is right?”
Genesis 18:25 (NLT, excerpt)

Abraham knew what was coming toward Sodom. He responded with prayer.

It began after a meal. Three visitors had eaten at Abraham’s tent, and as they left, they looked out toward Sodom. Then God spoke about His own plans. “‘Should I hide my plan from Abraham?’ the LORD asked.” Genesis 18:17 (NLT) God chose to tell His friend what He was about to do: “So the LORD told Abraham, ‘I have heard a great outcry from Sodom and Gomorrah, because their sin is so flagrant. I am going down to see if their actions are as wicked as I have heard. If not, I want to know.’” Genesis 18:20–21 (NLT)

Sodom’s sin was serious, yet God did not act hastily. He had heard the outcry, and He went to see.

Sin is serious to God, and so are the people caught in it.

“The other men turned and headed toward Sodom, but the LORD remained with Abraham.” Genesis 18:22 (NLT) Abraham drew closer after hearing the troubling news. His nephew Lot lived in Sodom. Years before, Abraham had rescued Lot and many other captives from enemy kings. His concern for them remained.

“Abraham approached him and said, ‘Will you sweep away both the righteous and the wicked?’” Genesis 18:23 (NLT) Then he asked about fifty righteous people, and ended with a question of trust: “Should not the Judge of all the earth do what is right?” Genesis 18:25 (NLT, excerpt)

Abraham appealed to the justice he already knew in God.

Intercession begins with confidence that the One hearing our prayer is both merciful and just.

God answered at once. If He found fifty righteous people, He would spare the city. Abraham spoke again, but with deep humility: “Since I have begun, let me speak further to my Lord, even though I am but dust and ashes.” Genesis 18:27 (NLT, excerpt) The phrase “dust and ashes” does not mean Abraham was worthless. It expresses his humility: “You are God, and I am not.”

So he kept asking: forty-five, forty, thirty, twenty. Each time God answered him patiently. Finally, “Abraham said, ‘Lord, please don’t be angry with me if I speak one more time. Suppose only ten are found there?’ And the LORD replied, ‘Then I will not destroy it for the sake of the ten.’” Genesis 18:32 (NLT)

This was not bargaining. Abraham asked nothing for himself and offered God nothing in exchange. He was pleading for other people. Ellen G. White tells us what moved him:

Ellen G. White“Love for perishing souls inspired Abraham’s prayer. While he loathed the sins of that corrupt city, he desired that the sinners might be saved. His deep interest for Sodom shows the anxiety that we should feel for the impenitent. We should cherish hatred of sin, but pity and love for the sinner.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 140 · PP 140.1

Abraham’s prayer became more daring without becoming less humble.

The cities were judged. Yet the Bible adds, “But God had listened to Abraham’s request and kept Lot safe, removing him from the disaster that engulfed the cities on the plain.” Genesis 19:29 (NLT) God heard Abraham and remained just. Abraham’s prayer mattered. We can pray with confidence that God hears us, even when the outcome is beyond our control.

Abraham’s prayer points us to Jesus. Abraham asked that sinners be spared. Jesus gave Himself for sinners, and He still prays for His people: “Therefore he is able, once and forever, to save those who come to God through him. He lives forever to intercede with God on their behalf.” Hebrews 7:25 (NLT)

Because Jesus intercedes for us, we can become people who pray for others. Think of someone whose choices trouble you, or someone far from God. Bring them to the Lord with humility, love, and perseverance.

Abraham prayed for people facing judgment. Jesus gave Himself to save sinners. Now He calls us to pray for others instead of only condemning them.

Reflect

  1. Am I more willing to talk about people’s sins than to pray for their salvation?
  2. Who has God placed on my heart that I need to keep bringing before Him in prayer?

Key Takeaways

  1. God is just. Abraham trusted that “the Judge of all the earth” Genesis 18:25 (excerpt) would do what is right.
  2. People matter. Abraham hated Sodom’s sin, but he still pleaded for its people.
  3. Prayer matters. “God had listened to Abraham’s request” Genesis 19:29 (excerpt). Jesus, our great Intercessor, invites us to pray for others too.

Prayer

Righteous and merciful God, You are the Judge of all the earth, and You always do what is right. Give me Abraham’s humble, caring heart. Help me reject sin, love people, and keep praying for those who are far from You. Thank You for Jesus, my great Intercessor, who gave His life for sinners. Amen.
