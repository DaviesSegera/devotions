Today's Devotion

When Thomas Doubted

John 20 · 7 min read
Key Verse
“Blessed are those who believe without seeing me.”
John 20:29 (NLT, excerpt)
“My Lord and my God!”
John 20:28 (NLT)

Thomas heard that Jesus was alive, but he wanted proof on his own terms. On the evening of the resurrection the disciples were meeting behind locked doors. “Suddenly, Jesus was standing there among them! ‘Peace be with you,’ he said. As he spoke, he showed them the wounds in his hands and his side.” John 20:19–20 (NLT) But John records: “One of the twelve disciples, Thomas (nicknamed the Twin), was not with the others when Jesus came.” John 20:24 (NLT) John tells us that he was absent; he does not tell us why.

When his friends said, “We have seen the Lord!” Thomas replied, “I won’t believe it unless I see the nail wounds in his hands, put my fingers into them, and place my hand into the wound in his side.” John 20:25 (NLT) This was the disciple who had once said, “Let’s go, too—and die with Jesus.” John 11:16 (NLT) Asking for understanding is one thing; announcing the conditions on which you will believe is another. Ellen G. White helps us see what was happening in him:

“He would not see through the eyes of his brethren, or exercise faith which was dependent upon their testimony. He ardently loved his Lord, but he had allowed jealousy and unbelief to take possession of his mind and heart.” The Desire of Ages, p. 807 · DA 807.1

Thomas loved Jesus, but he had made his own test the measure of whether Christ could be trusted.

Now notice what Jesus did not do. He did not leave Thomas identified only by his unbelief. “Eight days later the disciples were together again, and this time Thomas was with them. The doors were locked; but suddenly, as before, Jesus was standing among them. ‘Peace be with you,’ he said.” John 20:26 (NLT) The first word the doubting disciple heard from the risen Christ was peace. Then Jesus turned to him personally: “Put your finger here, and look at my hands. Put your hand into the wound in my side. Don’t be faithless any longer. Believe!” John 20:27 (NLT)

Jesus answered the very conditions Thomas had set—and He did not stop there. He called him to believe. Ellen G. White writes:

“These words showed that He was acquainted with the thoughts and words of Thomas. The doubting disciple knew that none of his companions had seen Jesus for a week. They could not have told the Master of his unbelief.” The Desire of Ages, p. 807 · DA 807.3

Christ knew Thomas’s words before Thomas had said one word to Him. Patient care and truthful correction stood together in a single sentence: an open invitation, and a clear call beyond unbelief.

Jesus met Thomas with peace and personal attention—and still called him to believe.

Between the invitation and the confession John records only this: “‘My Lord and my God!’ Thomas exclaimed.” John 20:28 (NLT) Thomas did not say it about Jesus; he said it to Him. This is not a man who simply felt better. It is a disciple acknowledging who the risen Jesus is and giving himself to Him personally—my Lord, my God. Ellen G. White describes the moment:

“He recognized the One before him as his Lord. He had no desire for further proof. His heart leaped for joy, and he cast himself at the feet of Jesus crying, ‘My Lord and my God.’” The Desire of Ages, p. 807 · DA 807.3

The wounds Thomas had demanded to see were the marks of the cross. The Saviour he had doubted was the Saviour who had died for him and risen.

Thomas did not merely feel better; he acknowledged the crucified and risen Jesus as his Lord and his God.

Then Jesus looked past that room. “You believe because you have seen me. Blessed are those who believe without seeing me.” John 20:29 (NLT) And John explains at once why he wrote: “these are written so that you may continue to believe that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God, and that by believing in him you will have life by the power of his name.” John 20:31 (NLT) We have not seen the risen Jesus as Thomas did, but we have not been left without His witness.

Peter, who had seen Him, wrote to believers who never had: “You love him even though you have never seen him. Though you do not see him now, you trust him.” 1 Peter 1:8 (NLT) Faith without sight is not faith without testimony; it is trust in the Jesus whom Scripture presents. Ellen G. White says: “Should the world now follow the example of Thomas, no one would believe unto salvation; for all who receive Christ must do so through the testimony of others.” The Desire of Ages, p. 807 · DA 807.4

And she draws the lesson for the church: “Jesus did not overwhelm Thomas with reproach, nor did He enter into controversy with him. He revealed Himself to the doubting one.” The Desire of Ages, p. 808 · DA 808.1 Jude says the same in one line: “And you must show mercy to those whose faith is wavering.” Jude 22 (NLT) So am I willing to trust the risen Christ beyond the conditions I have set for Him? Honest questions are not the problem; a private test that God has never promised to pass is.

Bring your questions to Jesus. Read John 20 again and notice what is actually reported. And when someone near you is struggling to believe, remember how Christ dealt with Thomas—with peace, personal attention, and a call to faith, not ridicule. He is the same crucified and risen Lord today.

Jesus met Thomas in his doubt, but called him beyond it—receive the witness of His Word, trust the risen Saviour, and treat struggling faith the way He did.
