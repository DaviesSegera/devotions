The Man Jesus Set Free

Mark 5:1–20 · 6 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-09-20

Jesus meets a man no one else could restrain, restores him, and sends him home to tell what the Lord has done. Reflect on Christ’s mercy and the testimony it gives us.

Forgiveness & graceHope & encouragementLove & service
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“Go home to your family, and tell them everything the Lord has done for you and how merciful he has been.”
Mark 5:19 (NLT, excerpt)

What happens when the person a whole town has learned to fear meets the mercy of Jesus? Jesus and His disciples had crossed the lake through a night of storm, and “they arrived at the other side of the lake, in the region of the Gerasenes.” Mark 5:1 (NLT) A man came out of the burial caves to meet Him. “This man lived in the burial caves and could no longer be restrained, even with a chain. Whenever he was put into chains and shackles—as he often was—he snapped the chains from his wrists and smashed the shackles. No one was strong enough to subdue him.” Mark 5:3–4 (NLT) Luke adds that “for a long time he had been homeless and naked, living in the tombs outside the town.” Luke 8:27 (NLT)

Matthew opens the frame wider—“two men who were possessed by demons met him” Matthew 8:28 (NLT, excerpt)—while Mark and Luke stay with one of them, and so will we. Ellen White gives us that morning as the disciples met it: “From some hiding place among the tombs, two madmen rushed upon them as if to tear them in pieces. Hanging about these men were parts of chains which they had broken in escaping from confinement.” The Desire of Ages, p. 337 · DA 337.1

He was strong enough to break every chain, and still not free.

The disciples ran. Jesus stayed. Ellen White writes, “He who had stilled the tempest, who had before met Satan and conquered him, did not flee before these demons.” The Desire of Ages, p. 337 · DA 337.2 Mark is careful with the order of things. The man “ran to meet him, and bowed low before him. With a shriek, he screamed, ‘Why are you interfering with me, Jesus, Son of the Most High God? In the name of God, I beg you, don’t torture me!’ For Jesus had already said to the spirit, ‘Come out of the man, you evil spirit.’” Mark 5:6–8 (NLT) The command had already been given; the voice we hear screaming is not the man’s own heart.

Asked for a name, the answer was, “My name is Legion, because there are many of us inside this man.” Mark 5:9 (NLT) Many—and Mark leaves us no number to count. Then “the evil spirits came out of the man and entered the pigs, and the entire herd of about 2,000 pigs plunged down the steep hillside into the lake and drowned.” Mark 5:13 (NLT, excerpt) The herd is not the story. The man is.

What no one else could subdue, Jesus set free with a word.

The herdsmen ran to the town, and the town came out to look. “A crowd soon gathered around Jesus, and they saw the man who had been possessed by the legion of demons. He was sitting there fully clothed and perfectly sane, and they were all afraid.” Mark 5:15 (NLT) Luke tells us where he was sitting: “at Jesus’ feet, fully clothed and perfectly sane.” Luke 8:35 (NLT, excerpt) Ellen White shows us that change from the inside:

“Meanwhile a marvelous change had come over the demoniacs. Light had shone into their minds. Their eyes beamed with intelligence. The countenances, so long deformed into the image of Satan, became suddenly mild, the bloodstained hands were quiet, and with glad voices the men praised God for their deliverance.” The Desire of Ages, p. 338 · DA 338.2

Seated, clothed, clear-minded, praising God. He prayed no impressive prayer and used no technique; Christ acted, and the man was given himself back. Then comes the strangest turn in the chapter. “The crowd began pleading with Jesus to go away and leave them alone.” Mark 5:17 (NLT, excerpt) Ellen White puts her finger on what was being weighed: “The loss of the swine seemed to them of greater moment than the deliverance of these captives of Satan.” The Desire of Ages, p. 338 · DA 338.3 A man they had feared for years sat quietly among them, and they asked the One who had done it to leave.

Jesus restored the man his own town had only ever feared.

One man wanted the opposite. “As Jesus was getting into the boat, the man who had been demon possessed begged to go with him. But Jesus said, ‘No, go home to your family, and tell them everything the Lord has done for you and how merciful he has been.’” Mark 5:18–19 (NLT) That “no” was not a rejection but an assignment, with a place to begin and a subject to speak about. Notice the order, because it never changes: Christ delivered him first, and only then sent him. “God saved you by his grace when you believed. And you can’t take credit for this; it is a gift from God.” Ephesians 2:8 (NLT) Grace first; then the work that grace prepared.

He had sat under no teaching and held no office, and the boat was already pulling away. Ellen White sets our own excuses beside his:

“They could not instruct the people as the disciples who had been daily with Christ were able to do. But they could tell what they knew; what they themselves had seen, and heard, and felt of the Saviour’s power. This is what everyone can do whose heart has been touched by the grace of God.” The Ministry of Healing, p. 99 · MH 99.1

So he went. “So the man started off to visit the Ten Towns of that region and began to proclaim the great things Jesus had done for him; and everyone was amazed at what he told them.” Mark 5:20 (NLT) Mark records amazement, not a tally of converts. He was faithful, and the results were God’s.

Home is the ordinary starting place—the people who already know your name. It is never an order to walk back into danger, and never a demand that anyone hand over private pain on cue. Nothing here turns illness, medication, counselling or a slow recovery into evidence of weak faith, and no one owes the church a performance. If you can say one true sentence this week—about a forgiveness you received, a change you could not have managed alone, a fear He is carrying for you now—that is the errand. “For he has rescued us from the kingdom of darkness and transferred us into the Kingdom of his dear Son, who purchased our freedom and forgave our sins.” Colossians 1:13–14 (NLT) We were rescued long before we had anything to say. What can I truthfully tell someone about the mercy Christ has shown me?

Christ’s mercy gives us something to tell.

Key Takeaways

  1. He was strong enough to break every chain, and still not free. “This man lived in the burial caves and could no longer be restrained, even with a chain.” Mark 5:3 (NLT) Ellen White tells us that hanging about these men were “parts of chains which they had broken in escaping from confinement” The Desire of Ages, p. 337 · DA 337.1—strength was never his problem, and it was never his cure.
  2. What no one else could subdue, Jesus set free with a word. “For Jesus had already said to the spirit, ‘Come out of the man, you evil spirit.’” Mark 5:8 (NLT) Ellen White writes that “He who had stilled the tempest, who had before met Satan and conquered him, did not flee before these demons” The Desire of Ages, p. 337 · DA 337.2—the disciples ran, and the Saviour stood still.
  3. Jesus restored the man his own town had only ever feared. “He was sitting there fully clothed and perfectly sane, and they were all afraid.” Mark 5:15 (NLT, excerpt) Ellen White completes the picture: “with glad voices the men praised God for their deliverance” The Desire of Ages, p. 338 · DA 338.2—and the town still weighed the loss of the pigs above it.
  4. Christ’s mercy gives us something to tell. “Go home to your family, and tell them everything the Lord has done for you and how merciful he has been.” Mark 5:19 (NLT, excerpt) Ellen White says the qualification is small and the errand is real: “they could tell what they knew; what they themselves had seen, and heard, and felt of the Saviour’s power” The Ministry of Healing, p. 99 · MH 99.1—grace comes first, and the telling comes after.

Ellen G. White: The Desire of Ages, chapter 35, “Peace, Be Still,” pp. 333–341 (DA 337.1, 337.2, 338.2 and 338.3 quoted), her account of the morning on the shore, of Christ standing His ground, of the change that came over the men, and of what the people weighed against it; The Ministry of Healing, chapter 6, “Saved to Serve,” pp. 95–107 (MH 99.1 quoted), her account of the same encounter, of the errand Christ gave the restored men (MH 98.1–98.5) and of the witness that carried through Decapolis, read alongside it.
