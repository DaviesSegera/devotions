Led by God: The Servant Finds Rebekah at the Well

Genesis 24:1–67 · 6 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-01

At the well, Abraham’s servant prays for guidance and Rebekah responds with willing kindness. Reflect on prayer, discernment, gratitude, and serving in response to Christ’s love.

Faith & trustPrayerWisdom & discernmentLove & service

Key Verse
“‘Praise the LORD, the God of my master, Abraham,’ he said. ‘The LORD has shown unfailing love and faithfulness to my master, for he has led me straight to my master’s relatives.’”
Genesis 24:27 (NLT)

A traveller arrived at a well with an important responsibility. There, his prayer for guidance met a young woman’s willingness to help.

Abraham was very old, and his son Isaac was not yet married. Abraham entrusted his oldest servant with the task of finding a wife for Isaac: “Go instead to my homeland, to my relatives, and find a wife there for my son Isaac.” Genesis 24:4 (NLT) He assured the servant that God “will send his angel ahead of you.” Genesis 24:7 (NLT, excerpt) Abraham also released the servant from his oath if the young woman was unwilling to come: “If she is unwilling to come back with you, then you are free from this oath of mine.” Genesis 24:8 (NLT, excerpt)

Faithfulness begins with the responsibility God has already placed before us.

After a long journey, the servant stopped beside a well outside the town. “It was evening, and the women were coming out to draw water.” Genesis 24:11 (NLT, excerpt) Before taking the next step, he turned to God in prayer: “‘O LORD, God of my master, Abraham,’ he prayed. ‘Please give me success today, and show unfailing love to my master, Abraham.’” Genesis 24:12 (NLT)

Ellen G. White describes his dependence on God:

“Remembering the words of Abraham, that God would send His angel with him, he prayed earnestly for positive guidance. In the family of his master he was accustomed to the constant exercise of kindness and hospitality, and he now asked that an act of courtesy might indicate the maiden whom God had chosen.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 172 · PP 172.1

In prayer, we entrust the outcome to God rather than try to control it.

“Before he had finished praying, he saw a young woman named Rebekah coming out with her water jug on her shoulder.” Genesis 24:15 (NLT, excerpt) He asked her for a drink. She gladly gave him one, then offered to do more: “I’ll draw water for your camels, too, until they have had enough to drink.” Genesis 24:19 (NLT, excerpt) “So she quickly emptied her jug into the watering trough and ran back to the well to draw water for all his camels.” Genesis 24:20 (NLT)

Rebekah responded to the needs of a stranger and his thirsty animals, continuing until the work was done.

A willing heart sees the person behind the need.

The servant took time to observe. “The servant watched her in silence, wondering whether or not the LORD had given him success in his mission.” Genesis 24:21 (NLT) He then asked about her family and whether they could accommodate him. He learned that she belonged to Abraham’s own family. She also told him, “we have room for guests.” Genesis 24:25 (NLT, excerpt)

On hearing this, the servant turned to God in worship. “The man bowed low and worshiped the LORD.” Genesis 24:26 (NLT) He then praised God with the words of our key verse.

The servant asked for help and took time to give thanks.

At Rebekah’s home, her family recognised God’s guidance in the servant’s arrival. They also asked Rebekah whether she was willing to go. “So they called Rebekah. ‘Are you willing to go with this man?’ they asked her. And she replied, ‘Yes, I will go.’” Genesis 24:58 (NLT) She set out with her childhood nurse and her servant girls. When she met Isaac, “He loved her deeply, and she was a special comfort to him after the death of his mother.” Genesis 24:67 (NLT, excerpt)

Married or single, young or old, we can all bring our choices to God and say yes to the good work He gives us.

Rebekah’s kindness points us to the far greater love of Jesus. Jesus said, “For even the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve others and to give his life as a ransom for many.” Mark 10:45 (NLT) We cannot earn His love. “God saved you by his grace when you believed. And you can’t take credit for this; it is a gift from God.” Ephesians 2:8 (NLT) His love gives us reason to trust Him and serve others gladly.

Come to Jesus, receive His grace, entrust your decisions to God, and willingly show kindness to those around you.

Reflect

  1. Am I asking God to guide my decisions, with a willingness to follow His wisdom even when it differs from what I want?
  2. Whose need can I meet with kindness today, without expecting anything in return?
  3. Will I trust God with my next step and serve faithfully where I am?

Key Takeaways

  1. Pray, then pay attention. The servant prayed, watched, and asked questions. “If you need wisdom, ask our generous God, and he will give it to you.” James 1:5 (excerpt)
  2. Kindness notices people. Rebekah helped a stranger and offered more than he had asked. “I’ll draw water for your camels, too.” Genesis 24:19 (excerpt)
  3. Give thanks when God leads. “The man bowed low and worshiped the LORD.” Genesis 24:26 Thanksgiving is part of a life of prayer.
  4. We serve because we are loved. “He has created us anew in Christ Jesus, so we can do the good things he planned for us long ago.” Ephesians 2:10 (excerpt)

A prayer for today

Father, thank You for Your unfailing love and faithfulness. Guide my decisions, and help me wait patiently when the answer is slow. Help me notice the needs of others and respond with a willing heart. Thank You for Jesus, who gave His life for me. Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
