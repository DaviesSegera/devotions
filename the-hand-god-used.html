The Hand God Used

Judges 3:12–30 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-01

God heard Israel's cry and raised up Ehud, a left-handed rescuer. Reflect on surrendering our abilities and limitations to God, giving Him the glory, and finding lasting freedom in Christ.

Key Verse
“But when the people of Israel cried out to the Lord for help, the Lord again raised up a rescuer to save them.”
Judges 3:15, NLT, excerpt

Most of us have wondered whether God could use someone like us. We may feel too ordinary, too different, or too burdened by past failures. In the book of Judges, our God used Ehud, a left-handed man, to set His people free. His story invites us to trust the Lord with the very things we think make us unsuitable for His service.

The account begins with Israel’s return to sin: “Once again the Israelites did evil in the Lord’s sight, and the Lord gave King Eglon of Moab control over Israel because of their evil.” Judges 3:12, NLT With the help of his allies, Eglon captured Jericho and subjected Israel to Moabite rule: “And the Israelites served Eglon of Moab for eighteen years.” Judges 3:14, NLT

Ellen G. White explains the spiritual cause of Israel’s repeated defeats:

“By their sins the Israelites were separated from God; His strength was removed from them, and they could no longer prevail against their enemies. Thus they were brought into subjection to the very nations that through God they might have subdued.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 544 · PP 544.4, excerpt

By turning away from the Lord, the Israelites had separated themselves from the source of their strength. Their bondage was the bitter fruit of that choice. Their need was deeper than freedom from a foreign ruler: they needed to return to God.

When we turn from God, we turn from the source of our strength.

At last, Israel cried out for help: “But when the people of Israel cried out to the Lord for help, the Lord again raised up a rescuer to save them.” Judges 3:15, NLT, excerpt The Lord heard His people and acted on their behalf. His response revealed the compassion that runs through the book of Judges: “For the Lord took pity on his people, who were burdened by oppression and suffering.” Judges 2:18, NLT, excerpt

Ellen G. White reminds us that the Lord had not abandoned His people, even through those years of unfaithfulness:

“Yet He did not utterly forsake His people. There was ever a remnant who were true to Jehovah; and from time to time the Lord raised up faithful and valiant men to put down idolatry and to deliver the Israelites from their enemies.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 545 · PP 545.1, excerpt

Israel’s suffering had followed their own disobedience, yet the Lord still heard their cry. We, too, can come to Him honestly when we have failed. His mercy gives us reason to turn back to Him, even when we are living with the consequences of our own choices.

Our failures do not put us beyond the reach of God’s mercy.

Scripture introduces the man God chose: “His name was Ehud son of Gera, a left-handed man of the tribe of Benjamin. The Israelites sent Ehud to deliver their tribute money to King Eglon of Moab.” Judges 3:15, NLT, excerpt Ehud carried the payment Moab demanded from Israel, a reminder of his people’s subjection.

The Hebrew phrase translated “left-handed” literally means “bound” or “restricted in his right hand.” Some interpreters understand this as a physical impairment. Others connect it with the skill of Benjamin’s left-handed warriors: “Among Benjamin’s elite troops, 700 were left-handed, and each of them could sling a rock and hit a target within a hairsbreadth without missing.” Judges 20:16, NLT Even the tribal name is striking: Benjamin means “son of the right hand.” The account does not tell us whether Ehud’s right hand was impaired. It does show that his left hand played a decisive part in Israel’s deliverance.

When Ehud returned to Eglon, he declared, “I have a message from God for you!” Judges 3:20, NLT, excerpt The significance of his left hand then became clear: “Ehud reached with his left hand, pulled out the dagger strapped to his right thigh, and plunged it into the king’s belly.” Judges 3:21, NLT This is a sobering account of deliverance under foreign oppression. In our own dealings with those who oppose us, we are called to obey this instruction: “Don’t let evil conquer you, but conquer evil by doing good.” Romans 12:21, NLT

The Lord used Ehud’s left hand to help bring about Israel’s deliverance. Writing about Gideon, Ellen G. White draws attention to our dependence on God:

“God does not always choose for His work men of the greatest talents, but He selects those whom He can best use.… He will make them strong by uniting their weakness to His might, and wise by connecting their ignorance with His wisdom.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 553 · PP 553.2, excerpt

We can entrust both our abilities and our limitations to the Lord. The power to accomplish His purposes comes from Him. Paul expresses this truth in the image of fragile clay jars: “We now have this light shining in our hearts, but we ourselves are like fragile clay jars containing this great treasure. This makes it clear that our great power is from God, not from ourselves.” 2 Corinthians 4:7, NLT

God can work through the abilities and limitations we surrender to Him.

After escaping, Ehud called his people to follow him: “When he arrived in the hill country of Ephraim, Ehud sounded a call to arms. Then he led a band of Israelites down from the hills. ‘Follow me,’ he said, ‘for the Lord has given you victory over Moab your enemy.’ So they followed him.” Judges 3:27–28, NLT, excerpt

Ehud’s call directed Israel’s confidence to the Lord. He recognised God as the source of their victory. The people followed, and the deliverance became a blessing to the whole nation: “So Moab was conquered by Israel that day, and there was peace in the land for eighty years.” Judges 3:30, NLT

Paul gives us the same counsel: “Therefore, as the Scriptures say, ‘If you want to boast, boast only about the Lord.’” 1 Corinthians 1:31, NLT When the Lord works through us, we have every reason to thank Him. Our gifts, our opportunities, and the strength to serve all come from His hand.

When God works through us, let us give Him the glory.

Eighty years of peace was a precious gift. Yet the opening of Judges 4 records a sorrowful return to the old ways: “After Ehud’s death, the Israelites again did evil in the Lord’s sight.” Judges 4:1, NLT Ellen G. White describes the recurring pattern of those years:

“But when the deliverer was dead, and the people were released from his authority, they would gradually return to their idols. And thus the story of backsliding and chastisement, of confession and deliverance, was repeated again and again.”Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 545 · PP 545.1, excerpt

Through Ehud, God freed Israel from Moabite rule. Yet outward freedom did not secure their lasting faithfulness to Him. The people still needed hearts surrendered to the Lord, and even the most faithful human deliverer could not remain with them forever.

Here we see our need for the lasting salvation found in Christ. Scripture says of Jesus: “Therefore he is able, once and forever, to save those who come to God through him. He lives forever to intercede with God on their behalf.” Hebrews 7:25, NLT Our Saviour lives forever. We can come to God through Him today, confident that His ministry on our behalf continues.

At the beginning of His public ministry, Jesus read these words in the synagogue at Nazareth and declared their fulfilment in Himself: “The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, for he has anointed me to bring Good News to the poor. He has sent me to proclaim that captives will be released, that the blind will see, that the oppressed will be set free…” Luke 4:18, NLT, excerpt His saving work reaches our deepest bondage: captivity to sin. Paul writes: “For he has rescued us from the kingdom of darkness and transferred us into the Kingdom of his dear Son, who purchased our freedom and forgave our sins.” Colossians 1:13–14, NLT

Ehud brought Israel a season of peace; Jesus brings lasting freedom.

Perhaps you doubt that God could use you, or you carry the burden of failures you cannot undo. Remember the Lord who heard Israel’s cry, raised up Ehud, and gave His people victory. He is our God, and we can entrust our lives to Him. Reflecting on Christ’s choice of His disciples, Ellen G. White offers this assurance:

“God takes men as they are, with the human elements in their character, and trains them for His service, if they will be disciplined and learn of Him. They are not chosen because they are perfect, but notwithstanding their imperfections, that through the knowledge and practice of the truth, through the grace of Christ, they may become transformed into His image.”The Desire of Ages, p. 294 · DA 294.4

Christ calls us to come as we are and to learn from Him. As we yield to His teaching and His grace, He transforms our character and prepares us for His service. We can trust Him both with the work He gives us and with the work He needs to do within us.

Bring Him your cry for help. Surrender your gifts, your limitations, and the parts of your life you struggle to place in His hands. Trust Him to lead you, and give Him the glory as He works through you. As you seek freedom from sin, hold firmly to Jesus’ promise: “So if the Son sets you free, you are truly free.” John 8:36, NLT

Our God can use us as we surrender to Him, and in Christ we find true freedom.

Reflect

  1. Where do I need God’s help today, and what do I need to confess as I turn to Him?
  2. What makes me feel unsuitable for God’s service, and how can I surrender it to Him?
  3. How can I give God the glory for what He is doing in and through my life?

Key Takeaways

  1. Our God hears His people’s cry. Israel’s disobedience had brought them into bondage, yet the Lord heard them and raised up a deliverer. We, too, can turn to Him for mercy and help. Judges 3:15, excerpt · quoted above
  2. God’s power is sufficient for His work. We can place our abilities and limitations in His hands, trusting Him for the strength to serve and giving Him the glory. 2 Corinthians 4:7 · quoted above
  3. Jesus brings lasting salvation. The peace Israel received through Ehud was temporary. Jesus lives forever to save those who come to God through Him and to intercede on their behalf. Hebrews 7:25 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Lord Jesus, thank You for hearing our cries and showing us mercy when we have turned away from You. Forgive our sins and draw our hearts back to You. We place our gifts, our limitations, and our past failures in Your hands. Teach us to rely on Your strength, prepare us for Your service, and help us to give You the glory for all You accomplish through us. Thank You for setting us free from sin and for Your continuing ministry on our behalf. Keep us close to You, and make us faithful as we follow You each day. In Your name we pray. Amen.
