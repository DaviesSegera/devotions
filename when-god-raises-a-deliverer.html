Today's Devotion

When God Raises a Deliverer

Judges 3 · 7 min read
Key Verse
“The Lord again raised up a rescuer to save them.”
Judges 3:15 (NLT, excerpt)
“The Lord has given you victory over Moab your enemy.”
Judges 3:28 (NLT, excerpt)

The most important turning point in Ehud’s story is not the moment in the palace. It is the moment the people cry to God and He raises a rescuer. But the story begins earlier, with a failure. “Once again the Israelites did evil in the Lord’s sight, and the Lord gave King Eglon of Moab control over Israel because of their evil.” Judges 3:12 (NLT) The word “again” matters. Judges 2 had already described a generation that “did not acknowledge the Lord or remember the mighty things he had done for Israel,” Judges 2:10 (NLT, excerpt) and the pattern that followed: “when the judge died, the people returned to their corrupt ways.” Judges 2:19 (NLT, excerpt)

Eglon of Moab gathered his allies, took Jericho, “and the Israelites served Eglon of Moab for eighteen years.” Judges 3:14 (NLT) The narrator is clear that this domination was not bad luck; it was the fruit of turning from God. Ellen G. White describes the same movement in the history of this period:

“By their sins the Israelites were separated from God; His strength was removed from them, and they could no longer prevail against their enemies. Thus they were brought into subjection to the very nations that through God they might have subdued.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 544 · PP 544.4

That is the narrator’s explanation of this episode, not a licence to explain every hardship a person suffers as punishment for a particular sin. But it does ask each of us whether we have become comfortable with something that is drawing our heart away from God.

What we turn to instead of God cannot give us the freedom we seek.

Eighteen years is a long time. Then comes the turning point, and it is not in the palace. “But when the people of Israel cried out to the Lord for help, the Lord again raised up a rescuer to save them. His name was Ehud son of Gera, a left-handed man of the tribe of Benjamin.” Judges 3:15 (NLT, excerpt) Notice the order: the people cry; God raises. Scripture does not record the words of their prayer or measure the sincerity of every heart; it records that they cried to the Lord and that He answered.

Why would He, when this was not their first failure? Judges 2 tells us what moved Him: “For the Lord took pity on his people, who were burdened by oppression and suffering.” Judges 2:18 (NLT, excerpt) The rescuer came not because Israel had earned another chance but because God is merciful. Ellen G. White, surveying this whole period, writes:

“Yet He did not utterly forsake His people. There was ever a remnant who were true to Jehovah; and from time to time the Lord raised up faithful and valiant men to put down idolatry and to deliver the Israelites from their enemies.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 545 · PP 545.1

A repeated failure is a reason to return to God, not a reason to hide from Him.

Israel’s failure did not make their prayer pointless: they cried to the Lord, and He raised a rescuer.

The text tells us little about Ehud—his father’s name, his tribe, and that he was left-handed—and it makes no claim that he was despised or weak. What it tells us is that God raised him. What followed can be told in a sentence: Ehud was sent to Eglon with Israel’s tribute and the men who carried it, returned to the king, killed him, escaped, and then called Israel to follow him. That was deliverance in an ancient conflict; it is not an instruction for how Christians treat people who oppose us.

But notice what Ehud said when he sounded the call to arms in the hill country of Ephraim: “‘Follow me,’ he said, ‘for the Lord has given you victory over Moab your enemy.’ So they followed him.” Judges 3:28 (NLT, excerpt) The man who had acted so boldly did not claim the victory; he named its source. The Israelites followed him, Moab’s forces were defeated, “and there was peace in the land for eighty years.” Judges 3:30 (NLT, excerpt) Ellen G. White names him among the deliverers of that era: “Othniel, Shamgar, and Ehud, Deborah and Barak, were raised up as deliverers of their people” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 545 · PP 545.2. The weight of her sentence, like the Bible’s, falls on the raising.

God raised the rescuer, Ehud gave the Lord the credit, and the whole people shared in the deliverance.

Eighty years of peace was a gift. But the next chapter opens with a sobering line: “After Ehud’s death, the Israelites again did evil in the Lord’s sight.” Judges 4:1 (NLT) Relief from an oppressor is not the same as a lasting return to God. That is the searching question this story leaves with us: Am I seeking only relief from my trouble, or am I returning to God Himself? It is possible to want the pressure to stop without wanting God—to pray hard in the crisis and drift once it passes.

The invitation today is not to a national deliverance but to an honest return: “If we confess our sins to him, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all wickedness.” 1 John 1:9 (NLT, excerpt) Do not wait until you feel worthy to return. Ellen G. White writes of the Saviour who receives us:

“Jesus loves to have us come to Him just as we are, sinful, helpless, dependent. We may come with all our weakness, our folly, our sinfulness, and fall at His feet in penitence. It is His glory to encircle us in the arms of His love and to bind up our wounds, to cleanse us from all impurity.” Steps to Christ, p. 52 · SC 52.2

And the deepest deliverance we need is not from a Moab. The angel told Joseph to name the child Jesus, “for he will save his people from their sins.” Matthew 1:21 (NLT, excerpt) Ehud’s deliverance gave Israel eighty years of peace; Jesus saves from sin and brings us to God.

God’s mercy calls us back to Him, not merely back to comfort—return to God, trust His mercy, offer Him your faithful service, and keep your hope in Jesus, who saves from sin.

  1.
