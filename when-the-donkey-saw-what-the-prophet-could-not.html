When the Donkey Saw What the Prophet Could Not

Numbers 22:21–35 · 6 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-09-16

Balaam’s donkey saw what the prophet could not. Reflect on Numbers 22 and the mercy of God’s correction when desire blinds the heart.

Wisdom & discernmentHumility & obedienceFaith & trust
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“Then the LORD opened Balaam’s eyes, and he saw the angel of the LORD standing in the roadway with a drawn sword in his hand.”
Numbers 22:31 (NLT)
“But Balaam was stopped from his mad course when his donkey rebuked him with a human voice.”
2 Peter 2:16 (NLT)

Israel was camped on the plains of Moab, and King Balak was afraid. He sent north for a man named Balaam: “Please come and curse these people for me.” Numbers 22:6 (NLT, excerpt). God’s answer was plain: “You are not to curse these people, for they have been blessed!” Numbers 22:12 (NLT, excerpt). Balaam sent the men away.

Balak did not give up. He sent more important men with a bigger offer. Balaam answered well: “Even if Balak were to give me his palace filled with silver and gold, I would be powerless to do anything against the will of the LORD my God” Numbers 22:18 (NLT). Then, in the same breath: “But stay here one more night, and I will see if the LORD has anything else to say to me.” Numbers 22:19 (NLT) That night God said, “Since these men have come for you, get up and go with them. But do only what I tell you to do.” Numbers 22:20 (NLT) In the morning he set off. And then: “But God was angry that Balaam was going, so he sent the angel of the LORD to stand in the road” Numbers 22:22 (NLT, excerpt). Ellen G. White shows us the man: “Thus far the Lord would permit Balaam to follow his own will, because he was determined upon it.” Patriarchs and Prophets, pp. 440–441.

There is a difference between asking what God wants and asking God to agree with what we want.

“Balaam’s donkey saw the angel of the LORD standing in the road with a drawn sword in his hand. The donkey bolted off the road into a field, but Balaam beat it and turned it back onto the road.” Numbers 22:23 (NLT) It happened again between two vineyard walls, where the donkey “tried to squeeze by and crushed Balaam’s foot against the wall” Numbers 22:25 (NLT, excerpt), and was beaten again. The third time there was no way past: “This time when the donkey saw the angel, it lay down under Balaam.” Numbers 22:27 (NLT, excerpt) It was not being stubborn. Each time it was backing away from a sword its master could not see. Ellen G. White puts it simply: “The animal saw the divine messenger, who was unperceived by the man, and turned aside from the highway into a field.” Patriarchs and Prophets, pp. 441–442.

This was God’s doing, not the animal’s: “Then the LORD gave the donkey the ability to speak.” Numbers 22:28 (NLT, excerpt) It asked only about the beatings. Balaam was too angry to find it strange: “If I had a sword with me, I would kill you!” Numbers 22:29 (NLT, excerpt) He wanted a sword. There was already a sword in that road, and he could not see it.

A man can say true things about God and still be blind to where his own desire is taking him.

Then everything changed: “Then the LORD opened Balaam’s eyes, and he saw the angel of the LORD standing in the roadway with a drawn sword in his hand.” Numbers 22:31 (NLT) He did not find the angel by being clever or religious. God opened his eyes.

“The eyes of Balaam were now opened, and he beheld the angel of God standing with drawn sword ready to slay him.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 442

The angel explained the three delays: “Three times the donkey saw me and shied away; otherwise, I would certainly have killed you by now” Numbers 22:33 (NLT, excerpt). What felt like three interruptions had been keeping him alive. But be careful: this story does not tell us to look for messages in flat tyres, delays or restless animals. Balaam did not need a sign. He needed to face what God had already said. So do we. God has given us something better than an omen: His written Word.

What Balaam kept beating out of his way was the mercy that was saving his life.

He did say, “I have sinned. I didn’t realize you were standing in the road to block my way.” Numbers 22:34 (NLT, excerpt) We should not sneer at that. But notice the reply: “Go with these men, but say only what I tell you to say.” Numbers 22:35 (NLT, excerpt) Permission is not approval. Peter tells us what was underneath. Balaam “loved to earn money by doing wrong” 2 Peter 2:15 (NLT, excerpt). And Jesus warned the church at Pergamum about people “whose teaching is like that of Balaam, who showed Balak how to trip up the people of Israel” Revelation 2:14 (NLT, excerpt). Ellen G. White traces it to the covetousness that had long held him Patriarchs and Prophets, pp. 439–440. She uses him as her example of guilt admitted without a changed heart: “Balaam, terrified by the angel standing in his pathway with drawn sword, acknowledged his guilt lest he should lose his life; but there was no genuine repentance for sin, no conversion of purpose, no abhorrence of evil.” Steps to Christ, chapter 3, p. 23.

So the question is not only whether we know God’s will, but whether we want it when obedience costs us. The invitation is open. The same Jesus who warned Pergamum said to them, “Repent of your sin” Revelation 2:16 (NLT, excerpt). Nobody has to work up enough sorrow to qualify: “If we confess our sins to him, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins.” 1 John 1:9 (NLT, excerpt) Ask God to open your eyes. Give Him the desire that competes with His will. Receive Christ’s mercy and follow His voice: “My sheep listen to my voice; I know them, and they follow me.” John 10:27 (NLT) Even a child can pray it: Jesus, help me listen when You teach me what is right.

When God’s Word corrects my direction, will I defend my way — or follow Jesus?

Reflect

  1. Is there something God has already made clear that my own desires are making hard to see?
  2. Am I surrendering my desires to God, or trying to make God’s will fit the outcome I want?
  3. When God’s Word corrects my direction, will I defend my way — or follow Jesus?

Key Takeaways

  1. There is a difference between asking what God wants and asking God to agree with what we want. God had already said, “You are not to curse these people, for they have been blessed!” Numbers 22:12 (NLT, excerpt). A bigger offer did not change the answer. Ellen G. White: “Thus far the Lord would permit Balaam to follow his own will, because he was determined upon it.” Patriarchs and Prophets, pp. 440–441.
  2. A man can say true things about God and still be blind to where his own desire is taking him. Three times the donkey turned from a sword Balaam could not see, and three times he struck it. “This time when the donkey saw the angel, it lay down under Balaam.” Numbers 22:27 (NLT, excerpt) Its speech was God’s gift, not its own wisdom: “Then the LORD gave the donkey the ability to speak.” Numbers 22:28 (NLT, excerpt)
  3. What Balaam kept beating out of his way was the mercy that was saving his life. “Three times the donkey saw me and shied away; otherwise, I would certainly have killed you by now” Numbers 22:33 (NLT, excerpt). Correction can be mercy. We test our direction by Scripture, not by unusual events.
  4. When God’s Word corrects my direction, will I defend my way — or follow Jesus? Balaam said, “I have sinned” Numbers 22:34 (NLT, excerpt), and kept going. Later Scripture shows where that led: “whose teaching is like that of Balaam, who showed Balak how to trip up the people of Israel” Revelation 2:14 (NLT, excerpt). Christ calls for the heart, and receives all who come: “My sheep listen to my voice; I know them, and they follow me.” John 10:27 (NLT)
