Jacob's Breeding Program: When God Blesses the Work of Our Hands

Genesis 30:25–43; 31:1–16 · 5 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-16

Jacob works faithfully while Laban repeatedly changes his wages. Reflect on God’s providence, honest work, fair treatment, and the care of Christ, the Good Shepherd.

Faith & trustWisdom & discernmentHumility & obedienceLove & service

Key Verse
“For I have seen how Laban has treated you.”
Genesis 31:12, NLT, excerpt

From Peeled Branches and Changing Wages to Divine Providence, Faithful Work, and Christ the Good Shepherd

What made Jacob's flocks grow so large? The peeled branches and his skill as a shepherd may catch our attention first. But to understand his story, we need to read Genesis 30 alongside Genesis 31, where Jacob tells of God's care through years of unfair treatment.

Years earlier, at Bethel, God had made Jacob a promise: “What’s more, I am with you, and I will protect you wherever you go. One day I will bring you back to this land.” Genesis 28:15, NLT, excerpt Jacob then went to live with his uncle Laban. He served Laban for many years in return for marrying Rachel and Leah. After his son Joseph was born, he wanted to go home.

Laban did not want to lose such a good worker. “I have become wealthy, for the LORD has blessed me because of you.” Genesis 30:27, NLT, excerpt Jacob agreed that Laban's flocks had grown under his care. Then he asked, “But now, what about me? When can I start providing for my own family?” Genesis 30:30, NLT, excerpt

To provide for his own household, Jacob offered to continue caring for Laban's flocks under a new agreement. His wages would consist of animals with distinctive markings: the speckled and spotted goats and the black sheep. Laban agreed. “But that very day Laban went out and removed the male goats that were streaked and spotted, all the female goats that were speckled and spotted or had white patches, and all the black sheep.” Genesis 30:35, NLT, excerpt His sons took those animals three days' journey away. Jacob was left to tend the unmarked animals.

“Then Jacob took some fresh branches from poplar, almond, and plane trees and peeled off strips of bark, making white streaks on them.” Genesis 30:37, NLT He placed the branches in the watering troughs where the flocks drank and mated. Streaked, speckled and spotted young were born. Jacob used the branches when the stronger females mated, but not with the weaker ones. Over time, his flock grew very large. “As a result, Jacob became very wealthy, with large flocks of sheep and goats, female and male servants, and many camels and donkeys.” Genesis 30:43, NLT

It would be easy to stop reading there and decide that the branches were the secret. But Genesis 31 tells us more.

As Jacob prospered, resentment grew in Laban's household. His sons grumbled, “Jacob has robbed our father of everything!” Genesis 31:1, NLT, excerpt Laban's attitude toward Jacob changed. Then God spoke: “Return to the land of your father and grandfather and to your relatives there, and I will be with you.” Genesis 31:3, NLT, excerpt

Jacob then called Rachel and Leah to the field and explained how their father had “cheated me, changing my wages ten times. But God has not allowed him to do me any harm.” Genesis 31:7, NLT, excerpt

Writing about Jacob's service despite the repeated changes to his wages, Ellen G. White observes, “Yet Jacob’s service was diligent and faithful.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 190 · PP 190.1 He remained diligent even when his employer treated him unfairly.

Despite all he had endured, Jacob recognised God's care. As he spoke to his wives, he credited God with the growth of his flocks: “In this way, God has taken your father’s animals and given them to me.” Genesis 31:9, NLT He described a dream during the mating season. In it, the angel of God said, “For I have seen how Laban has treated you.” Genesis 31:12, NLT, excerpt

The branches were in Jacob's hands, but his future was in God's hands.

Later, when Laban pursued him, Jacob recalled the demands of his years of service: “I worked for you through the scorching heat of the day and through cold and sleepless nights.” Genesis 31:40, NLT He added, “But God has seen your abuse and my hard work.” Genesis 31:42, NLT, excerpt

God sees our work, and He sees when we are treated unfairly.

Hard work and careful planning have their place, but our hope rests in the God who knows and cares for us. He guided Jacob out of an unfair situation, telling him to return home and promising to go with him. If you face unfair treatment, bring it to God in prayer, keep accurate records, and seek wise advice. Sometimes the right step is to leave. If you lead or employ others, keep your promises and pay what is fair.

Beyond the lessons about work and fairness, Jacob's years of caring for sheep and goats draw our attention to the Good Shepherd. Ellen G. White describes Christ's care:

Ellen G. White’s insight · quotation“His care for the flock is unwearied. He strengthens the weak, relieves the suffering, gathers the lambs in His arms, and carries them in His bosom.”Patriarchs and Prophets, ch. 17, “Jacob’s Flight and Exile,” p. 190 (PP 190.3)

Jesus said, “I am the good shepherd. The good shepherd sacrifices his life for the sheep.” John 10:11, NLT Jesus does not measure our worth by what we own. He gave His life for His sheep, the strong and the weak alike.

Work faithfully, treat others fairly, and trust the Shepherd who sees and cares.

Reflect

  1. How can I trust God more fully in my work and reflect His care in the way I treat others?

Key Takeaways

  1. Read the whole story. Jacob credited God: “In this way, God has taken your father’s animals and given them to me.” Genesis 31:9, NLT
  2. God sees unfair treatment. “For I have seen how Laban has treated you.” Genesis 31:12, NLT, excerpt
  3. Faithful work still matters. Ellen G. White: “Yet Jacob’s service was diligent and faithful.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 190 · PP 190.1

A prayer for today

Lord, thank You for seeing what others miss. Thank You for the work You give me and the strength to do it. Help me to work honestly and to treat others fairly. When I am treated unfairly, give me wisdom, patience and courage. Keep me from trusting my own plans more than I trust You. Jesus, Good Shepherd, carry me when I am weak, and teach me to care for others as You care for me. Amen.
