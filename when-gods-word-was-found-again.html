When God’s Word Was Found Again

2 Kings 22–23 · 6 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-09-17

When the Book of the Law was found, Josiah listened with humility and acted. Reflect on receiving God’s Word and letting Christ reshape our choices.

Humility & obedienceWisdom & discernmentForgiveness & grace
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“The king took his place of authority beside the pillar and renewed the covenant in the LORD’s presence… and all the people pledged themselves to the covenant.”
2 Kings 23:3 (NLT, excerpt)
“When the king heard what was written in the Book of the Law, he tore his clothes in despair.”
2 Kings 22:11 (NLT)

Josiah began well: “He did what was pleasing in the LORD’s sight” 2 Kings 22:2 (NLT, excerpt). Chronicles adds that he had already begun: “During the eighth year of his reign, while he was still young, Josiah began to seek the God of his ancestor David” 2 Chronicles 34:3 (NLT, excerpt). This is not a careless king who suddenly woke up, but a good king who had not yet heard everything God had said.

In his eighteenth year he ordered repairs to the temple, and in the middle of that ordinary work came the sentence that changed a nation. “Hilkiah the high priest said to Shaphan the court secretary, ‘I have found the Book of the Law in the LORD’s Temple!’” 22:8 (NLT, excerpt) Nobody had gone searching for it. It was found in the house of God, during repairs to it. Ellen G. White tells us how: “during his reign the temple copy of the book of the law, through careless neglect, had become lost. Thus for many years the people generally were deprived of its instruction.” Prophets and Kings, p. 392 · PK 392.2

The temple was being repaired, and all that time the Word that had built the nation lay unread inside it.

“Shaphan also told the king, ‘Hilkiah the priest has given me a scroll.’ So Shaphan read it to the king” 22:10 (NLT). Josiah did not read it himself that day; he listened. Ellen G. White writes, “Josiah was deeply stirred as he heard read for the first time the exhortations and warnings recorded in this ancient manuscript.” Prophets and Kings, p. 393 · PK 393.2 Then: “When the king heard what was written in the Book of the Law, he tore his clothes in despair” 22:11 (NLT). Tearing your clothes was how deep grief was shown then; we are shown a heart, not given a ritual.

He did not argue with the book. He asked for help to understand it: “Inquire about the words written in this scroll that has been found… We have not been doing everything it says we must do” 22:13 (NLT, excerpt). Notice the word we — he did not stand outside his people’s guilt.

“Overwhelmed with sorrow and dismay, Josiah rent his garments and bowed before God in agony of spirit, seeking pardon for the sins of an impenitent nation.” Prophets and Kings, p. 398 · PK 398.2

The prophet Huldah’s answer held two things together. The judgment was real: “I am going to bring disaster on this city and its people” 22:16 (NLT, excerpt). And God had seen one man’s heart: “You were sorry and humbled yourself before the LORD… And I have indeed heard you, says the LORD” 22:19 (NLT, excerpt). Holiness and mercy were in the same message.

Revival does not begin when we admire the Word. It begins when we let the Word tell the truth about us.

What Josiah did next makes this more than a moving moment. “Then the king summoned all the elders of Judah and Jerusalem… all the people from the least to the greatest. There the king read to them the entire Book of the Covenant” 23:1–2 (NLT, excerpt). The book had been read to him in a room; now he read it to a nation. Ellen G. White adds: “The royal reader was deeply affected, and he delivered his message with the pathos of a broken heart.” Prophets and Kings, p. 400 · PK 400.2

Then the key text: he “renewed the covenant in the LORD’s presence”, pledging “to obey the LORD by keeping all his commands, laws, and decrees with all his heart and soul”, and “all the people pledged themselves to the covenant” 23:3 (NLT, excerpts). The king went first, and the people followed. Ellen G. White notes: “The response was more hearty than the king had dared hope for.” Prophets and Kings, p. 400 · PK 400.3

What God says to us in private is not meant to stay there.

Then the promise touched real life. The objects used for false worship were burned; the shrines were shut down. The summary tells us why: “He did this in obedience to the laws written in the scroll that Hilkiah the priest had found in the LORD’s Temple” 23:24 (NLT, excerpt). Worship was restored, too: “You must celebrate the Passover to the LORD your God” 23:21 (NLT, excerpt). These were the acts of an ancient king over the national worship of Judah, not a licence for coercion today. What carries across is the principle, not the crown: what God shows me to be wrong, I deal with in my own life.

The account stays honest to the end. “Even so, the LORD was very angry with Judah” 23:26 (NLT, excerpt). One good king’s obedience did not undo generations of national rebellion. Ellen G. White says it plainly: “But the zeal of Josiah, acceptable though it was to God, could not atone for the sins of past generations.” Prophets and Kings, p. 405 · PK 405.1 A reform can clear the idols out of a land and still not change a heart. Only Christ can do that, and the Scriptures Josiah heard pointed to Him: “Then Jesus took them through the writings of Moses and all the prophets, explaining from all the Scriptures the things concerning himself” Luke 24:27 (NLT). Bring Him what His Word has shown you: “But if we confess our sins to him, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins” 1 John 1:9 (NLT, excerpt). Then do what you have heard: “But don’t just listen to God’s word. You must do what it says” James 1:22 (NLT, excerpt). Even a child can pray it: Jesus, help me listen to Your Word and do what is right.

Will I only hear God’s Word, or will I let Christ reshape my worship, choices and loyalties through it?

Reflect

  1. Has God’s Word become familiar in my surroundings but neglected in my decisions?
  2. What clear act of obedience should follow what I have heard?
  3. Will I only hear God’s Word, or will I let Christ reshape my worship, choices and loyalties through it?

Key Takeaways

  1. God’s Word can be near and still be neglected. The book was found inside the temple that was being repaired: “I have found the Book of the Law in the LORD’s Temple!” 2 Kings 22:8 (NLT, excerpt) Ellen G. White: “during his reign the temple copy of the book of the law, through careless neglect, had become lost.” Prophets and Kings, p. 392 · PK 392.2
  2. Humility receives correction instead of defending itself. “When the king heard what was written in the Book of the Law, he tore his clothes in despair” 22:11 (NLT), and he sent men to ask what it meant. God’s answer named exactly that: “You were sorry and humbled yourself before the LORD… And I have indeed heard you, says the LORD” 22:19 (NLT, excerpt).
  3. Hearing becomes doing, and it becomes shared. Josiah read the covenant book to the whole assembly and “renewed the covenant in the LORD’s presence… and all the people pledged themselves to the covenant” 23:3 (NLT, excerpt). Every reform that followed had an address in the book: “He did this in obedience to the laws written in the scroll” 23:24 (NLT, excerpt).
  4. Outward reform cannot change a heart; Christ can. “Even so, the LORD was very angry with Judah” 23:26 (NLT, excerpt), and Ellen G. White is honest about the limits: “the zeal of Josiah, acceptable though it was to God, could not atone for the sins of past generations.” Prophets and Kings, p. 405 · PK 405.1 Scripture leads us to Jesus, and mercy is found in Him: “But if we confess our sins to him, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins” 1 John 1:9 (NLT, excerpt).
