Enoch: Walking With God

Genesis 5:21–24 · 6 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-09-23

Enoch’s brief biography is defined by close fellowship with God. Reflect on faith, ordinary daily obedience, courageous witness, and hope in Christ’s return.

Faith & trustHumility & obedienceHope & encouragement
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“Enoch lived 365 years, walking in close fellowship with God. Then one day he disappeared, because God took him.”
Genesis 5:23–24 (NLT)
“For before he was taken up, he was known as a person who pleased God.”
Hebrews 11:5 (NLT, excerpt)

Genesis gives Enoch only a few verses, but repeats the one truth by which his life should be remembered: he lived in close fellowship with God.

Read the chapter he stands in and you hear why. Genesis 5 is a list of lives, and each closes the same way: a man is born, he has sons and daughters, he lives many years, “and then he died.” Genesis 5:5 (NLT, excerpt) Adam, Seth, Enosh, Kenan, Mahalalel, Jared — eight times a whole life is set down and closed with the same three words. It is a family tree with a shadow across it.

Genesis gives Enoch four verses, and spends two of them saying the same thing twice.

Enoch, son of Jared, became the father of Methuselah at sixty-five. “After the birth of Methuselah, Enoch lived in close fellowship with God for another 300 years, and he had other sons and daughters. Enoch lived 365 years, walking in close fellowship with God. Then one day he disappeared, because God took him.” Genesis 5:22–24 (NLT)

That is the whole biography: a relationship, stated twice, and an ending that breaks the chapter's rhythm.

Hebrews gives us the word for it. “It was by faith that Enoch was taken up to heaven without dying—‘he disappeared, because God took him.’ For before he was taken up, he was known as a person who pleased God.” Hebrews 11:5 (NLT) Then the letter turns from him to us: “it is impossible to please God without faith. Anyone who wants to come to him must believe that God exists and that he rewards those who sincerely seek him.” Hebrews 11:6 (NLT, excerpt) This is not the record of a spiritual athlete, but of a man who kept coming to God — and of a God who received him.

Faith does not make us impressive before God; it teaches us to depend on the God who is faithful.

So what did three hundred years of it look like? Ellen G. White shows us how it filled his days:

Ellen G. White“Enoch's walk with God was not in a trance or vision, but in all the duties of his daily life.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 85

She places him among people — “as a husband and father, a friend, a citizen” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 85 — labouring among them, then withdrawing for a season of prayer.

And that is where the repeated phrase lands: fellowship with God is not a retreat from the life you have. It is that life, lived consciously with Him — letting Scripture speak, telling Him the truth about a day, turning from what you know is wrong, and coming back when you have drifted.

That life also spoke. Jude remembers Enoch as a prophet: “Enoch, who lived in the seventh generation after Adam, prophesied about these people. He said, ‘Listen! The Lord is coming with countless thousands of his holy ones to execute judgment on the people of the world.’” Jude 14–15 (NLT, excerpt)

Communion with God gave Enoch courage to speak, but it never made him independent of God's mercy.

And then the sentence no one forgets: “Then one day he disappeared, because God took him.” Genesis 5:24 (NLT, excerpt) Enoch's going was God's gift. Most of God's people still fall asleep in death, and they are not forgotten. Paul writes so that those who mourn “will not grieve like people who have no hope” 1 Thessalonians 4:13 (NLT, excerpt), then names the hope: the Lord himself will come, and the dead in Christ, with those still living, will meet Him. “So encourage each other with these words.” 1 Thessalonians 4:18 (NLT)

Which leaves a question for today: not about disappearing, but about walking. “Remain in me, and I will remain in you… you cannot be fruitful unless you remain in me.” John 15:4 (NLT, excerpts) What grows in us grows from Christ; what is forgiven in us is forgiven through Him; and our standing with God was never bought by our record — “God saved you by his grace when you believed. And you can't take credit for this; it is a gift from God.” Ephesians 2:8 (NLT)

Enoch's story does not teach us to escape death by effort; it teaches us to hope in the God who has power over death.

Reflect

  1. Where in my ordinary life is God inviting me to walk more closely with Him?
  2. How can my life and words stay faithful without becoming harsh, fearful, or worldly?
  3. Will I walk with God today — in faith, fellowship, obedience, and hope?

Key Takeaways

  1. Genesis defines Enoch by fellowship, not by disappearance. The phrase stands twice in four verses — “lived in close fellowship with God” Genesis 5:22 and “walking in close fellowship with God” Genesis 5:24 — and it stands around an ordinary life: a father, other sons and daughters, three hundred years of ordinary days.
  2. Hebrews gives us the word for it. Enoch “was known as a person who pleased God” Hebrews 11:5 (excerpt), and it was faith that pleased Him Hebrews 11:6 — trust in a God who is really there and welcomes those who come.
  3. Communion made him a witness. Jude remembers Enoch as a prophet Jude 14–15; Ellen G. White describes labour and prayer growing together — “The greater and more pressing his labors, the more constant and earnest were his prayers” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 86. Courage and compassion belong in the same life.
  4. The ending is God's gift, and the hope is Christ's. “he disappeared, because God took him” Genesis 5:24 (excerpt) — God acted. For those who have died in Christ and those alive at His coming alike, the promise is His return 1 Thessalonians 4:13–18.

Prayer

Father, thank You that You welcome those who come to You. Teach me to live today in Your company — honest with You, attentive to Your word, ready to obey what I already understand. Forgive what needs forgiving and cleanse me through Jesus. Give me courage without harshness and compassion without compromise, and hold me in hope until Christ returns. Amen.
