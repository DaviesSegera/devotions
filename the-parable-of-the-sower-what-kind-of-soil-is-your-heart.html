The Parable of the Sower: What Kind of Soil Is Your Heart?

Luke 8:4–15 · 5 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-09

Jesus’ parable of the sower shows one seed falling on four kinds of ground. Reflect on what happens to God’s Word after we hear it, and the hope that Jesus can change the soil of our hearts.

Faith & trustHumility & obedienceHope & encouragementForgiveness & grace

Key Verse
“And the seeds that fell on the good soil represent honest, good-hearted people who hear God’s word, cling to it, and patiently produce a huge harvest.”
Luke 8:15 (NLT)

From Hearing God's Word to Bearing Lasting Fruit

Two people can sit through the same sermon and hear the same Bible text. One forgets it before lunch. The other carries it all week, and it begins to change how she lives. Jesus told a story that explains the difference, and it offers hope for every heart that wants to change.

A large crowd had gathered by the lake. Jesus “got into a boat. Then he sat there and taught as the people stood on the shore.” Matthew 13:2 (NLT, excerpt) He began, “A farmer went out to plant his seed. As he scattered it across his field, some seed fell on a footpath, where it was stepped on, and the birds ate it.” Luke 8:5 (NLT)

Other seed fell among rocks. “It began to grow, but the plant soon wilted and died for lack of moisture.” Luke 8:6 (NLT, excerpt) Other seed “fell among thorns that grew up with it and choked out the tender plants.” Luke 8:7 (NLT, excerpt) But some seed fell on fertile soil, and it “produced a crop that was a hundred times as much as had been planted!” Luke 8:8 (NLT, excerpt)

The disciples asked what the story meant. Jesus told them plainly, “This is the meaning of the parable: The seed is God’s word.” Luke 8:11 (NLT)

The farmer is the same and the seed is the same. Only the ground is different.

Ellen G. White explains that this is exactly where Jesus wanted His hearers to look. Through the story He was asking each of them:

Ellen G. White’s insight · quotation“The question of greatest importance to you is, How do you treat My message?”Christ’s Object Lessons, ch. 2, “The Sower Went Forth to Sow,” p. 43 (COL 43.2)

Jesus then described each kind of ground. The footpath stands for “those who hear the message, only to have the devil come and take it away from their hearts and prevent them from believing and being saved.” Luke 8:12 (NLT, excerpt) The seed never sinks in. We can hear a sermon and think it is meant for someone else.

The rocky ground stands for “those who hear the message and receive it with joy. But since they don’t have deep roots, they believe for a while, then they fall away when they face temptation.” Luke 8:13 (NLT, excerpt) A happy start is good. But roots grow out of sight, through daily trust in Jesus. Ellen White writes, “it is by the invisible union of the soul with Christ, through faith, that the spiritual life is nourished.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 47 · COL 47.1

The thorny ground stands for those whose message “is crowded out by the cares and riches and pleasures of this life. And so they never grow into maturity.” Luke 8:14 (NLT, excerpt) Thorns grow slowly beside the plant until it has no room left. Worry, money, and busy days can crowd out Jesus in the same quiet way.

These pictures are not labels to put on other people. We cannot see another person's heart, and someone who is tired, grieving, or struggling to understand needs our patience and help. Jesus asks each of us to look at our own heart.

The question is not only whether the Word entered our ears, but whether we have welcomed it into our lives.

Here is the hope. Ellen G. White writes:

Ellen G. White’s insight · quotation“The wayside, the stony-ground, the thorny-ground hearers need not remain such. The Spirit of God is ever seeking to break the spell of infatuation that holds men absorbed in worldly things, and to awaken a desire for the imperishable treasure.”Christ’s Object Lessons, ch. 2, “The Sower Went Forth to Sow,” p. 56 (COL 56.1)

We cannot change our hearts by ourselves. But the Holy Spirit is already working, and we can respond. We can turn from our sin, ask for forgiveness, and let Jesus clear the ground.

Then the good soil. Jesus describes “honest, good-hearted people who hear God’s word, cling to it, and patiently produce a huge harvest.” Luke 8:15 (NLT, excerpt) Ellen White assures us that this honest heart “is not a heart without sin” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 58 · COL 58.2. It is a heart that admits its need and welcomes God's mercy.

Good soil is not a perfect heart; it is a heart willing to let Jesus do His work.

Good soil hears the Word. It clings to the Word through the week, in hard conversations and quiet temptations. And it waits patiently, because fruit takes time.

That fruit comes from Jesus. He said, “Those who remain in me, and I in them, will produce much fruit. For apart from me you can do nothing.” John 15:5 (NLT, excerpt) The Holy Spirit grows “love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.” Galatians 5:22–23 (NLT, excerpt) Such fruit blesses our homes, our churches, and the people around us.

Jesus is the Sower who came to us and gave His life for us. He is still sowing His Word today. Come to Him as you are. Ask Him to soften what is hard, deepen your roots, and clear away what crowds Him out.

Bring Jesus your heart as it is, hold on to His Word, and let Him grow the fruit.

Reflect

  1. What happens to God's Word in me after I hear it?
  2. What is crowding Jesus out of my heart right now?
  3. What one change can I invite Him to begin in me this week?

Key Takeaways

  1. The seed is God's Word. Jesus said, “This is the meaning of the parable: The seed is God’s word.” Luke 8:11 (NLT)
  2. How we receive it matters. Some hear, but “the message is crowded out by the cares and riches and pleasures of this life.” Luke 8:14 (NLT, excerpt)
  3. Jesus can change the soil. Good soil belongs to people who “hear God’s word, cling to it, and patiently produce a huge harvest.” Luke 8:15 (NLT, excerpt)

A prayer for today

Lord Jesus, thank You for sowing Your Word in our hearts. Forgive us for hearing You and walking away unchanged. By Your Spirit, soften what is hard, deepen our roots in You, and clear away the cares that crowd You out. Help us hold on to Your Word and wait patiently for the fruit You grow. Amen.
