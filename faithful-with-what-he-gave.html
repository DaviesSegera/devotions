Faithful with What He Gave

Matthew 25:14–30 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-30

Jesus' parable of the talents invites us to trust God's love and use what He has given. Reflect on grace, freedom from fear, and faithfulness in the little things that serve others.

Key Verse
“Well done, my good and faithful servant. You have been faithful in handling this small amount, so now I will give you many more responsibilities. Let’s celebrate together!”
Matthew 25:21, NLT, excerpt

It is easy to look at others and feel we have little to offer. Someone else seems able to sing, teach or give more than we can, and we quietly begin to wonder whether our own part matters. Jesus’ parable of the talents speaks to that feeling.

In the days before His crucifixion, Jesus sat on the Mount of Olives and spoke to His disciples about His return. He urged them: “So you, too, must keep watch! For you do not know the day or hour of my return.” Matthew 25:13, NLT What does it mean to keep watch? Ellen G. White describes the parable that follows as Jesus’ answer:

“Then He showed what it means to watch for His coming. The time is to be spent, not in idle waiting, but in diligent working. This lesson He taught in the parable of the talents.”Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 325 · COL 325.1, excerpt

Jesus began: “Again, the Kingdom of Heaven can be illustrated by the story of a man going on a long trip. He called together his servants and entrusted his money to them while he was gone. He gave five bags of silver to one, two bags of silver to another, and one bag of silver to the last—dividing it in proportion to their abilities. He then left on his trip.” Matthew 25:14–15, NLT

Older translations call each bag of silver a “talent”. Even one talent was a fortune.

Even the servant who received the least was entrusted with something of great value.

Ellen G. White understood the master as Christ and the servants as His followers. She explained that the silver represents, above all, the gifts of the Holy Spirit, as well as every ability and blessing God gives us. The apostle Peter also reminds us to use what God has given: “God has given each of you a gift from his great variety of spiritual gifts. Use them well to serve one another.” 1 Peter 4:10, NLT

The servants received different amounts, but notice how the master welcomed the servant who had been given two bags: “The servant who had received the two bags of silver came forward and said, ‘Master, you gave me two bags of silver to invest, and I have earned two more.’ The master said, ‘Well done, my good and faithful servant. You have been faithful in handling this small amount, so now I will give you many more responsibilities. Let’s celebrate together!’” Matthew 25:22–23, NLT The servant who had gained five more bags had received exactly the same praise. The master valued the faithfulness of each servant, even though their results differed. Ellen G. White draws a comforting lesson from this:

“None need lament that they have not received larger gifts; for He who has apportioned to every man is equally honored by the improvement of each trust, whether it be great or small.”Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 328 · COL 328.3, excerpt

God looks for faithfulness, whatever the size of the gift.

Then the servant with one bag came forward. Earlier in the story, Jesus had said: “But the servant who received the one bag of silver dug a hole in the ground and hid the master’s money.” Matthew 25:18, NLT Now the servant explained his actions: “Master, I knew you were a harsh man, harvesting crops you didn’t plant and gathering crops you didn’t cultivate. I was afraid I would lose your money, so I hid it in the earth. Look, here is your money back.” Matthew 25:24–25, NLT, excerpt

He had neither wasted nor stolen the money, but he had left it unused. He was afraid because he believed his master was harsh. Ellen G. White recognised this same pattern in people’s relationship with God:

“Thus men excuse their neglect of God’s gifts. They look upon God as severe and tyrannical, as watching to spy out their mistakes and visit them with judgments. They charge Him with demanding what He has never given, with reaping where He has not sown.”Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 362 · COL 362.2

Ellen G. White responds to this view of God by pointing us to the cross: “All the blessings of this life and of the life to come are delivered to us stamped with the cross of Calvary.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 362 · COL 362.3, excerpt The God who gave His Son is not a harsh master waiting for us to fail.

We too can hide our gifts out of fear. We may be afraid of failing, or feel that we are not good enough. The apostle Paul offered this encouragement: “This is why I remind you to fan into flames the spiritual gift God gave you when I laid my hands on you. For God has not given us a spirit of fear and timidity, but of power, love, and self-discipline.” 2 Timothy 1:6–7, NLT Ellen G. White reminds us that true humility depends on God’s strength: “Real humility fulfills God’s purposes by depending upon His strength.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 363 · COL 363.3, excerpt

When we know God’s love, we are free to use what He has given.

The servant who buried his gift was the one who had received the least. That speaks to us when our own gifts seem small. Much of life is lived in ordinary moments: doing our work, caring for our family, offering a kind word or praying for a friend. Jesus taught: “If you are faithful in little things, you will be faithful in large ones.” Luke 16:10, NLT, excerpt Ellen G. White offers encouragement to anyone who feels they have little to give:

“However small your talent, God has a place for it. That one talent, wisely used, will accomplish its appointed work. By faithfulness in little duties, we are to work on the plan of addition, and God will work for us on the plan of multiplication. These littles will become the most precious influences in His work.”Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 360 · COL 360.1

We offer one small act of faithfulness, then another. God multiplies their effect, often in ways we never see.

What seems small to us is never small to God.

The parable calls us to take seriously the gifts we have received. It ends with a solemn warning: “To those who use well what they are given, even more will be given, and they will have an abundance. But from those who do nothing, even what little they have will be taken away. Now throw this useless servant into outer darkness, where there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth.” Matthew 25:29–30, NLT Ellen G. White explains that this loss begins even in this life: “As in the natural, so in the spiritual world: every power unused will weaken and decay.” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 364 · COL 364.1, excerpt

Does this mean we must earn God’s approval? Scripture offers this assurance: “God saved you by his grace when you believed. And you can’t take credit for this; it is a gift from God. Salvation is not a reward for the good things we have done, so none of us can boast about it. For we are God’s masterpiece. He has created us anew in Christ Jesus, so we can do the good things he planned for us long ago.” Ephesians 2:8–10, NLT We are saved by grace and called to a life of good works. Ellen G. White held faith and faithful service together: “In the parable, Jesus carries his hearers forward to the general Judgment, when every man’s case will be decided according to his faithfulness in the work left for him to do. Men are justified by faith, but judged and rewarded according to their works.” Signs of the Times, November 20, 1884 · ST November 20, 1884, par. 6, excerpt

Ellen G. White reminds us that the faithful servants took no credit for themselves. The money they had received belonged to their master:

“The capital was the Lord’s; the improvement is His. Had not the Saviour bestowed upon them His love and grace, they would have been bankrupt for eternity.”Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 360 · COL 360.5, excerpt

Grace gives us everything we have, and grace invites us to use it.

Jesus did more than tell this story. He lived a life of perfect faithfulness and service: “For even the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve others and to give his life as a ransom for many.” Matthew 20:28, NLT In the teaching that follows this parable, Jesus showed that serving others is also service to Him: “I tell you the truth, when you did it to one of the least of these my brothers and sisters, you were doing it to me!” Matthew 25:40, NLT, excerpt

Perhaps what you have to offer feels like just one bag of silver: a modest skill, a little time or a willing heart. Do not bury what He has given you. Ellen G. White invites us to ask: “The question that most concerns us is not, How much have I received? but, What am I doing with that which I have?” Christ’s Object Lessons, p. 329 · COL 329.2, excerpt Bring what you have to Jesus, and use it to serve the people He places around you.

We can serve Him faithfully with what He has given us and look forward to sharing our Master’s joy.

Reflect

  1. What gift, ability or opportunity has God entrusted to me that I have kept hidden?
  2. Has fear, or a belief that God is harsh, kept me from using what He has given me?
  3. What small act of faithfulness can I offer to God this week to serve someone else?

Key Takeaways

  1. God values faithfulness with whatever He gives us. The servant with two bags received the same praise as the servant with five. Matthew 25:22–23 · quoted above
  2. God’s love frees us to use our gifts. God has not given us a spirit of fear, but of power, love and self-discipline. 2 Timothy 1:6–7 · quoted above
  3. Grace comes first and leads us into service. We are saved by grace, not by works. In Christ, we are created anew to do the good things God has planned for us. Ephesians 2:8–10 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Gracious Father, thank You for every gift You have entrusted to us, and above all for Jesus, who gave His life for us. Forgive us for hiding Your gifts because we were afraid or thought they were too small to matter. Help us to trust Your love, and fill us with Your Spirit. Teach us to be faithful in little things and to serve those around us with willing hearts. Keep us watching for Jesus’ return and serving You day by day. In Jesus’ name we pray. Amen.
