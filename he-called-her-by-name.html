He Called Her by Name: Mary Magdalene and the Risen Lord

Luke 8:1–3; John 19:25; John 20:1–18 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-04

Jesus set Mary Magdalene free, drew near in her grief, and called her by name. Reflect on grateful love, resurrection hope, and sharing the good news that He lives.

Forgiveness & graceHope & encouragementFaith & trustLove & service

Key Verse
“Mary!” Jesus said. She turned to him and cried out, “Rabboni!” (which is Hebrew for “Teacher”).
John 20:16 (NLT)

What does it mean to follow Jesus with a heart full of gratitude for all He has done for you? Mary Magdalene’s story begins with freedom and leads to an empty tomb, where the risen Lord calls her by name.

Luke introduces Mary during Jesus’ ministry in Galilee: “Soon afterward Jesus began a tour of the nearby towns and villages, preaching and announcing the Good News about the Kingdom of God.” Luke 8:1, NLT, excerpt The Twelve went with Him, “along with some women who had been cured of evil spirits and diseases. Among them were Mary Magdalene, from whom he had cast out seven demons;” Luke 8:2, NLT The name Magdalene is usually understood to mean that she came from Magdala, a town on the shore of the Sea of Galilee.

In The Desire of Ages, Ellen G. White understands Mary of Bethany and Mary Magdalene to be the same woman. She describes Christ’s compassion towards Mary:

“Mary had been looked upon as a great sinner, but Christ knew the circumstances that had shaped her life. He might have extinguished every spark of hope in her soul, but He did not. It was He who had lifted her from despair and ruin. Seven times she had heard His rebuke of the demons that controlled her heart and mind.”The Desire of Ages, p. 568 · DA 568.1, excerpt

Ellen G. White adds, “When to human eyes her case appeared hopeless, Christ saw in Mary capabilities for good.” The Desire of Ages, p. 568 · DA 568.2, excerpt Jesus still sees what His grace can do in those whom others have given up on.

Mary responded to her freedom with grateful service. Luke also names Joanna and Susanna, “and many others who were contributing from their own resources to support Jesus and his disciples.” Luke 8:3, NLT, excerpt

Mary’s devotion grew from gratitude for what Jesus had done for her.

That devotion endured even at the cross. Mark tells us, “Some women were there, watching from a distance, including Mary Magdalene,” Mark 15:40, NLT, excerpt John describes her standing near the cross: “Standing near the cross were Jesus’ mother, and his mother’s sister, Mary (the wife of Clopas), and Mary Magdalene.” John 19:25, NLT

When Jesus’ body was taken down and laid in a tomb, Mary remained nearby: “Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joseph saw where Jesus’ body was laid.” Mark 15:47, NLT Then the women “went home and prepared spices and ointments to anoint his body. But by the time they were finished the Sabbath had begun, so they rested as required by the law.” Luke 23:56, NLT, excerpt

Ellen G. White describes that sorrowful evening:

“The women were last at the cross, and last at the tomb of Christ. While the evening shades were gathering, Mary Magdalene and the other Marys lingered about the resting place of their Lord, shedding tears of sorrow over the fate of Him whom they loved.”The Desire of Ages, p. 774 · DA 774.1, excerpt

Mary could not undo what had happened, yet she stayed close to the One she loved. Sometimes faithfulness means remaining near Jesus when we cannot make sense of our sorrow.

Mary stayed near Jesus even when her hopes seemed buried with Him.

When the Sabbath was over, she returned to the tomb: “Early on Sunday morning, while it was still dark, Mary Magdalene came to the tomb and found that the stone had been rolled away from the entrance.” John 20:1, NLT She ran to Simon Peter and the other disciple with distressing news: “They have taken the Lord’s body out of the tomb, and we don’t know where they have put him!” John 20:2, NLT, excerpt The two disciples ran to the tomb, saw the linen wrappings, and returned home.

Mary stayed behind. “Mary was standing outside the tomb crying, and as she wept, she stooped and looked in.” John 20:11, NLT Two angels spoke to her: “Dear woman, why are you crying?” the angels asked her. “Because they have taken away my Lord,” she replied, “and I don’t know where they have put him.” John 20:13, NLT John continues, “She turned to leave and saw someone standing there. It was Jesus, but she didn’t recognize him.” John 20:14, NLT

The same gentle question came again, this time from Jesus: “Dear woman, why are you crying?” Jesus asked her. “Who are you looking for?” She thought he was the gardener. “Sir,” she said, “if you have taken him away, tell me where you have put him, and I will go and get him.” John 20:15, NLT

Ellen G. White writes of that moment:

“In the garden, Mary had stood weeping, when Jesus was close beside her. Her eyes were so blinded by tears that she did not discern Him.”The Desire of Ages, p. 794 · DA 794.4, excerpt

To “discern” means to recognise. Grief can cloud our vision too. We may feel that Jesus is far away even when He is close beside us. He did not scold Mary for her tears; He gently asked whom she was seeking. He had promised His disciples, “So you have sorrow now, but I will see you again; then you will rejoice, and no one can rob you of that joy.” John 16:22, NLT

Jesus was closer to Mary than she knew, even while she wept.

Then Jesus spoke a single word: “Mary!” Jesus said. She turned to him and cried out, “Rabboni!” (which is Hebrew for “Teacher”). John 20:16, NLT

Ellen G. White captures the moment of recognition: “But now in His own familiar voice Jesus said to her, ‘Mary.’ Now she knew that it was not a stranger who was addressing her, and turning she saw before her the living Christ.” The Desire of Ages, p. 790 · DA 790.2, excerpt

Jesus had described Himself as the Good Shepherd: “He calls his own sheep by name and leads them out.” John 10:3, NLT, excerpt Mary had not recognised Jesus by sight, but she knew His voice when He spoke her name. She had come looking for Him, and He made Himself known to her.

The risen Lord knew Mary by name, and He knows each of us by name too.

Jesus then entrusted her with a message: “Don’t cling to me,” Jesus said, “for I haven’t yet ascended to the Father. But go find my brothers and tell them, ‘I am ascending to my Father and your Father, to my God and your God.’” John 20:17, NLT

Ellen G. White understood these words to mean that Jesus would first go to His Father to receive assurance that His sacrifice had been accepted: “He ascended to the heavenly courts, and from God Himself heard the assurance that His atonement for the sins of men had been ample, that through His blood all might gain eternal life.” The Desire of Ages, p. 790 · DA 790.3, excerpt Here, “atonement” means Christ’s work of bringing us back to God, and “ample” means more than enough.

Notice whom Jesus calls “my brothers.” Ellen G. White reminds us, “All the disciples had forsaken Jesus, and the call to meet Him again includes them all. He has not cast them off.” The Desire of Ages, p. 793 · DA 793.3, excerpt The risen Lord was welcoming back the very friends who had failed Him.

Mary obeyed: “Mary Magdalene found the disciples and told them, ‘I have seen the Lord!’ Then she gave them his message.” John 20:18, NLT

Ellen G. White connects the joy of that morning with the grace that had first set Mary free:

“Mary Magdalene, out of whom He cast seven devils, was the last at the Saviour’s tomb and the first whom He greeted in the morning of His resurrection.”Thoughts From the Mount of Blessing, p. 129 · MB 129.2, excerpt

To a woman others had once looked down on, Jesus entrusted the greatest news in history.

The one Jesus set free became the first to announce that He is alive.

Mary’s story invites us to walk the same path of faith. Christ sets us free, stays near us in grief, calls us by name, and sends us to tell others that He lives. He said, “Since I live, you also will live.” John 14:19, NLT, excerpt Ellen G. White writes, “Jesus lives, and because He lives, we shall live also.” The Desire of Ages, p. 794 · DA 794.6, excerpt

The risen Lord is still at work for us: “Therefore he is able, once and forever, to save those who come to God through him. He lives forever to intercede with God on their behalf.” Hebrews 7:25, NLT To intercede means to plead on someone’s behalf. Ellen G. White adds, “He lives to make intercession for us. Grasp this hope, and it will hold the soul like a sure, tried anchor.” The Desire of Ages, p. 794 · DA 794.6, excerpt

Perhaps, like Mary, you are searching for Jesus through tears today. Listen for His voice as you turn to His Word. The One who called Mary by name knows your name too.

Jesus sets us free, calls us by name, and sends us to tell the world that He lives. May we follow Him with faithful hearts.

Reflect

  1. What has Jesus set me free from, and how can I express my gratitude through the way I serve Him?
  2. When grief or disappointment blurs my sight, how can I listen for Jesus’ voice in His Word?
  3. Whom can I encourage this week with the good news that Jesus is alive and knows them by name?

Key Takeaways

  1. Grateful love grows in response to grace. Jesus set Mary free, and she responded by following Him and supporting His ministry from her own resources. Luke 8:2 · quoted above
  2. Jesus stays near us in our sorrow. As Mary wept at the empty tomb, the risen Lord stood beside her and gently asked why she was crying. John 20:13–15 · quoted above
  3. He calls us by name and sends us to share His good news. One word from Jesus turned Mary’s grief into joy, and she became the first to say, “I have seen the Lord!” John 20:16–18 · quoted above

A prayer for today

Lord Jesus, thank You for seeing what Your grace can do in those whom others have given up on. Thank You for setting Mary free, drawing near to her in her sorrow, and calling her by name. When grief clouds our vision, help us to hear Your voice in Your Word. Teach us to love You with the same devotion Mary showed. Comfort all who grieve today, and send us, as You sent her, to share the good news that You are alive. In Your name we pray. Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
