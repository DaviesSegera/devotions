When Hands Grow Weary: The Lord Is Our Banner

Exodus 17:8–16 · 5 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-10-11

When Moses’ arms grow weary, Aaron and Hur support him while Joshua fights below. Reflect on prayer, faithful work, sharing burdens, and giving the glory to God.

Key Verse
“Moses built an altar there and named it Yahweh-Nissi (which means ‘the LORD is my banner’).”
Exodus 17:15 (NLT)

Prayer, Shared Burdens, Faithful Action, and God-Given Victory

There are days when one person is facing the struggle, another is praying, and a third is simply trying to help someone keep going. Exodus 17 shows why each of those roles matters and why none of those who serve should take the honour that belongs to God.

Israel had just reached a place called Rephidim. There was no water, and the people had quarrelled with Moses and asked, “Is the LORD here with us or not?” Exodus 17:7 (NLT, excerpt) God answered by bringing water from a rock. Then another trial came. “While the people of Israel were still at Rephidim, the warriors of Amalek attacked them.” Exodus 17:8 (NLT)

Israel was a nation of former slaves, not an army. Years later, Moses reminded them that the attackers had come “when you were exhausted and weary, and they struck down those who were straggling behind.” Deuteronomy 25:18 (NLT, excerpt)

Moses did two things. He sent Joshua to the field: “Choose some men to go out and fight the army of Amalek for us.” And he went to the hill: “Tomorrow, I will stand at the top of the hill, holding the staff of God in my hand.” Exodus 17:9 (NLT, excerpts) Aaron and Hur climbed the hill with him.

On the hill, hands were raised in dependence. In the field, hands were busy with duty. Both belonged to one story under God.

As the battle went on, something remarkable happened. “As long as Moses held up the staff in his hand, the Israelites had the advantage. But whenever he dropped his hand, the Amalekites gained the advantage.” Exodus 17:11 (NLT)

The Bible does not record what Moses said on that hill. Ellen G. White explains that his raised hands expressed prayer and shows that prayer and work were meant to go together:

Ellen G. White’s insight · quotation“Yet divine strength is to be combined with human effort. Moses did not believe that God would overcome their foes while Israel remained inactive.”Patriarchs and Prophets, ch. 26, “From the Red Sea to Sinai,” p. 299 (PP 299.2)

The staff was not a magic stick. It was a signpost pointing beyond itself. Its message was simple: look to God. The psalmist asked the same question we all ask in times of trouble and answered it: “I look up to the mountains—does my help come from there? My help comes from the LORD, who made heaven and earth!” Psalm 121:1–2 (NLT)

The strength of prayer is not in how high our hands reach but in the God to whom we turn.

Then Moses grew tired. “Moses’ arms soon became so tired he could no longer hold them up. So Aaron and Hur found a stone for him to sit on. Then they stood on each side of Moses, holding up his hands. So his hands held steady until sunset.” Exodus 17:12 (NLT)

Notice what his friends did. They did not blame him. They noticed the need, found a practical way to help, took their places, and stayed until the day was done. Ellen White writes that, by this act, Aaron and Hur “showed the people their duty to sustain him in his arduous work” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 299 · PP 299.1.

That duty reaches beyond leaders. Every congregation has tired people: teachers, deacons, parents, caregivers, the sick, the grieving, and the lonely. “Share each other’s burdens, and in this way obey the law of Christ.” Galatians 6:2 (NLT) Holding up someone's hands may mean praying for them, bringing a meal, taking over a task so they can rest, or simply staying with them. It never means covering up wrongdoing or treating a leader as beyond question. And when our own arms are tired, we can sit on the stone that is offered to us.

A faithful church is not one where no hands ever grow tired; it is one where weary people are not left alone.

By sunset, the battle was over. “As a result, Joshua overwhelmed the army of Amalek in battle.” Exodus 17:13 (NLT) God told Moses to record the event on a scroll as a permanent reminder and to read it to Joshua, along with His solemn judgment on Amalek, an ancient nation that had attacked the weak. Then Moses built an altar and named it Yahweh-Nissi, which means “the LORD is my banner.” Exodus 17:15 (NLT, excerpt)

The altar did not honour Moses, Joshua, Aaron, or Hur. It did not honour the staff. It directed every gaze towards the LORD.

The same is true for us. Jesus is the One who never grows tired of His people. “He lives forever to intercede with God on their behalf.” Hebrews 7:25 (NLT, excerpt) In Him, we find grace to pray, strength to do our work, and love to help hold up someone else's hands.

God is our strength. Prayer and work belong together. When someone grows weary, we can help. And the LORD receives the glory.

Reflect

  1. What am I trying to face in my own strength when I need to seek God's help?
  2. Whose hands are growing weary beside me, and what practical help can I give this week?
  3. When help comes, who receives the glory—the LORD or I?

Key Takeaways

  1. Prayer and faithful work belong together. “Yet divine strength is to be combined with human effort.” Patriarchs and Prophets, p. 299 · PP 299.2
  2. No one should face the battle alone. “Then they stood on each side of Moses, holding up his hands.” Exodus 17:12 (NLT, excerpt)
  3. The glory belongs to the LORD. “Moses built an altar there and named it Yahweh-Nissi (which means ‘the LORD is my banner’).” Exodus 17:15 (NLT)

A prayer for today

LORD, You are our banner. When the struggle is greater than we can bear, teach us to look to You first and give us courage to do the work You have placed in our hands. Open our eyes to the people around us whose arms are tired, and make us faithful helpers who stay by their side. When we are weary, give us grace to receive help. Thank You, Jesus, for praying for us without growing tired. Make us one in Your love, and let all the glory be Yours. Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
