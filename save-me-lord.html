Save Me, Lord

Matthew 14:22–33 · 10 min read

By Dr. Davies Rene Segera · 2026-11-16

Peter steps out onto the water at Jesus’ word. When fear takes over, he cries for help and Jesus catches him. Reflect on trusting Christ in the storm and calling on Him when faith wavers.

Faith & trustPrayerHope & encouragement
Watch the Short

Key Verse
“Jesus immediately reached out and grabbed him. ‘You have so little faith,’ Jesus said. ‘Why did you doubt me?’”
Matthew 14:31 (NLT)

What do we make of a storm that comes while we’re doing what Jesus asked? The disciples faced that question one night on the Sea of Galilee. Before dawn, Peter would walk on water, begin to sink, and be pulled to safety by Jesus.

Earlier that day, Jesus had fed a crowd with five loaves and two fish. John describes how the crowd responded. “When Jesus saw that they were ready to force him to be their king, he slipped away into the hills by himself.” John 6:15, NLT Matthew picks up the story as Jesus sends His disciples across the lake. “Immediately after this, Jesus insisted that his disciples get back into the boat and cross to the other side of the lake, while he sent the people home.” Matthew 14:22, NLT

The disciples were reluctant to leave. Ellen G. White writes, “Never before had a command from Christ seemed so impossible of fulfillment.” The Desire of Ages, p. 378 · DA 378.3, excerpt They went as He asked, and Jesus withdrew to pray. “After sending them home, he went up into the hills by himself to pray. Night fell while he was there alone.” Matthew 14:23, NLT Ellen G. White gives us a glimpse of those hours of prayer. “For hours He continued pleading with God. Not for Himself but for men were those prayers.” The Desire of Ages, p. 379 · DA 379.1, excerpt

Jesus sent the disciples across the lake, and He prayed for them as they went.

The disciples obeyed Jesus, and still the storm came. “Meanwhile, the disciples were in trouble far away from land, for a strong wind had risen, and they were fighting heavy waves.” Matthew 14:24, NLT Mark tells us that Jesus saw their trouble. “He saw that they were in serious trouble, rowing hard and struggling against the wind and waves.” Mark 6:48, NLT, excerpt Ellen G. White describes how closely He watched over them.

“Jesus had not forgotten them. The Watcher on the shore saw those fear-stricken men battling with the tempest. Not for a moment did He lose sight of His disciples.”The Desire of Ages, p. 381 · DA 381.1, excerpt

Even as we follow Jesus, we may face illness in the family or the loss of a job. In the middle of such trouble, we can take heart that He sees us.

In the hours before dawn, Jesus came to them. “About three o’clock in the morning Jesus came toward them, walking on the water.” Matthew 14:25, NLT At first, the disciples did not recognise Him. “When the disciples saw him walking on the water, they were terrified. In their fear, they cried out, ‘It’s a ghost!’” Matthew 14:26, NLT Ellen G. White writes, “The One who has come for their help they count as an enemy.” The Desire of Ages, p. 381 · DA 381.2, excerpt Fear can make even the help God sends look like a threat.

Jesus spoke to calm their fear. “But Jesus spoke to them at once. ‘Don’t be afraid,’ he said. ‘Take courage. I am here!’” Matthew 14:27, NLT The NLT footnote explains that in Greek, Jesus simply says, “I am.” Long before that night, Job had spoken of what God alone could do. “He alone has spread out the heavens and marches on the waves of the sea.” Job 9:8, NLT The very waves they feared were beneath His feet.

Jesus came to His disciples while the storm was still raging.

Peter called back to Jesus. “Then Peter called to him, ‘Lord, if it’s really you, tell me to come to you, walking on the water.’” Matthew 14:28, NLT Ellen G. White describes Peter’s joy. “As soon as they could credit the wondrous fact, Peter was almost beside himself with joy.” The Desire of Ages, p. 381 · DA 381.4, excerpt Jesus invited Peter to come. “‘Yes, come,’ Jesus said. So Peter went over the side of the boat and walked on the water toward Jesus.” Matthew 14:29, NLT

Jesus’ invitation was enough for Peter to climb out of the boat and put his feet on the water.

Faith listens for Jesus’ call and trusts Him enough to take the next step.

The storm was still raging around Peter. “But when he saw the strong wind and the waves, he was terrified and began to sink. ‘Save me, Lord!’ he shouted.” Matthew 14:30, NLT Ellen G. White describes the moment Peter took his eyes off Jesus.

“Looking unto Jesus, Peter walks securely; but as in self-satisfaction he glances back toward his companions in the boat, his eyes are turned from the Saviour. The wind is boisterous. The waves roll high, and come directly between him and the Master; and he is afraid. For a moment Christ is hidden from his view, and his faith gives way.”The Desire of Ages, p. 381 · DA 381.5, excerpt

The same wind and waves had been there when Peter stepped out of the boat. Now they filled his view. A worrying test result, a mounting debt, or a broken friendship can fill our thoughts until it’s hard to see anything else.

Ellen G. White writes of Peter, “Faith and unbelief were blended in his words and actions.” Review and Herald, April 7, 1891 · RH April 7, 1891, par. 2, excerpt A worried father once brought that same struggle to Jesus. “I do believe, but help me overcome my unbelief!” Mark 9:24, NLT Jesus answered both cries for help.

As Peter sank, he turned back to Jesus. Ellen G. White describes the moment. “But while the billows talk with death, Peter lifts his eyes from the angry waters, and fixing them upon Jesus, cries, ‘Lord, save me.’” The Desire of Ages, p. 381 · DA 381.5, excerpt Peter had time for only a few words. That was enough.

Even as his faith wavered, Peter called to the One who could save him.

Jesus did not wait. “Jesus immediately reached out and grabbed him. ‘You have so little faith,’ Jesus said. ‘Why did you doubt me?’” Matthew 14:31, NLT Psalm 18 gives us words for a rescue like Peter’s. “He reached down from heaven and rescued me; he drew me out of deep waters.” Psalm 18:16, NLT

“So little faith” means there was still faith to build on. With Peter safe in His grasp, Jesus invited him to think about what had made him doubt. Ellen G. White describes their walk back to the boat. “Walking side by side, Peter’s hand in that of his Master, they stepped into the boat together.” The Desire of Ages, p. 381 · DA 381.6, excerpt

She then asks us to see ourselves in Peter’s place.

“When trouble comes upon us, how often we are like Peter! We look upon the waves, instead of keeping our eyes fixed upon the Saviour. Our footsteps slide, and the proud waters go over our souls. Jesus did not bid Peter come to Him that he should perish; He does not call us to follow Him, and then forsake us.”The Desire of Ages, p. 382 · DA 382.1, excerpt

She goes on to recall God’s promise to stay with His people. Here is how it reads in the NLT. “Do not be afraid, for I have ransomed you. I have called you by name; you are mine.” Isaiah 43:1, NLT, excerpt “When you go through deep waters, I will be with you. When you go through rivers of difficulty, you will not drown. When you walk through the fire of oppression, you will not be burned up; the flames will not consume you.” Isaiah 43:2, NLT Whatever deep waters we have to pass through, God promises to be with us.

Jesus caught Peter before asking why he had doubted.

Matthew tells us what happened when Jesus and Peter stepped back into the boat. “When they climbed back into the boat, the wind stopped.” Matthew 14:32, NLT “Then the disciples worshiped him. ‘You really are the Son of God!’ they exclaimed.” Matthew 14:33, NLT The same men who had cried out in fear now worshipped Jesus.

Ellen G. White explains what Jesus wanted Peter to learn that night. “In this incident on the sea He desired to reveal to Peter his own weakness,—to show that his safety was in constant dependence upon divine power.” The Desire of Ages, p. 382 · DA 382.2, excerpt Peter would need that lesson again. On the night before the cross, Jesus told him, “But I have pleaded in prayer for you, Simon, that your faith should not fail.” Luke 22:32, NLT, excerpt Peter stumbled again that night. The same Jesus who had caught him on the lake had already prayed for him.

Hebrews calls us to keep looking to Jesus. “We do this by keeping our eyes on Jesus, the champion who initiates and perfects our faith.” Hebrews 12:2, NLT, excerpt Ellen G. White describes what that kind of dependence looks like. “Only through realizing our own weakness and looking steadfastly unto Jesus can we walk securely.” The Desire of Ages, p. 382 · DA 382.4, excerpt

Perhaps you’re in a storm right now, and your faith feels small. Turn to Jesus with the faith you have. If a few words are all you can manage, let them be your prayer. The One who caught Peter sees you too.

When the waves rise, look to Jesus. When you begin to sink, call His name. His hand is still within reach.

Reflect

  1. What storm am I facing right now? Where can I see signs that Jesus is with me in it?
  2. What “waves” tend to draw my attention away from Jesus?
  3. If I called out to Jesus as simply and honestly as Peter did, what would I say?

Key Takeaways

  1. Jesus comes to us in the storm. Jesus saw the disciples struggling on the lake and came to reassure them while the storm was still raging. Matthew 14:24–27 · quoted above
  2. Keep your eyes on Jesus. Peter walked on the water at Jesus’ word. When the wind and waves filled his view, he began to sink. Matthew 14:30 · quoted above
  3. A few words can be enough. Peter cried out for help, and Jesus caught him at once. Only then did Jesus ask why he had doubted. Matthew 14:31 · quoted above

White, The Desire of Ages, ch. 40, “A Night on the Lake,” pp. 377–382 (DA 378.3, 379.1, 381.1, 381.2, 381.4, 381.5, 381.6, 382.1, 382.2, 382.4); Review and Herald, April 7, 1891, “The Character of Peter” (par. 2).

A prayer for today

Lord Jesus, thank You for seeing us when we struggle. When we’re afraid, help us hear You say, “Take courage.” Give us faith to step out at Your word. When trouble fills our thoughts, turn our eyes back to You. When we begin to sink, hear our cry and lift us up as You lifted Peter. Forgive us when we doubt You, and teach us to depend on You day by day. In Your name we pray. Amen.

— Dr. Davies Rene Segera
